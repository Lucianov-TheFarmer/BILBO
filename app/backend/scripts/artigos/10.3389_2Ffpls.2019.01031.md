#### 14 ABSTRACT

Soybean is an important economic crop for human diet, animal feeds and biodiesel due to high 15 protein and oil content. Its productivity is significantly hampered by salt stress, which impairs 16 plant growth and development by affecting gene expression, in part, through epigenetic 17 18 modification of chromatin status. However, little is known about epigenetic regulation of stress response in soybean roots. Here, we used RNA-seq and ChIP-seq technologies to study the 19 20 dynamics of genome-wide transcription and histone methylation patterns in soybean roots under 21 salt stress. 8798 soybean genes changed their expression under salt stress treatment. Wholegenome ChIP-seq study of an epigenetic repressive mark, histone H3 lysine 27 trimethylation 22 (H3K27me3), revealed the changes in H3K27me3 deposition during the response to salt stress. 23 24 Unexpectedly, we found that most of the inactivation of genes under salt stress is strongly 25 correlated with the *de novo* establishment of H3K27me3 in various parts of the promoter or 26 coding regions where there is no H3K27me3 in control plants. In addition, the soybean histone modifiers were identified which may contribute to *de novo* histone methylation and gene 27

silencing under salt stress. Thus, dynamic chromatin regulation, switch between active and

29 inactive modes, occur at target loci in order to respond to salt stress in soybean. Our analysis

30 demonstrates histone methylation modifications are correlated with the activation or inactivation

31 of salt-inducible genes in soybean roots.

#### 32 INTRODUCTION

33 Environmental changes affect the organisms in a wide range of situations (Lopez-Maury et al., 34 2008). Among the abiotic stress factors, salt stress is a well-known factor restricting germination and growth, seriously threatens the productivity of crops. Soybean, *Glycine max*, is one of the 35 36 most important crops with source of protein and oil in the human and animal diet, however its productivity is significantly affected by field condition such as soil salinity (Phang et al., 37 2008). In the northeast China, soybean used to be a major crop, and breeding soybean for 38 39 tolerance to high sodic conditions is important in some regions of China and the 40 world. Therefore, understanding the molecular mechanism of the soybean tolerance to salt stress

41 has been a major topic for crop scientists (Zhang et al., 2013).

Plants respond to abiotic stress by activation or inactivation of specific sets of genes to induce 42 43 certain molecular signaling pathways which rapidly alter physiological reactions and expression 44 initiation of responsive genes. Gene expression is directly influenced through chromatin states, 45 which is closely associated with epigenetic regulation including histone variants, histone post-46 translational modifications, and DNA methylation (Schwartz et al., 2010; Henikoff and Shilatifard, 2011; Lauria and Rossi, 2011). The modifications of the histone amino-terminal tails 47 are involved in assisting nucleosome remodeling as well as recruitment of specific transcription 48 49 factors. Specific amino acids within the N-terminal regions of histones are targets for a number 50 of covalent modifications, including methylation, phosphorylation, ubiquitination and acetylation. 51 Some of these marks, for example, acetylation of lysine 14 of histone H3 (H3K14ac) or 52 trimethylation of lysine 4 of Histone3 (H3K4me3), are generally associated with open, actively 53 transcribed genomic regions, whereas others, such as H3K9me3 or H3K27me3, are indicative of 54 a repressed chromatin state (Zhang et al., 2007; Li et al., 2008; Charron et al., 2009; Zhang et al., 2009; He et al., 2011). 55

56 The epigenetic changes including DNA methylation and/or histone modifications are 57 associated with altered gene expression for defense responses under abiotic (e.g., salt) stress 58 (Alexandre et al., 2009; Chinnusamy and Zhu, 2009; Ding et al., 2009; Zong et al., 2013; Chen et 59 al., 2014). In plants, there are increasing studies of regulating gene expression by histone 60 modification under various stresses (Chinnusamy and Zhu, 2009; Kumar and Wigge, 2010; Luo 61 et al., 2012a; Feng et al., 2016; Deng et al., 2017). In crop breeding, it is hard to keep balance of 62 disease resistance and yield. Recent studies showed that the rice *Pigm* locus contains a subset of genes encoding nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat (NLR) receptors. These receptors can lead 63 to durable resistance to the fungus without productivity penalty through DNA methylation 64 regulation (Deng et al., 2017). To cope with environmental stresses, plants often adopt a memory 65 response when facing primary stress for a quicker and stronger reaction to recurring stresses. 66 Feng et al., found that salt stress-induced proline accumulation is memorable. HY5- dependent 67 light signaling through H3K4me3 modification on a  $\Delta 1$ -pyrroline-5-carboxylate synthetase 1 68 69 (P5CS1) is required for such a memory response (Feng et al., 2016).

The covalent modifications were deposited or erased from target loci by the histone modifiers 70 71 including histone methyltransferase (HMTs) and histone demethylases (HDMs). All the known 72 HMTs in plants have a highly conserved domain, SET (Su(var)3-9, Enhancer-of-zeste, Trithorax) 73 which was also named as SDG (SET domain groups) proteins (Ng et al., 2007; Thorstensen et al., 74 2011). Many epigenetic modifiers' function has been well characterized. It has been reported that 75 some modifiers have been shown to be integrated in abiotic stress signaling pathways (Schubert 76 et al., 2006; Grini et al., 2009; Jeong et al., 2009; Guo et al., 2010; Ding et al., 2011; Lu et al., 77 2011; He et al., 2012; Yao et al., 2013; Gu et al., 2014; Cui et al., 2016). A plant trxG factor, ARABIDOPSIS HOMOLOG OF TRITHORAX1 (ATX1) with H3K4me3 methyltransferase 78 79 activity can promote transcription initiation by recruiting RNA Polymerase II (Alvarez-Venegas and Avramova, 2005; Saleh et al., 2008). ATX1 was found to be involved in drought stress 80 81 signaling in both ABA dependent and ABA-independent pathways, and an *atx1* mutant was shown to be hyposensitive to drought stress (Ding et al., 2009; Ding et al., 2011). Therefore, 82 chromatin modifications and epigenetics are directly linked to plants' responses to environmental 83 84 cues.

It is important to note, however, that most of currently studies focus on epigenetic 85 modifications at individual stress genes in plants. Second, there are more and more studies on 86 87 Arabidopsis, rice and maize, but limited knowledge of regulation of salt stress response through chromatin modifications in soybean plants. Moreover, there are no data on genome-wide 88 89 modification patterns when response to stress in soybean plants. In this study, we provide a 90 global view of H3K27me3 patterns in chromatin isolated from soybean roots with or without salt 91 stress treatment. Genome-wide expression patterns in control and salt stressed soybean were 92 compared with changes in the H3K27me3 levels of nucleosomes on stress-induced differentially 93 expressed genes. Using chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) of H3K27methylation antibodies 94 combined with genome-wide sequencing (ChIP-seq), we revealed different dynamic changes in H3K27me3 profiles taking place upon salt stress. The specific patterns of the H3K27me3 95 96 distributions including *de novo* methylation at up-regulated and down-regulated genes were 97 identified during the stress treatment. Moreover, we provide a comprehensive overview of the 98 histone modifiers which may work together to regulate differential H3K27me3 modification 99 leading to activation or inactivation of gene expression during salt stress in soybean.

#### 100 MATERIALS AND METHODS

#### **101 Plant Materials and Growth Condition**

102 The *Glycine* line, *Glycine max* Williams 82, was used in this study. Seeds were sterilized with 103 75% ethanol and then germinated in pots filled with coconut fiber. Soybean seedlings were 104 grown in soil in an incubator with 25°C/20°C (light/dark) and 16h/8h (light/dark) cycles until the second trifoliate leaves started expand. For the salt stress treatment, the uniformly growing plants 105 106 were kept in 0 mM/L, 50 mM/L, 75 mM/L, 100 mM/L, 150 mM/L and 200 mM/L of NaCl 107 solutions for 30 hours. After the treatment, the root tissues were harvested and frozen in liquid 108 nitrogen. As a control, the untreated seedlings (0 mM/L) were planted and harvested at the same 109 time with the stress-treated plants. The 100 mM/L salt treated seedlings were used for RNA-seq 110 and ChIP-seq analysis since the phenotypic differences were clear at this concentration which is 111 also commonly used for salinity test on soybean (Belamkar et al., 2014; Zeng et al., 2018). Three 112 replicates of the root samples both from control and 100 mM/L salt treatment were prepared for 113 consistency of the analysis.

#### 114 RNA-seq Library Construction and Analysis

115 Total RNA was extracted from the root of soybeans with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen) according 116 to the manufacturer's instructions. Library making, RNA-seq and data analysis were performed as described previously (Xu et al., 2018). PolyA<sup>+</sup> libraries were constructed using Illumina's 117 118 TruSeq Stranded mRNA Library Prep Kit. The size and quality of the resulting libraries was 119 examined using a Bioanalyzer 2100 and cDNA libraries from the RNA samples were prepared 120 for high throughput Illumina sequencing. Paired-end reads were generated with the Illumina 121 HiSeq 2500 system. Three independent biological sample replicates were employed. The RNA 122 sequencing reads were aligned to the *Glycine max* reference genome (*Glycine* max Wm82.a2.v1) 123 using TopHat2 (Kim et al., 2013). Genes that met the criterion of a detectable expression signal 124 in control or salt plants were further analyzed. The fold change (FC) was calculated by 125 comparing the expression level of the salt samples to control (salt/control). Briefly, the "Log2FC > 1 and p-adj < 0.05" was used as the threshold to judge the significance of gene 126 127 expression difference. Genes that display a greater than 2-FC in the salt-treated were designated 128 as up- or down-regulated if the salt RNA level was higher or lower than that of control plants, 129 respectively.

#### 130 **Real-Time Quantitative RT-PCR (qPCR)**

131 RT-qPCR was performed as described previously with minor modifications (Xu et al., 2018). 132 cDNAs were reverse transcribed with oligo (dT) from the total RNAs. RT-qPCR reaction was 133 carried out in a QuantStudio 6 Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems). At least three 134 independent experiments employing biological replicates were performed and three technical 135 replicates were done for each sample. Amplification of *Tubulin (Tub)* was used as an internal 136 control to normalize all data. Quantification was determined by applying the  $2^{-\Delta Ct}$  formula (Pu et 137 al., 2008). All gene-specific primers are listed in **Supplementary Table 1**.

#### 138 Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP) Assays and ChIP-seq Analysis

- 139 ChIP assay was performed from approximately 2 g of soybean roots as previously described
- 140 (Kim et al., 2012b; Xu et al., 2018). Briefly, fresh tissues of whole seedlings were infiltrated in
- 141 1% formaldehyde solution under a vacuum for 20 minutes to cross-link the chromatin. The

142 reaction was stopped by adding 0.1 M glycine. Formaldehyde fixed tissues were ground in liquid 143 nitrogen, nuclei isolated, chromatin extracted and sheared by sonication (Diagenode, Bioruptor 144 Plus; 1 minute on and 30 seconds off for 15 minutes) to generate 0.5 to 2 kb DNA fragments. 145 The aliquot of 1–2 ul of mix DNA samples and electrophoretic was used to determine the 146 sonication efficiency and average size of DNA fragments. A smear from 200 to 2,000 bp, but 147 concentrated 500 bp was observed in the sonicated samples and for further analysis. Anti-148 H3K27me3 (Millipore, #07-449) antibody was used to immunoprecipitate the fragmented 149 chromatin (IP, 200ul of IP solution plus 1ul of antibody as 200 times dilution). Cross-linking of 150 IP was reversed with 5 M NaCl, and DNA was precipitated with 100% EtOH. For the Input 151 control (Input), 5M NaCl was added to 0.5% of total chromatin before immunoprecipitation to 152 reverse the cross-linking and DNA was precipitated with 100% EtOH. The relative amount of 153 DNA was determined using a spectrophotometer (NanoDrop, ND1000). ChIP purified DNA was 154 amplified for 14 cycles using the Sigma Genomeplex Whole Genome Amplification (WGA2) kit 155 following the manufacturer's protocol (Sigma-Aldrich Co, Catalog Number WGA2). More than 20 ng of IP DNA from each sample was used for library generation following the manufacturer's 156 157 instructions. Three independent biological sample replicates were employed.

158 Library construction and deep sequencing were performed as described previously (Wang et al., 159 2016; Xu et al., 2018). ChIP DNA samples described above were prepared for high throughput 160 Illumina sequencing (one hundred and fifty pair-end read sequencing). The ChIP-seq data was 161 analyzed as described previously (Wang et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2018). The first 30 base pairs 162 from the 5' end containing primer or adapter sequences were trimmed. The 3' end of the 163 sequencing reads were trimmed based on base-call quality using the BWA quality trim algorithm 164 (Li and Durbin, 2009). The sequencing reads were aligned Glycine max reference genome 165 (Glycine max Wm82.a2.v1). Only uniquely mapped reads that mapped to one location of the genome only (as opposed to those that mapped to multiple reads) were retained for peak calling. 166 167 Three biological replicates were performed for each sample. Each input was used as a control for peak calling for each sample using MACS 1.4 (Zhang et al., 2008). The statistical identification 168 of peaks was performed for each sample using MACS with the default  $10^{-5}$  *p*-value cutoff. The 169 170 three replicates results were overlapped using BedTools (Quinlan and Hall, 2010). The resulting 171 BED format files that contain the peak location were visualized with the Integrated Genome

172 Viewer (Robinson et al., 2011; Thorvaldsdottir et al., 2013).

173 ChIP-seq results were verified by ChIP-qPCR for selected genes as previously described (Xu et 174 al., 2018). The relative amounts of Input and IP DNA of all samples were determined using a spectrophotometer (NanoDrop, ONE C). The diluted ChIP DNA was analyzed by qPCR 175 176 according to the procedure described above for RT-qPCR. Three replicates were done for each sample. Quantification was determined by applying the 2<sup>-Ct</sup> formula (SuperArray ChIP-qPCR) 177 178 user manual; Bioscience Corporation). Average immunoprecipitates from chromatin isolated 179 independently are expressed on graphs as percentage of corresponding input DNA, with error 180 bars representing the standard deviations. All gene-specific primers are listed in Supplementary 181 Table 1.

The *p*-value for the gene expression changes of methylated or *de novo* H3K27me3 genes in salttreated soybean was calculated by using hypergeometric statistical test as described previously
(Xu et al., 2018).

#### 185 Plasmid Constructions and *Arabidopsis* Transformation

The resulting vector was 187 188 mobilized into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101. T3 progeny homozygotes were obtained for further analysis.

#### 194 **RESULTS**

#### 195 Gene Expression Change in Soybean in Response to Salt Treatment

196 Salt stress is a major abiotic stress that limits the yield of many crop species. In many plants,

197 roots are the primary site of salinity perception. To better understand the mechanisms active in

198 the response of roots to salt stress, we studied salt response in soybean with different 199 concentrations of salt treatment (see methods and materials). We first evaluated the salt 200 concentration that stressed soybean growth. Three biological replicates were subjected to well-201 watered, salt treatment at different concentrations. We observed the phenotypes of salt-treated 202 plants and found as the concentration of salt stress increased, root growth was increasingly 203 retarded. As a result, we selected to grow roots in 100 mM salt to study the impact of salt stress 204 on gene expression in soybean (Figure 1A). We employed RNA-seq technology to analyze 205 genome-wide mRNA transcript levels in soybean roots under 0 mM (control) and 100 mM of 206 salt treatment (salt). The RNA samples from the soybean roots grown with and without salt were 207 sequenced by the Illumina Genome Analyzer. For each sample, we obtained approximately 42-208 54 million reads, of which 89.15-96.65% were mapped to the soybean reference genome 209 (Supplementary Table 2).

210 From the sequence alignment data, the expression quantification for each sample was 211 calculated using Cufflinks (Trapnell et al., 2013). To identify the salt responsive genes, a core set 212 of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) under salt stress in soybean were examined. We 213 classified them as up- or down-regulated genes with statistically significant two-fold expression 214 changes in the samples treated with 100 mM salt compared to 0 mM control plants. A total of 215 44,346 soybean genes with confident expression were analyzed (Supplementary Table 3). Out of 216 these genes, 8,798 (19.9%) were found to be differentially expressed genes under salt treatment 217 compared to control plants, in which 4646 genes are up-regulated and 4152 were down – 218 regulated (Figure 1B). There are a little bit more up-regulated genes than down-regulated genes 219 in soybean roots after salt treatment.

#### 220 GO Analysis of Salt Response Gene in Soybean

regulation, response to stress, defense response, regulation of defense response and histone
methylation represented by the up-regulated enriched genes in these categories (Figure 2).

#### 260 Trimethylation of H3K27 under Salt Stress in Soybean

261 Trimethylated histone H3 at lysine residues 27 (H3K27me3) has been detected in many 262 organisms, including Arabidopsis, rice and maize (Butenko and Ohad, 2011). It is a hallmark of gene silencing (Schubert et al., 2006; Zheng and Chen, 2011). However, whether this repressive 263 264 mark is involved, and to what extent, in salt stress response in soybean is unknown. To 265 determine the alteration of chromatin dynamics and transcriptional apparatus that respond to 266 environmental changes, we applied ChIP-seq to monitor the changes of H3K27me3 levels at 267 genome-wide scale under salt stress treatment in soybean (Figure 4). ChIP-seq was performed by 268 using an antibody specifically recognizing H3K27me3 (Pu et al., 2013; Xu et al., 2018), and the 269 precipitated DNAs were then sequenced. After sequencing, we obtained about 50 million of 270 clean reads with 75-85 % of the reads that could be mapped to the soybean genome 271 (Supplementary Table 4). Verification of ChIP-seq results using Pearson correlation analysis 272 showed statistically significant correlation coefficients among the biological replicates for each 273 sample (Supplementary Figure 2). Genomic regions associated with H3K27me3 modification 274 were identified by using MACS software (Zhang et al., 2008). The average length of peaks is 275 around 700 bp in samples of control and salt-treated plants (Supplementary Figure 3).

276 The MACS peak finding program identified thousands of H3K27me3 enriched peaks in control and salt-treated samples ( $p < 10^{-3}$ ) across the whole chromosome (Figure 4A and 277 278 Supplementary Figure 4), which correspond to 1707 and 746 annotated genes, respectively 279 (Supplementary Figure 3B). As reported previously in Arabidopsis (Kim et al., 2012b; Xu et al., 280 2018), H3K27me3 peaks tend to be broad, often covering the entire transcriptional unit, hence 281 we used a very strict statistical cutoff for peak identification. In control plants, the 1707 genes 282 were termed K27 genes in the next analysis vs. de novo\_K27 genes (Supplementary Table 5 and 283 Supplementary Table 6). We plotted the average H3K27me3 signal of the 1707 K27 genes 284 across the 7 kb region surrounding the transcription start site (TSS) and the transcription end site 285 (TES) in the control soybean (Figure 4B). Similar to that of *Arabidopsis*, a broad H3K27me3

286 enrichment covers the entire transcriptional unit with the strongest signal around the TSS region, 287 whereas the H3K27me3 signal gradually declined towards the 3' end and increased around the 288 TES region, suggesting the conservation and divergence of epigenetic patterns across plant 289 species (Figure 4B). We then checked the histone modification pattern of 746 H3K27me3 290 marked genes in salt-treated soybean which remained remarkably similar to that in control plants 291 (Figure 4B and Supplementary Table 6). Compared to H3K27me3 pattern in control plants, the 292 epigenetic marks of H3K27me3 showed a greater enrichment in salt-stressed samples (Figure 293 4B), suggests that stress caused changes in chromatin structure and histone modification which 294 accompany changes in gene expression in response to abiotic stresses.

#### 295 Relationship between Changes in H3K27me3 and Gene Expression under Salt Stress

296 H3K27me3 has been proposed to be correlated with gene silencing in many organisms (Pu and 297 Sung, 2015). So we questioned if H3K27me3 modification is correlated with a different 298 expression level under salt stress. We combined the specific H3K27me3 modification datasets 299 with our DEGs to identify the relationship between H3K27me3 modification and different expression levels. We found that 829 of the 1707 H3K27me3 specifically modified genes were 300 301 not expressed in both of control and salt samples, despite some of them were not trimethylated 302 (Table 2) which may be caused by our criteria used for analysing the RNA-seq data (see 303 materials and methods) as reported in our previous study (Xu et al., 2018). It is also possibly 304 caused by the fact that not all genes expressed in soybean roots. By excluding those nonexpressed genes, only 878 (51%) expressed genes were trimethylated on H3K27 in the control 305 306 and salt datasets (Table 2). These specific H3K27me3 genes were then checked for the 307 expression level changes in the corresponding treatment, and the numbers of up- and down-308 regulated genes in each of the specific H3K27me3 modification datasets were further analysed 309 (Table 2). Under salt stress treatment, 170 of 336 K27 genes (50.6%) were up-regulated (Table 310 2). Statistical tests of the genome-wide relationship between reduced H3K27me3 and 311 transcriptional deregulation (Table 2) in the salt-treated plants showed that 50.6% (pvalue=  $1.03 \times 10^{-15}$ ) of up-regulated genes also had reduced H3K27me3 levels. 312

We noticed that H3K27me3 is associated with expression changes of specific salt stress genes
which likely contribute to response to environmental changes. 11 genes, *Glyma.17G006800*,

315 *Glyma.*08G070700, *Glyma.*08G127000, *Glyma.*15G252200, *Glyma.*12G104800,

316 *Glyma.07G110300, Glyma.17G223600, Glyma.20G021200, Glyma.04G131800,* 

- 317 *Glyma.04G187000, Glyma.20G072600* were significantly up-regulated in salt treated samples
- 318 (Supplementary Table 3). Six salt stress genes, *Glyma.08G070700*, *Glyma.08G127000*,
- 319 *Glyma.12G104800, Glyma.07G110300, Glyma.04G131800, Glyma.04G187000,* showed lower
- H3K27me3 levels and higher mRNA expression levels after salt treatment (Figure 3 and Figure
- 5A). To confirm the ChIP-seq results, we performed ChIP-qPCR on three selected salt response
- 322 genes, *Glyma.07G110300*, *Glyma.04G131800*, *Glyma.04G187000*, that showed enhanced
- expression levels under stress, using the un-methylated *Glyma*.05G203800 (*Tubulin*) gene as the
- negative control (Figure 5B). We found that these salt response genes had much higher
- H3K27me3 levels in the control plants which decreased greatly under salt stress indicating that
- 326 salt stress can remove the deposition of repressive chromatin marks at these loci during stress
- 327 treatment (Figure 5). These results show that plants respond to the environmental changes
- through the transcriptional machinery in which transcription was turned on or shut down by
- 329 changing the mode of histone modifications between activation and inactivation on all of stress
- 330 response genes.

## 331 Salt Stress Causes *de novo* Histone Methylation and Gene Silencing under Salt Stress in 332 Soybean

Surprisingly, we found that there were only 5 of 878 K27genes (0.5%) with increased 333 334 H3K27me3 marks in salt treatment. We then asked what happened to the de novo\_K27 genes 335 after salt stress treatment. It has been reported that de novo methylation can occur in a locus-336 specific manner during development in yeast, plant and animals (Ooi et al., 2007; Bouyer et al., 337 2011; Morselli et al., 2015; Stewart et al., 2015). Among 746 H3K27me3 marked genes in salt-338 treated soybean, we found 651 genes appeared to be marked *de novo* H3K27me3 in salt-treated 339 plants which mainly contributed to the greater H3K27me3 pattern in salt-treated plant 340 (Supplementary Table 6).

- 341 Among 651 genes, there are 294 genes with expression data in our RNA-seq analysis (Table
- 342 3). Statistical tests of the genome-wide relationship between increased H3K27me3 and
- 343 transcriptional deregulation (Table 3) in the salt-treated plants showed that 33.7% (p-

value= $1.9 \times 10^{-31}$ ) of down-regulated genes also had increased H3K27me3 levels. Some of these

genes are stress-responsive genes such as *Glyma.14G213600*, *Glyma.11G204800*,

346 *Glyma.09G041000*, *Glyma.13G043800* and *Glyma.17g022500* (Figure 3 and Figure 5).

347 Although the gain of H3K27me3 is not associated with all down-regulated genes, it contributed

the greater level of H3K27me3 modification in salt stress condition compared to that in controlplants.

## Changes in Histone Methylation and Demethylation Contribute to Changes in H3K27me3 Modification Levels under Salt Stress in Soybean

352 The H3K27 was trimethylated by histone methyl transferases (HMTs) and demethylated by

HDMs (Papp and Muller, 2006; Horton et al., 2010; Pu and Sung, 2015). To explore how histone

354 modifications were regulated when under salt stress in soybean, we used RNA-seq data to

investigate the candidate causal genes of methyltransferase or demethylase for salt response. We

356 identified 43 HMT proteins from Soybase according to the protein sequence homology with

357 *Arabidopsis* HMTs (Grant et al., 2010). Specifically, 9 soybean genes which are homologous to

358 Arabidopsis known methyltransferase genes CURLY LEAF (CLF), ATX and SDG, were down-

regulated, and 2 genes are up-regulated with significant *p*-value in salt-treated plants (Table 4).

360 CLF has been well characterized in *Arabidopsis* to work specifically as H3K27

361 methyltransferases (Katz et al., 2004; Schatlowski et al., 2010). ATX and SDG proteins were

known to methylate H3K4 and limit deposition of H3K27me3 on target loci (Ding et al., 2007;

363 Carles and Fletcher, 2009; Grini et al., 2009; Berr et al., 2010; Guo et al., 2010; Tang et al., 2012;

Yao et al., 2013). To verify the RNA-seq results, we examined the RNA levels of 4 selected

methyltransferase genes, *Glyma.16G207200*, *Glyma.06G223300*, *Glyma.17G215200* and

366 *Glyma.11G054100*, by qPCR (Figure 6A). The expression levels determined by qPCR and RNA-

seq were highly correlated (Figure 6A), indicating that the results obtained by the independent

368 methods are consistent. Therefore, these soybean proteins may function as methyltransferase to

alter histone modifications of target loci for salt response.

370 Jumonji C (JmjC) proteins are known to demethylate all of the mono-, di and trimethylated

371 lysines of histones (Chen et al., 2011). There are over 20 JmjC proteins have been discovered in

372 *Arabidopsis* which are able to demethylate lysine H3K4, H3K9, H3K27, and H3K36. We

373 checked the expression pattern of 21 JmjC proteins in salt-treated soybeans (Table 5).

- 374 Interestingly, we found that 3 of JmjC proteins were down-regulated, and 1 was up-regulated
- 375 (Table 5 and Figure 6B). 2 of down-regulated genes, *Glyma.04G192000* and *Glyma.20G181000*,
- are homologues to *Arabidopsis Early flowering 6 (REF6)* and *Relative of ELF6 (ELF6)* which
- are known demethylases to mediate histone methylation (Yu et al., 2008; Lu et al., 2011). These
- results indicated the involvement of histone modifiers in the changing H3K27me3, subsequently
- 379 transcript levels during salt stress.

## 380 Overexpression of One Soybean Gene Enhances the Salt Tolerance in Transgenic 381 Arabidopsis

382 The differentially expressed genes identified through RNA-seq were considered to be 383 preferentially genes involved in abiotic stress responses, suggesting their stress regulation in the 384 soybean plants. To investigate whether these genes would affect the stress response, we selected 385 one of mis-regulated genes, Glyma. 17G022500 and studied its effect on salt tolerance or 386 sensitivity in Arabidopsis. The transgenic Arabidopsis plants containing Glyma. 17G022500 under the control of the CaMV35S promoter in the pCAMBIA1301 vector were generated. 387 Independent transgenic lines were obtained by Hygromycin-resistance selection and confirmed 388 389 by genotyping PCR. The homozygous T3 generation of three independent overexpression lines, 390 namely OE-1, OE-2, OE-3, and the control line (WT) were used for further analysis 391 (Supplementary Figure 5). To avoid adverse effects of salt treatment on germination, we 392 transferred 5 days' seedlings of WT, OE-1, OE-2 and OE-3, from MS plates to MS medium 393 containing 150 mM of salt and grew them for an additional 5 days under SD conditions. After 5 394 days' salt stress, the transgenic *Glyma*. 17G022500 lines displayed a higher salt tolerance than the 395 WT plants (Figure 7). As shown in Figure 7A, the WT plants became severely wilted and 396 impaired with white cotyledons and leaves after salt stress. However, the transgenic 397 *Glyma.17G022500* lines showed more open, green leaves in all three independent lines (Figure 398 7A). The *Glyma*. 17G022500 transgenic lines displayed significantly higher survival rate than the 399 WT plants after salt treatment (Figure 7B). Furthermore, we found that the roots in the transgenic 400 lines grew longer than that in the WT plants on salt plates (Figure 7C). These results indicate that 401 overexpression of *Glyma*.17G022500 enhances *Arabidopsis* salt stress tolerance which

402 confirmed our RNA-seq results.

#### 403 **DISCUSSION**

The soybean gene methylation pattern is characteristic of plant methylation pattern. Here, we 404 405 investigated the modification profiles of H3K27me3 after salt stress treatment in soybean. 406 H3K27me3 was detected mainly in TSS and TES regions and 1707 annotated genes were 407 identified with H3K27me3 marks (Figure 4), which displayed the conservation and divergence 408 of epigenetic patterns to previous studies in Arabidopsis (Zhang et al., 2007; Kim et al., 2012b). 409 We further analysed K27, de novo K27 genes as well as DEGs and revealed different dynamic 410 changes in H3K27me3 profiles taking place upon salt stress. The specific H3K27me3 patterns 411 including *de novo* methylation at up-regulated and down-regulated genes were identified during 412 the stress treatment. In addition, a comprehensive overview of the histone modifiers were 413 identified which may regulate differential H3K27me3 modification leading to activation or 414 inactivation of gene expression during salt stress in soybean. The certain proportion of 415 H3K27me3-modified genes without expression support also implies that the H3K27me3 level 416 may be associated with expression levels of a subset of genes in soybean genome by working 417 together with other factors, such as the HMT, SDG proteins which bind the H3K27me3 site (Papp and Muller, 2006; Schuettengruber et al., 2011; Thorstensen et al., 2011). 418

419 The differentially expressed genes identified in this study were considered to be the key genes 420 involved in the stress response mechanism in the plants. Some of them have been shown to be 421 related to slat response in soybean. For example, *GmSALT3/GmCHX1* (*Glyma.03G171600*) 422 which is a gene associated with salt tolerance with great potential for soybean improvement 423 showed down-regulated expression pattern after salt treatment (Guan et al., 2014; Qi et al., 2014; 424 Liu et al., 2016). The Na + /H+ antiporter gene GmNHX1 (*Glyma.20G229900*) which can 425 enhance salt tolerance of soybean roots (Li et al., 2006; Sun et al., 2006; Yang et al., 2017). A 426 soybean glycogen synthase kinase 3 gene which can enhance to lerance to salt was up-regulated 427 in salt treated soybean in this study (Wang et al., 2018). Other known salt responsive genes 428 identified through RNA-seq analysis (Zeng et al., 2018) such as *Glyma.02G228100*, Glvma.04G180400, Glvma.03G226000, Glvma.08G189600, Glyma.02G228100 et al., were also 429 430 identified in this study. We also identified new candidate genes for salt response in soybean. For 431 example, the gene on Chr. 7, *Glyma.07G110300*, which was up-regulated in the salt-treated 432 plants (Figure 3) was annotated as "UDP-glucosyltransferase superfamily protein" in this study,

433 which was in agreement with earlier observations that the glucosyltransferase modulates abiotic 434 stress tolerance in Arabidopsis (Tognetti et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2015). The gene 435 Glyma.04G131800, which was annotated as "prohibitin-3, mitochondrial", was also up-regulated 436 in the salt-treated soybeans (Figure 3). The members of prohibitin family acted in stress response 437 (Wang et al., 2010; Seguel et al., 2018). The gene Glyma.04G187000 encodes a histone 438 deacetylase which is a histone modifier with direct function in regulation of stress response in 439 plants (Chen et al., 2010; Chen and Wu, 2010; Luo et al., 2012b; Zheng et al., 2016). 440 Overexpression of one mis-regulated gene, Glyma. 17G022500, in Arabidopsis resulted in higher 441 survival rates than those in WT lines under salt stress (Figure 7B), and the resistance to salt was 442 significantly different (Figure 7). Therefore, we conclude that *Glyma*. 17G022500 has an important effect on resistance to salt stress. This analysis of gene expression patterns between 443 444 control and salt plants provides a number of candidate genes which might be directly or indirectly involved in the stress response trait. The further genetic analysis and transformation 445 446 experiments could be used to confirm their roles in salt stress response in the soybean genotypes.

The repression of genes in development mediated by H3K27me3 modification is a highly 447 conserved mechanism in both plants and animals. There are several thousand genes, ~ 20% of all 448 449 transcribed genes, are marked by such modifications in Arabidopsis (Zhang et al., 2007; Hennig 450 and Derkacheva, 2009; Lu et al., 2011; Kim et al., 2012b; Xu et al., 2018). Here, there are only 451 ~2000 (5%) genes identified as H3K27me3 marked genes in soybean which is lower than the 452 average percentage in Arabidopsis. Our results showed that H3K27me3 was correlated with 453 only small parts of genome-wide transcript changes of mis-regulated genes during salt stress response in soybean (Table 2 and Table 3). This may be due to H3K27me3 not being the only 454 455 repressive histone modification marks for gene silencing in soybean since other repressive or 456 active histone modification marks may play a vital role in regulating gene expression in response to stress (Chinnusamy and Zhu, 2009; Kim et al., 2012a; Liew et al., 2013; Zong et al., 457 458 2013; Yang et al., 2014; Liu et al., 2018). It has been reported that many histone modification 459 marks such as active marks: H3K27ac, H3K4me3, H3K36me3, H3K9ac, and repressive marks: 460 H3K9me3, H2K119ub (Bratzel et al., 2010; Gu et al., 2013; Yang et al., 2014; Xu et al., 2016), 461 are known to be positively or negatively correlated with active or silencing transcription in plants. Indeed, here we found the gene of *Glyma.04G187000* which encodes a histone 462

deacetylase was up-regulated in soybean under salt stress (Figure 3), suggesting that it mayregulate gene expression through histone acetylation.

465 Chromatin accessibility is defined as the availability of DNA sequences for molecular interactions, typically mediated through by DNA binding factors and nucleosomes that are the 466 467 major factors of chromatin accessibility (van Steensel, 2011). Nucleosome-free regions have 468 been observed in many organisms and are associated with transcriptionally active genes 469 (Henikoff and Shilatifard, 2011). How do these H3K27me3 marks induce silencing of genes 470 expression? The H3K27me3 marks are mainly mediated by Polycomb group (PcG) proteins 471 which cause gene expression by histone modification and nucleosome condensation. Recent 472 studies reported that PcG-mediated H3K27me3 can spread on chromosome and lead to 473 chromatin compaction (Xu et al., 2018). In the absence of PcG genes, the maintenance of 474 chromatin integrity with gene repression by directly associating with target gene loci became 475 lesser extent. The chromatin cannot be a tightly folded structure with lower levels of H3K27me3 476 modifications (Becker and Workman, 2013; Kingston and Tamkun, 2014). Therefore, more 477 repressive histone marks on a given gene lead to lower transcript levels, whereas less marks 478 cause higher expression levels, which is consistent with our results in this study. Despite K27 or 479 de novo\_K27 genes, the decreasing in H3K27me3 levels accompanies the de-regulation of gene 480 expression in salt stress treatment (Figure 3 and Figure 5). However, *de novo* enrichment of 481 H3K27me3 on target genes leads to repression of expression (Figure 3 and Figure 5). These 482 results indicated that H3K27me3 play vital roles in maintaining the appropriate chromatin 483 conformation and integrity, thereby avoiding uncontrolled transcriptional activity when response 484 to abiotic stresses (Figure 8).

485 Indeed, H3K27me3 was negatively correlated with transcript levels in all organisms. A high 486 level of histone H3K27 methylation results in low transcript levels and low H3K27me3 487 modification levels, lead to actively transcribed genes (Pu and Sung, 2015). In our study, we 488 noticed that most H3K27me3 marked genes were identified in control plants with the basal 489 expression levels (Supplementary Table 3). The decrease in repressive H3K27me3 marks of 490 most H3K27me3 genes identified in control plants with up-regulated expression in salt stress is 491 consistent with the notion that the absence of repressive chromatin marks could result in the 492 activation of transcript (Table 2). However, the fact that the whole H3K27me3 pattern in salt

493 stress plants was greater than that in control plants was unexpected, although consistent with the 494 differential gene expression pattern (Figure 4). In other words, most K27me3 marked genes 495 were mainly those with low expression levels, under stressed conditions. In contrast, genome-496 wide H3K27me3 pattern in salt treated plants did not show such a trend, indicating that new or 497 de novo H3K27me3 marks occurred after salt treatment which may underlie the association of 498 salt-responsive patterns, down-regulation, with differential expression levels. Indeed, we found 499 650 H3K27me3 marked genes which were not trimethylated in control plants gained 500 H3K27me3 marks after salt stress treatment. The *de novo* methylation has been reported that the 501 new modification can occur in a locus-specific manner during development in yeast, plant and 502 animals (Ooi et al., 2007; Bouyer et al., 2011; Morselli et al., 2015; Stewart et al., 2015). This de 503 novo methylation event observed in our study largely shapes the methylation pattern of K27 504 genes after salt treatment, with additional changes occurring in gene expression required for 505 stress response.

506 In plants and animals, the histone modification of H3K27me3 maintains the developmentally regulated genes in silenced chromatin status. The removal and establishment of H3K27me3 507 508 marks at specific target genes is a dynamic and reversible process and is therefore critically 509 important for normal development. These modifications are carried out by the histone modifiers 510 - histone methyltransferases and histone demethylases. To address how methylation and 511 demethylation were well established in salt stress, we identified some modifiers which may 512 cause changes of H3K27me3 pattern and gene expression observed in our study (Table 4 and 513 Table 5). In plants, all the HMTs have a well-known conserved SET domain and also named as 514 SET domain groups (SDG) proteins (Thorstensen et al., 2011). In Arabidopsis, the methyltransferases trimethylate H3K27, including CLF, MEDEA (MEA) and SWINGER (SWN) 515 516 (Hennig and Derkacheva, 2009; Liu et al., 2010; Butenko and Ohad, 2011; Zheng and Chen, 517 2011). Here, we found 43 potential HMT genes with expression in control plants which 518 correspond with different Arabidopsis HMTs, such as CLF, ATX, ATXR, SDG and SUVH (Table 519 4). There are 11 genes which showed different expression pattern in salt stress (Table 4 and 520 Figure 6A). Histone methylation was reversible through the JmjC Jumonji C domain containing 521 proteins and the lysine-specific demethylase (LSD1). Interestingly, we found 21 JmjC genes 522 were expressed in control plants and 4 of them showed differential expression levels (Table 5

523 and Figure 6B). Among them, *Glyma.04G192000* and *Glyma.20G181000* are homologues of 524 Arabidopsis REF6 and ELF6 respectively, which are well characterized demethylases. It has 525 been reported that the REF6 protein, also known as JMJ12, can specifically demethylate 526 H3K27me3 at its target gene loci to active gene expression (Lu et al., 2011). The REF6 527 mutants cause the ectopic accumulation of H3K27me3 at hundreds of genes and a number of 528 defective developmental phenotypes (Yu et al., 2008; Cui et al., 2016; Li et al., 2016). ELF6 529 identified as an H3K27me2/3-specific demethylase closely related to *REF6* was rquired for 530 removal of H3K27me3 from the *Flowering Locus C (FLC)* locus in developing embryos in 531 vernalized plants (Crevillen et al., 2014). These results suggested that these potential HMT or 532 JmjC protein may function as similar roles to response for establishment or removal of 533 H3K27me3 with conserved mechanisms of the dynamic regulation of H3K27me3 between 534 Arabidopsis and soybean.

535 Based on our results and previous studies, we proposed a hypothesis to illustrate epigenetic 536 regulation of salt stress response in soybean (Figure 8): The DNA sequence of genes with low 537 expression levels may be tightly wrapped around the nucleosome and blocked from transcript 538 activation by an unknown mechanism. When plants are subjected to salt stress, for K27 marked 539 genes, decreased levels of H3K27me3 mediated by JmjC proteins release the DNA sequence 540 from the nucleosome for the induced transcription process. DNA sequences of genes with high 541 expression levels are often maintained with a low density of nucleosomes and low levels of 542 inactive histone modification. In contrast, for de novo\_K27 genes under stress conditions, 543 increased modification levels of inactive marks mediated by HMT proteins on target genes can 544 cause chromatin compaction, thus reducing the gene expression level. However, many details, 545 such as how these JmjC and HMT genes find the proper context and being recruited to establish 546 repressive modification in this hypothesis, need to be clarified by further experiments.

Taken together, our findings described here support a model in which H3K27me3 was
closely associated to salt responsive genes under stress conditions in soybean. H3K27me3
modification levels were negatively correlated with the expression level changes of a portion of
the salt-responsive genes in soybean. The salt stress can cause *de novo* methylation events in
gene regulation for stress response. We identified histone methyltransferases and JmjC domaincontaining demethylases in soybean, providing an overview of H3K27me3-mediated salt

553 responsive network. These results suggest that histone modifications may play important but 554 largely unknown roles in the stress responses. It will be of interest to determine and explore 555 how these proteins play roles at specific target genes to mediate local histone methylation 556 enrichments when response to abiotic stress. The information gathered here will be of particular 557 interest for future studies on the evolution of epigenetic-mediated stress mechanism and the 558 divergence of functionality in crop plants. In addition to the potential roles of histone 559 modifications in influencing stress response, the combination of technical innovation such as 560 synthetic biology and genome editing, will allow greater control of conferring stress tolerance 561 for crop improvement in future agriculture.
