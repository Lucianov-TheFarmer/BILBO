## Histone deacetylases HDA6 and HDA9 coordinately regulate valve cell elongation through affecting auxin signaling in Arabidopsis

Liangbing Yuan <sup>a</sup> , Xue Chen <sup>a</sup> , Huhui Chen <sup>a</sup> , Keqiang Wu <sup>b</sup> , Shangzhi Huang a, \*

<sup>a</sup> State Key Laboratory of Biocontrol and Guangdong Key Laboratory of Plant Resource, School of Life Sciences, Sun Yat-Sen University, Guangzhou, China <sup>b</sup> Institute of Plant Biology, College of Life Science, National Taiwan University, Taipei, 106, Taiwan

## abstract

Both Histone Deacetylases HDA6 and HDA9 belong to class I subfamily of RPD3/HDA1 HDACs. Loss-offunction mutants of HDA9 form slightly blunt siliques. However, the involvement of HDA6 in regulating silique tip growth is unclear. In this study, we show that HDA6 acts redundantly with HDA9 in regulating the elongation of valve cells in the silique tip. Although the hda6 single mutant does not exhibit a detectable silique phenotype, the silique tip of hda6 hda9 double mutant displays a more severe bulge, a morphology we termed as "nock-shaped". The valve cells of the silique tip of hda9 are longer than wild-type, and loss of HDA6 in hda9 enhances the valve cell elongation phenotype. The transcript levels of auxin-signaling-related genes are mis-regulated in hda9 and hda6 hda9 siliques, and the GFP reporter driven by the auxin response promoter DR5 is weaker in hda9 or hda6 hda9 than wild-type or hda6. Thus, our findings reveal that HDA6 and HDA9 coordinately control the elongation of silique valve cells through regulating the expression of auxin-related genes in silique tips.

## 1. Introduction

Acetylation and deacetylation of histones play vital roles in the regulation of gene expression in eukaryotes [[1](#page-4-0)]. It has been shown that the acetylation of lysines is highly dynamic and regulated by the opposing actions of two families of enzymes, histone acetyltransferases (HATs) and histone deacetylases (HDACs) [[2,3](#page-4-0)]. The acetylation of histone tails by HATs is associated with active transcription, while deacetylation by histone deacetylase complexes normally results in gene repression [\[4](#page-4-0)]. The eukaryotic HDACs have been grouped into three families based on their homology with the yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) proteins RPD3 (reduced potassium deficiency 3)/HDA1 and SIR2 (silent information regulator 2) [\[5\]](#page-4-0).

Twelve HDACs belong to the RPD3/HDA1 group [[5](#page-4-0)] and are involved in various biological processes. HDA6, one of the RPD3/ HDA1 family protein, is involved in jasmonic acid mediated plant defense responses, senescence and flowering [[6,7](#page-4-0)]. HDA6 is also involved in the repression of embryonic properties after germination in Arabidopsis [[8\]](#page-4-0). HDA9 is another member of the RPD3/HDA1 family proteins and has close relationship with HDA6. HDA9

promotes histone H3 deacetylation and aging, negatively regulates salt and drought stress responses, and represses seedling traits in Arabidopsis [[9](#page-4-0)e[13](#page-4-0)]. Despite the close relationship between HDA6 and HDA9, the roles of the two HDACs in regulating flowering are different. The HDA6 mutant, axe1-5, displays late flowering [\[7\]](#page-4-0), while the HDA9 mutant, hda9-1, displays early flowering [\[14](#page-5-0)].

Auxin response factors (ARFs) are transcription factors that could act as transcription activator or repressor in auxin response pathways. Loss-of-function mutations in the majority of the ARF or Aux/IAA genes does not result in a visible phenotype, or only causes relatively mild phenotypic disruptions [[15\]](#page-5-0). However, reduction (silencing?) or elimination of some members of the ARF family genes, notably ARF3/ETTIN (ETT) and ARF5/MONOPTEROS (MP), leads to obvious silique phenotypes [[16\]](#page-5-0). The arf3/ett and arf5/mp mutants display a reduction of the valve tissue in silique and an expansion of stigmatic, abaxial style, and basal internode tissues [\[16](#page-5-0)e[18](#page-5-0)]. Moreover, the transportation of auxin also affects gynoecium development. The PIN-FORMED (PIN) family of efflux carriers is localized to a particular pole of the cell, exporting IAA selectively in the direction corresponding to PIN's localization [[19,20\]](#page-5-0).

In this study, we report a partial functional redundancy between HDA6 and HDA9 in regulating silique morphology. Loss of HDA9 causes a slightly bulged silique tip. Although the hda6 single mutants do not exhibit a detectable silique phenotype, the silique tip of \* Corresponding author. hda6 hda9 double mutant displays a more severe bulge as a result of enhanced cell elongation in valve tissue. Transcriptome analyses reveal that the transcription levels of many auxin-related genes are altered in the siliques of hda9 and of hda6 hda9. These findings suggest that HDA6 and HDA9 act redundantly to regulate valve cell elongation, possibly through regulating the expression of auxinrelated genes in silique tips.

#### 2. Material and methods

#### 2.1. Plant materials and growth conditions

hda6 (axe1-5) [\[21](#page-5-0)]. hda9 (SALK\_007123) are Columbia background [[14,22](#page-5-0)]. Sterilized seeds were stratificated at 4 C for 3 days, and seeds were then sown on Murashige and Skoog (MS) plates. Seedling were grown under a 16h-light/8-h-dark condition at 22 C. Mutant genotypes were determined by PCR (for primers, see Supplemental Table 1).

#### 2.2. Microscopy

Homozygous transgenic plants were used for confocal imaging. Confocal images were taken using Zeiss LSM 710 laser scanning microscope with excitation and emission (488 nm/505 to 530 nm) for GFP and acquired with the same microscope setting. The objective lense 20 was used.

#### 2.3. Yeast two-hybrid assays

Yeast two-hybrid assays were performed according to the manufacturer's instructions for the Matchmaker GAL4-based twohybrid system 3 (Clontech). All constructs were transformed into yeast strain AH109 by the lithium acetate method, and the yeast cells were grown on a minimal medium/-Leu-Trp according to the manufacturer's instructions (Clontech). Transformed colonies were plated onto a minimal medium/-Leu/-Trp/-His/-Ade to test for possible interactions.

#### 2.4. BiFC assays

HDA6 and HDA9 were cloned into the pEarleyGate202-YN and pEarleyGate201-YC vectors [[23](#page-5-0)e[25\]](#page-5-0). Constructed vectors were transiently transformed into tobacco leaves. Transfected leaves were then examined using a Zeiss LSM 710 laser scanning microscope.

#### 2.5. RNA extraction and qRT-PCR analysis

Total RNAs were extracted from seedlings using TRIZOL (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer's instructions. qRT-PCR was performed in the Roche 480 System following the manufacturer's instructions using the SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Toyobo). qRT-PCR was conducted with three biological replicates, and each sample was conducted in triplicate and normalized using ACT2 as an internal control. The primers used for qRT-PCR are listed in Supplemental Table 1.

#### 2.6. Genome-wide mRNA sequencing

Total RNA was prepared using a standard TRIZOL Reagent extraction method from 0.2 g 4 days after pollination siliques. Libraries were sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq™ 2500. All clean reads were mapped to the TAIR10 genome. RPKM (reads per kilobase of exon model per million mapped reads) values were computed based on these mapped reads using RackJ2, and student T-tests were carried out based on RPKM values. Genes were consider as significantly differentially expressed with a p-value < 0.05 and relative change threshold of two-fold (Supplementary dataset 1).

#### 2.7. Scanning electron microscopy

For scanning electron microscopy, samples were vacuum infiltrated with 4% formaldehyde (w/v) in PBS for 10 min and fixed with fresh solution for 16 h at 48 C. Samples were dehydrated in an ethanol series and critical point dried in liquid CO2 (Polaron E300 apparatus). Dried samples were mounted on stubs; when necessary, several outer whorl organs of individual flowers were removed manually. Then, samples were coated with gold palladium (4:1) in a Sputter Coater SCD005 (BALTEC). Scanning electron microscopy was performed with a Hitachi S-3400N microscope. Cell length was measured with image J software.

## 3. Results

#### 3.1. HDA6 and HDA9 share similar protein domains

Previous studies have demonstrated that the silique tip of hda9 is slightly blunt [[14\]](#page-5-0), indicating HDA9 is involved in regulating the formation of silique apex. To investigate whether other HDACs that belong to the RPD3/HDA1 clade as HDA9 also participate in the control of silique development, protein domains of the RPD3/HDA1 superfamily members were analyzed to find out which HDACs are closer to HDA9. Our sequence homology analysis suggests that HDA6, HDA9, HDA7 and HDA19 are grouped into Class I ([Fig. 1](#page-2-0)A). We further analyzed the conserved domains of Class I HDACs and found that only HDA6 shares exactly the same protein domains as HDA9, including the HD domain, Active site, Poly-Gly, Asp-rich, and Phosphoserine domains [\(Fig. 1A](#page-2-0)). Therefore, we suspected that HDA6 might be associated with HDA9 in regulating the development of silique tip in Arabidopsis.

## 3.2. HDA9 acts coordinately with HDA6 to regulate the formation of silique apices

HDA6 and HDA9 show a similar expression pattern (Supplementary Fig. 1), suggesting that HDA6 and HDA9 may function in similar developmental processes. Particularly, HDA6 and HDA9 are co-expressed in the stigma and silique (Wu et al., 2008; Kang et al., 2015). Therefore, hda6 hda9 double mutants were generated to investigate whether HDA6 interacts genetically with HDA9 in silique growth. Loss of HDA9 causes a slightly bulged silique tips, while the hda6 single mutants do not exhibit an obvious silique phenotype ([Fig. 1B](#page-2-0)). Interestingly, the silique tip of hda6 hda9 displays a more severe bulge than that of hda9 ([Fig. 1B](#page-2-0) and Supplementary Fig. 2). Moreover, the distal end of had6 hda9 valve outgrows exceedingly such that it wraps the stigma to form a "nock-shaped" structure. To confirm that the "nock-shaped" siliques of hda6 hda9 are caused by the mutations in HDA6 and HDA9, complementation experiment was carried out by introducing HDA6-GFP or HDA9-GFP fusion genes into hda6 hda9 plants. The siliques of HDA6-GFP hda6 hda9 plants are similar to those of hda9, while the siliques of HDA9-GFP hda6 hda9 are similar to those of hda6 (Supplementary Fig. 3). These results demonstrated that loss-of-function mutations in both HDA6 and HDA9 are responsible for the "nock-shaped" siliques of hda6 hda9 ([Fig. 1B](#page-2-0)).

#### 3.3. HDA9 and HDA6 coordinately regulate valve cell elongation

The flower and fruit development were divided into ten stages [26]. In the stage 1, the flower primordium is visible as a bulge on the inflorescence meristem [26]. In stage 12, the gynoecium becomes ready for fertilization and the valves, valve margins, replum, and style start to become morphologically distinct. In stage 13, the flower opens and self-pollinates; and in stage 14, the stamens extend above the top of the stigma [26]. To further characterize the morphological changes of the blunt tips of hda9 and the "nockshaped" siliques of hda6 hda9, we observed siliques at the stages 12 and 14, respectively. The distal end of the blunt tips of hda9 reaches the middle of style, while the distal end of valve of hda6 hda9 passes over the style and stigma (Fig. 1C and Supplementary Fig. 5). To investigate the cause of overgrowth, we measured the length of valve cell in wild-type, hda6, hda9, and hda6 hda9. The cell length of hda6 is not different from wild-type. However, the cell length of hda9 is longer than wild-type and hda6, and hda6 hda9 is longer than hda9 (Fig. 1D and E). These results reveal that an increase of the length of valve cell contributes to the blunt tips of hda9 and "nock-shaped" siliques of hda6 hda9.

# 3.4. HDA6 and HDA9 are involved in the regulation of auxin signaling pathway in silique

To further understand the molecular mechanism of the phenotype of *hda9* and *hda6 hda9* silique, we investigated the transcriptome of wild-type, *hda6*, *hda9*, and *hda6 hda9* (Fig. 2A–C). We identified 883 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) (626 up vs 257 down) in *hda6*, 1074 DEGs (436 up vs 638 down) in *hda9*, and 1047 DEGs (565 up vs 556 down) in *hda6 hda9*. Among the misregulated genes in *hda6 hda9*, 434 genes and 336 genes of them are also mis-regulated in *hda6* and *hda9*, respectively. There are 201 genes are commonly mis-regulated genes found in *hda6*, *hda9* and *hda6*. In addition, among the mis-regulated genes found in *hda6*, *hda9* and *hda6* hda9, 140 genes are co-regulated genes. These results suggest that HDA6 and HDA9 may act together in the regulation of some developmental processes.

Auxin Response Factors 3 (ARF3), ARF4, and ARF5 participate in the development of silique valve [\[27,28](#page-5-0)]. The transcript level of ARF4 was increased in hda6 hda9 compared with wild-type, hda6, and hda9 (Fig. 2E). In addition, the transcripts levels of ARF12, ARF20 and ARF21 are all decreased in hda6, hda9, and hda6 hda9 compared with wild-type (Fig. 2E).

The Small auxin-up RNA (SAUR) gene family is the largest family of early auxin response genes in higher plants, which have been implicated in the regulation of multiple biological processes [[29](#page-5-0)]. The transcripts levels of SAUR22, SAUR62, SAUR64, SAUR68, SAUR67 are decreased in hda6 hda9 compared with wild-type in our transcriptome data. We further confirmed these results by qRT-PCR and show that these genes are down-regulated in hda9 and hda6 hda9, but remain unchanged in wild-type and hda6 (Fig. 2F). In addition, PIN5 and PBP1, genes encoding auxin transport proteins, were upregulated in hda9 and hda6 hda9 compared with wild-type (Fig. 2G).

To investigate if the auxin signaling pathways might be impaired in the siliques of hda6, hda9, and hda6 hda9, a auxin reporter line, DR5:GFP, was crossed into hda6, hda9, and hda6 hda9. The results show that there is no obvious difference between wildtype and hda6, while the strengths of GFP signal in hda9 and hda6 hda9 are weaker than that in wild-type and hda6 [\(Fig. 3](#page-4-0)). Together, these results suggest that the blunt and "nock-shaped" siliques of hda9 and hda6 hda9, caused by cell elongation of silique valve, may be due to the impaired auxin signaling pathway in these mutant plants.

#### 4. Discussion

Several studies have reported that HDA9 and HDA6 offten play antagonistic roles in the same developmental process such as flowering [\[14,22,30](#page-5-0)]. Nevertheless, HDA9 and HDA6 also participate in the same developmental process, such as aging. HDA9 promotes histone H3 deacetylation and aging [\[11,](#page-4-0)[12\]](#page-5-0), while HDA6 also promotes senescence [\[12](#page-5-0)]. Loss of HDA9 results in enlarged siliques tip [\[12,31](#page-5-0)]. In this study, we found that HDA6 and HDA9 act synergistically to regulate the morphology of silique tip [\(Fig. 1](#page-2-0)AeD and Supplementary Fig. 2). The siliques of the hda6 hda9 plants displays a more severe silique phenotype compared with those in the hda9 and hda6 single mutants ([Fig. 1](#page-2-0)BeD), supporting that both HDA9 and HDA6 are involved in the regulation of silique tip development. However, there is no protein-protein interaction

between HDA6 and HDA9 (Supplementary Fig. 4), suggesting that HDA6 and HDA9 do not form a protein complex in regulating silique development.

The blunt and "nock-shaped" silique of hda9 and hda6 hda9 mutants are likely caused by the increase of valve cell length ([Fig. 2D](#page-3-0) and E). HDA6 and HDA9 may coordinately regulate the development of silique tips via regulating different genes that are involved in impacting cell elongation. Previous studies suggest that the auxin signaling pathway is an important factor in regulating cell elongation [\[32\]](#page-5-0). The valve tissues of arf3 and arf5 are reduced and the arf3 arf5 displays a dramatically enhanced defects in siliques [\[16,18,33,34](#page-5-0)]. ARF3 and ARF4 are redundantly required for the specification of abaxial cell types, and ARF4 could partially restore the reduced valve cell of gynoecium in arf3 mutants [[35](#page-5-0)]. We found that ARF4 is up-regulated in the hda6 hda9 double mutant, but not in either of the single mutants, suggesting that HDA6 shows functional redundancy with HDA9 in the regulation of ARF4 expression in silique valve ([Fig. 2E](#page-3-0)).

The formation of gynoecium depends on the auxin gradient [\[36,37](#page-5-0)]. It was proposed that high auxin apically causes in style and stigma proliferation, an intermediate auxin level is essential for ovary formation, and a low auxin level is vital for gynophore formation. Reduction of polar auxin transport give rise to blocking of apically produced auxin and thus in a steeper auxin gradient, leading to boundary shifts [[36](#page-5-0),[37](#page-5-0)]. This reveal that intermediate auxin level is required for valve development, and a slightly decrease in the apical auxin level can result in longer apical valves. This model is supported by the fact that pin1 as well as pin3 pin7 mutants have severely distorted gynoecia [[38,39\]](#page-5-0). The reduced transcription levels of ARF12, ARF20, and ARF21 suggest that auxin signaling may be impaired in had6 hda9 silique [\(Fig. 2E](#page-3-0)). The weaker DR5:GFP signal detected in the gynoecia of hda9 and hda6 hda9 (Fig. 3) indicates the reduced auxin content in valve cells in the tips of gynoecia of hda9 and hda6 hda9. The transcript levels of ARFs (ARF12, ARF20 and ARF21) ([Fig. 2](#page-3-0)E) and SAURs (SAUR22, SAUR62, SAUR64, SAUR67, and SAUR68) ([Fig. 2](#page-3-0)F) are reduced in hda9 and hda6 hda9, supporting that auxin signaling is decreased in hda9 and hda6 hda9 [\(Fig. 2G](#page-3-0)). In addition, the up-regulated transcription of PIN5 and PBP1 in hda6 hda9 [\(Fig. 2G](#page-3-0)) may also affect the auxin gradient. Taken together, these results suggest that the enhanced elongation of valve cells in the hda9 and hda6 hda9 mutants may be due to the reduction of the auxin signaling and the abnormal auxin gradient.
