# **Transcriptome profle analysis reveals the regulation mechanism of foral sex diferentiation in** *Jatropha curcas* **L**

**Wenkai Hui1, Yuantong Yang2, Guojiang Wu3, Changcao Peng2, Xiaoyang Chen1,2 & Mohamed Zaky Zayed2,4**

**The seeds of** *Jatropha curcas* **contain a high percentage of biodiesel. However, low seed yield which was limited by its poor female fowers was a bottleneck for its utilization. Here, we compared the transcriptomic profles of fve diferent samples during foral sex diferentiation stages using Illumina Hiseq 4000. Our results showed that hundreds of diferentially expressed genes (DEGs) were detected in foral sex initiation period, but thousands of DEGs were involved in the stamens and ovules development process. Moreover, the DEGs were mainly shown up-regulation in male foral initiation, but mainly down-regulation in female foral initiation. Male foral initiation was associated with the favonoid biosynthesis pathway while female foral initiation was related to the phytohormone signal transduction pathway. Cytokinin (CTK) signaling triggered the initiation of female foral primordium, thereafter other phytohormones co-promoted the female foral development. In addition, the foral organ identity genes played important roles in foral sex diferentiation process and displayed a general conservation of the ABCDE model in** *J. curcas***. To the best of our knowledge, this data is the frst comprehensive analysis of the underlying regulatory mechanism and the related genes during foral sex diferentiation in** *J. curcas***, which help in engineering high-yielding varieties of** *J. curcas***.**

*Jatropha curcas* L., commonly known as the physic nut and belongs to the family Euphorbiaceae. It is a native species of Mexico and Central America, is widely distributed in the tropical and subtropical regions[1](#page-10-0) . *J. curcas* is a bioenergy tree and its seeds contain a high oil content, which has the potential to be exploited as a source of biofue[l2](#page-10-1) . However, the low seed yield of *J. curcas* is a major drawback in its utilization as bioenergy plant, which was caused by the lower number of female fowers. Terefore, revealing the potential regulatory mechanism involved in foral sex diferentiation in *J. curcas*, especially female foral diferentiation, is critical for the improvement of female fowers and the cultivation of high-yielding *J. curcas* germplasms.

Angiosperms mostly consist of bisexual fowers and but also bear unisexual fowers, which may be monoecious or dioecious[3](#page-10-2) . To understand the potential regulatory events during foral sex diferentiation, it is important to understand the diferent pathways used by the plant for its propagation. Te common foral organs are initiated in the whole foral primordium, but the specifc diferentiation of stamens or pistils governs the formation of unisexual fowers[4](#page-10-3) . In *J. curcas*, the diferentiation of the foral primordium to form male and female fowers is regulated by some endogenous induction efects[5](#page-10-4) , which may afect the action of homeotic genes to initiate the formation of gynoecium or androecium meristem. Te availability of the *J. curcas* reference genome assembly has facilitated further fundamental and applied studies on this plan[t6](#page-10-5)[,7](#page-10-6) . Comparative transcriptome analysis between gynoecious and monoecious *J. curcas* plants revealed 32 genes related to the foral development and 70 involved

Moreover, the MADS-box gene family – an important foral sex determinant in plants – was also not described[9](#page-11-1) .

Te MADS-box transcription factors play vital regulatory roles in the foral diferentiation process[10](#page-11-2). Te core domain structures of these genes are very conservative[11](#page-11-3),[12](#page-11-4). Tere are 107 members related to MADS-box genes, which were clustered into class A, B, and C in *Arabidopsis thaliana* and 46 of these members belonged to the type-II subfamil[y13](#page-11-5). Te genes of the ABC model co-determine the fate of foral organ primordium by complex crosstalk networ[k14.](#page-11-6) Moreover, some genes from class D and E could also join in the network to contribute to male and female foral diferentiation. Te ABCE model plays role during the foral diferentiation of *J. curcas*[15.](#page-11-7) AP1 stimulates fowering and regulates the *B* genes, which allow the diferentiation from inforescence meristem to fower bu[d16.](#page-11-8) *JcAP1* was shown to trigger early fowering in *Arabidopsis thaliana*, but not *J. curcas*[17](#page-11-9). Te overexpression of *JcFT* in *J. curcas* was shown to induce early fowering[18,](#page-11-10)[19](#page-11-11). However, *JcTFL* represses the fowering of *J. curcas*, and its overexpression in *J. curcas* delays fowering[20](#page-11-12). Te crosstalk among *JcAP1*, *JcFT*, and *JcTFL* might co-regulate the fowering time, but the essential genes involved in the diferentiation of stamens or pistils of *J. curcas* have not yet been identifed.

Moreover, foral diferentiation could be regulated by phytohormones, which show complex interaction networks. Gibberellins (GAs) play vital roles during foral diferentiation[21](#page-11-13), and their signal transduction could be achieved by degrading the DELLA protein[s22.](#page-11-14) Auxins (IAA) have a signifcant efect on the maturation of female and male fowers[9,](#page-11-1)[23.](#page-11-15) Jasmonic acid (JA) regulates the diferentiation of foral organs, and the homologs of JA biosynthesis were down-regulated in the gynoecious inforescences of *J*. *curcas*[8](#page-11-0) . Te function of Cytokinins (CTKs) was reversed with GAs such that it could induce female foral diferentiation in *Mercurialis annua*[24.](#page-11-16) Brassinolides (BRs) were involved in crosstalk with GAs, and the co-regulated factors were the DELLA proteins[25](#page-11-17),[26](#page-11-18). Moreover, BRs directly interact with SVP by activating BZR1, which is a new avenue in the foral-regulating network[27](#page-11-19). Plant growth regulators, such as 6-Benzyl aminopurine (6-BA), thidiazuron (TDZ) and paclobutrazol (PAC), could signifcantly improve female fower formation in *J. curcas*[28–](#page-11-20)[31](#page-11-21). However, the phytohormone signal transduction pathway during fower sex diferentiation in *J. curcas* is still not understood.

Terefore, the present study was carried out to compare fve diferent transcriptomic profles of foral sex diferentiation stages using the Illumina Hiseq 4000 to reveal the underlying regulatory mechanism of male and female foral diferentiation. Moreover, the numerous diferentially and specifcally expressed genes in male and female foral diferentiation process were identifed. Tis study were also provides new insights into foral sex diferentiation in *J. cur*cas.

#### **Results**

**Morphological observation of fower buds at diferent developmental stages.** From the morphological observation of male and female foral diferentiation process (M1-M9, and F1-F9) as shown in Fig. [1,](#page-2-0) the male fower buds were tended to develop a spherical shape. Meanwhile, the female fower buds were ellipsoid. Flower buds in the inforescence stage of 0.5 cm diameter (IND stage) were in the primordial period with no diferentiation into stamen and pistil fowers. Five stages of the foral diferentiation were selected and defned as stamen primordia beginning to diferentiate (STD1) or (M1), ten complete stamens formed (STD2) or (M4), carpel primordia beginning to diferentiate (PID1) or (F1), three distinct carpels formed (PID2) or (F4), and undiferentiated inforescence of 0.5 cm diameter (IND). M1 stage was the initial stage of male foral diferentiation. In this stage, the ten stamens were triggered from the male primordial, whereas in the M4 stage, stamens were formed and the complete stamens could be observed. Meanwhile, F1 stage was the initial stage of female foral diferentiation and in this stage, the carpel primordial was triggered, while the complete carpel and ovule could be observed in the F4 period. Te male and female fowers matured gradually afer F4 and M4. Moreover, the accuracy of the connection between the foral exterior structure and interior diferentiation stage was more than 93% (Supplementary Table S1).

**Sequence analysis of diferent cDNA libraries.** IND, STD1 (M1), STD2 (M4), PID1 (F1) and PID2 (F4) were selected as diferent samples of foral sex diferentiation stages for transcriptome sequencing (Fig. [1e](#page-2-0)). Transcriptome sequencing was done using Illumina HiseqTM 4000 platform and paired-end reads. Afer trimming adapters and low quality bases, 7.46G, 6.86G, 7.84G, 6.56G and 7.15G high quality clean bases were remained for IND, STD1, STD2, PID1 and PID2, respectively (Supplementary Table S2). Te error rate of RNA-seq was only 0.02%, all of the Q30 were more than 91%, and the GC content was more than 43% in each sample. In addition, the alignable reads ranged from 35.57 million (STD1, 77.73%) to 40.73 million (IND, 81.94%) based on clean reads (Supplementary Table S3). Among the mapped reads, 34.71–39.88 million were uniquely aligned reads, with IND making up the highest percentage (80.25%).

**Regulatory patterns of female foral diferentiation.** In this study, 2,017 DEGs were identifed in PID1 *vs*. IND and PID2 *vs*. PID1 (Fig. [2\)](#page-2-1). Te up-regulated DEGs were still signifcantly related to plant endogenous hormone signal transduction pathways (Supplementary Fig. S7) such as fve of them were involved in IAA signal (JC18784), GA signal (JC13432), ETH signal (JC07165), BR signal (JC22124), and SA signal (JC06956) pathways (Table [3,](#page-7-0) Supplementary Fig. S3). Te down-regulated DEGs were still related to the carbohydrate metabolism and favonoids biosynthesis pathway. Tus, the phytohormones signifcantly regulated the female foral initiation and development process, whereas the favonoids biosynthesis pathway was signifcantly involved in male foral initiation and development process.

**DEGs analysis involved in foral diferentiation and development.** According to the DEG analysis of our fve samples, 30 genes were up-regulated both in STD1 *vs*. IND and PID1 *vs*. IND, whereas down-regulated both in STD2 *vs*. STD1 and PID2 *vs*. PID1 (Fig. [3b](#page-3-0)). One of them was annotated to MADS-box transcription factor, JC12152 (*JcPI*), which was related to the petal development (Table [4](#page-8-0)). Moreover, two of them, JC13576 (*JcTGA9*) and JC25510 (*JcTGA10*), were involved in SA signal transduction pathways (Supplementary Fig. S3). Tese DEGs were associated with the initiation of foral primordia, but they have no signifcant roles in foral sex determination and subsequent development process. In addition, 28 genes were down-regulated both in STD1 *vs*. IND and PID1 *vs*. IND, whereas up-regulated both in STD2 *vs*. STD1 and PID2 *vs*. PID1 (Fig. [3c](#page-3-0)). Tese genes were mainly related to energy supplement in subsequent foral development to ensure the normal diferentiation of foral organs. Only JC14209 annotated with seed development process (*JcCYP78A9*) was signifcantly down-regulated in the entire stages of female and male foral diferentiation. Tere were not any DEGs up-regulated in all comparisons. Tese results indicated that some genes were common in male and female foral initiation or development process to play similar roles in *J. curcas*.

**DEGs analysis related to the MADS-box transcription factors.** In a previous study, the MADS-box genes play vital regulatory roles in the foral diferentiation process. Among the DEGs in our comparisons, 21 DEGs related to MADS-box transcription factors were isolated in male and female foral diferentiation process (Table [4\)](#page-8-0). Furthermore, these genes were only signifcantly up-regulated at one stage (IND, STD1, STD2, PID1 or PID2) (Supplementary Fig. S8). Seven of these genes were up-regulated in IND, 8 in STD1, 2 in STD2, and 4 in PID2; however, none of them were signifcantly up-regulated in PID1.

JC18694 and JC18739 (*JcAGL22/SVP*), JC25595 and JC15742 (*JcAGL7/AP1*), JC14482 (*JcAGL20/SOC1*), and JC26434 (*JcAGL10/CAL1*) were mainly related to the transition from vegetative to reproductive period in IND. Tey played the key roles in male foral initiation. Two DEGs annotated to *JcAGL104* (JC11997, JC24097) were not only up-regulated in STD2, but also involved in stamen development and maturation. Four DEGs: JC00165, JC07991, JC14484 and JC21634, were signifcantly up-regulated in PID2. Tey were annotated to *JcAGL1/SHP1*, *JcAGL11/STK*, *JcAGL6* and *JcAG* respectively, all of them were involved in carpel maturation.

**qRT-PCR validation.** To validate the RNA-seq data, 74 DEGs identifed by RNA-seq were tested by qRT-PCR (Supplementary Table S4). Tese genes were selected because of their important function in foral sex diferentiation process, including 39 up-regulated genes and 35 down-regulated genes. All of them were consistent with the same trend of up- or down- regulation between the two diferent expression analysis platforms (Fig. [7\)](#page-9-1). Te correlation of the two expression measurements was 0.92 between these 74 genes (*R*<sup>2</sup><sup>=</sup>0.92). In fact, the results of RNA-seq and qRT-PCR were consistent.

#### **Discussion**

Te present study showed that some key specifc genes were activated in STD1 or PID1 to trigger male or female foral initiation, but they were shut down in the subsequent development process. Afer foral sex determination, the numerous DEGs were activated to complete the development of stamens or ovules. Similar regulated events were observed in gymnosperms and lower angiosperm[s32–](#page-11-22)[34,](#page-11-23) which indicated that this regulated pattern is conserved in plants. However, the down-regulated DEGs in STD1 were also down-regulated in STD2, but the down-regulated DEGs in PID1 could be activated in PID2. Tus, female foral diferentiation was a more complex set compared with male foral diferentiation.

In previous studies, the numerous down-regulated DEGs were attributable to the gynoecious mutation against with monoecious wild-type in Cucumbe[r35](#page-11-24). Te similar result in our study was that the DEGs were mainly up-regulated in male foral diferentiation process, but down-regulated in female foral initiation. However, the DEGs up-regulated from PID1 *vs*. IND to PID2 *vs*. PID1 were more than those in the male fower diferentiation from STD1 *vs*. IND to STD2 *vs*. STD1 (Fig. [2\)](#page-2-1). Based on the scanning electron microscope observation, the foral diferentiation process was divided into 12 stages in *J. curcas*[5](#page-10-4) . Te foral primordium is originally diferentiated to male fowers from frst to sixth stage, but it might be reversed to female foral diferentiation by some endogenous induction efects in the seventh stage. As a result of which, the male foral primordium underwent abortion gradually in the subsequent development process. Recently, the certain genes including *CUC2, TAA1, CKX1*, and *PIN1* promote the formation of female fowers in *J. curcas* while the genes *SUP* and *CRY2* are involved in female fower transition and male primordia abortio[n36.](#page-11-25) In addition, the diferentiation of arrested pistil primordia could be activated in male fowers by repressing the expression of the *TS2* which is required for carpel abortion in *J. curcas*[37.](#page-11-26) Tus, we suggested that some genes could continuously inhibit the initiation of female foral primordium in the original period of foral diferentiation in *J. curcas*. #### **Materials and Methods**

**Morphological observation of flower buds at different developmental stages.** Male and female flowers occur at a specific location on the *J. curcas* plants. The top of the main inflorescence rachis and the central location, usually the female sites, triggered the formation of female flowers (Supplementary Fig. S9), while the male flowers were formed at the end of the floral branch<sup>5</sup>. In the present study, flower buds were divided into nine stages, based on size, from primordium to mature male or female flowers (M1-M9 and F1-F9). Additionally, 30 floral buds were used to confirm the connection between floral exterior structure and interior differentiation stage respectively. And then, five stages of the floral differentiation were selected and defined as: stamen primordia beginning to differentiate (STD1), ten complete stamens formed (STD2), carpel primordia beginning to differentiate (IND). These stages were identified by stereoscope and paraffin section and sequenced by RNA-seq, respectively.

**Floral samples collection.** The *J. curcas* clone of Nujiang in Yunnan province (25.85°N, 98.85°E) was selected as the experimental material, which was planted in a forestry trial base of South China Agricultural University (23.24°N, 113.64°E). Five mixed samples with ten plants, IND, STD1, STD2, PID1 and PID2, were collected respectively, and flash frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C until further use for RNA extraction.

**Total RNA extraction and transcriptome sequencing analysis.** Total RNA from all samples was extracted separately using the TIANDZ Plant RNA Kit (TIANDZ column type RNAout 2.0, Beijing). The quantity and purity of total RNA were examined using agarose gel electrophoresis and Nanodrop 2100 (Agilent, USA). The five cDNA libraries in our study, including IND, STD1, STD2, PID1, and PID2 were constructed and sequenced using the Illumina HiSeq<sup>TM</sup> 4000 (Illumina, USA) platform at Novegene Bioinformatics Technology Co. Ltd (Beijing, China). Sequence adaptors, N's more than 10% and low-quality reads (Qphred  $\leq$  20 for >50% read) were removed<sup>53</sup>. When the quality control (QC) finished, the clean reads was mapped to the genome sequence in the NCBI used Tophat<sup>54</sup> (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/?term=Jatropha%20curcas), then the expression abundance were calculated (used HTSeq) and normalized to the expected number of fragments per kilobase of transcript sequence per million fragments mapped (FPKM)<sup>55</sup>.

**Identification and annotation of DEGs.** All DEGs were divided into 6 comparisons (STD1 vs. IND, STD2 vs. STD1, PID1 vs. IND, PID2 vs. PID1, PID1 vs. STD1, PID2 vs. STD2). All of the up- or down- regulated genes in the following description were regulated in the first comparison component. Differential expression analysis of each comparison was performed using the DEGSeq R package (1.20.0). Fold change (FC) is the gene expression difference between different samples. We used the threshold |log2 (FC)| > 1 and q < 0.005 as the criteria for identifying the DEGs. Functional enrichment and classification of DEGs were performed according to the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes database (KEGG, http://www.kegg.jp/). KOBAS 2.0 software was used to estimate the statistical enrichment of DEGs in KEGG pathways<sup>56</sup>. Corrected *p*-value of 0.05 was set as the threshold for the significant enrichment of KEGG pathways. Transcription factor prediction analysis was performed by iTAK software (iTAK 1.2)<sup>57,58</sup>. The Blastx alignment was carried out between the DEGs and the TAIR 10 database (https://www.arabidopsis.org/). The heat maps in this study were drawn by R software (R-2.15.3-win), and the normalization method was performed using scale package<sup>59</sup>.

**Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) validation.** To validate the transcriptomic results, we isolated total RNA from the same plant materials, and a total of 74 DEGs were selected for qRT-PCR analysis. These genes were selected because of their important function in floral sex differentiation process according to DEGs analysis in the present study. The cDNA synthesis for the five stages (IND, STD1, STD2, PID1 and PID2) was performed using PrimeScript<sup>®</sup> II first Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (TaKaRa, Japan). The specific primers were designed by Primer Premier 5.0 (Supplementary Table S5). qRT-PCR was performed on the Roche LightCyler480 system (Roche, Germany) with SYBR Premix Ex Taq<sup>TM</sup> II (TaKaRa, Japan)<sup>60</sup>. The cycling reaction was 94 °C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of 94 °C for 10 s, 55 °C for 10 s and 72 °C for 20 s. Three replicates were included for each gene, and  $\beta$ -actin (*Jcactin*) and *JcGAPDH* were used as internal controls<sup>61</sup>. The 2<sup>- $\Delta\Delta$ Ct</sup> method was used to calculate the relative expression level of DEGs<sup>62</sup>.
