# **Leaf transcriptomic signatures for somatic embryogenesis potential of** *Elaeis guineensis*

**Siew‑Eng Ooi1  [·](http://orcid.org/0000-0002-6621-5456) Ishak Feshah1 · Azimi Nuraziyan1 · Norashikin Sarpan1 · Nabeel Ata1 · Chin‑Ching Lim2 · Chin‑Nee Choo<sup>3</sup> · Wei‑Chee Wong3 · Foo‑Hin Wong2 · Choo‑Kien Wong3 · Meilina Ong‑Abdullah1**

#### **Abstract**

*Key message* **Potentially embryogenic oil palms can be identifed through leaf transcriptomic signatures. Diferential expression of genes involved in fowering time, and stress and light responses may associate with somatic embryogenesis potential.**

**Abstract** Clonal propagation is an attractive approach for the mass propagation of high yielding oil palms. A major issue hampering the efectiveness of oil palm tissue culture is the low somatic embryogenesis rate. Previous studies have identifed numerous genes involved in oil palm somatic embryogenesis, but their association with embryogenic potential has not been determined. In this study, diferential expression analysis of leaf transcriptomes from embryogenic and non-embryogenic mother palms revealed that transcriptome profles from non- and poor embryogenic mother palms were more similar than highly embryogenic palms. A total of 171 genes exhibiting diferential expression in non- and low embryogenesis groups could also discriminate high from poor embryogenesis groups of another tissue culture agency. Genes related to fowering time or transition such as FTIP, FRIGIDA-LIKE, and NF-YA were up-regulated in embryogenic ortets, suggesting that reproduction timing of the plant may associate with somatic embryogenesis potential. Several light response or photosynthesisrelated genes were down-regulated in embryogenic ortets, suggesting a link between photosynthesis activity and embryogenic potential. As expression profles of the diferentially expressed genes are very similar between non- and low embryogenic groups, machine learning approaches with several candidate genes may generate a more sensitive model to better discriminate non-embryogenic from embryogenic ortets.

**Keywords** Flowering time · Embryogenic potential · Cloning · Stress · Light · Transcriptome profling

# **Introduction**

Palm oil has a diversity of uses and contributes to about 40% of global vegetable oil consumption (Weckx et al. [2019;](#page-13-0) FAS/USDA [2020](#page-11-0)). While breeding eforts continue to improve oil palm planting materials, another worthwhile approach to help increase yields is through clonal propagation of selected high yielding mother palms called ortets. Records from clonal trials demonstrated a fresh fruit bunch yield increase of 7–34% over standard seed-derived plants (Soh et al. [2011\)](#page-13-1). Clonal plantings can potentially increase yields by at least 20% (Kushairi et al. [2010;](#page-12-0) Weckx et al. [2019](#page-13-0)).

Since its commercial inception in the 1960s, oil palm cloning has undergone multiple optimizations and trials to monitor for abnormalities and yield improvements (Soh et al. [2011](#page-13-1)). Commercial oil palm tissue culture laboratories in Malaysia use leaf explants as the initial tissue type for somatic embryogenesis due to its abundance and easy accessibility. One of the bottlenecks in the current somatic embryogenesis process remains the low average embryogenesis rates of 3–6% from leaf explants (Kushairi et al. [2010](#page-12-0); Gomes et al. [2017](#page-11-1)). To reach commercial production targets, oil palm tissue culture laboratories have to clone a large pool of selected ortets, which contributes to the high overall cloning cost (Soh et al. [2011\)](#page-13-1). A predictive assay using biomarkers for embryogenesis would defnitely help to refne the selection process of potential embryogenic ortets, therefore reducing cost, time and tediousness of the entire tissue culture process.

Previous studies have attempted to address the poor embryogenesis issue using various technologies to look for genes specifc for somatic embryogenesis in oil palm embryogenic cultures and even at the explant stage (Roowi et al. [2010](#page-12-1); Ooi et al. [2012](#page-12-2), [2016;](#page-12-3) de Carvalho Silva et al. [2014](#page-11-2); Santos et al. [2018;](#page-12-4) Aroonluk et al. [2020](#page-11-3)). Although many genes and proteins with up-regulated expression in oil palm embryogenic tissues have been discovered, their use as embryogenesis screening markers was not efective or has yet to be determined.

Stress has long been linked to the clonal propagation of plants. Diferent plants and diferent explant types are exposed to diferent types of stresses to induce embryogenesis. A high number of stress-related genes exhibit expression changes during embryogenesis induction (Thibaud-Nissen et al. [2003](#page-13-2); Jin et al. [2014](#page-12-5)). Somatic embryogenesis induction is also preceded by the transcription of numerous stress-related genes (Thibaud-Nissen et al. [2003](#page-13-2); Salo et al. [2016](#page-12-6)). Moreover, modulation of stress levels may be important for somatic embryogenesis (Altamura et al. [2016](#page-11-4)) and the physiological state of plants prior to physical stresses imposed during tissue culture may be relevant. Phytohormones play an important role in somatic embryogenesis and many hormone-related genes are also stress responsive (Verma et al. [2016](#page-13-3); Xie et al. [2019](#page-13-4)). This suggests that their roles in stress response and embryogenesis may be linked.

In this study, we examined whether the cellular and physiological state of the ortet plants can be associated with its embryogenic potential through their leaf transcriptome profles. These ortets had previously been cultured to produce clonal plantlets and their embryogenic performance had been tabulated. As transcriptomes are dynamic and infuenced by many extrinsic and intrinsic factors, a large number of ortets were sampled from the feld for diferential transcriptomics analysis.

## **Materials and methods**

#### **Plant materials**

Spear leaf (defned as frond F0 in this study) tissues were sampled from selected oil palms in two oil palm tissue culture agencies, L1 and L2, whereby these palms were ortets (mother palms) sampled for cloning previously. The total number of embryoid lines produced per ortet for a period of 18 months was used to categorize the ortets into three embryogenesis groups: zero (no embryoid produced), low (< 90 embryoid lines), and high embryogenesis (90 embryoid lines or more). A total of 44 ortets were sampled from agency L1's breeding and clonal trial plots in Johor and Perak, Malaysia, while a total of 59 ortets were sampled from agency L2 in Perak, Malaysia. To assess the reproducibility of embryogenic performance upon resampling (whereby the same ortet is sampled again for cloning after several years), embryogenesis data for this were available for 37 ortets from L1 and 89 ortets from L2, giving a total of 126 ortets. The interval between the frst and second sampling ranged from 2 to 8 years. The total embryoid lines tabulated for each sampling per ortet was used for statistical analysis.

#### **RNA extraction and Illumina sequencing**

Total RNA was extracted using the InnuPREP Plant RNA kit (Analytik Jena AG) according to the manufacturer's instructions or a CTAB-extraction method (Liao et al. [2004](#page-12-7)). DNase1 treatment was then carried out using an on column digestion method with RNeasy Mini kit (Qiagen) to remove any genomic DNA carryover. RNA quality and quantity were evaluated using the 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent) with the Bioanalyzer RNA 6000 Nano Kit (Agilent) and Qubit fuorometer (ThermoFisher Scientifc). Total RNA (500 ng) was used as input for the generation of RNA-sequencing libraries using the QuantSeq 3′ mRNA-Seq Library Prep Kit FWD for Illumina (Lexogen) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Sequencing libraries were prepared for 20 samples from L2 and 32 samples from L1; randomly selected from each embryogenesis group. The libraries were sequenced on an Illumina platform to generate 7 million–10 million single-end SE50 (L2 samples) or SE100 (L1 samples) reads.

#### **Bioinformatics analysis**

The raw reads were processed and trimmed to Q20 quality using FLEXBAR tool (Dodt et al. [2012\)](#page-11-5) with options –qtrim-post-removal –aa TruSeq –x 12 –hr A –hi 5 –qt 20. The processed reads were mapped to *Elaeis guineensis* reference genome Accession ID ASJS00000000 (BioProject PRJNA192219) (Singh et al. [2013](#page-13-5)), using STAR 2.7.2b read-mapper (Dobin et al. [2013](#page-11-6)) with options –outFilter-MultimapNmax 20 –alignSJoverhangMin 8 –alignSJD-BoverhangMin 1 –outFilterMismatchNmax 999 –outFilterMismatchNoverReadLmax 0.05 –alignIntronMin 20 –alignIntronMax 30000. The mapped reads were then used for read-counting to the gene feature of annotated products from NCBI Elaeis guineensis Annotation Release 101 using HTSeq-Count version 0.11.2 (Anders et al. [2015](#page-11-7)). All raw reads and read counts have been deposited in NCBI's Gene Expression Omnibus (Edgar et al. [2002\)](#page-11-8) under GEO Accession No. GSE167149. Diferential expression was analyzed using DESeq2 version 1.22.2 (Love et al. [2014](#page-12-8)), with the zero or zero-low embryogenesis group designated as the control group and model design ~ genotype + embryogenesis. Subsequently, the diferentially expressed genes (DEGs) (Benjamini–Hochberg *p*adj<0.05) were submitted for BLASTX analysis to the UniProt/Swiss-Prot database and the remaining unannotated DEGs were searched to RefSeq database, limiting the search to fowering plants. The annotated DEGs were submitted for ID mapping (Huang et al. [2011\)](#page-12-9) to UniProt using their corresponding orthologues' UniProt ID as input. Gene expression heatmaps were generated using ClustVis (Metsalu and Vilo [2015\)](#page-12-10).

#### **Real‑time quantitative PCR**

Primers were designed for 39 selected DEGs (Table S1) using Primer3Plus (Untergasser et al. [2012\)](#page-13-6). Target amplifed regions (approximately 100–150nt) were located at the 5′ or 3′ regions, or UTRs of the transcript sequence, avoiding conserved domains. First-strand cDNA synthesis was conducted from 1.5 µg of total RNA using Quantitect® Reverse Transcription kit (Qiagen), according to the manufacturer's instructions. Real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) was conducted with 3 µl of 50X diluted frst-strand cDNA, 0.25 µM each primer and 1X Luna Universal qPCR Master Mix (NEB) in a 10 µl PCR fnal volume. PCR was conducted at 95 °C, 1 min; 40 cycles of 95 °C, 15 s, 65 °C, 30 s; with a fnal melt curve analysis. Analysis was conducted based on the delta-delta-Ct method with three reference genes normalization and three inter-run calibrators (Hellemans et al. [2007\)](#page-12-11). PCR efciency of each primer set was determined using the real-time PCR Miner program (Zhao and Fernald [2005\)](#page-13-7). Endogenous reference genes used were *PD569*, *G6PD,* and *GAPDH* (Fig. S1), as determined through Ref-Finder (Xie et al. [2012](#page-13-8)) on a set of 13 endogenous reference candidate genes (Ooi et al. [2012;](#page-12-2) Xia et al. [2014;](#page-13-9) Yeap et al. [2014](#page-13-10)).

#### **Statistical analysis**

Statistics was conducted using Stata 14.0 (StataCorp, 2015) and Rstudio ver. 1.3.1073 in R ver. 4.0.3. The concord program in Stata (Steichen and Cox [2002](#page-13-11)) was used to evaluate the concordance correlation coefcient, *ρ*c (Lin [1989,](#page-12-12) [2000](#page-12-13)).

#### **Results**

#### **Embryogenesis performance is generally reproducible on resampled ortets**

Previous studies on oil palm somatic embryogenesis led to the identifcation of expression profles or genes that can distinguish embryogenic from non-embryogenic tissues, with many focusing on stages or tissues involved during the tissue culture process itself (Low et al. [2008](#page-12-14); Ooi et al. [2012;](#page-12-2) de Carvalho Silva et al. [2014](#page-11-2); Ribeiro et al. [2019](#page-12-15)). This study however, aimed to discover the relationship between the physiological states of ortets, through their leaf transcriptomic signatures, to their embryogenesis potential in vitro. Physiological state of the mother plant is one of the factors infuencing somatic embryogenesis (Isah [2016](#page-12-16); Wójcikowska and Gaj [2016;](#page-13-12) Campos et al. [2017\)](#page-11-9). For example, somatic embryogenesis has been reported to be afected by the age, maturity, and environmental growth conditions of the mother plants.

Leaf explant is the main explant type used in many commercial oil palm tissue culture laboratories in Malaysia. We compared leaf transcriptomes from ortets that were previously non-embryogenic or embryogenic. The reproducibility of embryoid production from the frst and second cloning (i.e., termed resampling) of the same ortets was evaluated using concordance correlation analysis (Lin [1989;](#page-12-12) Steichen and Cox [2002](#page-13-11)) on log-transformed embryoid line values (Fig. [1\)](#page-3-0). The concordance correlation coefcient, ρc, evaluates the degree to which pairs fall on the line of perfect concordance (Lin [1989](#page-12-12)). Lin's concordance correlation coeffcient, *ρ*c, of 0.656 (*p*<0.001, *n*=126) indicates a moderate concordance between embryogenesis rates of both samplings. The correlation between the diference and mean was − 0.042, which is very close to zero, implying concordance. The reduced major axis line was close to the line of perfect concordance and *C*b was 0.982, indicating a high measure of accuracy (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)a). The bias correction factor, *C*b, which ranges from 0 to 1, measures how much the best-ft line deviates from the line of perfect concordance (Lin [1989\)](#page-12-12). Pearson's *r*, which measures how far each observation deviates from the best-ft line, was 0.668, suggesting moderate precision. When categorized into embryogenic or non-embryogenic groups, 82% of the ortets were found to generate the same embryogenesis outcome upon resampling (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)c). Ortets were thus selected based on their latest embryogenesis performance (Table S2). The physiological or stressed status of the plants was examined through their spear leaf transcriptomes in this study. The spear leaf extending out of the crown area was sampled and analyzed rather than the young leaf usually used as in vitro explants; the latter is more difcult to access as it is located within the crown (Fig. [2](#page-3-1)). Moreover, it would be simpler to assay the more easily accessible spear leaf for future screening applications. Due to the large number of ortets used in this study, 3′ mRNA short-read sequencing technology was conducted for gene expression profling purpose with the advantage of lower cost compared to standard mRNA sequencing (Moll et al. #### **Leaf transcriptomes cluster according to agencies**

From PCA, the leaf transcriptomes clustered into two groups which associated with the source of the ortets, i.e., the two tissue culture agencies (Fig. [3a](#page-4-0)). The variance between agencies was higher than the variance between embryogenesis groups of each agency. Therefore, diferential transcriptomics was separately analyzed for each agency. The zero (Z) and low (L) embryogenic clusters from agency L2 overlapped, while the high (H) embryogenesis ortets comprised a distinct separate cluster (Fig. [3](#page-4-0)b). This suggested that transcriptomes of the zero and low groups were more similar compared to the high embryogenesis group. The overlapping of zero and low embryogenic clusters was even greater for agency L1 samples (Fig. [3](#page-4-0)c). However, the sample sizes for each embryogenesis group were also higher for L1 (*n*(Z)=17, *n*(L)=15) compared to the sample sizes for L2 (*n*(Z)=6, *n*(L)=6, *n*(H)=8). As high embryogenic palms are rare with current commercial tissue culture practices, candidate genes for qPCR verifcation were therefore selected from the diferential expression analysis of L1 samples (Table [1\)](#page-5-0). Diferential analysis of low embryogenic compared to non-embryogenic (zero) groups revealed only 171 (*p*adj<0.05) diferentially expressed genes (DEGs) for the Dumpy Yangambi AVROS genotype of L1, while 2319 DEGs were identifed for the high vs. zero-low L2 embryogenesis groups (Tables S3 and S4). [3](#page-4-0)d, [4](#page-6-0)). #### **qPCR verifcation of RNA‑seq analysis with selected DEGs on new ortets**

A total of 26 candidate genes were selected from the 171 L1 DEGs (*p*adj<0.05) and 13 more selected from the next 134 DEGs (0.05<*p*adj<0.1) for quantitative PCR verifcation experiments. These genes were selected based on the general uniformity of their expression profles across samples within each embryogenesis group. Several of these candidate genes were also evaluated on new ortet samples not used in the RNA-sequencing experiment. Pearson correlation coefcients of above 0.7 (*p*<0.001) between qPCR and RNA-seq expression were obtained for 21 of the selected DEGs (Fig. [6](#page-8-0) and Fig. S3). Expression profles of DEGs 3, 16, 23, 29, 103, 125, 128, 144, 192, 195, 197, 218, 279, and 282 in 94 ortets suggested an association to embryogenesis potential (Fig. [7](#page-9-0) and Fig. S4). These DEGs may be further tested as candidate markers to distinguish highly embryogenic from non or poor embryogenic ortets. # **Discussion**

This study explored the relationship between the physiological or stressed status of *E. guineensis* ortet palms, via transcriptome profling, to their somatic embryogenesis potential. The assumption was that embryogenic performance is generally reproducible upon resampling of the same mother palm, as indicated by embryogenesis data from the frst and second sampling of the same plants. Samples from two tissue culture agencies, L1 and L2, were analyzed as they had sufciently high numbers of samples per embryogenesis group that were suitable for this study.

PCA of the transcriptomes indicated that samples from each agency clustered into distinct groups, which led to separate diferential expression analysis for each agency. Several possible factors may contribute to this variance between agencies, including the location of the sampled palms and genotype background. L2 samples were planted in coastal riverine alluvium soil, while L1 samples were generally planted on sandy soil type. More importantly, samples from L1 agency comprised a Dumpy genotype, which is not present in L2 samples. As the variance between embryogenic groups was indicated by PCA of the transcriptomes within each agency, especially L2 agency, we conducted separate diferential expression analyses as our goal was to examine embryogenesis potential. Based on the PCA transcriptome clusters per agency, the L1 samples were categorized into zero and low embryogenesis groups, while L2 samples were categorized into zero, low, and high groups. Diferential expression analysis of low vs. zero embryogenesis L1 groups revealed only 171 DEGs, while 2319 DEGs were identifed from high vs. zero–low embryogenesis L2 groups. The big diference in the number of DEGs was already indicated by the large overlap observed between zero and low embryogenesis transcriptome clusters, while the high embryogenesis transcriptomes formed a separate cluster. The clustering of transcriptomes from L2 according to embryogenesis groups further suggests that leaf transcriptomes may associate with

Since it is more crucial to distinguish potentially non-embryogenic and embryogenic ortets, the 171 DEGs from L1 were further analyzed and several DEGs were found associated with reproductive processes. Many of these DEGs were linked to fower development, including *FRIGIDA-LIKE*, *FT-INTERACTING PROTEIN*, *LEUNIG\_ HOMOLOG*, *BOBBER1*, and *GHD7*.

#### **Early fowering and reproductive genes are diferentially expressed in leaf tissues of embryogenic ortets**

Gene ontology analysis suggested that a substantial portion of the DEGs were associated with response to stimulus, a GO category possibly related to amenity in tissue culture which involves stimulus of plant tissues. Several DEGs were related to developmental processes, including embryo development. Of possible interest are the *NF-YA*, *FRIDIGA-LIKE* (*FRL*), *BOBBER1*, and *LUH* genes that are linked to embryogenesis processes. While the *LUH* gene was identifed as diferentially expressed, qRT-PCR analysis on more ortet samples indicated that its expression diference between embryogenesis groups was minimal. Some DEGs were also linked to phytohormone responses, particularly abscisic acid (ABA) response. Common transcription factors involved in somatic embryogenesis of diferent plants have been associated with hormone and stress responses, as well as embryo and fower development (Nowak and Gaj [2016\)](#page-12-19).

The *FLOWERING LOCUS C* (*FLC*) and *FRIGIDA-LIKE* (*FRL*) genes were found to be highly expressed during somatic embryogenesis of *Cofea arabica* (Vieira et al. [2019\)](#page-13-13). As in foral development, *FRL* genes may trigger *FLC* expression during somatic embryo development. In general, the *FRL* genes are involved in regulating fowering time by working with *FRIGIDA* to upregulate the foral inhibitor *FLC* (Bäurle and Dean [2006;](#page-11-10) Geraldo et al. [2009](#page-11-11); Shi et al. [2020\)](#page-13-14). Higher expressions of three putative *FRL* genes were detected in the embryogenic L1 ortets through RNA-seq analysis. Two of these were further tested via qRT-PCR and only one (DEG103) demonstrated the same higher expression pattern in embryogenic ortets, while the other exhibited a contrasting expression pattern.

An increasing expression trend of an *FT-INTERACTING PROTEIN* (*FTIP*) gene in embryogenic ortets was detected, relative to non-embryogenic ortets. However, overexpression of *FTIP1* leads to late fowering, suggesting that subtle regulation of *FTIP1* is required for fowering induction (Liu et al. [2012](#page-12-20); Wang et al. [2017\)](#page-13-16). The *GRAIN NUMBER, PLANT HEIGHT, AND HEADING DATE* (*GHD*) genes regulate heading time in rice, i.e., the time taken from sowing to emergence of the grain-bearing organ, by suppressing *FT*-like expression and increase grain yield (Liu et al. [2019](#page-12-21)). Higher yields had been observed through an increased number of spikelets per spike and forets per spikelet in the *ft2* wheat mutant (Shaw et al. [2019](#page-13-17)). Increased number of spikelets or forets would infer higher number of fruits and therefore higher numbers of embryos. Therefore, increased expression of the two putative *GHD7* genes in oil palm embryogenic ortets from RNA-seq analysis together with the higher *FTIP* expression suggested *FT* or *FT*-like expression was suppressed, implying delayed fowering. Moreover, only two *GHD* genes are present in oil palm predicted genes (NCBI Elaeis guineensis Annotation Release 102 on Accession No. GCF\_000442705.1). Although the *FT* gene was not detected as diferentially expressed in this study, the higher expression of *GHD7* and *FTIP* genes may infer a tendency towards higher fruit and embryo production. Incidentally, an *FT-INTERACTING PROTEIN 1-LIKE* gene was also found expressed in callus, globular, and torpedo embryo stages in oil palm (Aroonluk et al. [2020\)](#page-11-3), suggesting a possible role of this gene during somatic embryogenesis.

Increased expression of an *NF-YA* gene in embryogenic ortets also suggests later fowering time as indicated by overexpression studies of several *NF-YA7* gene members in other plants (Leyva-González et al. [2012](#page-12-22); Siriwardana et al. [2016](#page-13-18)). Overexpression of *NF-YA1*, *5*, *6,* and *9* together induced the formation of somatic embryos from vegetative tissues of *Arabidopsis thaliana* (Mu et al. [2013](#page-12-23)). The NUCLEAR FACTOR (NF-Y) transcription factors function as heterotrimeric complexes composed of single subunits from each of the NF-YA, NF-YB, and NF-YC protein families (Petroni et al. [2012\)](#page-12-24). Overexpression of several *NF-YA* genes is suffcient to induce somatic embryogenesis from vegetative tissues (Mu et al. [2013\)](#page-12-23). *NF-YA* has also been implicated in ABA response (Li et al. [2008;](#page-12-25) Zhang et al. [2010](#page-13-19)) and the role of the ABA phytohormone in stress response has been widely reported. The *NF-YA* family is also targeted by the stress responsive miR-169 (Jones-Rhoades and Bartel [2004](#page-12-26); Zhao et al. [2009](#page-13-20); Leyva-González et al. [2012](#page-12-22)). An *NF-YB* gene (DEG209) was also diferentially expressed between zero and low embryogenesis groups in this study.

### **Diferential expression of photosynthesis or light associated genes associated with embryogenic potential**

Of the 171 DEGs identifed from low vs. zero embryogenesis groups, 22 of these exhibited higher expression in zero embryogenesis L2 group based on the heatmap clustering profle. A major part of these DEGs constituted photosynthesis-related or light response genes such as the *HIGH CHLOROPHYLL FLUORESCENT 107* (DEG16), *EARLY LIGHT-INDUCED PROTEIN* (DEG23), *ULTRAVIOLET-B RECEPTOR UVR8* (DEGs26 and 27), the two *GHD7- LIKE* genes (DEGs48 and 91), *LNK1* gene (DEG51), and *FERREDOXIN-A* (DEG158). Five of these, DEGs16, 23, 27, 48, and 51, were further tested on new samples via qRT-PCR. Expressions of DEGs16 and 23 in particular, were down-regulated in the high embryogenesis group, consistent with RNA-seq analysis. We surmised that the positive fold change of these DEGs from RNA-seq analysis, indicating upregulation in low embryogenesis L1 group, may be due to the smaller sample size compared to qPCR testing.

Genes encoding the early light-induced protein, high chlorophyll fuorescent 107, UVR8 are involved in light response. The early light-induced proteins (Elips) are light stress-induced proteins and may provide photoprotective function (Heddad et al. [2006\)](#page-12-27). The *Arabidopsis* HIGH CHLOROPHYLL FLUORESCENT 107 (HCF107) is an RNA-binding protein required for the stabilization, processing, and translation of the plastid-encoded *psbH* transcript important for the formation of the photosystem PSII complex (Levey et al. [2014](#page-12-28)). The UVR8 photoreceptor senses environmental UV-B levels and is involved in gene regulation to avoid harmful UV efects (Favory et al. [2009](#page-11-12); Rizzini et al. [2011](#page-12-29); Fraser et al. [2016](#page-11-13)).

The *NIGHT LIGHT-INDUCIBLE AND CLOCK-REGU-LATED* (*LNK*) genes play a key role in linking light regulation of gene expression to the daily and seasonal rhythms in *Arabidopsis thaliana,* controlling circadian rhythms, photomorphogenic responses, and photoperiodic dependent fowering (Rugnone et al. [2013\)](#page-12-30). The *LNK* genes are also associated with fower development as fowering time genes *FKF1*, *FT*, and *SOC1* were down-regulated in *lnk1 lnk2 Arabidopsis* mutants. While downregulation of these light response genes in the high embryogenesis group was observed in L2 samples, with two of them exhibiting this expression pattern verified through qPCR analysis, the implication of light response or photosynthesis processes with embryogenic potential is unclear and requires further investigation.

#### **DEGs associated with phytohormone regulation**

Abscisic acid and auxin signaling may be associated with embryogenesis potential as gene ontology terms related to these two phytohormones were predominant, compared to other phytohormones. The DEGs associated with ABA response were orthologues of *MAPKKK* (DEG125), *NUP85* (DEG121), *SPLICING FACTOR 1* (DEG102), and *DEAD-BOX RNA HELICASE*s (DEG20 and DEG62) which are also likely to be involved in stress response (Vashisht and Tuteja [2006;](#page-13-21) Jang et al. [2014;](#page-12-31) Matsuoka et al. [2015;](#page-12-32) Zhu et al. [2017](#page-13-22)). The *Arabidopsis MAPKKK18* regulates the timing of senescence in an ABA-dependent manner (Matsuoka et al. [2015\)](#page-12-32). Several nucleoporins are reportedly involved in abiotic stress response and *NUCLEOPORIN 85* (*NUP85*) helps to regulate ABA and salt stress response (Zhu et al. [2017](#page-13-22)). Mutation of the *Arabidopsis AtSF1* exhibited developmental defects, including early fowering and ABA sensitivity (Jang et al. [2014](#page-12-31)). The DEAD-box RNA helicase A RH8 interacts with protein phosphatase PP2CA and both likely function in the same ABA-signaling pathway (Baek et al. [2018\)](#page-11-14). Several DEGs encode PP2C-type phosphatases (DEGs164, 174, 197, and 198) and this group of proteins are associated with activities of the early auxin response *SAUR* gene family as well as stress response (Schweighofer et al. [2004](#page-13-23); Ahuja et al. [2010;](#page-11-15) Spartz et al. [2014](#page-13-24)). In addition to fower development (Perez et al. [2009\)](#page-12-33), *BOBBER1* (*BOB1*) may be required for establishing proper auxin distribution or signaling during embryogenesis as *bob1* mutants arrest at the globular stage of embryo development and exhibit abnormal leaf and inforescence development (Jurkuta et al. [2009](#page-12-34); Perez et al. [2009](#page-12-33)).

Many of the genes that exhibited diferential expression in leaf tissues of embryogenic and non-embryogenic ortets are associated with fowering time and development, ABA, and stress responses. Potentially embryogenic ortets may exhibit a higher stressed state, indicated by the diferential expression of genes associated with ABA response which may be linked back to genes such as *NF-Y*, *FTIP*, and *GHD* to regulate fowering transition. DEG279 (putative *NF-YA*) expression profle appeared to correlate well to embryogenesis groups and *NF-Y*s are involved in ABA response as well as fowering time (Zhao et al. [2017\)](#page-13-25). Many genes associated with stress response, such as *BOBBER1*, *PREFOLDIN*s, *PEROXIDASE*s, and *PROTEIN PHOS-PHATASE 2C* (Ahuja et al. [2010](#page-11-15)), were found in the set of DEGs in this study. In *Arabidopsis*, about 40% of differentially expressed transcription factors in embryogenic cultures were annotated to stress-related functions (Gliwicka et al. [2013](#page-11-16)). Despite the expression variability of the DEGs in non-embryogenic and embryogenic groups, these DEGs provide glimpses to possible developmental processes linked to embryogenic potential, including fowering time, light, ABA-signaling, and stress responses. Although the roles inferred by the various DEGs give an indication of the processes or physiological states associated with the potential for somatic embryogenesis, further investigation is required to gain more evidence and understanding of the underlying mechanisms. In addition, development of potential applications using these candidate leaf expression biomarkers may incorporate supervised learning techniques such as linear discriminant analysis.
