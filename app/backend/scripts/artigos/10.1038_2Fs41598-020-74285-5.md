# **eQTL mapping of the 12S globulin cruciferin gene** *PGCRURSE5* **as a novel candidate associated with starch content in potato tubers**

**Dorota Sołtys‑Kalina**\***, Katarzyna Szajko, Emil Stefańczyk, Paulina Smyda‑Dajmund, Jadwiga Śliwka & Waldemar Marczewski**\*

**Tuber starch content (TSC) is a very important trait in potato** *(Solanum tuberosum* **L.). This study is the frst to use expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) mapping of transcript-derived markers for TSC in potato. Thirty-four diferentially expressed genes were selected by comparing the RNA-seq data of contrasting bulked segregants. For the 11 candidate genes, we determined their relative expression levels across the segregating diploid potato population using RT-qPCR. We detected 36 eQTL as candidate genes distributed on all twelve potato chromosomes, and nine of them overlapped with QTL for TSC. Peaks for two eQTL, eAGPaseS-a and ePGRCRURSE5, were close to the corresponding loci of the large subunit of ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase (***AGPaseS-a***) and the 12S globulin cruciferin gene (***PGCRURSE5***), respectively. The eQTL peaks for** *AGPaseS-a* **and** *PGRC RURSE5* **explained 41.0 and 28.3% of the phenotypic variation at the transcript level. We showed the association of the DNA markers for** *AGPaseS-a* **and** *PGRCRURSE5* **with QTL for TSC, and signifcant correlation between the expression level of** *PGRCRURSE5* **and TSC. We did not observe a signifcant correlation between the expression level of** *AGPaseS-a* **and TSC. We concluded that the cruciferin gene**  *PGRCRURSE5* **is a novel candidate involved in the regulation of starch content in potato tubers.**

Most agronomic traits of crop plants are afected by multiple loci, the environment and their interactions. Quantitative trait loci (QTL) mapping is a routine approach for studying the genetic architecture of complex traits. It yields information and the approximate genomic positions of the factors controlling a quantitative trait, but it does not identify its molecular basis[1](#page-7-0) . Combining QTL analysis with genome-wide expression profling has been termed expression QTL (eQTL) mapping or genetic genomics and creates great opportunities for dissecting quantitative traits[2](#page-7-1) . eQTL data can be used to examine genome-wide gene expression levels and fnd candidate genes for a trait of interest. eQTL are empirically divided into two classes: *cis* and *trans*. A *cis*-eQTL represent a polymorphism physically located near the gene itself, and *trans*-eQTL reside at locations distant from the genes, frequently on diferent chromosome[s3](#page-7-2) . Te identifcation of genes underlying a trait can be more efective when the bulked sample analysis (BSA) method is applied. In the BSA protocol, plants with contrasting phenotype from a segregating population are pooled and then commonly screened to identify specifc marker[s4](#page-7-3)[,5](#page-7-4) . In potato (*Solanum tuberosum* L.), RNA pools that consist of genotypes based on contrasting phenotypic or marker data were used to select candidate genes for tuber fesh colour and cooking typ[e6](#page-7-5) .

Potato is one of the most important crop plants in the world. Among numerous characteristics that are subject to selection in potato breeding, tuber starch content (TSC) is one of the most agronomically important. Te starch content of potato tubers ranges from 10 to 25% of the tuber fresh weight[7](#page-7-6) , and starch biosynthesis and breakdown in potato tubers are fairly well characterized metabolic network[s8](#page-7-7)[,9](#page-7-8) . Linkage mapping of QTL for specifc gravity or TSC has been performed in numerous experimental populations of diploid potatoes[10](#page-7-9)–[12](#page-7-10) in addition to association studies[8,](#page-7-7)[13.](#page-7-11)

Several enzymes involved in starch metabolism have been identifed and characterized at the biochemical and molecular level[s14](#page-7-12),[15](#page-7-13). ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase (AGPase) is the key enzyme in the regulation of

For the large subunit AGPaseS, three loci were identifed on potato chromosomes I, IV and VIII and for the small subunit AGPaseB, two loci on chromosomes VII and XI[I1](#page-7-0)[,8](#page-7-7) . Recent advances in 'omics' technologies have made great progress in phenotypic variations and genotypic diversity for complex traits in plant sciences. Te application of RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) technologies has changed transcriptome analyses and gene expression studie[s20.](#page-7-18) High-throughput RNA-seq technology was used to identify eQTL associated with diverse biological processes in tomato[21](#page-7-19) and eQTL related to quantitative trait variation in maize[22](#page-7-20) and to identify gene networks involved in *Verticillium dahliae* disease resistance in potat[o23](#page-7-21).

Here, we used a combination of BSA, comprehensive transcriptome analysis of diferentially expressed genes (DEGs) and QTL/eQTL mapping to confrm possible candidates involved in the regulation of the starch content in potato tubers.

### **Materials and methods**

**Plant material.** Te plant material consisted of the potato diploid population 12-3 (F1 progeny, N=175) from a cross of the seed parent DG 00-683 and the pollen parent DG 08-28/13. In the F1 progeny, the TSC segregated and was estimated from the ratio of tuber weight in air (g) to that in water (g) as described by Lunden[24](#page-7-22). Te mean TSC values (percent fresh weight), evaluated in years 2012–2014, for DG 00-683 and DG 08-28/13 were 20.8% (±3.5) and 11.8% (±0.1), respectivel[y12](#page-7-10). In our previous study, we used population 12-3 for DArT map construction and QTL analysis of the TSC and sucrose content in potato leave[s12.](#page-7-10) Te F1 individuals were grown in three replications in a random pattern and scored for TSC directly afer being harvested. Data set of TSC is presented in Supplementary Table S1. Samples (5 g) of tubers harvested in 2014 were collected in three replications per genotype, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C.

**Isolation of total RNA.** Total RNA was isolated according to the protocol of Chomczyński and Sacchi[25](#page-7-23) using TRIZOL reagent. Briefy, frozen tubers were ground in liquid nitrogen, and 1 g of ground tissues was taken prior to the addition of 4 ml of TRIZOL reagent. Afer incubation at room temperature and centrifugation, the supernatants were transferred to fresh tubes. Te extraction was performed twice in 3 ml of chloroform. Te RNA was precipitated 15 min afer the addition of 0.6 ml of salt solution (0.8 M sodium citrate and 1.2 M sodium chloride) and 0.6 ml of isopropanol. Te RNA concentration and quality were determined using a biophotometer (Eppendorf) at 260 nm, 280 nm and 230 nm. Te RNA was used for reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) and quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) experiments.

**Construction of bulk RNA and Illumina sequencing.** For the RNA-seq study, the quality and quantity of the total RNA were established using Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent). Four bulk RNA samples were constructed, each with two biological replicates. For each RNA bulk sample, equal amounts of total RNA (1 µg) from the tubers of six plants were pooled together. Bulks H1 and H2 consisted of high TSC genotypes, ranging from 19.0 to 23.4%; bulks L1 and L2 were made of low TSC genotypes, ranging from 12.5 to 15.0%. Plants in bulks H1 and L1 strongly expressed *AGPaseS-a*, whereas those in bulks H2 and L2 exhibited low levels; *AGPaseS*-*a* expression was determined as described i[n12](#page-7-10). Te mRNA was isolated using the NEBNext Poly(A) mRNA magnetic Isolation Module (New England Biolabs, E7490), and cDNA libraries were prepared using the NEBNext Ultra Direction RNA Library Kit for Illumina (New England Biolabs, E7420S). Te established cDNA libraries were sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq 4000 sequencing platform (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) to generate 100-bp paired-end reads (PE100). RNA-seq reads were conducted by Genomed S.A. (Warsaw, Poland). Quality control (QC) was done using Trimmotatic sofware (FASTQ-Illumina Phred+33) for the raw data, which were trimmed by removing all empty and low-quality reads (Q<30 and length <50 bp), as well as all adaptor sequences, in order to obtain clean reads. Te QC data are shown in Supplementary Table S2. Ten, the index of the reference genome [\(https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/assembly/GCF\\_000226075.1\)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/assembly/GCF_000226075.1) was built using Bowtie v2.1.0, and pairedend clean reads obtained for bulks H and L were aligned to the reference genome using TopHat v2.0.9 (Broad Institute, Boston, MA). Next, HTSeq v0.5.3 was used to count the number of reads mapped to each gene. Te DEGs were identifed by DESseq package. A comparison of DEGs from bulks H and L is shown in Supplementary Table S3. A scheme of the methodology used in this study is shown in Supplementary Fig. S1.

**Selection of diferentially expressed genes.** DEGs were identifed based on the RNA-seq data by comparing H1 vs. L1 and H2 vs*.* L2, H1 and H2 were control samples. Data from these comparison represents up-regulated and down-regulated genes in bulks L1 and L2. A false discovery rate (FDR) of 0.05 and absolute values of log2 ratios ≥1.5 and ≤−1.5 for up- and downregulated genes were used as the threshold for determining the signifcance of gene expression diferences. To confrm RNA-seq data and develop the transcript-derived markers, semi-quantitative RT-PCR assays were performed on bulked samples. Reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript Master Mix (TaKaRa, no. #RR036A) cDNA synthesis kit, and 2 µg of total RNA was used for each reaction. Semi-quantitative RT-PCR was performed using DreamTaq DNA polymerase (Termo Fisher Scientifc, No. EP0703) and products were visualized in 2% agarose gel with ethidinum bromide. Te primer sequences and PCR condition applied in semi-quantitative RT-PCR are presented in Supplementary Table S4.

**Construction of the genetic map with transcript‑derived markers.** Standard PCR markers were developed using the selected candidate gene sequences. Te primer sequences and PCR parameters for amplifcation of cleaved amplifed polymorphic sequence (CAPS) and one sequence characterized amplifed region (SCAR) markers are described in Supplementary Table S5. JoinMap 4 sofwar[e26](#page-7-24) was used for mapping on the previously constructed DArT map, as described i[n12.](#page-7-10)

**eQTL mapping.** Te expression of the ten selected candidate genes and *AGPaseS-a* was examined in the F1 progeny of the 12-3 population by RT-qPCR. SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Roche, Switzerland) and 96-well plates with a LightCycler 480 II system (Roche, Switzerland) were used. Te RT-qPCR was performed as previously described in Śliwka et al*.* [12](#page-7-10). Te 1 μl of cDNA corresponding to 50 ng of total RNA was taken for analysis of each sample. Potato α-tubulin was used as the reference gene. Termal cycling conditions were: 4 min denaturation at 95 °C followed by 55 cycles of 10 s at 90 °C, 20 s at temperature for primer annealing, and 30 s at 72 °C. To confrm amplifcation of gene-specifc products, PCR product melting point was determined in the range of 65–97 °C. Te primer sequences and RT-qPCR parameters are shown in Supplementary Table S6. Four technical replicates of the parents and F1 progeny of population 12-3 were performed. Relative expression levels were calculated in Microsof Excel 2010. *T* tests for ΔΔ*C*t cycle threshold values[27](#page-7-25) and calculation of standard errors of the mean (SE) were performed with Statistica sofware (Stat Sof Inc.). For eQTL mapping, MapQTL 6 sofware was utilized[28](#page-7-26), with internal mapping and a logarithm of odds (LOD) ≥3.0 as the threshold of signifcance. Te Pearson correlation coefcient (*r*) and the probability value (*p*) were used to display correlations and the signifcance of diferences in expression levels between the candidate genes and TSC. A probability value of *p*<0.05 was considered to indicate statistical signifcance.

Additionally, the expression profles of *PGRCRURSE5* and *AGPaseS-a* were examined in tubers of the parental clones DG 00-683 and DG 08-28/13 at three developmental stages: stage I, the beginning of tuber formation (1 cm diameter tubers); stage II, tuber building, tuber approximately 2 cm diameter; and stage III- tuber maturity. Te experiment was carried out in the same way as that for candidate gene expression in the F1 progeny of the 12-3 population.

**Cloning and sequencing of PGCRURSE5 amplicons.** PCR-based amplicons 1587 bp in size for the marker PGCRURSE5 were obtained from both parents and purifed using a Clean-Up Kit (A&A Biotechnology, Gdynia, Poland) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Te amplicons were blunted using a Fast DNA End Repair Kit (Termo Fisher Scientifc) and cloned into a blunt pCRScript Amp SK cloning vector (Promega, Madison, Wisconsin, USA). *E. coli* Top10 chemocompetent cells were used for transformation, and colonies with inserts of interest were picked and sequenced bidirectionally. Sequencing reactions were performed using the BigDye Terminator v3.1 kit (Life Technologies Polska Ltd., Warsaw, Poland), and products were resolved on an ABI3730XL genetic analyser at the Laboratory of DNA Sequencing and Oligonucleotide Synthesis (Institute of Biochemistry and Biophysics, Polish Academy of Sciences, Warsaw, Poland).

**eQTL analyses.** In our previous study, we performed QTL analysis of TSC using a phenotypic mean dataset (2012–2014) in population 12-[312.](#page-7-10) In the current research, the TSC linkage map was enriched by a set of 9 DNA markers, for which we found polymorphism in parents and F1 individuals (Table [2](#page-3-1), Supplementary Table S7). For 11 genes, expression products were obtained in RT-qPCR (Table [3](#page-5-0) and Supplementary Fig. S2). Te number of eQTL detected for particular candidate genes ranged from one (*AGPaseS*) to seven (*ANR*). We found eQTL located both close to the loci encoding the genes (*cis*-eQTL) and at independent locations (*trans-*eQTL). In total, 36 eQTL were mapped in population 12-3 (Table [3](#page-5-0)).

**Colocalization of eQTL with QTL for TSC.** Nine of the 36 eQTL identifed for candidate genes overlapped with QTL for TSC (Table [3](#page-5-0)). On chromosome I, two eQTL, for *AGPaseS-a* and *PGRCRURSE5*, overlapped with the strongest QTL for TSC (Fig. [1](#page-6-0)). Te eQTL peaks for *AGPaseS* and *PGRCRURSE5* were found at 99.3 and 84.3 cM, explaining 41.0 and 28.3% of the variance in the expression of these genes, respectively (Table [3](#page-5-0)). Both peaks were located near the loci encoding those genes (99.6 and 80.4 cM, Table [2](#page-3-1)).

On chromosome II, two eQTL were detected within the QTL for TSC 35.0–68.0 cM: eQTL located at 50.0–52.7 cM and explaining 17.2% of the variance in eWAT1 and located at 62.5–62.6 cM and explaining 8.4% of the variance in eIRL. Te second eQTL for *PGRCRURSE5*, found on chromosome III, partially overlapped with the QTL for TSC mapped at 65.1–71.1 cM and explained 10.9% of the variance in ePGRCRURSE5. On chromosome III, we also detected the eQTL for *MLP34* located at 124.4–130.5 cM and explained 9.4% of the variance in the expression of MLP34 that partially overlapped with the QTL for TSC at 129.4–130.5 cM. Chromosome IV possessed eQTL for *R1B-23* that covered 44.3–95.1 cM (R2=25.1%) and overlapped with the QTL for TSC at 49.5–50.2 cM. On chromosome V, we detected a QTL for TSC at 46.0–50.7 cM that explained 8.6% of the variance in this trait. It overlapped with an eQTL for *UnCh865* located at 40.5–55.6 cM that explained 14.8% of the variance observed in the expression of this gene. An eQTL for *UnCh835* at 40.3–41.6 cM on chromosome VIII overlapped slightly with the QTL for TSC at 26.0–40.6 and explained up to 23.6% of the variance in eUnCh835 (Table [3](#page-5-0)). In addition, we detected 20 eQTL on chromosomes I, III, IV, V, VIII, IX and XII that were outside the QTL for TSC (Table [3\)](#page-5-0). Seven eQTL were identifed on chromosomes VI, VII and IX, whereas no QTL for TSC were found on these chromosomes (Table [3](#page-5-0)). Signifcant positive correlations were found between the expression levels of a few pairwise combinations of the candidate genes. Te eQTL for *PGRC RURSE5*, *MLP34*, *IRL* and *Pat3-k1* exhibited signifcant positive correlations with TSC (Supplementary Table S9).

*AGPaseS-a* **and** *PGRCRURSE5* **expression assay during major stages of tuber development.** Te markers PGCRURSE5 and AGPaseS-a were signifcantly associated with QTL for TSC (Table [2](#page-3-1)). In addition, eQTL for *AGPaseS-a* and *PGRCRURSE5* overlapped with the strongest QTL for TSC on chromosome I (Fig. [1](#page-6-0)). Terefore, we evaluated the expression of *AGPaseS-a* and *PGRCRURSE5* during major stages of tuber development. Te expression level of *AGPaseS-a* was similar (not signifcantly diferent) in both parents at stage I of tuber development and signifcantly higher in the high-starch parent DG 00-683 than in the other parent at stages II and III. In the case of *PGRCRURSE5*, diferential expression was observed between DG 00-683 and DG 08-28/13 at all three stages. *PGRCRURSE5* expression in DG 00-683 was highest at stage II, while in the lowstarch parent DG 08-28/13, its transcript level increased during tuber development and was highest at stage III in DG 08-28/13 (Fig. [2\)](#page-6-1).

### **Discussion**

Starch is the most abundant storage compound in plants. As in other higher plants, starch synthesis in potato is under transcriptional control, circadian and redox control, and phosphorylation regulation[29,](#page-7-27)[30.](#page-7-28) Te *AGPaseS* loci, in particular, the locus *AGPaseS-a* on chromosome I, colocalized with QTL for TSC, and the data indicated a small efect on this trait in the mapping populations[18,](#page-7-16)[31.](#page-7-29) In association studies, the amplicons AGPsS-9a and AGPsS-10a, both derived from the *AGPaseS-a* locus, were correlated either positively or negatively with TSC[32.](#page-7-30) Previously, we showed the large QTL region for TSC on potato chromosome I that overlapped with the *AGPaseS-a* locus. Tis result potentially means that the chromosomal segment also includes other genes that either directly or indirectly afect the starch content. Te expression of *AGPaseS-a* was signifcantly higher in the high-TSC parent DG 00-683 than in the low-TSC parent DG 08-28/13 in the potato population 12-[312.](#page-7-10) Te *AGPaseS-a* allele contributed signifcantly to but was not necessary for a high TSC[12.](#page-7-10)

Here, we detected the diferences between the parental clones in the expression levels of *AGPaseS-a* in tubers at diferent growth stages. Te highest expression was detected in the tuber building stage, when high AGPase activity is required, as the tuber is a sink organ accumulating large amounts of starch[33](#page-7-31). Our results confrmed that AGPase activity remained high even when starch synthesis was inhibited in potato tubers detached from the mother plan[t34](#page-7-32). In the current sequencing experiments, *AGPaseS-a* was difcult to analyse by RNA-seq, which was potentially due to the small transcript size restricted by the constructed RNA libraries and/or sequence overlap with other transcript[s35,](#page-7-33)[36.](#page-7-34)

We mapped the cruciferin (12S globulin) gene *PGCRURSE5* to chromosome I and demonstrated that it also had a signifcant efect on TSC. In *Arabidopsis thaliana* and other crucifers, cruciferin is a main seed storage protein. Seed storage proteins serve as a source of nitrogen and amino acids that are necessary for germination and plant growt[h37](#page-7-35)[,38.](#page-8-0) In potato tubers, the inhibition of starch synthesis was accompanied by a massive reduction in the expression of storage proteins, suggesting that the expression of storage protein genes is involved in starch metabolism in potato tuber[s39.](#page-8-1) Our study revealed higher expression of *PGCRURSE5* in the high-starch parent DG 00-683 than in the low-starch parent DG 08-28/13 during the tuber formation and building stage, and we therefore concluded that the cruciferin protein can afect starch metabolism. Te *cis*-eQTL are likely mediated by polymorphisms within the corresponding genes, including the promoter regions, or by mRNA stability[40](#page-8-2). Te overlap of QTL and eQTL may indicate a strong association between the genetic variation in the phenotypic trait and the gene transcript level[41.](#page-8-3) Te genes for *AGPaseS-a* and *PGRC RURSE5* accounted for 18.5 and 18.8% of the variance in TSC, respectively. Te eQTL peaks for *AGPaseS-a* and *PGRCRURSE5* explained 41.0 and 28.3% of the phenotypic variance at the transcript level, respectively. Te high association values between eQTL peaks and the explained expression variations could account for genetic sources of variation associated with dominance and epistasis as well as for non-genetic infuences, such as developmental and environmental factors[42](#page-8-4). Colocalization of *cis-*eQTL and QTL seems to be more informative than that of *trans*-eQTL. *Trans*-eQTL are interpreted as evidence for *trans*-acting regulatory proteins such as transcription factors and other signalling proteins or small RNAs that may control the expression of a number of genes elsewhere in the genom[e43](#page-8-5).

Te role of AGPase as the frst rate-limiting enzyme in starch biosynthetic pathways is well known[8,](#page-7-7)[44](#page-8-6)[,45](#page-8-7). We showed the association of the DNA marker for *AGPaseS-a* with QTL for TSC as well as the relationship between this marker and gene transcription. However, we did not observe a signifcant correlation between the expression level of *AGPaseS-a* and TSC. Te abundance of mRNA transcripts only partially correlates with protein abundances, and these relationships are comple[x46,](#page-8-8)[47.](#page-8-9) Terefore, in the case of the enzyme AGPase, their subunit structure and transcriptional regulation can afect the net activity of this enzyme complex.

Our study demonstrates the association between the marker PGCRURSE5 and QTL for total starch content, the relationship between this marker and the eQTL for *PGRCRURSE5*, and signifcant correlation between *PGCRURSE5* expression and starch content in potato tubers. Recently, Sueng et al*.* [48](#page-8-10) has shown that nonenzymatic protein, termed Protein Targeting to Starch (PTST), is involved in starch synthesis in Arabidopsis. Our results identifed the gene cruciferin as a novel candidate involved in the regulation of starch metabolism in potato tubers. It suggests that cruciferin may be a novel PTST protein in potato tubers.
