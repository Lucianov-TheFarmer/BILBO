- transcriptome in developing aleurone and 6.7% in starchy endosperm. Processes regulated by
- NKD1 and NKD2 include gene expression, epigenetic functions, cell growth and division,
   hormone pathways and resource reserve deposition. The NKD1 and NKD2 proteins bind a
- hormone pathways and resource reserve deposition. The NKD1 and NKD2 proteins bind consensus DNA sequence of TTGTCGT with slightly different properties. This motif was
- 26 enriched in the promoters of gene transcripts differentially expressed (DE) in mutant
- 27 endosperm. DE genes with a NKD binding motif in the 5' promoter region were considered as
- 28 likely direct targets of NKD1 and NKD2 regulation and these putative direct target genes were

#### 36 **INTRODUCTION**

- 37 Cereal endosperm nourishes the developing embryo and germinating seedling,
- 38 composes a major portion of human and livestock diets, and has important industrial
- 39 applications. Maize endosperm consists of seven cell types as defined by histology and
- 40 gene expression patterns (Leroux et al., 2014; Zhan et al., 2015). In mature kernels, the
- 41 major cell types include starchy endosperm (SE), basal endosperm transfer (BET) cells
- 42 and aleurone (AL) (Becraft and Gutierrez-Marcos, 2012). SE cells constitute the
- 43 majority of the endosperm mass and function in nutrient reserve storage, primarily
- 44 starch and protein. BET cells transport nutrients from maternal tissue into the
- 45 developing endosperm. AL is important for digestion and remobilization of stored
- 46 reserves during germination as well as mineral storage and pathogen defense (Stewart
- 47 et al., 1988; Fath et al., 2000; Jerkovic et al., 2010).
- 48 Endosperm development begins with the formation of the coenocyte followed by
- 49 cellularization. Subsequent cell differentiation involves the perception and response to
- 50 positional cues that specify the different cell fates (Becraft and Asuncion-Crabb, 2000;
- 51 Geisler-Lee and Gallie, 2005; Gruis et al., 2006). Maturation entails genomic
- 52 endoreduplication and the accumulation of resource reserves in SE, acquisition of
- 53 desiccation tolerance in AL, culminating in programmed cell death of the SE and BET
- 54 cells, metabolic quiescence of the AL, and desiccation of the grain as a whole (Sabelli
- 55 and Larkins, 2009; Becraft and Gutierrez-Marcos, 2012).
- 56 The duplicate genes *naked endosperm1* (*nkd1*) and *nkd2* encode INDETERMINATE
- 57 DOMAIN (IDD) proteins (Yi et al., 2015), and the *nkd1 nkd2* double mutant shows
- 58 pleiotropic effects, including multiple layers of peripheral endosperm cells with
- 59 compromised cell identity, decreased anthocyanin accumulation, opaque and floury
- 60 endosperm texture, decreased carotenoid accumulation, decreased kernel dry weight,
- 61 and occasional vivipary (Becraft and Asuncion-Crabb, 2000; Yi et al., 2015). These
- 62 phenotypes indicate that *nkd1* and *nkd2* functions are required for cell patterning and
- 63 differentiation, resource reserve deposition and seed maturation. In wild type (WT),

64 *nkd1* and *nkd2* transcripts accumulate in both AL and SE, consistent with the pleiotropic 65 phenotype (Yi et al., 2015).

66 IDD proteins are a plant-specific family of transcription factors (TFs) whose members 67 function in a broad range of developmental and signaling processes (Morita et al., 2006; 68 Welch et al., 2007; Tanimoto et al., 2008; Hassan et al., 2010; Feurtado et al., 2011; 69 Ogasawara et al., 2011; Seo et al., 2011b; Cui et al., 2013; Wu et al., 2013; Yoshida et 70 al., 2014; Yoshida and Ueguchi-Tanaka, 2014; Jost et al., 2016). The IDD is composed 71 of a nuclear localization signal and four highly conserved tandem zinc fingers: one 72 standard C2H2 zinc finger, one irregular C2H2 zinc finger, and two irregular CCHC zinc 73 fingers. There are 17 IDD family members in maize, some with tissue-specific 74 expression differences (Colasanti et al., 2006; Sekhon et al., 2013; Yi et al., 2015). The 75 founding member of the IDD family, INDETERMINATE1 (ID1), controls flowering time in 76 maize (Colasanti et al., 1998). ID1 localizes to the nucleus and binds an 11 base pair 77 DNA consensus sequence of TTTGTCGTTTT which NKD1 (IDDveg9) can also bind, 78 although with different specificity (Kozaki et al., 2004; Wong and Colasanti, 2007; Yi et 79 al., 2015).

80 In this study, we analyze NKD1 and NKD2 function in developing maize endosperm. We 81 identify the DNA binding specificities of NKD1 and NKD2, discern that NKD1 and NKD2 82 proteins can homo- and heterodimerize through their IDDs, and demonstrate they 83 regulate transcription by binding DNA. RNA sequencing (RNAseq) analysis of *nkd1*  84 *nkd2* versus WT endosperm revealed that NKD1 and NKD2 regulate widespread 85 processes, including nutrient reserve deposition as well as cell growth and proliferation. 86 Analyses of transcriptomic data lead to functional predictions that were validated by 87 phenotypic analyses of *nkd1 nkd2* mutants.

### 89 **RESULTS**

90 **Identification of Endosperm Gene Transcripts Regulated by NKD1 and NKD2**

91 To identify genes and biological processes directly or indirectly regulated by NKD1 and 92 NKD2 (NKD1/2) in developing endosperm, a transcriptomic analysis was undertaken to 93 identify gene transcripts differentially expressed (DE) between WT and *nkd1 nkd2* 94 mutant. Laser capture microdissection (LCM) coupled with RNAseq was previously 95 performed on AL and SE cells from WT (B73 inbred) versus *nkd1 nkd2* mutant 96 endosperms at 15 days after pollination (DAP) (Yi et al., 2015). These 50-base paired-97 end reads were re-aligned and mapped to the maize reference genome (B73 RefGen-V3, GSE61057). A total of 1.14x10<sup>9</sup> 98 raw reads were generated, with a minimum of 256x10<sup>8</sup> 99 per tissue, summed over 3 biological replicates (Supplemental Table 1). Reads 100 that uniquely mapped to the genome and were properly paired were selected for further 101 analysis, which ranged from 40.7% to 72.6% of the raw reads per tissue. Expression of 102 34,014 genes was detected in AL cells whereas 32,629 genes were expressed in SE. 103 Among these, 31,792 genes were expressed in all endosperm tissues sampled (WT 104 and *nkd1 nkd2* mutant; AL and SE).

105 Differential expression (DE) analysis comparing *nkd1 nkd2* mutant to WT identified

106 2,188 DE genes in AL and 2,193 in SE (adjusted p-value ≤ 0.01) (Figure 1;

107 Supplemental Table 2; Supplemental Datasets 1 and 2). An MA plot of gene expression

108 changes did not reveal any systemic bias in the data (Supplemental Figure 1A).

109 Multidimensional scaling (MDS) analysis of gene expression variance among samples

110 revealed that AL exhibited lesser variance than SE (Supplemental Figure 1B).

111 Furthermore *nkd1 nkd2* genotype had a greater overall impact on gene expression than

112 did tissue type. Transcript abundance among the DE genes ranged from highs with

mean normalized read counts up to 8 x 10<sup>6</sup> 113 to lows of less than 10. Among DE genes,

114 63.48% had decreased transcript abundance in mutant AL and 58.28% had decreased

115 transcript abundance in mutant SE. The AL and SE DE datasets showed statistically

116 significant overlap and similar transcript fold change trends (P<0.001; Z-score of 80.8).

117 A total of 935 genes were DE between WT and mutant in both tissues (Figure 1A).

118 To validate the RNAseq data, 15 DE genes were tested by qRT-PCR on independent,

119 unamplified RNA from 15 DAP WT and *nkd1 nkd2* AL. The qRT-PCR expression values

showed a positive correlation (R2 120 =0.7036) with the RNA sequencing log2 fold changes,

- 121 indicating minimal bias in LCM-RNA sequencing results (Figure 1C). Eight of these
- 122 transcripts exhibited statistically significant DE as expected based on the RNAseq
- 123 analysis (Supplemental Figure 1C). Verified transcripts showing increased expression in
- 124 *nkd1 nkd2* AL included cell cycle related genes (*tubulin1, cell division cycle2-like*, *actin-*
- 125 *1*, *proliferating cell nuclear antigen2*) and genes annotated as involved in epigenetic
- 126 regulation (e.g., one encoding FASCIATA1-LIKE). Transcripts showing decreased
- 127 mutant expression included the AL cell identity marker *phospholipid transfer protein*
- 128 *homolog1* (Gruis et al., 2006), and genes related to nutrient reserve accumulation
- 129 (*granule bound starch synthase*, *prolamin-box binding factor1*).

# 130 *nkd1* **and** *nkd2* **Regulate Genes Involved in Diverse Processes**

131 To determine biological processes regulated by *nkd1* and *nkd2*, pathway analyses were 132 performed on DE genes using Gene-Ontology (GO), MaizeCyc, and CornCyc (Monaco 133 et al., 2013). Complete lists of significantly enriched GO terms are shown in Tables 1 134 and 2 and individual genes within each pathway are reported in Supplemental Datasets 135 1 and 2. There were notable effects on genes functioning in the regulation of gene 136 expression, including 80 TFs with altered expression in mutant AL and 86 in SE (Figure 137 2). Epigenetic related factors were also affected, showing a general trend of increased 138 expression in both mutant AL and SE. Thirty-seven epigenetic related factors were DE 139 in AL and 23 in SE. Cell division and cell growth pathways trended to increased 140 expression in the mutant AL. Thirty-three cell division-related factors were DE in AL and 141 18 in SE, while 17 cell-growth factors were DE in AL and 17 in SE. The AL and SE each 142 also showed wide ranging effects on hormone-related pathways including biosynthesis, 143 signaling and response in systems including auxin, abscisic acid (ABA), ethylene, 144 jasmonate, brassinosteroids, cytokinins and gibberellins (Figure 2; Supplemental 145 Figures 2 and 3).

146 Pathways involved in the metabolism or accumulation of storage compounds generally 147 showed decreased transcript levels in *nkd1 nkd2* endosperm (Figure 2; Supplemental

148 Figures 2 and 3). Notably, many pathways involved in carbohydrate metabolism were 149 affected. The mutant SE and AL both exhibited decreased expression of genes 150 functioning in starch/glycogen biosynthesis, sucrose biosynthesis, and sucrose, 151 galactose, and glycerol degradation, while mutant AL also showed decreased 152 expression of gluconeogenesis genes. Protein metabolism was also affected, with 153 mutant SE and AL both showing decreased expression of genes for storage protein 154 accumulation (nutrient reservoir activity) and amino acid biosynthesis, while mutant SE 155 also showed decreased expression of chorismate biosynthesis genes. Mutant AL also 156 displayed decreased expression of lipid metabolism pathways, including linoleate 157 biosynthesis and triacylglycerol degradation (Supplemental Figure 2A). SE showed 158 decreased expression of genes involved in nutrient transport pathways (Figure 2). Of 159 final note, genes involved in defense response were altered in both AL and SE, with an 160 overall decrease in defense response-related gene expression in the mutant 161 (Supplemental Figures 2C and 3C). Functional characterization performed by evaluating 162 GO overrepresentation produced results consistent with pathway analyses, with AL DE 163 genes enriched for GO-terms related to storage proteins (nutrient reservoir activity), 164 sugar metabolism, epigenetics, microtubule related processes and cell division (Table 165 1). SE GO-terms were enriched for DNA replication, nucleolus, plastid and cell wall 166 (Table 2).

167 Pathway analysis suggested that *nkd1* and *nkd2* may regulate cellular transport 168 activities. BET cells are sites of intensive nutrient transport from the maternal plant 169 tissue into the developing endosperm (Thompson et al., 2001) and decreased BET cell 170 transport activity would be consistent with the decreased kernel weight and storage 171 reserve accumulation in the mutant (Table 3). Because BET tissues were not included 172 in the transcriptomic analysis, potential NKD1 NKD2 regulation of BET gene expression 173 was tested using qRT-PCR on BET cell marker genes. RNA was isolated from the lower 174 quarter of 12 DAP WT and *nkd1 nkd2* endosperms. The expression of *maternally*  175 *expressed gene1* (*meg1*, p=0.027), and *basal endosperm transfer layer2*/*bap2* (*betl2*, 176 p=0.0195) were significantly increased in *nkd1 nkd2* mutant, while *basal endosperm* 

- 177 *transfer layer1* (*betl*, p=0.482) and *myb related protein1* (*mrp1*, p=0.1445) were not 178 significantly different (Supplemental Figure 4). These results suggest that *nkd1* and
- 179 *nkd2* likely regulate transcription in BET cells but are not required for BET cell
- 180 differentiation.

### 181 **Effects of the** *nkd1 nkd2* **Mutant on Endosperm Composition**

182 The *nkd1 nkd2* mutant was previously reported to cause decreased total kernel weight 183 and opaque endosperm texture (Yi et al., 2015), and transcriptomic analysis suggested 184 alterations in starch and storage protein pathways, prompting examination of seed 185 composition. WT and *nkd1-Ds nkd2-Ds0297* mutant kernels from the same F2 186 segregating ears were compared. Mutants contained less vitreous endosperm than WT 187 (Figure 3A) and showed a 41.8% decrease of total kernel dry weight with a 50.8% 188 reduction in endosperm (Table 3). The *nkd1-Ds nkd2-Ds0297* mutant kernels showed a 189 32.5% reduction in total starch abundance as determined by amyloglucosidase 190 digestion quantified by colorimetric glucose oxidase-peroxidase (GOPOD) assays 191 (Table 3). Some of the DE gene transcripts were implicated in determining starch 192 structure (Figure 2), of which glucan branch chain length distribution is a major 193 component. Glucan branch chain length abundance was measured by fluorophore-194 assisted carbohydrate electrophoresis of de-branched endosperm starch (O'Shea and 195 Morell, 1996). Difference plots of *nkd1 nkd2* mutant minus WT starch chain length 196 abundance show the mutant starch had a decrease in the frequency of branch chains of 197 6 and 27-28 glucose units, while chains 13-18 glucose units in length were significantly 198 increased in abundance (Figure 3B).

199 Starch grain morphology was examined with scanning electron microscopy (SEM).

200 Whereas the WT starch granules had smooth surfaces and were nearly spherical in

201 shape, mutant granules had distinctly pitted surfaces and were more irregularly shaped,

- 202 sometimes appearing faceted (Figure 3C and 3D; Supplemental Figure 5). Further,
- 203 bisected mutant starch grains often showed irregular hollow cores with internal pitting,
- 204 while WT grains were nearly solid with a small hollow center (Figure 3E and 3F).

205 Protein content in WT versus *nkd1-Ds;nkd2-Ds0297* mutant endosperm was estimated 206 from total nitrogen content measured using Dumas N combustion (Schindler and 207 Knighton, 1999). There was a 52.11% reduction in total protein content in endosperm of 208 mutant segregants (p=0.0054, Table 3).

209 Fluorescently tagged transgenes were available for several genes DE in *nkd1 nkd2*  210 mutant endosperm, including 2 storage protein genes, *floury2* (*fl2*) (Coleman et al., 211 1997), *globulin3* (*glb3*) (Woo et al., 2001) and a putative ABA response gene 212 *responsive to aba17* (*rab17*) (Kizis and Pages, 2002). The *nkd1-R nkd2*-R mutant 213 alleles were introduced into FL2-RFP, GLB3-RFP and RAB17-YFP transgenic lines, 214 which consist of fluorescent proteins translationally fused to the maize gene coding 215 sequences controlled by their native regulatory elements (Mohanty et al., 2009). Mature 216 kernels were sampled from segregating F2 ears and epifluorescence imaging 217 demonstrated that each reporter showed decreased fluorescence intensity in mutant 218 compared to WT (Figure 4). Thus, decreased expression levels of these transgenic 219 reporter proteins were consistent with the RNAseq analysis of native gene transcripts.

220 Many variables can influence grain yield and composition but principal component 221 analysis (PCA) clearly identified genotype as the primary contributor to the observed 222 variance in these traits. Genotype explained 99.9% of the variance for endosperm 223 weight and total protein, as well as for starch content and branch length, while the 224 remaining 0.1% of variance was explained by independent cob, or independent kernel 225 for protein and starch, respectively (Supplemental Figure 6).

#### 226 **Identification of NKD1 and NKD2 Target DNA Binding Sequences**

227 To identify DNA binding sequences for NKD1 and NKD2 proteins, selection and

228 amplification binding (SAAB) was undertaken. Columns were generated with the

- 229 conserved DNA-binding ID domains of NKD1 (NKD1ID) and NKD2 (NKD2ID). Each
- 230 protein was bacterially expressed as a GST fusion and purified proteins were covalently
- 231 bound to ester agarose columns. The identity of purified recombinant protein was
- 232 confirmed by SDS PAGE and MS/MS (Supplemental Figure 7). A column of purified

233 GST from the empty vector served as a negative control for nonspecific binding. 234 Libraries enriched for DNA-sequences that interact with the NKDID proteins were 235 generated by passing random double-stranded oligonucleotides over each protein 236 column for 6 cycles of selection and amplification. After cloning and sequencing 237 selected oligos, a total of 77 unique sequences were recovered for NKD1 and 54 for 238 NKD2. Motif analysis of the recovered sequences identified an 8-bp binding consensus 239 sequence (BCS) of [TA]-T-[TCG]-G-T-[CGA]-G-T for NKD1 produced from 27 of the 240 recovered sequences (Figure 5A; Supplemental Table 3). For NKD2, a 6bp BCS of T-G-241 T-[CT]-G-[TG] was identified from 19 recovered sequences, which was similar to the 242 NKD1 BCS (Figure 5B; Supplemental Table 4). No sequences were recovered from the 243 GST negative controls.

244 Electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSAs) were performed to further examine the 245 DNA binding properties of the NKD1 and NKD2 ID domains. Oligonucleotide probes 246 were designed based on frequency of base occupancy in the SAAB selected BCSs 247 (Supplemental Figure 8A and B). To test specific DNA base requirements, each base 248 was substituted individually and assayed with both the NKD1ID-GST and NKD2ID-GST 249 proteins. The GST protein tag purified from the empty vector showed no interaction with 250 any of the oligonucleotide probes (Supplemental Figure 8C and E). Relative binding 251 affinity was determined for each mutant by comparing the amount of shifted probe 252 relative to the non-mutant BCS probe. For NKD1ID, binding was abolished by a 253 substitution of cytosine in position 8, and strongly decreased by a substitutions of 254 thymine at position 4 or adenine at position 5 (Figure 5C and 5D; Supplemental Figures 255 8E and 9). Base substitutions at positions 2 and 7 produced moderate decreases in 256 binding and substitutions at positions 3 and 6 produced slight decreases in binding 257 affinity. Although position 1 appeared selected by SAAB, a cytosine substitution did not 258 decrease binding affinity. Taken together, NKD1ID recognizes a 7-bp BCS of T-T-G-T-259 C-G-T (NKDcore). NKD2ID recognizes the same 7bp NKDcore BCS but with different 260 binding tolerances for off consensus base substitutions in positions 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8. 261 (Figure 5C and 5F; Supplemental Figure 8E). Although position 2 was not selected by

262 NKD2ID SAAB, binding was abolished in EMSA by a cytosine substitution. Binding was 263 also abolished by a substitution in position 4, and strongly decreased by a substitution 264 in position 5. Moderate decreases in DNA binding were observed for substitutions in 265 positons 7 and 8. Competition EMSA with mutant probes showed binding to the BCS 266 was specific and confirmed that NKD1ID and NKD2ID have different tolerances for 267 sequence variants at positions 2 and 8 (Figure 5E and 5G).

#### 269 **NKD1 and NKD2 Direct Target Gene Prediction and Motif Enrichment**

270 To determine likely direct targets of NKD1 and NKD2, proximal promoter regions 271 (PPRs) of all genes with transcripts detected in SE and AL were searched for the 272 presence of NKD1, NKD2, and NKDcore BCSs using the MEME suite motif analysis 273 tool Find Individual Motif Occurrences (FIMO) (Bailey et al., 2015). Sequence elements 274 shown by EMSA not to bind NKD1 or NKD2 were removed from the analysis. NKDID 275 binding to six of the variant BCSs identified by FIMO were confirmed by EMSA, verifying 276 the efficacy of the FIMO analysis for predicting in vitro binding ability (Figure 5C, 5D and 277 5F; Supplemental Figure 8E). A total of 32,621 PPRs were identified from the 15 DAP 278 AL transcriptome, including 2,135 DE genes and 31,389 PPRs were identified from the 279 SE transcriptome, including 2,192 DE genes (Supplemental Dataset 3). The 280 occurrences of NKD BCSs are summarized in Table 4, Supplemental Figure 10, and 281 Supplemental Dataset 3. Due to the relatedness among the identified BCSs, there was 282 considerable overlap among the FIMO-identified promoter elements (Supplemental 283 Figure 10). Furthermore, because only 6 bp were selected for NKD2 while 8 bp were 284 selected for NKD1, there are considerably more potential NKD2 sites throughout the 285 genome. Nearly half the predicted NKD1 and NKDcore sites are included among the 286 NKD2 predicted sites and all the predicted NKD1 sites are included among the 287 NKDcore sites.

288 To test whether NKD BCSs were enriched in the promoters of genes DE in the *nkd1*  289 *nkd2* mutant endosperm, the proportion of PPRs with one or more BCS among DE

290 genes was compared to that of the 15 DAP transcriptome. In AL, NKD2 and NKDcore 291 motifs were significantly enriched in DE genes, while the NKD1 motif was not (Table 4; 292 Supplemental Dataset 3). Conversely, in SE, the NKD1 motif was enriched in DE 293 genes, while NKD2 and NKDcore motifs were not significant (Table 4; Supplemental 294 Dataset 3). BCS enrichment controls consisted of three 'shuffle motifs' each for NKD1 295 and NKD2 generated by randomly rearranging bases in the BCSs. Enrichment was not 296 detected for any of the 'shuffle motifs', indicating that the enrichment of the NKD motifs 297 was specific (Supplemental Figure 11).

### 313 **NKD1 and NKD2 Proteins Dimerize via the ID Domain**

314 To determine if NKD1 and NKD2 can dimerize as shown for other IDD protein family 315 members (Seo et al., 2011b; Yoshida et al., 2014), bimolecular florescence 316 complementation (BiFC) and pull-down assays were performed. For BiFC, the N- or C-317 terminal portion of the YFP protein was fused to the C-termini of the NKD1 and NKD2

318 proteins. Both full-length NKD1 and NKD2 proteins as well as truncated proteins

- 319 containing only the ID domains were tested. Constructs were biolistically introduced into
- 320 onion epidermal cells with a 35S:mCherry construct co-bombarded to identify transiently
- 321 transformed cells. For the full-length constructs, reconstituted YFP florescence was
- 322 detected, indicating homodimerization of NKD1 and NKD2, as well as
- 323 heterodimerization between NKD1 and NKD2 (Figure 6A). Likewise for the ID domain
- 324 truncations, NKD1ID and NKD2ID could homo- and heterodimerize (Supplemental
- 325 Figure 12). Control bombardments consisting of combinations of NKD full length-nYFP+
- 326 empty vector cYFP or NKD full length-cYFP+ empty vector nYFP did not produce YFP
- 327 fluorescence indicating that YFP reconstitution was dependent on interactions among
- 328 the NKD1 and NKD2 proteins (Figure 6 and Supplemental Figure 12).
- 329 Pull-down assays were performed on NKD1 and NKD2 full-length and ID domain
- 330 proteins expressed in *E. coli* cells with a C-terminal 6x His-tag or a N-terminal GST-tag.
- 331 Co-pull downs were performed by mixing lysates of NKD-6x His and GST-NKD proteins,
- 332 precipitating protein complexes using Ni resin for 6x His and immunoblotting with GST
- 333 antibodies, or precipitating with Glutathione Sepharose for GST tags and
- 334 immunoblotting with 6x His antibodies. Co-precipitation was observed in all
- 335 combinations of NKD1 and NKD2 full-length proteins, but not with the empty vector
- 336 controls (Figure 6B; Supplemental Figure 13). Likewise, for the ID domain truncated
- 337 protein constructs, co-precipitation was observed for all NKD1ID and NKD2ID
- 338 combinations (Figure 6C; Supplemental Figure 13). Thus, NKD1 and NKD2 homo- and
- 339 heterodimerize, and these interactions are mediated by residues in the ID domain.

# 340 **NKD1 and NKD2 Function as Transcription Factors**

- 341 To test the transcriptional regulatory activity of NKD1 and NKD2, transcription assays
- 342 were designed using the promoter regions of select predicted direct targets of NKD1
- 343 and NKD2. Reporter constructs contained the promoters of interest cloned upstream of
- 344 the firefly luciferase (LUC) coding sequence. Promoter regions tested included *xylanase*
- 345 *inhibitor protein 1* (*zmX1P-1*pro:LUC), *opaque2* (*o2*pro:LUC), 22KD *zein protein 22.1*

346 (*zp22.1*pro:LUC), *nkd1* (*nkd1*pro:LUC), *viviparous1* (*vp1*pro:LUC), *jasmonate induced*  347 *protein* (JIPpro:LUC), *mother of FT-like* (MTFpro:LUC) and *WRKY transcription factor 29* 348 (*wrky29*pro:LUC). Effector constructs contained the NKD1 or NKD2 cDNAs under the 349 regulation of the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter (35Spro:NKD1, 35Spro:NKD2). 350 The empty effector vector (35Spro:null) was used as a control. A 35S promoter:Renilla 351 luciferase construct (35Spro:RLUC) served as the internal normalization standard. The 352 reporter, effector, and normalization constructs were co-introduced into protoplasts of 353 *nkd1-Ds; nkd2-Ds0297* mutant endosperms using PEG-calcium transfection. 354 Transcriptional activity for each effector and test promoter construct pair was 355 determined by comparing the firefly/renilla luminescence ratio to the 35Spro:null effector. 356 Transcriptional activation was detected for the NKD1 effector from the *zmX1P-*1pro 357 (p<0.0001), *o2*pro (p=0.0409) and *vp1*pro reporter constructs (p= 0.038; Figure 7B). No 358 significant transcriptional regulation was observed for NKD1 effector from the *zp22.1*pro 359 or *nkd1*pro reporters (p=0.3368, p=0.1008, respectively; Figure 7B). Transcriptional 360 activation was detected for the NKD2 effector from the *zmX1P-1*pro (p=0.0009), *o2*pro 361 (p=0.0025), *zp22.1*pro (p=0.0014), and the *vp1*pro reporters (p=0.0317, Figure 7B). 362 Transcriptional repression was detected for NKD2 effector from the *nkd1*pro reporter 363 (p=0.0140, Figure 7B). No significant transcriptional activation or repression was 364 detected for NKD1 or NKD2 from the JIPpro, MTFpro or *wrky29*pro reporters (p>0.05). 365 The relative luciferase ratios for each reporter were compared between NKD1 and 366 NKD2 effectors. NKD1 showed significantly higher activity from the *zmX1P-*1pro (p≤ 367 0.001) and *o2*pro (p=0.048) reporters, while NKD2 transcriptional activities were higher 368 for *zp22.1*pro (p≤0.001) and *vp1*pro (p= 0.020) reporters. For the *nkd1*pro (p=0.025) 369 reporter, NKD2 effector repressed activity while activation by NKD1 was not statistically 370 significant (Figure 7B). These results suggest possible functional differences between 371 NKD1 and NKD2 transcriptional activities, consistent with observed differences in DNA 372 binding specificities (Figure 5; Supplemental Figure 8). Overall, these results were 373 consistent with direct target gene predictions and confirmed NKD1 and NKD2 function 374 to regulate gene transcription.

375 To test whether NKD1 and NKD2 require their BCS to regulate transcription, a mutant *vp1* promoter construct (*vp1*-mutpro:LUC), was cloned with the thymine in the 7th 376 position 377 of the second NKDcore BCS substituted with a cytosine (TTGTCG**T** to TTGTCG**C**). This 378 substitution abrogated binding in EMSAs (Figure 5D and 5F). Transcriptional activation 379 of the *vp1*-mutpro:LUC reporter construct was not detected for NKD1 effector (p=0.3120; 380 Figure 8B), while NKD2 was still able to activate transcription (p=0.0377). The level of 381 *vp1*-mutpro reporter activity was decreased compared to WT *vp1*pro for both NKD1 and 382 NKD2 effectors (p=0.0139, p=0.0437, respectively). Also, transcriptional activity from 383 the *vp1*-mutpro reporter was significantly different between NKD1 and NKD2 effectors 384 (p=0.007). These results demonstrate that NKD1 and NKD2 transcriptional activity is 385 mediated by binding to their BCS in the promoter regions of target genes, validates the 386 bioinformatic predictions of NKD direct target genes, and further indicate NKD1 and 387 NKD2 have differences in transcriptional activity.

#### 389 **DISCUSSION**

390

#### 391 **Functions of NKD1 and NKD2 in developing endosperm**

392 In this study, we revealed the functions of the NKD1 and NKD2 transcription factors in 393 maize endosperm development by analyzing the AL and SE transcriptomes of WT 394 compared to *nkd1 nkd2* double mutant. Previous genetic analysis indicated that *nkd1*  395 and *nkd2* are largely redundant with *nkd1* single mutants, producing a mild opaque 396 phenotype and no discernable *nkd2* mutant phenotype (Yi et al., 2015). Therefore, we 397 chose to focus on the double mutant for this study. Pathway analyses showed NKD1 398 and NKD2 are regulators of diverse biological processes including cell growth, division 399 and differentiation, hormone metabolism and signaling, resource reserve deposition, 400 and seed maturation. These results were largely consistent with the *nkd1 nkd2* mutant's 401 pleiotropic endosperm phenotype (Becraft and Asuncion-Crabb, 2000; Yi et al., 2015) 402 which provides biological validity to the transcriptomic and bioinformatic analyses.

403 NKD1 and NKD2 are required to restrict AL to a single cell layer and to promote AL cell 404 identity (Becraft and Asuncion-Crabb, 2000; Yi et al., 2015). Consistent with these 405 functions, NKD1 and NKD2 were found to regulate genes associated with cell cycle, cell 406 growth and division. NKD1 and NKD2 are predicted to be direct transcriptional 407 repressors of *retinoblastoma-related1* and *mitotic cyclin 3B-like* (Figure 2; Supplemental 408 Datasets 1, 2 and 3). The *defective kernel1* (*dek1*) gene is required for aleurone 409 differentiation (Becraft et al., 2002; Lid et al., 2002) and regulates cell wall orientation 410 and cell division plane via the regulation of cell cycle (Liang et al., 2015). These results 411 suggest the regulation of cell division is important for aleurone development and that the 412 multiple peripheral cell layers in the *nkd1 nkd2* mutant may result in part from the 413 misregulation of cell proliferation genes.

414 Grain weight of *nkd1 nkd2* mutants was decreased compared to WT, which is likely 415 related to NKD1 and NKD2 functions in multiple aspects of resource reserve deposition 416 including starch and storage protein accumulation. Starch is the major product for grain 417 yield, and starch structure can greatly affect physical properties that influence end use. 418 The *nkd1 nkd2* mutant endosperm displayed a reduction in total starch abundance as 419 well as altered glucan chain length distribution. Decreased starch accumulation is 420 consistent with decreased expression of starch biosynthetic genes (Table 3; Figure 2). 421 Notably, transcript levels of *shrunken-2* (*sh2*) and *brittle-2* (*bt2*) were both decreased 422 (Figure 2); these genes encode subunits of ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase, which 423 catalyzes a rate-limiting step in starch biosynthesis (Tuncel and Okita, 2013). Other key 424 starch biosynthetic genes showing decreased transcript abundance included *sucrose*  425 *synthase2*, *waxy1, starch synthase1* (*ss1*) and *sugary2* (*su2*) (Figure 2). Likewise, 426 altered glucan chain length distribution (Figure 3B) could result from decreased 427 expression of *amylose-extender1* (*ae1*), which encodes starch branching enzyme IIb 428 (BEIIb) (Nishi et al., 2001), and *ss1* and *su2* genes, which catalyze glucan chain 429 elongation. Direct target analysis predicted that NKD1 and NDK2 are direct 430 transcriptional activators of *ss1, su1* and *wx1* (Supplemental Dataset 3).

431 The *nkd1 nkd2* mutant starch granules showed irregular morphology that could not be 432 obviously attributed to specific genes (Figures 2, 3C and 3D). While WT starch granules 433 are nearly spherical, mutants showed a mild faceting somewhat reminiscent of other 434 starch mutants like *opaque5* or various starch branching mutants (Myers et al., 2011; 435 Zhao et al., 2015). The pitted surface of *nkd1 nkd2* mutant starch granules resembled 436 starch grains subjected to amylase digestion (Dhital et al., 2014). Given the propensity 437 for vivipary in *nkd1 nkd2* mutant kernels and decreased ABA signaling, it is possible that 438 amylase expression is prematurely activated (Yi et al., 2015). Also, the irregular hollow 439 core of *nkd1 nkd2* mutant starch granules is fascinating, and it is unclear whether this 440 might be caused by internal amylase digestion or whether this reflects aberrant starch 441 grain initiation.

442 Pathway analyses indicated that NKD1 and NKD2 promote storage protein 443 accumulation, both directly and indirectly, consistent with decreased storage protein 444 content in the *nkd1 nkd2* mutant. NKD2 activated transcription from the 22KD *zein*  445 *protein 22.1* promoter (Figure 7B), while NKD1 and NKD2 both activated transcription of 446 the *o2* promoter. Furthermore, *prolamin binding factor1* (*pbf1*) is a predicted direct 447 target of NKD1 and NKD2. O2 and PBF1 are TFs both well known to promote 448 expression of zein storage protein genes (Zhang et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2016). The 449 FL2-RFP and GLB3-RFP transgenes showed decreased expression in *nkd1 nkd2*  450 mutant endosperm corroborating the positive regulation of storage protein gene 451 expression by NKD1 and NKD2 (Table 3, 5 and 6; Figure 4A and 4B). 452 NKD1 and NKD2 promote carotenoid accumulation because mutant endosperm is pale

453 yellow, sometimes almost white. While carotenoid biosynthesis did not meet the 454 pathway criterion of having at least 3 DE genes, 2 key genes showed decreased 455 expression in the mutant; *yellow endosperm1* (*y1*) encodes phytoene synthase and 456 *viviparous5* (*vp5*) encodes phytoene desaturase (Buckner et al., 1996; Hable et al., 457 1998). Both represent rate-limiting steps in carotenoid biosynthesis and mutations in 458 either gene cause carotenoid deficiency. Carotenoids are precursors for ABA 459 biosynthesis, so this is also likely to contribute to the vivipary phenotype. Additionally, 460 the *nkd1 nkd2* mutant showed decreased expression of *aldehyde oxidase1* and *2* genes

- 461 encoding enzymes that catalyze the final step in ABA synthesis (Seo et al., 2000),
- 462 which is also consistent with vivipary and the decreased expression of ABA response
- 463 pathways (Figure 2; Supplemental Datasets 1 and 2).

464 The *nkd1 nkd2* mutant was originally identified based on its anthocyanin-deficient

- 465 phenotype (Becraft and Asuncion-Crabb, 2000), and our analysis suggests the
- 466 regulation of anthocyanin biosynthesis is indirect. The *colored1* (*r1*) and *colored*
- 467 *aleurone1* (*c1*) genes encode transcription factors that together activate expression of
- 468 anthocyanin biosynthetic genes, and VP1 is a transcriptional activator of *c1* (Cone,
- 469 2007). NKD1 and NKD2 directly activate *vp1* transcription and are predicted to directly
- 470 activate *r1* (Figure 7B; Supplemental Datasets 1, 2 and 3).
- 471 A phenotype we have not systematically evaluated is the proclivity of *nkd1 nkd2* mutant
- 472 kernels to develop fungal infections. It is unclear whether this is a direct mutant defect
- 473 or an indirect effect caused by soft texture or impaired seed maturation. However, the
- 474 defense response pathway showed decreased activity in the *nkd1 nkd2* mutant
- 475 endosperm (Supplemental Figures 2 and 3) and genome-wide promoter scan analysis
- 476 suggests NKD1 and NKD2 are direct positive regulators of defense responses
- 477 (Supplemental Datasets 1, 2 and 3). The aleurone is the outermost cell layer of the
- 478 endosperm and the only cell type alive at maturity; thus, it is fundamentally important for
- 479 defense to pathogens (Jerkovic et al., 2010). Among predicted direct targets was
- 480 *xylanase inhibitor protein-1* (XIP-1), which is involved in fungal defense (Moscetti et al.,
- 481 2013) and whose promoter showed transcriptional activation by NKD1 and NKD2
- 482 (Figure 7B). These results implicate NKD1 and NKD2 as potentially having a direct
- 483 regulatory role in mediating defense.

# 484 **NKD1 and NKD2 are key regulators of endosperm gene expression**

- 485 NKD1 and NKD2 regulate widespread gene expression in developing endosperm and
- 486 direct target analysis revealed approximately 6% of maize gene promoters contain a
- 487 NKDcore BCS. 80 DE genes in AL and 86 in SE were TF-related, with 36 and 33,

488 respectively, predicted as direct NKD targets (Figure 2; Supplemental Datasets 1 to 4). 489 Additionally, epigenetic regulation was enriched in GO terms of DE genes. Taken 490 together, NKD1 and NKD2 appear to function as central regulators in the gene networks 491 governing multiple aspects of maize endosperm development.

492 Several key TFs were shown or predicted to be direct targets of NKD regulation, 493 including O2, PBF1, VP1 and R1. O2 is an important promoter of zein storage protein 494 gene expression, significant for its importance in quality protein maize with enhanced 495 lysine content (Gibbon and Larkins, 2005). PBF1 is a Dof TF that also promotes 496 expression of storage protein genes (Marzábal et al., 2008). O2 binds a DNA motif 497 known as GCN4 while PBF1 binds the prolamin box. These motifs are frequently 498 combined into a bifactorial 'endosperm box' conserved among storage protein genes in 499 all cereals (Marzábal et al., 1998). Recent analyses showed O2 and PBF1 also function 500 broadly, controlling multiple classes of zeins, as well genes involved in carbon and 501 nitrogen metabolism, pathogen and stress responses, as well as other TFs (Li et al., 502 2015; Zhang et al., 2016). Comparison of the direct target genes of O2 with NKD1 and 503 NKD2 revealed 23 genes related to resource reserve deposition in common 504 (Supplemental Tables 5 and 6). Furthermore, GO terms associated with protein 505 synthesis and storage were overrepresented in both NKD and O2 regulated genes 506 (Supplemental Tables 7 and 8), suggesting O2, NKD1 and NKD2 may function together 507 to regulate storage protein accumulation. VP1 (ABI3 ortholog) is a B3 TF required for 508 ABA responses to promote seed maturation and inhibit germination (Suzuki et al., 509 2003). As mentioned, VP1 is also required for anthocyanin accumulation in the aleurone 510 via its transcriptional activation of the *c1* gene, which encodes a myb TF (Cone, 2007). 511 C1 heterodimerizes with R1, a bHLH TF, to activate expression of structural genes in 512 anthocyanin biosynthesis (Cone, 2007). The *r1* gene is also predicted to be directly 513 regulated by NKD1 and NKD2. The *c1* and *r1* genes are historically important in early 514 genetic studies by McClintock and others.

515 It was previously reported that the *nkd* genes were subject to feedback regulation 516 because *nkd1* transcript levels increased in a *nkd2* single mutant and vice versa (Yi et

- 517 al., 2015). Here we showed that NKD2 repressed expression of the *nkd1* gene
- 518 promoter; thus, the observed feedback appears to be due to direct transregulation
- 519 between the *nkd1* and *nkd2* duplicate factors.
- 520 The *nkd1* and *nkd2* genes were recently shown to be direct targets of transcriptional
- 521 activation by DOF3 (Qi et al., 2016), hereafter referred to as DOF36
- 522 (GRMZM2G137502) in accordance with NCBI and MaizeGDB annotations. RNAi
- 523 knockdown of *dof36* generated similar effects as the *nkd1 nkd2* double mutant including
- 524 abnormal starch deposition and multiple layers of compromised aleurone cells. Some of
- 525 the same genes involved in sugar and starch metabolism were identified as direct (and
- 526 indirect) targets for DOF36 as for NKD1 and NKD2. Interestingly, *dof36* transcript shows
- 527 decreased expression in *nkd1 nkd2* mutant (Supplemental Dataset 1), although it was 528 not identified as a direct target. This indicates *dof36* belongs to the same GRN as *nkd1*
- 529 and *nkd2*, and that *dof36* expression is reinforced by feedback, albeit indirect, from *nkd* 530 genes.

# 531 **Functions of NKD1, NKD2 and other IDD family members**

532 NKD1 and NKD2 bind DNA and regulate transcription via a consensus motif 533 (TTGTCGT) similar to the ID1 binding site (TTTGTCGTTTT) although shorter (Kozaki et 534 al., 2004). Despite 97% amino acid identity (118 of 122 residues) between their ID 535 domains, NKD1 and NKD2 showed differences in their DNA-binding and transcriptional 536 regulation activities. Mutation of position 1 in the NKDcore motif to C decreased binding 537 by NKD1 but abolished NKD2 binding, whereas mutation of position 7 to C abolished 538 NKD1 binding but NKD2 could still bind (Figure 5). In transcription assays, NKD1 539 showed higher activity than NKD2 on the XIP-1 and *o2* promoters, whereas NKD2 540 showed greater activation of *vp1* and *zp22.1* promoters (Figure 7). On the *nkd1* 541 promoter, NKD2 repressed transcription, whereas NKD1 activated, though not 542 statistically significant. Whether these functional differences relate to differences in DNA 543 binding activity or interactions with unknown cofactors remains to be explored, as do the 544 functional differences between NKD1 and NKD2 on a genome-wide scale. These

545 differences indicate that NKD1 and NKD2 are not completely redundant at the 546 molecular level and that they have undergone subfunctionalization since the most 547 recent maize genome duplication event that generated these loci.

548 Different IDD protein family members function directly as DNA-binding transcriptional 549 regulators, indirectly as co-factors for other transcription factors, or both. DELLA 550 proteins, such as Arabidopsis RGA1, are GRAS family TFs that regulate gene 551 expression in response to GA signalling, among other things. RGA1 activates 552 transcription of *SCARECROW-LIKE3* (*SCL3*) via interaction with any of 5 IDD proteins, 553 AtIDD3, -4, -5, -9, and -10. The IDD proteins bind DNA and function as scaffolds for the 554 DELLA proteins, which lack intrinsic DNA binding activity (Yoshida et al., 2014; Yoshida 555 and Ueguchi-Tanaka, 2014). SCL3 is another GRAS protein that interacts with the IDD 556 proteins to competitively inhibit the binding and action of RGA1. This provides feedback 557 to modulate gene expression responses to GA signalling. Similarly, the IDD protein 558 JACKDAW (JKD) regulates transcription of *SCARECROW* (*SCR*) in Arabidopsis root 559 development. JKD activity is enhanced by the GRAS protein SHORTROOT (SHR), as 560 well as by the target gene product, SCR, another GRAS protein (Ogasawara et al., 561 2011). Further, the activity of JKD is also modulated by interactions with other IDD 562 proteins, MAGPIE and BALDIBIS (Long et al., 2015; Ogasawara et al., 2011). In 563 addition to modulating transcriptional activity of this complex, these interactions 564 prevented intercellular trafficking of the mobile protein SHR.

565 We found NKD1 and NKD2 could each homodimerize as well as heterodimerize with 566 one another. The functional significance of this is unknown and we do not yet know 567 whether they can dimerize with other IDD family members. Arabidopsis IDD14 also 568 homo- and heterodimerizes, and this dimerization modulates function (Seo et al., 569 2011b). Under standard laboratory temperatures, IDD14α binds DNA and regulates 570 transcription of target genes, but under cold temperatures the *IDD14* gene produces an 571 alternatively spliced transcript that encodes a product, IDD14β, that lacks the DNA-572 binding domain. IDD14β can heterodimerize with IDD14α and decreases DNA binding 573 activity.

574 IDD gene family members are implicated in many biological functions including the 575 regulation of carbohydrate metabolism, gravitropism, seed germination, lateral organ 576 morphogenesis, cellular patterning, flowering time and hormone signaling (Morita et al., 577 2006; Welch et al., 2007; Tanimoto et al., 2008; Hassan et al., 2010; Feurtado et al., 578 2011; Ogasawara et al., 2011; Seo et al., 2011a; Cui et al., 2013; Wu et al., 2013; 579 Yoshida et al., 2014; Yoshida and Ueguchi-Tanaka, 2014; Jost et al., 2016). It is striking 580 how many similar processes are regulated by the *nkd* genes during seed development. 581 For example, Arabidopsis IDD8 and IDD14 transcriptionally regulate carbohydrate 582 metabolism and starch accumulation by modulating expression of some of the same 583 target genes as NKD1 and NKD2, such as *ss1* (Seo et al., 2011b; Seo et al., 2011a)

584 (Figures 2, 3 and 4A).

585 An intriguing functional analogy is with IDD proteins (a.k.a. BIRD proteins) in

586 Arabidopsis root development. Four IDD proteins, JKD, BALDIBIS (BIB),

587 NUTCRACKER (NUC) and MAGPIE, have overlapping functions in the specification of

588 the cortical cell layer, and *jkd bib* double mutants contained extra layers of cells with

589 indistinct identity (Ogasawara et al., 2011; Long et al., 2015). The normal pattern of cell

590 division is mediated in part through the transcriptional repression of the *CYCLIND6*

591 gene (Sozzani et al., 2010; Long et al., 2015). Similarly, in *nkd1 nkd2* mutants of maize,

592 the normally single layer of aleurone cells is replaced by multiple layers of cells with

593 compromised identity. Further, *scarecrow-like1* (*scl1*) is predicted to be directly

594 activated by NKD1 and NKD2, while the cell cycle-related genes *retinoblastoma-*

595 *related1* and mitotic *cyclin 3B-like* are predicted to be negatively regulated by NKD1 and

596 NKD2 (Figure 2; Supplemental Dataset 3). Future work will seek to resolve potential

597 mechanistic conservation among these processes.

#### 798 **Total Pericarp, Endosperm, Embryo, and Seed Dry Weight Analysis**

799 For pericarp, endosperm, embryo and total seed weight analysis, 31 WT and 31 *nkd1*  800 *nkd2* mutant mature kernels from the same F2 segregating *nkd1-Ds; nkd2-Ds0297* 801 segregating ear were randomly selected. This was done on three independent ears for 802 a total of 93 WT kernels and 93 *nkd1 nkd2* mutant kernels. Mature kernels were 803 imbibed in double distilled water for 12 hours and were frozen solid at 4⁰C and thawed 804 to allow for efficient dissection. Pericarp, endosperm, and embryos were then dissected 805 into pools of tissue from 31 kernels and placed in a 55⁰C oven for 36 hours to re-806 desiccate the materials. Samples were then weighed to obtain pooled kernel pericarp, 807 endosperm, and embryo dry weights. Total seed weights were determined by adding 808 the pericarp, endosperm and embryo dry weights together for each independent ear. 809 Average pericarp, endosperm, embryo and total seed weights per kernel for WT and 810 *nkd*1 *nkd2* mutant were determined and statistical analysis was performed using a 811 Student's t-test.

### 863 **Protoplast Isolation and Transformation**

864 To avoid confounding effects from endogenous NKD1 and NKD2 protein, 18-20 DAP 865 *nkd1-Ds; nkd2-Ds0297* kernels were used for aleurone protoplast isolation as 866 described (Bethke and Jones, 2001). Aleurone peels from 30-40 kernels (~0.5g) of the 867 same ear were harvested and placed immediately in 15 ml of TVL solution (0.3 M 868 sorbitol; 50 mM CaCL2). 20 ml of Enzyme solution (0.5 M sucrose, 10 mM MES-KOH 869 [pH 5.7], 20 mM CaCl2, 40 mM KCl, 1% Cellulase Onozuka R-10, 1% Macerozyme 870 R10) were then added and the tissue shaken at 35 rpm at room temperature for 16-18 871 hours. Protoplasts were collected by passing through 10 micro meter nylon mesh 872 (spectrum labs) with W5 solution (154 mM NaCl, 125 mM CaCl2, 5 mM KCl, 2 mM MES 873 [pH 5.7]). Protoplasts were recovered from the nylon mesh by rinsing with 15 ml of W5 874 solution followed by centrifugation for 5 minutes at 60 g. Protoplast pellets were gently 875 re-suspended in 15 ml of fresh W5 solution, centrifuged for 5 minutes at 60 g, then re-876 suspended 1 ml MMg solution (0.4 M mannitol, 15 mM MgCl2, 4 mM MES [pH 5.7]). 877 Protoplast integrity and quantification was determined by optical visualization on a light 878 microscope.

879 Protoplast transformation was performed following the protocol outlined by Bethke and 880 Jones (2001). For each transformation, 100 μl of fresh protoplasts (~2x10^4) were

881 added to a 2 ml microfuge tube. Effector, reporter and normalization plasmids in 10 μl 882 total volume (10-20 μg DNA total) were added and 110 μl of PEG solution (40% 883 weight/volume PEG 4000, 0.2 M mannitol,100 mM CaCl2) was added, gently mixed, 884 and incubated at room temperature for 10 min without shaking. W5 solution (400 μl) 885 was added to stop the transfection process, tubes were centrifuged at 100 g for 2 min 886 at room temperature, and protoplast pellets gently re-suspended in 1 ml WI solution 887 (0.5 M mannitol, 4 mM MES [pH 5.7], 20 mM KCl) and incubated at room temperature 888 on a rotator for 14 h.
