#### **Abstract**

# *Key message* **We reported that knockdown of** *OsDCL3b* **decreased grain yield but increased grain quality in rice, which is helpful for molecular breeding in crops.**

**Abstract** Multiple *DICER-LIKE* (*DCL*) genes usually exist and show diverse biochemical and phenotypic functions in land plants. In rice, the biochemical function of *OsDCL3b* is known to process 24-nucleotide panicle phased small RNAs, however, its phenotypic functions are unclear. Here we reported that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* led to reduced pollen fertility, seed setting rate, and decreased grain yield but increased grain quality in rice. To reveal the molecular mechanism of the above phenomena, extracted RNAs from rice panicles of the wild type (WT) and *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 were analyzed by deep sequencing. It showed that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* affected the biogenesis of both 21- and 24-nucleotide small RNAs including miRNAs and phased small RNAs. Using RNA-seq, 644 up- and 530 down-regulated mRNA genes were identified in panicles of line S6-1, and 550 and 273 differentially spliced genes with various alternative splicing (AS) events were observed in panicles of line S6-1 and WT, respectively, suggesting that *OsDCL3b* involved in influencing the transcript levels of mRNA genes and the AS events in rice panicles. Thus, our results show that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* will affect the biogenesis of small RNAs, which is involved in regulating the transcription of mRNA genes, and consequently influence the grain yield and quality in rice.

**Keywords** OsDCL3b · Pollen fertility · Seed-setting rate · Grain yield · Grain quality · Small RNA

plants (Carrington and Ambros [2003](#page-10-0); Bartel [2004](#page-10-1)). It is well known that these two types of small RNAs are distinguished by the derivation of their precursors: miRNAs are usually processed from single-stranded RNAs with imperfect stem-loop structures, whereas siRNAs are processed from long double-stranded RNAs (dsRNAs) with presumably perfect complementarity (Tang [2005](#page-11-0); Meyers et al. [2008](#page-10-2); Arikit et al. [2013\)](#page-10-3). However, both miRNAs and siRNAs can induce RNA silencing and play important roles in regulating genome stability, plant growth and development as well as adaptation to adversity (Meyers et al. [2008;](#page-10-2) Ramachandran and Chen [2008](#page-10-4)).

Dicer and Dicer-like (DCL) proteins, the RNase III-type enzymes, are key components of both miRNA and siRNA biogenesis pathways in processing long double-stranded RNAs into mature small RNAs (Bartel [2004](#page-10-1); Meyers et al. [2008](#page-10-2)). These endonucleases generally have six types of domains (Margis et al. [2006\)](#page-10-5), and some of these domains including tandemly repeated RNase III domain, C-terminal dsRNA-binding domain and N-terminal helicase domain are

evolutionarily conserved in most of eukaryotes (Liu et al. [2005](#page-10-6)). However, numbers of Dicer or DCL proteins in the genome are variable for different organisms (Margis et al. [2006\)](#page-10-5). In land plants, *DCL* genes usually form a small family with more than four members (Liu et al. [2005;](#page-10-6) Margis et al. [2006](#page-10-5); Kapoor et al. [2008\)](#page-10-7), whereas only one Dicer protein is usually found in mammals and nematodes (Yeung et al. [2005](#page-11-1); Margis et al. [2006;](#page-10-5) Kapoor et al. [2008\)](#page-10-7).

Notably, members of dicer or DCL family from different species play both specific and redundant roles in the small RNA biogenesis pathways (Lee et al. [2004;](#page-10-8) Parent et al. [2015;](#page-10-9) Wu et al. [2017\)](#page-11-2). For example, two Dicers were identified in *Drosophila*, Dicer-1 was required for miRNA production, and Dicer-2 was responsible for siRNA biogenesis (Lee et al. [2004](#page-10-8)). In *Arabidopsis*, four DCLs with different functions were characterized (Xie et al. [2004;](#page-11-3) Wu et al. [2017](#page-11-2)). DCL1 was associated with miRNA production (Park et al. [2002](#page-10-10); Kurihara and Watanabe [2004](#page-10-11)); DCL2 was involved in siRNA productions from natural *cis*-acting antisense transcripts and played roles in viral resistance (Xie et al. [2004;](#page-11-3) Borsani et al. [2005\)](#page-10-12); DCL3 was in charge of producing 24-nucleiotide (nt) endogenous RDR2-dependent siRNAs for a guide of chromatin modification (Xie et al. [2004](#page-11-3); Wu et al. [2017](#page-11-2)); DCL4 participated in maturation of 21-nt phased *trans-acting* siRNAs (ta-siRNAs) from endogenous *TAS* genes (Gasciolli et al. [2005](#page-10-13); Xie et al. [2005](#page-11-4)). Both DCL1 and DCL3 were shown redundant functions on promoting flowering via repressing the expression of FLOWERING LOCUS C in *Arabidopsis thaliana* (Schmitz et al. [2007\)](#page-11-5). Redundant functions were also observed for DCL2 and DCL4 with respect to their antiviral defense (Bouche et al. [2006](#page-10-14)), and for DCL2, DCL3 and DCL4 in the biogenesis of siRNA and tasiRNA (Henderson et al. [2006](#page-10-15)).

In rice, eight *OsDCL* genes have been identified and functions of most *OsDCL* genes have been reported (Kapoor et al. [2008](#page-10-7)). *OsDCL1* plays important roles in processing of miRNAs and tasiRNAs (Liu et al. [2005](#page-10-6), [2007\)](#page-10-16), and loss of function of *OsDCL1* may cause pleiotropic phenotypes such as severe dwarfism and dark green color in shoot and root of rice seedlings (Liu et al. [2005](#page-10-6)), which is similar to the phenotypes of *AtDCL1* mutation in *Arabidopsis* (Schauer et al. [2002\)](#page-11-6). *OsDCL2* negatively affects the maintenance of OsEV (*Oryza sativa* endornavirus) by accumulating the virus siRNA during cell division, resulting in dwarf rice plants with significantly lower spikelet fertility (Urayama et al. [2010](#page-11-7)). *OsDCL4* is responsible for the processing of 21-nucleotide siRNAs including tasiRNAs and phased small RNAs (Liu et al. [2007](#page-10-16); Song et al. [2012\)](#page-11-8), and both knockdown and loss of function of *OsDCL4* lead to vegetative and reproductive development defects such as radialized coleoptiles and sterile spikelet (Liu et al. [2007\)](#page-10-16). Both *OsD-CL3a* and *OsDCL3b* are homologs of *Arabidopsis AtDCL3* gene (Liu et al. [2005\)](#page-10-6). *OsDCL3a* has been reported to have multiple functions. On the one hand, it is involved in the biogenesis of long miRNAs (lmiRNAs), which can further direct cytosine DNA methylation in *cis* and in *trans* (Wu et al. [2010\)](#page-11-9). On the other hand, it is also responsible for processing transposable element-associated 24-nt siRNAs, and down-regulated expression of *OsDCL3a* will cause abnormal phenotypes like larger flag leaf angle, dwarfism, and fewer secondary branches (Wei et al. [2014](#page-11-10)). To date, *OsDCL3b* is known to process the 24-nt phased small RNAs (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)), which are preferentially expressed in rice panicle (Johnson et al. [2009\)](#page-10-17). Moreover, *OsDCL3b* is reported to abundantly expressed in reproductive development related organs (Fei et al. [2016](#page-10-18); Ono et al. [2018\)](#page-10-19), indicating the potential roles of *OsDCL3b* in rice reproductive development. However, to our knowledge, the phenotypic functions of *OsDCL3b* in rice reproductive development have not been reported.

In this study, we found that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* led to lower pollen fertility and seed setting rate, which resulted in decreased grain yield in rice. Interestingly, at the same time, we also observed that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* could increase grain quality in rice. In order to reveal the molecular mechanism of the above phenotypic functions, we analyzed the RNAs extracted from rice panicles of the wild type rice and *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 by deep sequencing. Small RNA analysis showed that *OsDCL3b* affected the biogenesis of both 21- and 24-nt small RNAs in rice panicles, which was consistent with the previously reported results (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)). Transcriptome in rice panicles was also studied to identify the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the wild-type and mutant lines, and two DEGs *Os03g07250* and *Os10g34360* are known to affect the development of pollen and anther, and consequently influence the rice grain yield (Li et al. [2010](#page-10-20); Wang et al. [2013](#page-11-11)). In addition, multiple splice variants were validated for lots of the protein-coding genes, indicating that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* might also influence the alternative splicing (AS) of precursor messenger RNA (pre-mRNA). These results herein should contribute to further understanding the biological functions of *OsDCL3b* and other plant *DCL* genes, which is useful for molecular breeding in crops.

## **Materials and methods**

#### **Plant materials and growth conditions**

Seeds of *Oryza sativa* L. ssp. *japonica cv*. Zhonghua 11 (ZH11) and *OsDCL3b*-RNAi lines were grown in paddy fields of Nanchang city. And young panicles (~ 5 cm) were collected and instantly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C for the following experiments including nextgeneration sequencing and PCR assays.

#### **Vector construction and plant transformation**

The cDNAs from rice young panicles were used as a template for amplifying the sense orientation RNAi fragment with primer pairs of OsDCL3bRNAi1 and OsDCL3bRNAi2 (Table S1). pYLRNAi5, a derivative of pRNAi-Ubi (Hu and Liu [2006\)](#page-10-21), containing a maize Ubiquitin promoter and one *NOS* terminator, was used for the *OsDCL3b* RNAi vector construction. The plasmids were transformed into *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* EHA105, and then transformed into rice callus as the previous described (Huang et al. [2016](#page-10-22)).

#### **Investigation of grain yield related traits**

Anthers from mature spikelet were crushed and stained in 1% I2-KI for several minutes (Shi et al. [2011\)](#page-11-12). And then those treated materials were observed and photographed under the bright-field illumination of the Olympus BX53 microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan, [http://www.olympus](http://www.olympus-global.com/en/)[global.com/en/](http://www.olympus-global.com/en/)). Percentage of darkly stained pollen grains from at least 25 plants of every transgenic line was counted for statistic analysis, and it was repeated three times. Three panicles from at least 20 individual plants from three parallel lines were selected at random.

Seed-setting rate (%) and grain yield per plant (g) were scored at maturity as previously described (Li et al. [2006](#page-10-23)).

#### **Next‑generation sequencing and data analyses**

RNAs were extracted from young panicles (~ 5 cm) of rice plants (wild type Zhonghua 11 and *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1), and shipped to the Majorbio (Shanghai, China) for small RNA sequencing. Clean reads were generated firstly through removing adaptor sequences and low quality reads. And then resultant reads were aligned to Nipponbare genome (TIGR release 7). Reads in the size ranging from 18 to 30 nt and perfectly matched to the reference genome, excluding those matching tRNAs, rRNAs, snRNAs and snoRNA, were retained for following analysis. Finally, the phasing analysis was performed as the previous study (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)).

For RNA sequencing, RNA-seq libraries were constructed with a TruSeq Stranded mRNA kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, California, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer's instruction. Qualities of RNA-seq libraries were assessed applying a Fragment Analyzer (Advanced Analytical), and libraries that passed quality control were sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 instrument with 150 nt pair-end reads. The edgeR package in R was used to identify genes with differential expression between *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line and wild type. Genes with False Discovery Rates (FDR) less than 0.05 based on edgeR and fold change of FPKM greater than two were characterized as DEGs. Then functional enrichment analysis for those identified DEGs was performed using GOstats, and hypergeometric test function with following *p*-value less than 0.05 as threshold was applied for Gene Ontology enrichment analysis.

Four major types of AS events including AA, AD, ES and IR were analyzed as previously described (Sammeth et al. [2008\)](#page-11-13). Only genes with AS patterns in all three biological replicates of S6-1 and/or WT panicles are considered as differentially spliced genes. Specific AS events referred to those only expressed in all three biological replicates of S6-1 or WT panicles. The integrative genomics viewer (IGV, version 2.3.94) free software available from [http://www.broad](http://www.broadinstitute.org/igv) [institute.org/igv](http://www.broadinstitute.org/igv) was explored to visualize the read coverage signal map of the differentially spliced genes in S6-1 and WT panicles (Thorvaldsdottir et al. [2013\)](#page-11-14).

#### **PCR assays**

Samples from different tissues were collected and ground in liquid nitrogen. Total RNAs were isolated using TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, California, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Then cDNA preparation, RT-PCR and qRT-PCR were carried out as described previously (Zhan et al. [2015](#page-11-15); Wang et al. [2017](#page-11-16)). And stem-loop qRT-PCR was carried out with the same samples used for RNA sequencing as the previously described method (Chen et al. [2005\)](#page-10-24). Details of primers used in this study are listed in Table S1.

## **Analysis of protein, amino acid, and lipid contents in rice grains**

The harvested and dried grains of S6-1 and WT were dehulled and milled for evaluating nutrient components of head rice. The rice protein, amino acid and fat contents were evaluated based on the dry weight of head rice. Nitrogen content of each sample was measured by the Kjeldahl method, and a nitrogen conversion factor of 5.95 was used to calculate the protein content of head rice. After the protein isolates of head rice were hydrolyzed with 6 M HCl for 24 h at 110 °C (Zhao et al. [2014\)](#page-11-17), the amino acid contents were measured by an automatic amino acid analyzer (L-8900, Hitachi, Japan). Rice fat content of each head rice sample was determined by the Soxhlet method as used previously (Hu et al. [2004](#page-10-25)). All measurements were performed in triplicates.

#### **Availability of data and materials**

The data sets supporting the results in this article are available in NCBI's GEO database repository, and the accessible GEO accession number is GSE102747.

## **Results**

# **Knockdown of** *OsDCL3b* **decreases rice grain yield**

In order to further explore the function of *OsDCL3b*, RNAi strategy was performed to decrease the expression level of *OsDCL3b* in transgenic rice plants. Finally, two independent homozygous rice mutant lines (S4-6 and S6-1) with significantly lower expression levels of *OsD-CL3b*, which have been grown for three generations were chosen for further study (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)a–c, g). In order to check the specificity of *OsDCL3b* knockdown, expressions of *OsDCL* members including *OsDCL1, OsDCL2a*/*b, OsD-CL3a, OsDCL3b* and *OsDCL4* were also investigated. And qRT-PCR analysis proved that transcriptional decrease was only found in *OsDCL3b* not in other members of *OsDCL* family in the two *OsDCL3b* RNAi transgenic lines (Fig. S1).

Interestingly, it was observed that the fertility of mature pollens in the two *OsDCL3b* RNAi lines was severely damaged by histochemical staining with 1% I2-KI solution compared to the WT. Most mature pollens of the WT were black and round in the I2-KI solution, while many pollens of the two *OsDCL3b* RNAi lines at the same development period were yellow and shriveled (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)d–f). Moreover, the pollen number per spikelet was also obviously reduced in the two *OsDCL3b*-RNAi lines compared to the WT (Fig. [1d](#page-3-0)–f).

The seed setting rate and grain yield per plant of rice were also considerably affected in the two *OsDCL3b* RNAi lines (Fig. [1h](#page-3-0)–i). The seed setting rate of the two *OsDCL3b*-RNAi lines S4-6 and S6-1 were largely decreased as about 70% and 64%, respective, whereas that of the WT was as high as about 92% (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)h). The grain yield per plant of the two *OsDCL3b* RNAi lines S4-6 and S6-1 were also extremely reduced to approximately 19.91 ± 0.46 g and 19.42±0.60 g, respective, while that of the WT was as high as 41.29±0.86 g (Fig. [1i](#page-3-0)).

These results showed that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* would lead to reduced pollen fertility and seed-setting rate, and consequently result in decreased grain yield in rice.

#### **Knockdown of** *OsDCL3b* **increases rice grain quality**

Rice grain is a major plant source of energy and protein for humans and animals worldwide (Zhao et al. [2014\)](#page-11-17). However, grain quality (e.g. grain protein) may be negatively associated with grain yield in crops (Pleijel et al. [1999](#page-10-26); Rharrabti et al. [2001;](#page-10-27) Triboi et al. [2006](#page-11-18)). To further explore the possible roles of *OsDCL3b* in grain quality and reveal the relationship between grain quality and grain yield, nutrient components of head rice including protein, amino acids and fat contents were analyzed. Compared to wild type, the total protein contents of head rice of *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 increased about 15.4% and 12.4% from two independent experiments in 2015 and 2016, respectively (Table [1](#page-4-0)). At the same time, fifteen major amino acids of head rice were measured. Interestingly, the contents of seven amino acids (Asp, Thr, Val, Ile, Leu, Phe and Lys) essential to the human body in the *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 were increased markedly than those in the WT (Table [1](#page-4-0)). However, there was no significant change in the fat contents of rice seeds between *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 and WT (Table [1](#page-4-0)). These results indicated that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* could increase protein and amino acid contents of head rice and consequently improve rice grain quality.

*OsDCL3b* **influences the amounts of phased small RNA and microRNA**

It is known that *OsDCL3b* is involved in the processing of 24-nucleotide (nt) phased small RNAs (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)). For determining the extent of *OsDCL3b* action on a genomewide scale, two small RNA libraries were constructed from panicles of the wild-type Zhonghua 11 and *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1. Then both libraries were used for deep sequencing on the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform, and over 18 million clean reads were generated (Table S2). Consistent with the previous report found in rice, 21- and 24-nt small RNAs were the most predominant sizes in wild-type (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)). And we found that mutated *OsDCL3b* caused obvious reductions on both 21- and 24-nucleotides small RNAs (Fig. [2](#page-5-0)), which is different with the previous observation that there is only a reduction of 24-nucleotide small RNA in *Arabidopsis thaliana dcl3* mutant (Kasschau et al. [2007](#page-10-28)). Moreover, effects of *OsDCL3b* on 21- and 24-nt phased small RNAs at the genome-wide level were explored. In the *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1, both the abundance and cluster number of 21- and 24-nt phased small RNAs were clearly reduced compared to the wild type (Table S3), indicating that biogenesis of 21- and 24-nt phased small RNAs are dependent on the activity of *OsDCL3b*.

# **Differentially expressed genes in young panicles of** *OsDCL3b***‑RNAi lines**

To unravel the transcriptional consequences for reproductive development difference caused by knockdown of *OsD-CL3b*, we analyzed genome-wide expression levels in young panicles from both *OsDCL3b*-RNAi transgenic line S6-1 and ZH11. In total, more than 300 million 125-nucleotide (nt) long paired-end reads were generated from WT and the line S6-1 with three independent biological replicates by the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform (Table S4). High mapping rates, ranging from 93.4 to 94.17%, were obtained when mapping reads to the reference genome *Oryza sativa* L. ssp. *japonica* cv. Nipponbare (International Rice Genome Sequencing, 2005). Then significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between two samples. 644 up- and 530 down-regulated genes were identified by comparing the line S6-1 to ZH11 (Table S5). Gene Ontology (GO) overrepresentation analysis of these DEGs showed that "cellular component organization", "cellular component organization or biogenesis" and "anatomical structure morphogenesis" were the most significant enrichments for down-regulated DEGs (Fig. [4a](#page-6-0), Table S6). However, the most significant GO function enrichments for up-regulated DEGs were "catalytic activity", "secondary metabolic process" and "single-organism metabolic process" (Fig. [4](#page-6-0)b, Table S6), potentially supporting changes found in seed quality of *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line.

After that we applied qRT-PCR to verify results of transcriptome analysis, and 2 up-regulated and 2 down-regulated genes were chosen for the following qRT-PCR test. [2005;](#page-10-29) Ye et al. [2015](#page-11-21); Zhang et al. [2010](#page-11-22)), which are worthy of further study to reveal their biological functions and molecular mechanisms. These results suggested that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* affects transcription of genes associated with reproductive development in rice and consequently influence the formation of rice grain yield.

## **Knockdown of** *OsDCL3b* **affects alternative splicing of rice panicle pre‑mRNA**

Alternative splicing (AS) of precursor messenger RNA (premRNA) can generate different transcript isoforms from a given gene (Lee and Rio [2015\)](#page-10-30), and these different transcript isoforms may play various important roles in normal growth and development as well as adversity adaptation in plants (Staiger and Brown [2013;](#page-11-23) Zhan et al. [2015](#page-11-15)). To determine the impacts of *OsDLC3b* knockdown on pre-mRNA splicing, we analyzed our RNA-seq data sets to identify the AS events in both *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 and ZH11. As it has been reported that alternative acceptor (AA), alternative donor (AD), exon skipping (ES) and intron retention (IR) account for the vast majority of AS events (Zhang and Gassmann [2007](#page-11-24)), herein these four major types of AS events were investigated on our samples (Fig. [5](#page-8-0)a, Table S7). In total, 631 specific AS events from 550 genes were observed only in the S6-1 but not in the WT, including 239 AA events, 229 AD events, 53 ES events and 110 IR events (Fig. [5](#page-8-0)a–b, Table S7). In addition, 509 specific AS events from 273 genes were observed only in the WT but not in the S6-1, including 143 AA events, 86 AD events, 21 ES events and 59 IR events (Fig. [5a](#page-8-0)–b, Table S7). Out of these differentially spliced genes (Fig. [5](#page-8-0)b), seven identical spliced genes (*Os02g57180, Os03g03610, Os03g14040, Os03g16369, Os03g52840, Os06g01440* and *Os07g22390*) were found for both the S6-1 and WT, however, the AS events of these seven spliced genes happened in the S6-1 and WT were completely different from the AS type or splicing location (Table S7). For example, for *Os02g57180*, the AS event happened in the S6-1 was ES, while the AS event happened in the WT was AA (Table S7). These results indicated that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* should affect the AS events in the S6-1 line compared to those in the WT.

Then we validated the IR results identified from our RNA-seq data sets at first through designing primers unique to the intron of genes that are retained in the S6-1 plants. Four independent IR events occurred in four genes including *Os04g41960, Os05g30430, Os03g14040* and *Os02g11940* (Fig. [5](#page-8-0)c) were selected for semi-quantitative RT-PCR analysis. *Os04g41960* encodes NADP-dependent oxidoreductase; *Os05g30430* and *Os03g14040* are expressed proteins without annotated domains or function annotations at the present; *Os02g11940* encodes sterol glucosyltransferase. Semi-quantitative RT-PCR analysis showed that each of *Os04g41960, Os05g30430, Os03g14040* and *Os02g11940* has a transcript containing an intron in the S6-1 plants but not in the WT plants (Fig. [5](#page-8-0)d, Fig. S3). For example, for *Os04g41960*, a 340-bp band comprising intron retention (IR) sequence was observed in the S6-1 but not detected in the WT (Fig. [5](#page-8-0)d). Moreover, semi-quantitative RT-PCR analysis also proved an exon skipping event that the tenth exon of *Os01g04190* encoding a transporter family protein was skipped in the S6-1 plants compared to that in the WT plants (Fig. S4, Table S7), which was in agreement with the result that an 181 bp band was only detected in the WT but not observed in the S6-1 (Fig. [5](#page-8-0)d, Fig. S3). Thus the semiquantitative RT-PCR results verified our RNA-seq data sets of both IR and ES events in the S6-1 plants, and the involvement of *OsDCL3b* in affecting AS events in rice panicles was further confirmed.

# **Discussion**

More than four DCL family genes are usually observed in land plant species (Liu et al. [2005](#page-10-6); Margis et al. [2006](#page-10-5); Kapoor et al. [2008\)](#page-10-7), and each of these DCL family genes can have specific or multiple functions in the biogenesis of various types of small RNAs (Lee et al. [2004;](#page-10-8) Parent et al. [2015](#page-10-9); Wu et al. [2017](#page-11-2)). As for *OsDCL3b*, it has been reported to process the 24-nucleotide phased small RNAs in rice panicles (Song et al. [2012\)](#page-11-8). In the present study, we found that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* could affect the biogenesis of both 21- and 24-nucleotide small RNAs in rice young panicles (Fig. [2](#page-5-0)), including the 21- and 24-nucleotide phased small RNAs (Table S3) as well as miRNAs (Fig. [3\)](#page-5-1). This indicates that *OsDCL3b* has multiple functions in the biogenesis of small non-coding RNAs.

Through controlling the biogenesis of non-coding RNAs, DCL family genes show various phenotype functions in growth and development as well as adversity adaptation in land plants (Xie et al. [2004](#page-11-3); Liu et al. [2005,](#page-10-6) [2007;](#page-10-16) Urayama et al. [2010](#page-11-7); Wei et al. [2014](#page-11-10)). For example, knockdown of *OsDCL1* caused pleiotropic phenotypes including severe dwarfism and dark green color of rice seedling shoot and root due to abnormal metabolism of miRNAs (Liu et al. [2005](#page-10-6)). Both knockdown and knockout of *OsDCL4* resulted in vegetative and reproductive development defects like radialized coleoptiles and sterile spikelet in rice due to a disorder of 21-nucleotide siRNA biogenesis (Liu et al. [2007\)](#page-10-16). In

<span id="page-8-0"></span>**Fig. 5** *OsDCL3b* affects splicing of gene transcripts. **a** Numbers of events for each AS type differentially happened in S6-1 or WT. **b** Numbers of differentially spliced genes for S6-1 or WT. **c** Visualization with the integrative genomic viewer (IGV) of intron-retention (IR) events in S6-1 and WT plants. Annotated gene structures are

shown (bottom), with thick lines representing exons and thin lines representing introns. Wiggle plots represent the normalized read coverage on an auto scale. **d** Validation of specific intron-retention (IR) and exon-skipping (ES) events in S6-1 plants by semi quantitative RT-PCR

the present study, we found that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* decreased pollen fertility, seed setting rate and rice grain yield (Fig. [1\)](#page-3-0), which might be due to the abnormal biogenesis of 21- and 24-nucleotide sRNAs (Table S3, Figs. [2](#page-5-0), [3\)](#page-5-1). In addition, we also found that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* could increase the total contents of seed storage protein and amino acid (Table [1\)](#page-4-0), which might be partly explained by the upregulated DEGs with catalytic activity, secondary metabolic process or single-organism metabolic process functions (Fig. [4,](#page-6-0) Table S6). Thus, our results show that *OsDCL3b* is essential for rice reproductive development including pollen development and the formation of seed-setting rate, grain yield and grain quality in rice, which is consistent with the previous speculation that the *OsDCL3b*-dependent phased small RNAs may act at specific stages during rice reproductive development (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)).

Several kinds of small non-coding RNAs have been proved to be expressed and function specifically in the reproductive organs of plants. For example, in *Arabidopsis*, some retrotransposon-derived 21-nucleotide siRNAs have been found to regulate transposon activity in male gametes (Slotkin et al. [2009\)](#page-11-25), and another kind of sperm-specific natural *cis*-antisense siRNAs have also been observed to play important roles in double fertilization (Ron et al. [2010](#page-10-31)). In rice, it has been reported that the 21- and 24-nucleotide phased small RNAs are preferentially expressed in panicles (Johnson et al. [2009](#page-10-17); Song et al. [2012\)](#page-11-8). Consistent with the previous reports, we also found that the 21- and 24-nucleotide small RNAs were the most predominant sizes in the rice panicles of both *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 and WT (Fig. [2](#page-5-0)). Moreover, obvious reductions on both 21- and 24-nucleotide small RNAs including miRNAs and phased small RNAs were found in the panicles of *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 (Figs. [2,](#page-5-0) [3,](#page-5-1) Table S3). Interestingly, one down-regulated miRNA in the panicles of *OsDCL3b*-RNAi lines S6-1 and S4-6 (Fig. [3](#page-5-1)), OsmiR397 has been known that its overexpression can increase the grain size and weight and ultimately improve the grain yield of rice (Zhang et al. [2013](#page-11-20)), suggesting that the 21- and 24-nucleotide small RNAs do preferentially present and play roles in rice panicle development (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)). However, further studies are needed to confirm functions of other putative differentially expressed small RNAs that may act directly in the pathway controlling the abnormal panicle development of the *OsD-CL3b* mutant lines (Fig. [1\)](#page-3-0).

Based on RNA-seq data sets, we systematically analyzed DEGs in young panicles between *OsDCL3b*-RNAi transgenic line S6-1 and ZH11, a total of 644 up- and 530 down-regulated protein genes were identified (Table S5). Interestingly, two down-regulated DEGs verified by qRT-PCR (Fig. [4](#page-6-0)c), *Os03g07250* and *Os10g34360* that encode Cytochrome P450 CYP704B2 protein and stilbene synthase, respectively, have been shown to play important roles in rice pollen and anther development (Li et al. [2010;](#page-10-20) Wang et al. [2013](#page-11-11)), indicating that down-regulation of *OsDCL3b* affects transcription of genes associated with panicle development. Usually, Dicer and DCL affect the expressions of other protein genes by controlling the biogenesis of various non-coding RNAs including miR-NAs and phased small RNAs (Song et al. [2012;](#page-11-8) Zhang et al. [2013](#page-11-20)). However, the corresponding small RNAs that directly target *Os03g07250* and *Os10g34360* have not been found from the differentially expressed small RNAs in the panicles between *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 and WT (Table S2, S3). One possible reason would be that some phased small RNAs could function in a non-cleavagebased manner (Song et al. [2012](#page-11-8)). Another explanation is that targets of *OsDCL3b* rather than itself indirectly regulate expressions of both *Os03g07250* and *Os10g34360*.

miRNA has been found to mediate DNA methylation in rice (Wu et al. [2010](#page-11-9)), and DNA methylation is associated with alternative splicing (Wang et al. [2016\)](#page-11-26), thus we analyzed our RNA-seq data sets to identify the specific AS events in the *OsDCL3b*-RNAi line S6-1 and ZH11. A total of 550 and 273 differentially spliced genes with different AS events were observed in line S6-1 and WT, respectively (Fig. [5b](#page-8-0), Table S7), and some IR and ES events differentially observed in S6-1but not in WT were verified by semi-quantitative RT-PCR (Fig. [5c](#page-8-0)–d, Fig. S3). From these results, we confirm that *OsDCL3b* should have been involved in regulating the AS events in line S6-1. This is possible for the reason that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* could affect the biogenesis of small non-coding RNAs including miRNAs and phased small RNAs (Figs. [2,](#page-5-0) [3,](#page-5-1) Table S3), which could consequently regulate the specific AS events in the line S6-1 (Kalsotra et al. [2010\)](#page-10-32). Moreover, we also think that the AS events differentially happened in S6-1 or WT might be involved in regulating the reproductive development of the *OsDCL3b*-RNAi lines (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)) since AS is known to play important roles in plant growth, development and adversity adaptation (Staiger and Brown [2013](#page-11-23); Zhan et al. [2015](#page-11-15)). However, further studies are needed to reveal the complex pathways mediated by knockdown of *OsDCL3b* for regulating the abnormal reproductive development of the *OsDCL3b*-RNAi lines (Fig. [1](#page-3-0)).

To conclude, knockdown of *OsDCL3b* mainly affects the biogenesis of both 21- and 24-nucleotide small RNAs including miRNAs and phased small RNAs (Song et al. [2012\)](#page-11-8), which regulates both the RNA expression levels of protein genes (Zhang et al. [2013\)](#page-11-20) and alternative splicing of pre-mRNAs (Kalsotra et al. [2010\)](#page-10-32), leading to regulate the reproductive development of rice. To our knowledge, this is the first report on the multiple phenotypic functions of *OsDCL3b* that knockdown of *OsDCL3b* will cause decreased pollen fertility, seed setting rate and grain yield but increased grain quality in rice.
