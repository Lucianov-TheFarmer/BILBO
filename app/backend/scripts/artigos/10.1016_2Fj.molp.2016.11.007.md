## 24 ABSTRACT

25

26 Light is a crucial environmental signal that promotes photomorphogenesis, the developmental process containing a series of light-dependent alterations for plants to 27 adapt various external challenges. Chromatin modification has been proposed to be 28 involved in such light-mediated growth, but the underlying mechanism is still elusive. 29 Here, we identify that the four Arabidopsis thaliana Nuclear Factor-YC homologues 30 31 NF-YC1, NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and NF-YC9 (NF-YCs) function redundantly as the repressors of light control of hypocotyl elongation via histone de-acetylation. Obvious 32 etiolation phenotypes are observed in NF-YCs loss-of-function mutant seedlings 33 grown under light conditions, including significant elongated hypocotyls, less opened 34 cotyledons. Our results reveal that, in the light, NF-YCs interact with HDA15, a 35 histone deacetylase, to co-target to the hypocotyl elongation-related genes' promoters 36 and modulate histone H4 acetylation levels in these associated chromatins, thus 37 repressing gene expression. Contrastingly, NF-YC-HDA15 complex is dismissed 38 39 from the target genes in the dark, resulting in H4 acetylation increase and consequent etiolated growth. Further analyses verify that transcriptional repression activity of 40 NF-YCs on the light control of hypocotyl elongation partially depends on 41 de-acetylation function of HDA15, and loss of HDA15 function rescues the short 42 hypocotyl phenotype of NF-YCs overexpression plants. Taken together, our findings 43 shed new lights on the roles of NF-YCs as transcriptional co-repressors with HDA15 44 to inhibit hypocotyl elongation in photomorphogenesis during early seedling stage, 45 supporting that NF-YCs regulate plant development in response to environmental 46 47 cues via an epigenetic regulation.

## 48 INTRODUCTION

49

50 Light is one of the most important environmental factors for the autotrophic plants, which triggers a series of photomorphogenic growth during various developmental 51 processes. At the post-germination stage, compared to the dark-grown seedlings with 52 elongated hypocotyls, apical hooks, unexpanded cotyledons, and undifferentiated 53 chloroplasts, the light-grown seedlings undergoing photomorphogenesis are 54 55 characterized by short hypocotyls, open and expanded cotyledons, and green chloroplasts (de Wit et al., 2016). In addition, under shade conditions, neighbor plants 56 usually adjust their architectures for light perception by accelerating elongation of 57 internodes and petioles, elevating leaf angles from the horizontal, and reducing shoot 58 59 branching, which are collectively referred to as the shade avoidance syndrome (Casal, 2012). These photomorphogenic growth changes confer plants more flexibility on 60 survival when facing various light conditions. 61

Light signals need to be perceived and transducted to trigger photomorphogenic 62 63 growth of plants. It has been revealed that plants precisely sense light intensity, duration, wavelength, and direction by several kinds of photoreceptors (Kami et al., 64 2010). Phytochromes are well-known as the red/far-red (R/FR) photoreceptors. 65 Phytochrome A (phyA), a light labile phytochrome, mediates the far-red 66 light-dependent response, whereas the light stable phytochrome phyB is involved in 67 the red light-dependent response (Chen and Chory, 2011). Red light triggers the 68 conversion of inactive Pr form of phyB to an active Pfr form which then translocates 69 to nucleus and interacts with PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTORs (PIFs), a 70 71 group of negative regulators in photomorphogenesis. The interaction between phyB and PIFs results in phosphorylation of PIFs and their subsequent degradation by the 72 26S proteasome, thereby promoting photomorphogenesis (Leivar and Quail, 2011). 73 The active Pfr form of phyB induced by red light can be reverted by far-red light. 74 Far-red light also triggers the translocation of phyA into the nucleus by interacting 75 76 with FAR-RED ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 1 (FHY1) and FHY1-LIKE (FHL), and promotes photomorphogenesis as active Pfr-phyB does (Wang and Wang, 2015). 77

On the other hand, accumulation of CONSTITUTIVE PHOTOMORPHOGENIC 1 78 (COP1) proteins in the nucleus, which is inhibited by light, mediates the degradation 79 of ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 5 (HY5), a pivotal positive regulator of 80 photomorphogenesis (Sheerin et al., 2015). The light-dependent PIFs degradation and 81 HY5 accumulation contribute to the repression of cell elongation-related genes, such 82 as auxin responsive genes INDOLE-3-ACETIC ACID INDUCIBLE PROTEINs (IAAs), 83 gibberellin responsive gene PACLOBUTRAZOL RESISTANCE 1 (PRE1), and cell 84 85 wall loosening enzymes XYLOGLUCAN ENDOTRANSGLUCOSYLSE/HYDROLASEs (XTHs), thus inhibiting hypocotyl growth (Bai et al., 2012; Lee et al., 2006; Okello et 86 al., 2016; Vandenbussche et al., 2005). Similar to phytochromes, the blue light 87 receptors CRYPTOCHROME 1 (CRY1) and CRY2 also regulate photomorphogenic 88 89 development by suppressing COP1-mediated degradation of HY5 and activity of PIFs (Liu et al., 2011; Pedmale et al., 2016). 90

Eukaryotic DNA is packaged to form chromatin with nuclear histone proteins of 91 which modifications, such as histone acetylation and histone methylation, are crucial 92 93 for eukaryotic transcriptional regulation (Luo et al., 2012a). Histone acetylation is essential for light-dependent gene activation through the relaxation of chromatin by 94 histone acetyltransferases (HATs) (Fisher and Franklin, 2011). For example, 95 loss-of-function mutant of GENERAL CONTROL NON-REPRESSIBLE 5 (GCN5), a 96 97 HAT in Arabidopsis, produced a long hypocotyl phenotype under light conditions due to reduced H3 and H4 acetylation (H3ac and H4ac) on target loci and the activation of 98 early light-responsive genes (Benhamed et al., 2006; Benhamed et al., 2008). On the 99 other hand, chromatin histone acetylation can be removed by histone deacetylases 100 (HDACs), resulting in chromatin condensation and repression of gene expression. 101 Based on sequence similarity and cofactor dependency, HDACs are grouped into 102 three families: REDUCED POTASSIUM DEPENDENCE 3/HISTONE 103 DEACETYLASE 1 (RPD3/HDA1), SILENT INFORMATION REGULATOR 2 104 (SIR2), and HISTONE DEACETYLASE 2 (HD2)-related protein families (Ma et al., 105 106 2013). Among 18 HDACs indentified in Arabidopsis, 12 of them belong to the RPD3/HDA1 superfamily. RPD3/HDA1 members have been revealed to play vital 107

roles in different biological processes, including seed development and dormancy 108 (HDA6, HDA7, and HDA19) (Cigliano et al., 2013; Tanaka et al., 2008; Zhou et al., 109 2013), leaf development (HDA6) (Luo et al., 2012c), flowering-time control (HDA5, 110 HDA6, HDA9, and HDA19) (Kang et al., 2015; Krogan et al., 2012; Luo et al., 2015; 111 Yu et al., 2011), abiotic stress response (HDA6 and HDA19) (Chen and Wu, 2010; 112 Luo et al., 2012b), and light signaling response (HDA6, HDA15, and HDA19) 113 (Benhamed et al., 2006; Liu et al., 2013; Tessadori et al., 2009). A number of 114 evidences showed that HDACs function as transcriptional co-repressors with other 115 regulators (Wang et al., 2014). A previous study showed that HDA15 is recruited by 116 PIF3 to co-repress the expression of light responsive genes by reducing H4ac levels 117 and, thus, inhibiting chlorophyll biosynthesis and photosynthesis in the dark (Liu et 118 al., 2013). Since PIF3 does not contribute to HDA15-mediated photomorphogenic 119 hypocotyl growth under light conditions (Liu et al., 2013), it remains to investigate 120 how HDA15 functions in photomorphogenesis with other unidentified cofactors. 121

NUCLEAR FACTOR-Y C proteins (NF-YCs), of which core regions are closely 122 related to histone H2A, functionally act as one subunit of the NF-Y heterotrimeric 123 transcriptional factor (TF) that specifically recognizes and helps to establish 124 permissive chromatin modifications at the CCAAT-box containing promoters in 125 eukaryotes (Nardini et al., 2013; Petroni et al., 2012). In plants, NF-YCs function as 126 important regulators in various developmental and stress-related processes, including 127 flowering-time control (NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and NF-YC9) (Hou et al., 2014; 128 Kumimoto et al., 2010), seed germination (NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and NF-YC9) (Liu et 129 al., 2016), abiotic stress response (NF-YAs and NF-YC2) (Hackenberg et al., 2012; 130 Levva-Gonzalez et al., 2012), and endoplasmic reticulum stress (NF-YC2) (Liu and 131 Howell, 2010). The diverse roles of NF-YCs, together with those of the other two 132 NF-Y subunits NF-YA and NF-YB, imply the widely flexible formation of NF-Y 133 complexes spatially and temporally regulated by diverse developmental and growth 134 conditions. It was reported that the NF-YC closet homologues NF-YC1, NF-YC3, 135 *NF-YC4*, and *NF-YC9* were expressed in the light- and dark-grown young *Arabidopsis* 136 seedlings (Siefers et al., 2009). A most recent study demonstrated that NF-YC3, 137

NF-YC4, and NF-YC9 function as positive regulators of photomorphogenesis in *Arabidopsis* probably via a HY5-independent light signaling pathway (Myers et al.,
2016).

In this study, we reveal that the NF-YC homologues NF-YC1, NF-YC3, NF-YC4, 141 and NF-YC9 regulate photomorphogenic growth via histone modifications. NF-YCs 142 physically interact with the histone deacetylase HDA15 to repress hypocotyl 143 elongation-related genes expression in a light-dependent manner through modulating 144 histone H4ac levels. In the dark, the DNA affinity of NF-YC-HDA15 complex to the 145 target gene loci is reduced or abolished, resulting in H4ac increase and consequent 146 etiolated growth. Thus, our results establish that NF-YCs and HDA15 function as a 147 transcriptional co-repressor of light control of hypocotyl elongation via histone 148 149 de-acetylation during early seedling stage.

## 152 NF-YC homologues play positive roles in photomorphogenic growth

In Arabidopsis, NF-YC belongs to a multi-gene family containing thirteen members. 153 NF-YC1, NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and NF-YC9 are the closest NF-YC homologues in 154 NF-YC family (Petroni et al., 2012). To investigate whether NF-YC function in 155 photomorphogenesis, we analyzed the light-mediated growth phenotype of *nf-yc1-1*, 156 157 nf-yc3-2, nf-yc4-1, and nf-yc9-1 single and combinatorial T-DNA insertion mutants. There was little difference in hypocotyl elongation between all single mutants, various 158 double mutants of nf-vc3/4/9, and Columbia wild-type (Col) seedlings under the 159 diverse light or dark conditions (Figure S1A and S1B; data not shown). Strikingly, 160 nf-yc3-2 nf-yc4-1 nf-yc9-1 triple mutant (nf-ycT) and nf-yc1-1 nf-yc3-2 nf-yc4-1 161 nf-yc9-1 quadruple mutant (nf-ycO) seedlings exhibited longer hypocotyls than Col 162 under white, red, far-red, or blue light conditions, while they showed no differences in 163 the dark (Figure 1A and 1B; Figure S1A and S1B). Considering that *nf-ycQ* had a 164 longer hypocotyl than *nf-vcT*, which indicates a redundant function of *NF-YC1* with 165 other NF-YCs in light control of growth (Figure S1A and S1B), we hence employed 166 nf-ycQ mutant to investigate NF-YC function in our subsequent experiments. To 167 further verify the possible role of NF-YC in photomorphogenesis, we created several 168 individual NF-YC3 and NF-YC9 overexpressing lines to examine the hypocotyl 169 elongation phenotype. As expected, 35S:NF-YC3-6HA (NF-YC3OE) and 170 35S:NF-YC9-6HA (NF-YC9OE) exhibited shorter hypocotyls than the wild-type in the 171 light, whereas they showed similar phenotype in the dark (Figure 1A and 1B). 172 Unexpectedly, NF-YC3OE showed no statistical difference with the wild-type in blue 173 light. We then examined the NF-YC3/9 protein levels in NF-YC3 and NF-YC9 174 overexpression lines under red and blue light and found that NF-YC3 and NF-YC9 175 similarly expressed in different transgenic lines of NF-YC3OE and NF-YC9OE, 176 respectively, suggesting that NF-YC3 might exert a weaker role in blue 177 light-dependent suppression of hypocotyl than that in red and far-red light (Figure S2). 178 Interestingly, *nf-ycQ* seedlings grown in low frequency of red light also exhibited 179

decreased cotyledons angles, together with elongated hypocotyls, mimicking an etiolated phenotype in the dark (Figure S3A). Moreover, adult nf-ycQ plants grown under standard white light conditions showed the classic shade avoidance features including significantly elongated petioles and elevated leaf angles from horizontal compared with the normal phenotype of wild-type (Figure S3B-S3D). These observations indicate the overlapping function of *NF*-*YC1*, *NF*-*YC3*, *NF*-*YC4*, and *NF*-*YC9* in photomorphogenic growth.

187 To further confirm the role of NF-YCs in photomorphogenesis, the expression of several hypocotyl elongation-related genes was examined by quantitative real-time 188 PCR (qRT-PCR). Consistent with the seedlings phenotype, auxin responsive genes 189 IAA6 and IAA19, GA responsive gene PRE1, and the cell-wall loosening enzyme gene 190 XTH17, were significantly up-regulated in *nf-ycQ* and down-regulated in *NF-YC3OE* 191 and *NF-YC9OE* in the red light, respectively (Figure 1C). Though *nf-ycO* seedlings 192 only exhibited different hypocotyl length with the wild-type in the light, all of 193 NF-YC1, NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and NF-YC9 expressed in both red light- and dark-grown 194 Arabidopsis seedlings (Figure S4A and S4B). These results support that NF-YC 195 homologues play positive roles in photomorphogenesis through functioning as the 196 repressors in light control of hypocotyl elongation. 197

## 199 NF-YCs interact with HDA15

It has been reported that *Arabidopsis* NF-YCs regulate flowering via direct protein interaction with cofactors (Kumimoto et al., 2010; Hou et al., 2014). To test whether NF-YCs function with other partners in light control of hypocotyl growth during photomorphogenic development, we performed yeast two-hybrid screening and found that NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and NF-YC9 strongly interacted, and NF-YC1 weakly interacted with HDA15, an *Arabidopsis* histone deacetylase, in yeast (Figure 2A).

Next, we performed pull-down assays to test the in vitro interaction between NF-YCs and HDA15. Purified His-NF-YC recombinant proteins were incubated with glutathione S-transferase (GST)-HDA15 fusion protein and GST protein, respectively.

209 The results showed that His-NF-YC1, His-NF-YC3, His-NF-YC4, and His-NF-YC9

were co-precipitated by GST-HDA15, but not by GST, demonstrating the physical interaction between NF-YCs and HDA15 (Figure 2B). When crossing pNF-YC9:NF-YC9-FLAG (*NF-YC9-FLAG*) into *nf-ycT* mutant, we found that *NF-YC9-FLAG* was sufficient to rescue the elongated hypocotyl phenotype of *nf-ycT* in the light (Figure S5), suggesting the significant biological function of NF-YC9 in photomorphogenic growth. Hence, we hereafter used NF-YC9 as the representative of the four NF-YC homologues for further investigation.

217 Bimolecular fluorescence complementation (BiFC) assay was next performed in tobacco leaf epidermal cells to examine the interaction between NF-YC9 and HDA15 218 in vivo. The results showed that co-expression of EYFP<sup>N</sup>-HDA15 and 219 NF-YC9-EYFP<sup>C</sup> reconstituted a functional YFP in the nucleus, but the control did not 220 (Figure 2C). Notably, communoprecipitation of protein extracts from nf-yc9-1 221 NF-YC9-FLAG 35S:HDA15-GFP seedlings further confirmed that the interaction 222 between NF-YC9 and HDA15 in plants occurred under light but not dark conditions 223 (Figure 2D). Taken together, these results support that NF-YCs interact with HDA15 224 both in vitro and in vivo. 225

Previous study reported that the loss-of-function mutant hda15 has relative longer 226 hypocotyl than the wild-type (Liu et al., 2013). We here confirmed the role of HDA15 227 in light control of hypocotyl elongation. *hda15* showed an elongated hypocotyl 228 phenotype, while 35S:HDA15-GFP had shorter hypocotyls than the wild-type, under 229 white, red, far-red, and blue light conditions (Figure 3A and 3B). By contrast, both 230 hda15 and 35S:HDA15-GFP exhibited no difference with the wild-type seedlings in 231 the dark (Figure 3A and 3B). These observations imply the possibility that NF-YCs 232 might exert function in repression of hypocotyl elongation in the light together with 233 HDA15. Thus, it prompted us to further investigate the biological relationship of 234 NF-YCs and HDA15. 235

# NF-YCs and HDA15 synergistically regulate hypocotyl elongation in the light via directly binding to downstream promoters

239 To determine whether the interaction between NF-YCs and HDA15 regulates

photomorphogenic growth, we first examined the mRNA levels of hypocotyl 240 241 elongation-related genes under different HDA15 genetic backgrounds. Similar to that in *nf-ycQ* and overexpression lines of *NF-YCs*, the expression of *IAA6*, *IAA19*, *PRE1*, 242 and XTH17 was significantly up-regulated in hda15 and down-regulated in 243 35S:HDA15-GFP under red light conditions (Figure 1C and Figure 3C). By contrast, 244 these genes expression showed no difference in *hda15* and *nf-ycQ* seedlings grown in 245 the dark (Figure S6), consistent with the observations on hypocotyl phenotype, 246 247 indicating that NF-YCs and HDA15 co-suppress hypocotyl elongation-related genes expression in a light-dependent manner. 248

Given that NF-Y and HDA15 have been respectively shown to mediate gene 249 expression via association with DNA in plants (Cao et al., 2014; Liu et al., 2013), to 250 learn how NF-YC and HDA15 co-regulate the expression of genes involved in light 251 responsive growth, we performed chromatin immunoprecipitation assay (ChIP) to 252 investigate the binding enrichment of these proteins in IAA19 and XTH17 genes, the 253 common downstreams of NF-YC9 and HDA15. The results showed that NF-YC9 254 strongly bound to the regions at P1 and P2 in IAA19 promoter and P2 and P3 in 255 XTH17 promoter in the light-grown seedlings. Similarly, enrichment of HDA15 to the 256 promoter regions of IAA19 and XTH17 was also detected in 35S:HDA15-GFP 257 seedlings grown in the light (Figure 4A-4C). The chromatin affinity of NF-YC and 258 HDA15 to the target gene loci was dramatically reduced or abolished in the 259 dark-grown seedlings when compared with those in the light (Figure 4B and 4C). 260 These results raise the possibility that NF-YC9 and HDA15 may co-repress hypocotyl 261 elongation-related genes expression in the light via direct binding to promoters of 262 263 these genes.

To further examine the repressive activity of NF-YCs and HDA15 on target genes expression, we performed transient expression assays using an ~2 kb fragment of *IAA19* promoter fused to the  $\beta$ -glucuronidase (GUS) gene as the reporter. The effector constructs of 35S:EYFP<sup>N</sup>-HDA15 and 35S:NF-YC9-EYFP<sup>C</sup> were transformed individually or together with the reporter into Arabidopsis mesophyll protoplasts (Figure 4D). After a 2 h of culture in darkness to activate the reporter gene expression,

the protoplasts were then transferred to culture in the red light overnight. The results showed that either EYFP<sup>N</sup>-HDA15 or NF-YC9-EYFP<sup>C</sup> alone presented significant repressive effect on the *IAA19* gene expression. Remarkably, a more dramatically decrease of GUS activity was observed when co-expressing HDA15 and NF-YC9 (Figure 4E), demonstrating NF-YC9 and HDA15 synergistically repress target genes expression.

Because both NF-YC and HDA15 contribute to the hypocotyl elongation repression 276 277 in light-grown seedlings (Figure 1 and Figure 3), to investigate the genetic relationship between HDA15 and NF-YC, we generated several combinatorial lines 278 with different HDA15 and NF-YC genetic background. The double mutant hda15 279 *nf-ycQ* displayed an extremely elongated hypocotyl phenotype under light conditions 280 compared with *hda15*, *nf-ycQ*, and the wild-type seedlings (Figure 5A and 5B; Figure 281 S7). Consistent with this, expression of the hypocotyl elongation-related genes was 282 substantially increased (Figure 5C), confirming the synergistical regulation of HDA15 283 and NF-YC in inhibition of hypocotyl elongation. Furthermore, the shortened 284 hypocotyl phenotype of NF-YC3OE and NF-YC9OE was significantly rescued by 285 loss-of-function of HDA15 under red, far-red, and blue light conditions (Figure 5A 286 and 5B; Figure S7). Expression of the hypocotyl elongation-related genes in hda15 287 NF-YC3OE or hda15 NF-YC9OE was also obviously higher than that in NF-YC 288 289 overexpression lines (Figure 5C), indicating that the repressive role of NF-YC on hypocotyl elongation in the light partially depends on HDA15 function. 290

## 292 NF-YCs and HDA15 mediate light control of hypocotyl elongation by 293 co-regulating a set of downstream genes

To clarify the regulatory expression profile by NF-YC in light signaling pathway, we performed transcriptomic analyses of Col wild-type and nf-ycQ seedlings grown under red light conditions. Upon the criteria of 1.5-fold cutoff with statistically significant *p*-value < 0.05, 1000 genes were up-regulated and 1067 genes were down-regulated in *nf*-ycQ mutants, respectively, compared with the wild-type (Tables S2 and S3). The genes up-regulated and down-regulated in *nf*-ycQ mutants are

involved in a variety of biological processes, including the following GO categories:
response to biotic, abiotic and endogenous stimuli, reproduction, embryonic and
post-embryonic development, cell death, and some metabolic processes, such as
photosynthesis, carbohydrate, lipid, and secondary metabolisms (Figure S8).

In combination with the RNA-seq data of hda15 mutant, we found that NF-YCs 304 and HDA15 co-repress 116 genes and co-activated 130 genes in de-etiolated seedlings, 305 respectively (Figure 6A; Tables S4 and S5). Bioinformatic analysis using DAVID 306 307 (Huang et al., 2009) with high stringency revealed that the genes co-repressed by NF-YC and HDA15 are involved in glycosinolate biosynthetic process, seed storage 308 protein, leucine biosynthetic process, heme, cell wall, thioredoxin fold, water 309 transporter activity, flavonoid biosynthetic process, and oxygen binding (Figure 6B). 310 311 Notably, the hypocotyl elongation-related genes, such as IAA6, IAA19, XTH17, and *PRE1*, which have been identified as common downstreams of NF-YCs and HDA15 312 in preceding expression analyses, were also confirmed to up-regulated in both hda15 313 and *nf-ycQ* mutants in this transcriptome data. Collectively, these findings suggest 314 that NF-YCs and HDA15 mediate light control of hypocotyl elongation by 315 co-regulating a set of downstream genes. 316

## 318 NF-YCs modulate histone acetylation via HDA15

HDA15 is identified as a histone deacetylase in Arabidopsis, which functions together 319 with other TFs to repress transcription through removing acetyl groups from lysine 320 residues of histone tail (Liu et al., 2014). We thus examined whether interaction 321 between NF-YCs and HDA15 are involved in histone acetylation modification in their 322 323 direct targets. ChIP assays were performed to evaluate the distribution of H4ac, a favored substrate of HDA15 in Arabidopsis (Liu et al., 2013). The H4ac levels in 324 IAA19 and XTH17 chromatins were increased in red light-grown hda15 seedlings 325 compared with those in the wild-type (Figure 7A). Strikingly, high deposition of H4ac 326 in these target loci was similarly observed in *nf-ycQ* and *hda15 nf-ycQ* under red light 327 conditions. By contrast, deposition of H4ac in dark-grown hda15 and nf-ycQ 328 seedlings was significantly reduced or even abolished compared with those grown in 329 12

red light (Figure 7A and 7B). Consistent with the increasing expression of NF-YC and 330 331 HDA15 target genes and the elongated hypocotyl phenotype in these mutants under light conditions (Figure 5), the results suggest the possibility that NF-YC and HDA15 332 play repressive roles on gene transcription probably via mediating H4ac modification. 333 To verify this hypothesis, we measured the H4ac levels in NF-YC overexpression 334 transgenic seedlings. In contrast to the intensive deposition of H4ac in nf-vcO, 335 overexpression of NF-YC9 dramatically attenuates H4ac levels of IAA19 and XTH17 336 337 chromatin in the light (Figure 7A and 7C). Interestingly, loss-of-function of HDA15 completely restored the reduced H4ac levels in NF-YC9OE seedlings (Figure 7C), 338 indicating the effect of NF-YCs on H4 deacetylation depends on HDA15 function. 339 Together with the expression and genetic analyses (Figure 5; Figure S7), these results 340 strongly support the conclusion that NF-YCs and HDA15 co-repress light control of 341 hypocotyl elongation by mediating histone acetylation on target loci in the light. 342 343

## 344 **DISCUSSION**

345

NF-YC serves as one of subunits of NF-Y transcriptional factor complex which 346 composes of three subunits: NF-YA, NF-YB, and NF-YC, and plays diverse roles 347 together with transcriptional factors in various developmental processes including 348 flowering, root growth, embryogenesis, and abiotic stress response (Laloum et al., 349 2013). Several pieces of evidence showed that NF-YC genes are involved in 350 351 light-mediated gene regulation (Warpeha et al., 2007; Stephenson et al., 2010). Especially, a most recent study reported that Arabidopsis NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and 352 NF-YC9 play redundant positive roles in photomorphogenesis (Myers et al., 2016). 353 However, the detailed regulatory mechanism how NF-YCs function in 354 355 photomorphogenesis so far remains unidentified. Here, we showed that the four NF-YC homologues NF-YC1, NF-YC3, NF-YC4, and NF-YC9 mediate light control of 356 hypocotyl elongation during early seedling stage via interacting with HDA15, an 357 RPD3/HDA1 type HDAC. HDA15 acts as a co-repressor to regulate gene expression 358 359 in multiple development processes by modulating chromatin structure status (Ma et al., 2013). Similar to hda15, loss-of-function of NF-YCs exhibits elongated hypocotyl, 360 while NF-YCs overexpression inhibits hypocotyl growth, under various light 361 conditions. Upon the findings in this study, we propose a model describing that, in the 362 light, the NF-YC subunits of NF-Y complex interact with HDA15, and recognize the 363 promoter of hypocotyl elongation-related genes, such as IAA19 and XTH17, 364 repressing their expression via H4 deacetylation and thus promoting 365 photomorphogenic growth. In the dark, HDA15 is dismissed from the target genes, 366 resulting in elevated H4ac levels in these loci. The "opened" chromatin subsequently 367 recruits the dark-related TFs to promote the expression of hypocotyl 368 elongation-related genes and etiolated growth (Figure 7D). Thus, our findings 369 elucidate that NF-YCs promote photomorphogenic growth via a chromatin 370 modification. 371

It is well-documented that epigenetic regulation plays fundamental roles in most
 biological processes including photomorphogenesis (Wu, 2014). Among them, histone

acetylation is a reversible chromatin modification mediated by histone 374 acetyltransferases HATs and histone deacetylases HDACs. HAT is considered as one 375 of the major factors that facilitate the chromatin relaxation and subsequent gene 376 transcription activation, while HDAC has a reverse function (Liu et al., 2014). Studies 377 of HAT and HDAC in Arabidopsis reveal the role of histone acetylation in 378 light-activated gene expression. Two HATs, GCN5 and HAF2, both function in HY5 379 related pathway to regulate light-responsive genes expression in plant development 380 381 (Benhamed et al., 2008; Bertrand et al., 2005). The HDAC gene HDA19 is involved in the negative regulation of light-responsive genes (Benhamed et al., 2006), while 382 HDA6 acts as positive regulator of light-controlled chromatin compaction (Tessadori 383 et al., 2009). HDA15, belonging to RPD3/HDA1 superfamily together with HDA6 384 385 and HDA19, was reported to regulate chlorophyll biosynthesis and photosynthesis in the dark via PIF3 (Liu et al., 2013). Dark-accumulated PIF3 recruits HDA15 via 386 proteins interaction, and then represses gene expression by decreasing the histone 387 acetylation levels, while the interaction between HDA15 and PIF3 is dissociated 388 389 under light conditions due to the light-triggered degradation of PIF3 proteins (Liu et al., 2013). However, loss-of-function mutant and overexpression lines of HDA15 390 exhibit PIF3-independent changes on hypocotyl growth in the light (Liu et al., 2013; 391 Figure 3), suggesting a novel role of HDA15 in light-mediated hypocotyl elongation. 392 Our findings shed lights on such a mechanism that NF-YCs associate with HDA15 to 393 co-repress hypocotyl elongation in a light-dependent manner. Interestingly, the protein 394 levels of HDA15 were reported to be constant during dark-light transition (Liu et al., 395 2013), but another study described that light drives the nucleocytoplasmic shuttling of 396 HDA15 in hypocotyls (Alinsug et al., 2012). It is wondered whether the light-induced 397 translocation of HDA15 facilitates NF-YCs function in photomorphogenesis. 398

NF-Y complex recognizes CCAAT-box in promoter through NF-YA subunit to regulate gene expression in eukaryotes (Laloum et al., 2013). Here our ChIP analysis showed that NF-YC9 and HDA15 were associated with CCAAT-box-containing regions in promoters of *IAA19* and *XTH17*, the targets of NF-YC and HDA15 (Figure 403 4A-4C), suggesting NF-YC appears to work together with NF-YA and NF-YB as

NF-Y trimer, and recruit HDA15 to regulate light responsive gene expression. 404 According to previous knowledge on regulatory mode of NF-Y (Laloum et al., 2013), 405 it is possible that other light responsive transcriptional factors might be also involved 406 in such regulation. The fact that NF-YC and HDA15 play similar roles under different 407 light conditions to mediate hypocotyl elongation indicates their function as general 408 negative regulators in light control of hypocotyl elongation. HY5 is the master 409 positive regulator in photomorphogenic growth. Light triggers R/FR/B photoreceptors 410 411 shuttling into the nucleus and suppressing COP1, which promotes HY5 accumulation through preventing from COP1-mediated degradation by proteasome (Jiao et al., 412 2007). Remarkably, HY5 shares a significant portion of targets, including IAA19 and 413 XTH17, with NF-YCs and HDA15 (Jing et al., 2013). Although a recent study 414 reported a physical interaction between NF-YC9 and HY5 by FRET-FLIM analysis, 415 those authors proposed that NF-YCs likely represent a parallel light signaling 416 pathway independent of HY5 since nf-yc3 nf-yc4 nf-yc9 hy5 mutants had significant 417 synergistic defects in light control of hypocotyl growth including both cell elongation 418 and cell division (Myers et al., 2016). Instead, COP1-mediated degradation might be 419 involved in the regulation of NF-Y (Myers et al., 2016). Therefore, it is still doubtful 420 whether the regulation of NF-YC and HDA15 in hypocotyl elongation-related genes 421 needs HY5 or not. In addition, since HY5 is degraded in the dark, and PIFs act as the 422 423 main positive regulators of skotomorphogenesis through competing with HY5 to recognize the same targets (Toledo-Ortiz et al., 2014), PIFs might play the potential 424 role of TFs in the dark described in the model (Figure 7D). These hypotheses need a 425 further investigation. 426

NF-Y, with nucleosome-like protein properties, has been shown to be important for the establishment of histone posttranslational modifications such as methylation and acetylation on active promoters through recruitment of relevant enzymes (Dolfini et al., 2012; Nardini et al., 2013). Previous studies mainly focused on the roles of NF-Y in flowering control and abiotic stress response in plants, but NF-Y function and the relevant epigenetic regulation in photomorphogenesis remain obscure. We demonstrated that NF-YCs regulate light-dependent hypocotyl growth inhibition via

HDA15-mediated histone H4 de-acetvlation. Consistent with this, the genome-wide 434 analysis revealed that NF-YCs and HDA15 co-regulate a subset of downstream genes 435 in the light. However, the NF-YC-regulated transcriptome profile also contains a 436 significant number of genes unregulated by HDA15, which are either up-regulated or 437 down-regulated by NF-YCs. Previous study has shown that NF-Y directly activates 438 the expression of SOC1, an important flowering activator, partly through the H3K27 439 demethylase REF6 (Hou et al., 2014). We therefore suppose that NF-YC may function 440 441 together with different epigenetic factors to play a dual role in transcriptional repression or activation during various developmental stages. It is intrigued to 442 examine whether NF-YC also mediates photomorphogenic growth via histone 443 methylation modification. A previous study reported that PICKLE (PKL) directly 444 regulates the expression of hypocotyl elongation-related genes in photomorphogenesis, 445 including IAA19 and XTH17 (Jing et al., 2013). PKL encodes an ATP-dependent 446 chromatin remodeling factor. In the light, PKL interacts with HY5 and represses 447 genes expression by depositing more histone H3 lysine 27 (H3K27) trimethylation 448 marks in targeted chromatin (Jing et al., 2013). To further seek the possible 449 relationship between NF-Y and PKL will provide insights into understanding how TFs 450 control plant development response to light via chromatin modification. 451

We previously reported that PIF3-HDA15 co-represses the expression of 452 chlorophyll biosynthesis and photosynthesis related genes in the dark (Liu et al., 453 2013). Notably, light-grown *nf-ycO* seedling exhibited etiolated phenotypes except a 454 normal chlorophyll biosynthesis. As light triggers degradation of PIF3 (Leivar and 455 Monte, 2014), it is plausible that HDA15 and NF-YCs are probably not involved in 456 chlorophyll biosynthesis in the light. Apart from the etiolated phenotypes, including 457 elongated hypocotyls and reduced cotyledons angles, in early light-grown seedlings of 458 *nf-ycQ*, remarkable longer petioles and elevated leaf angles from the horizontal were 459 also observed in the nf-ycQ adult plants compared with the wild-type, indicating that 460 NF-YCs might be involved in the regulation of shade avoidance (Figure S3A-S3D). 461 However, while *hda15* exhibited similar elongated hypocotyls with *nf-vcO* during 462 early seedling stage, we did not observe the shading syndrome in adult *hda15* plants. 463

It indicates that HDA15 is not involved in NF-YCs-mediated shade avoidance 464 response. When plants are grown under shading conditions, the photoreceptor phyB 465 perceives the less ratio of R:FR and converses into inactive form, thus maintaining the 466 stabilization of PIFs (Pacin et al., 2016; Pierik and de Wit, 2014). In addition, shading 467 signals are also perceived by blue light photoreceptor CRY1, which triggers the 468 degradation of HY5 (Pedmale et al., 2016). These changes on TFs stability induce a 469 series of gene expression, conferring plants accelerated growth of the hypocotyls, 470 471 stems, and leave petioles, increased leaf hyponasty, and reduced shoot branching (Liu et al., 2011). Whether and how NF-YCs function with these TFs in the shade 472 avoidance response remains investigation in future. 473

## 476 Plant Materials and Growth Conditions

All *Arabidopsis thaliana* plants used in this study are in Columbia background. *nf-yc1-1* (SALK\_086334), *nf-yc4-1* (SALK\_032163), *nf-yc9-1* (SALK\_058903), and *hda15* (SALK\_004027) were obtained from the *Arabidopsis* Biological Resource

The adult plants were grown in soil under 489 full-spectrum white fluorescent light (16 h light/ 8 h dark) with a frequency of 100 490  $\operatorname{umol} \cdot \operatorname{m}^{-2} \cdot \operatorname{s}^{-1}$  at 22°C. 491
