### a b s t r a c t

Primary and secondary metabolism in grape berries is under the control of complex interactions among environmental conditions, genotypes, and management practices. To obtain an interpretation from the view of transcriptome on distinct metabolite accumulation between ecologically different regions in China, next-generation sequencing technology was performed on E-L 31, 35, and 38 stages of Cabernet Sauvignon grape berries from Changli (CL, eastern) and Gaotai (GT, western). The transcript abundance of epoxycarotenoid dioxygenase and xanthoxin dehydrogenase required for ABA biosynthesis was significantly higher in the GT berries at E-L 35 and 38 stages compared with the CL berries, which may explain the relatively short maturation period of berries in the western region. Some genes required for carbohydrate metabolism, such as hexose transporter, l-idonate dehydrogenase, and phosphoenolpyruvate carboxylase, were significantly up-regulated in the CL berries in relation to the GT berries, which positively correlated with the sugar and organic acid accumulations. Pathway enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes revealed that the CL berries had higher levels of phenylpropanoid biosynthesis at E-L 38 stage than the GT berries, which may relate to the quick fading of the GT wines because of weak co-pigmentation. This observation lays a foundation for further study concerning the molecular basis for environmental effects on berry quality formation.

### **Introduction**

Grape is a non-climacteric fruit and berry development from fruit-set to ripening follows a double-sigmoid pattern, which is classically divided into three distinct phases on the basis of chemical and morphological traits ([Coombe,](#page-11-0) [1995\).](#page-11-0) The initial stage of berry growth involves rapid growth and cell division, organic acids (mainly malate and tartrate) accumulation in the vacuole, and the synthesis of tannins and hydroxycinnamates. During the transition phase, termed véraison by viticulturists, the growth declines and berries start to soften and accumulate sugars and anthocyanins which are the predominant pigments of grape berries. Ecological factors, such as geological conditions, soil characteristics and climatic variables (sunlight, temperature, water status, etc.), as well as viticulture management practices, have been known to influence the development and flavor composition of grape berries, and could thereby affect wine quality [\(Jackson](#page-11-0) [and](#page-11-0) [Lombard,](#page-11-0) [1993\).](#page-11-0) For example, it has been reported that grape berries exposed to sunlight generally contained higher level of sugars, anthocyanins and other phenolics, as well as lower level of titratable acidity, malate, juice pH, and berry weight when compared with non-exposed or canopy shaded fruits ([Bergqvist](#page-10-0) et [al.,](#page-10-0) [2001\).](#page-10-0) Low temperatures (20–25 ◦C) during maturation have been shown to promote berry coloration, while high temperatures (30–35 ◦C) suppressed the expression of the genes required for anthocyanin biosynthesis and enhanced anthocyanin degradation in grape skins ([Yamane](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2006;](#page-11-0) [Koshita](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2011\).](#page-11-0) Water deficit accelerated the sugar accumulation and malic acid breakdown in the juice, and increased the concentration of anthocyanins by enhancing biosynthesis and inhibiting berry growth [\(Koundouras](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2006;](#page-11-0) [Castellarin](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2007\).](#page-11-0)

In comparison with other countries in the world whose grapegrowing regions are relatively centralized, China has a very scattered distribution of grape-growing regions with a distance of over 2000 kmeither fromeastto west or fromsouth to north, whose ecological conditions are significantly different ([Li](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2011\).](#page-11-0) The Changli (CL) region of Hebei province is located on the plain of China's east coast, and the Gaotai (GT) region of Gansu province, about 1660 km west of CL, is located at the edge of the desert. According to the Köppen–Geiger climate classification system, the CL region has a humid continental climate (Dwa), with heavy rainfall, while, in contrast,the GT region has a cold desert climate (BWk) with less rainfall, abundant sunshine, and large daily temperature difference ([Rubel](#page-11-0) [and](#page-11-0) [Kottek,](#page-11-0) [2010\).](#page-11-0) These different climate characteristics between the two regions caused some differences in the quality of mature grape berries as well as the flavors and sensory typicality of wines [\(Li](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2011;](#page-11-0) [Jiang](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2013\).](#page-11-0)

The genome-wide transcriptome dynamics underlying berry ripening has been investigated using gene chip and RNA-seq technologies ([Deluc](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2007;](#page-11-0) [Zenoni](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2010;](#page-11-0) [Sweetman](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2012\).](#page-11-0) However, the knowledge about transcriptional diversity and the mechanisms of berry quality formation under different ecological conditions remains largely unstudied. Recently, the development of next-generation high-throughput DNA sequencing technology was widely used to map and quantify transcriptomes ([Wang](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2009\).](#page-11-0) A combined approach of de novo transcriptome assembly from short-read sequencing data integrated with digital gene expression (DGE) tag profiling is an efficient and economic choice to obtain expressed gene catalogs for non-model organisms lacking reference genomes. For some species with available reference genomes, de novo transcriptome assembly should also be performed because it could improve existing reference genome annotations and detect novel cultivar-specific genes [\(Martin](#page-11-0) [and](#page-11-0) [Wang,](#page-11-0) [2011;](#page-11-0) [Venturini](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2013\).](#page-11-0) As a result, we performed the de novo transcriptome sequencing to construct a comprehensive and integrated transcriptome of Vitis vinifera cv. Cabernet Sauvignon berries at three developmental stages from CL and GT regions, and subsequently compared the gene expression profiles of each sample using DGE system. The objective of this study is to gain overall insight about differential expression of genes in grape berries from two Chinese grape-producing regions with greatly different climates.

### **Materials and methods**

#### Plant material and biochemical analysis

Grape berries of Vitis vinifera cv. Cabernet Sauvignon (clone 338) were collected from two commercial vineyards in the Changli (CL) region of Hebei province (39◦35 N, 118◦82 E, 212 m above sea level) and the Gaotai (GT) region of Gansu province (39◦18 N, 99◦41 E, 1377 m above sea level), located in the coastland and inland of China, respectively (Fig. [A1\).](#page-8-0) Berries were sampled every other week during the periods from three weeks after flowering (waf) to the end of véraison and then collected weekly until ripening during the 2010 growing season. The ten-year-old own-rooted vines inbothofthe vineyards weremanaged onamodifiedVertical-Shoot-Positioned trellis system (12–15 buds per vine and 1–2 clusters per shoot), arranged in north-south rows with 2.5 m × 1 m vine spacing and drip irrigated. Both of the vineyards were managed according to standard viticulture practices for the cultivar and region. Pest management was carried out according to local standard practices and canopy management practices were all performed manually with no leaf removal. The meteorological data gathering from the two regions during berry development in 2010 are shown in Table A.1, and the weather conditions of the sampling day and the day before sampling are shown in Table A.2 (provided by National Meteorological Information Center). Representative samples were obtained according to the method reported [\(Boulton](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [1995\).](#page-11-0) In detail, 300 berries were collected from the sunny and shady sides of at least 70 clusters, and placed into

a Ziplock bag. The clusters were selected from 35 different vines located on the vineyard's north site and 35 different vines on the south site. Three biological replicates were collected with similar method at each sampling date. The sampling time was fixed at 10:00 to 11:00 am. After being washed with distilled water, a subsample of berries was frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately and transported to the lab in dry ice for the subsequent transcriptional analysis, the rest were subjected to berry developmental measurements, including total soluble solids (◦Brix) content, pH and berry weight, as well as concentrations of primary and secondary metabolites.

### HPLC analysis of sugars and organic acids

Samples of 30 g berries of every biological replicate were macerated at 4 ◦C; 1 mL clear juice was passed through a SEP-C18 cartridge (Supelclean ENVI C18 SPE), diluted to 5 mL with ddH2O then filtered through a 0.22 m filter (cellulose acetate and nitrocellulose, CAN). Three independent extractions were conducted for each grape sample. An analysis for sugars were carried out on an Agilent HP-1100 series LC-MSD trap VL, equipped with a DAD detector and reverse phase column (Aminex HPX-87P, 300 mm × 7.8 mm, 9 m), and 20 -L was injected for analysis. ddH2O was used as an eluent at a flow rate of 1 mL/min. The column was maintained at 80 ◦C and eluted compounds were detected by UV absorbance at 280 nm. Organic acids were analyzed using an Agilent HP-1200 series LC-MSD trap VL, equipped with a DAD detector and a Kromasil C-18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 m) with a guard column cartridge (Agilent C-18). The column was set at 25 ◦C. The mobile phase was 0.02 M KH2PO4 with 3% CH3OH and the flow rate was 0.8 mL/min. The injection volume was 10 -L. Eluted compounds were detected by UV absorbance at 210 nm. The products detected were identified by comparing their retention times with those of the standards under the same HPLC conditions, and quantified according to the calibration curves based on standards for each compound.

### RNA extraction, transcriptome sequencing and data analysis

Based on biochemical parameters, we selected the berries of three development stages (pea-size berries, early-véraison and mature berries) from the two regions corresponding to the 31, 35 and 38 of the modified E-L system [\(Coombe,](#page-11-0) [1995\)](#page-11-0) to conduct transcriptome analysis ([Fig.](#page-1-0) 1D). Approximately 50 berries were randomly selected from a 300-berry biological replicate for RNA extraction. Total RNAs for RNA-seq analysis and realtime polymerase chain reaction (PCR) experiments were extracted from frozen deseeded berries using a General Plant Total RNA Extraction Kit (BioTeke, China), and further incubated with DNase I (Promega, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Quantification and quality assessment of the purified RNA were analyzed using the Aglient 2100 Bioanalyzer. To obtain an integrated reference transcriptome, equal quantities of total RNA from three biological replicates of each sample were pooled to generate one lane. Corresponding six sampling points, a total of six lanes with 2 g RNA per lane were mixed together to create a custom normalized cDNA library, which was sequenced by Illumina HiseqTM2000 with a 100-bp paired-end (PE) module and processed for assembly into contigs and unigenes using the short read assembling program Trinity with the fixed default k-mer size of 25. The sequencing data was deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) sequence database with accession number SRR1656853, and the assembled unique sequences have been deposited at DDBJ/EMBL/GenBank under the accession GBXP00000000. The version described in this paper is the first version, GBXP01000000.

For the functional annotation, unigene sequences were firstly aligned by BLASTx against several protein databases including the National Center for Biotechnological Information (NCBI) non-redundant protein sequences (Nr) database and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway database, and aligned by BLASTn against the V. vinifera genome sequences (Genoscope, 12× coverage) and the NCBI nucleotide collection (Nt) database, with (E)-value threshold of 1e−5. Based on Nr annotation, Blast2GO program and WEGO software were used to get the Gene Ontology (GO) annotation and the functional classification of unigenes, respectively.

### Digital gene expression (DGE) profiling and mapping to reference

Equal quantities of total RNA from three biological replicates were pooled to generate the cDNA library of each sample (Zenoni et al., 2010), which were sequenced individually by Illumina HiSeq<sup>TM</sup> 2000 with a 50-bp single read module. All clean reads obtained were mapped back to the assembled transcripts using the alignment software SOAPaligner, allowing no more than two nucleotides mismatched. For each of the six DGE libraries, the sequencing saturation analysis was performed to check whether the number of detected genes continues to increase when sequencing amount (total read number) increases. The transcript abundance of each gene was calculated by using the RPKM (reads per kilobase per million) method, which was able to eliminate the influence of variation in gene length and total reads numbers on the calculation of gene expression (Mortazavi et al., 2008). The data have been deposited in the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database and are accessible through GEO accession GSE63512.

A statistical analysis of the frequency of each unambiguous clean read in the different cDNA libraries was performed to screen differentially expressed genes (DEGs) among samples using the modified algorithm described previously (Audic and Claverie, 1997). "FDR (False Discovery Rate)  $\leq$  0.001 and the absolute value of log<sub>2</sub>Ratio  $\geq$  1" were used as the threshold to judge the significance of the gene expression difference. For pathway enrichment analysis, all DEGs were mapped to the terms in the KEGG databases and gene numbers of the significantly enriched KEGG terms were calculated through the hypergeometric test. The calculating formula used was shown below:

$$P = 1 - \sum_{i=0}^{m-1} \frac{\binom{M}{i}}{\binom{N-M}{n-i}}$$

In this formula, *N* is the number of all genes with KEGG annotation; *n* is the number of DEGs in *N*, *M* is the number of all genes that are annotated to the specific pathways, and *m* is the number of DEGs in *M*. Pathways with a *p*-value  $\leq$ 0.05 were defined as being significantly enriched in at least one of the DEGs.

### Quantitative real-time PCR

All the purified RNA samples mentioned above were adjusted to the same concentration  $(100 \text{ ng/}\mu\text{L})$  with RNase free ddH<sub>2</sub>O, then reverse-transcribed into cDNA using AMV reverse transcriptase (Takara, Japan) following the manufacturer's instructions. The quantification of the transcripts was performed using SYBR® Premix Ex Taq<sup>TM</sup> (Takara, Japan) on a 7300 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, USA). Each real-time PCR reaction (20 µL) contained 2.0 µL of the cDNA template, 1.6 µL of the primer mixture (10  $\mu$ M each, mixed with equivalent volume), 10.0  $\mu$ L of 2× SYBR Premix Ex Tag II and 0.4 µL of 50× ROX Reference Dye (Takara, Japan), and 6.0 µL ddH<sub>2</sub>O. Reactions were performed via a two-step method: 95 °C for 30 s, 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s, 60 °C for 31 s, and a melt cycle from 60 °C to 95 °C. Gene-specific primers are shown in Table A3. Each RNA sample (including three independent biological replicates) was performed from three technological replicates in analysis of real-time PCR. The normalized relative expression levels of target genes were calculated by  $2^{-\Delta Ct}$  ( $\Delta Ct = Ct_{Target} - Ct_{Ubiquitin}$ , Ct: cycle threshold) using VvUbiquitin1 as an internal standard

(Bogs et al., 2005). The target genes and *VvUbiquitin1* were analyzed simultaneously.

### Results

### Phenotypic evaluation of grape berries

The flowering date of grapevines in CL was one week earlier than those in GT during the 2010 growing season. Berries reached peasize at 3 weeks after flowering in both regions, the dates of véraison and ripening were set at 10 and 8 waf, 17 and 16 waf in CL and GT, respectively. Compared to berries of GT, the berries of CL contained similar concentrations of soluble solids and juice pH at pea-size stage, but showed higher concentrations at the subsequent development stage. At the last sample date, the concentration of soluble solids was almost the same in berries of the two regions, but juice pH in berries of GT was still lower than that in CL (Fig. 1A and B). The berries from GT were also lighter in weight than those of CL during the whole developmental stages (Fig. 1C). Glucose and fructose concentrations in berries of CL were significantly higher than those of GT at early-véraison (11 and 9 waf in CL and GT. respectively), but had no significant difference between the two regions at harvest. Compared with the samples from GT, the grape berries from CL had higher concentration of tartaric acid at the peasize period but lower at both early-véraison and mature period. Meanwhile, malic acid presented higher level in both pea-size and mature berries from CL but lower level in early-véraison berries of CL, in comparison with the berries from GT (Fig. 2).

## Construction and functional annotation of a Cabernet Sauvignon reference transcriptome

To obtain a non-redundant set of transcripts for V. vinifera cv. Cabernet Sauvignon berries, a normalized cDNA library constructed from a single pool of all six samples was sequenced, producing a total of 53,029,974 filtered clean reads with a total of 4,772,697,660 nt and an average GC content of 47.78%. De novo assembly yielded a total of 52,359 unigenes with an average sequence length of 803 nt, and the N50 value of 1296 nt. The numbers of unigenes annotated with the databases of Genoscope, NCBI nucleotide collection (Nt), NCBI non-redundant protein sequences (Nr), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) were 52,355, 40,030, 52,044, 21,520 and 27,440, respectively. Among the 52,044 unigenes annotated with Nr database, nearly 96.7% of the top matches hit the V. vinifera database itself, while only 3.3% of the identified transcripts showed the highest similarity with genes from Arabidopsis thaliana, Populus trichocarpa, Zea mays and other species.

### Gene ontology biological process analysis of DEGs between regions

The terms "metabolic process", "cellular process" and "response to stimulus" were the three most abundant GO biological process codes represented by the largest percentage of DEGs between the two regions, containing 491, 441, and 293 at E-L 31 stage, 728, 663, and 446 at E-L 35 stage, as well as 398, 376, and 241 at E-L 38 stage, respectively. Notably, the number of up-regulated unigenes in the three terms mentioned above was significantly higher in samples from CL than GT at E-L 31 stage, with a ratio of 352/139, 307/134, and 223/70, respectively. Furthermore, the percentage of DEGs to the total number of genes annotated to the term of "response to stimulus" was much larger than that in other terms. Among those DEGs assigned to the term of "response to stimulus", several unigenes were annotated to heat shock protein (HSP) or other molecular chaperone such as HSP20 and HtpG proteins. All the genes encoding HSP20 and HtpG proteins were expressed higher in the CL samples than GT samples at E-L 31 stage. In addition, many genes annotated as key components in the signal transduction pathway of stress response, such as abscisic acid (ABA) signaling and biosynthesis genes and pathogenesis-related (PRs) genes were differentially expressed between the two regions. The transcript abundance of genes encoding 9-cis-epoxycarotenoid dioxygenase (NCED1) and xanthoxin dehydrogenase (ABA2), two key enzymes related toABAbiosynthesis, was significantly higher in berries from GT at E-L 35 and 38 stages, as compared with berries at the same developmental stage from CL. Regarding the unigenes annotated to PR families, nine unigenes showed significant differences in expression level between samples from CL and GT. In the samples from the CL region,the expression oftwo PR-1 genes and two PR-5 genes at E-L 31 stage, as well as the expression of one PR-1 gene and four PR-10 genes at E-L 35 stage were significantly higher than that observed in GT samples. In contrast,the GT samples at E-L 38 stage presented higher expression level of two PR-5 genes mentioned above.

### **Discussion**

The application of next generation sequencing technology had provided a rapid and cost-effective strategy of transcriptome sequencing and characterization. Based on the reference genome, the expression profile of 17,324 genes was analyzed during berry development in grapevine cultivar Corvina ([Zenoni](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2010\).](#page-11-0) Another transcriptional profile analysis of grape berries (cultivar Shiraz) provided 17,942–18,729 (at least 75%) transcripts of the NCBI RefSeq during the immature green phase, at early and late véraison, and in ripe berries [\(Sweetman](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2012\).](#page-11-0) Because of the processes of sexual reproduction, vegetative propagation and somatic mutations, cultivated grapes generally have genetic diversity and differentiation [\(Aradhya](#page-10-0) et [al.,](#page-10-0) [2003;](#page-10-0) [This](#page-10-0) et [al.,](#page-10-0) [2006\).](#page-10-0) The application of de novo transcript assembly approach allowed the further identification of a substantial amount of novel proteincoding genes in non-annotated or unassembled regions of the reference genome being reconstructed from cultivars Corvina and Tannat, respectively ([Da](#page-11-0) [Silva](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2013;](#page-11-0) [Venturini](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2013\).](#page-11-0) Here, we adopted a strategy based on Illumina RNA-seq technology and de novo short-read assembly to generate a new reference transcriptome with a total of 52,359 unique sequences from berries of Cabernet Sauvignon, and characterized the transcriptome differences of samples from the two geographically different regions through Illumina DGE technology. The large number of de novo assembly transcriptome sequences greatly extended the existing sequence resources for V. vinifera database and will provide abundant genetic information for further investigating the molecular mechanism of berry development and response to distinct environmental conditions. In the present study, the grape berries from the two different regions showed distinct gene expression patterns in primary and secondary metabolisms. Transcript abundance of the genes encoding invertase, sucrose synthase and hexokinase, which were involved in the accumulation of glucose and fructose ([Dai](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2011\),](#page-11-0) showed no significant difference between samples of the two regions during ripening. Nevertheless, the berries from CL, in comparison with the GT berries, presented higher expression levels of VvHAT2 at E-L 35 stage. VvHAT2 is a hexose transporter thatfacilitated the transport and accumulation of sugar within pulp cells of ripening berries [\(Hayes](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2007\).](#page-11-0) This may explain the observation thatthe CL berries at E-L 35 stage contained higher concentrations of glucose and fructose. LidnDH catalyzes the conversion of l-idonate to 5-keto d-gluconic acid in plant and is proposed to be a rate-limiting step in biosynthesis of tartaric acid from ascorbic acid [\(Melino](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2009\).](#page-11-0) This study indicated that the significant difference in transcript abundance for two unigenes encoding LidnDH well corresponded to different concentration of tartaric acids in the berries from the two regions at E-L 31 stage, while the quicker decrease in the concentration of tartaric acid in the berries from CL during ripening might be attributed to the dilution effect ([Dokoozlian,](#page-11-0) [2000\),](#page-11-0) which was caused by the faster increasing of berry weight in this region ([Fig.](#page-1-0) 1C). The accumulation of malate in grape berry was thought to occur mainly through the combined activities of PEPC and cytosolic malate dehydrogenase (cytMDH), which convert phosphoenolpyruvate to malate via oxaloacetate. The rapid decrease in malate concentration during mature is usually attributed to its degradation through the reverse reaction of mMDH or its oxidation by mitochondrial NAD-malic enzyme or cytosolic NADP-malic enzyme (ME) to yield pyruvate ([Sweetman](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2009\).](#page-11-0) Here,there was no significant difference in the expression level of unigenes encoding cytMDH or ME between samples from the two regions, but higher expression of genes encoding a PEPCat E-L 31 stage and amMDHat E-L 35 stage intheCL berries likely contributed to higher concentration of malate at E-L 31 stage butlower at E-L 35 stage in this region. Previous researches have indicated that water status, light conditions and temperature have noticeable effects on berry ripening and the timing of véraison [\(Bergqvist](#page-10-0) et [al.,](#page-10-0) [2001;](#page-10-0) [Koundouras](#page-10-0) et [al.,](#page-10-0) [2006\).](#page-10-0) In general, water deficit and cold stress would lead to an increase in the content of ABA, a hormone that implicated in the control of grape berry ripening ([Kuhn](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2013\).](#page-11-0) Influenced by dry climate and the lack of rainfall, berries from GT region exhibited more activation of genes required for ABA biosynthesis, which might result in the shorter developmental period of grapes from fruit-set to harvest and earlier initiation of berry coloration in this region. Adverse conditions such as high light, chilling and heat can predispose plants to the photoinhibitory stress [\(Greer](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [1986;](#page-11-0) [Long](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [1994\),](#page-11-0) and water deficit has also been reported to induce photoinhibition in grape berries during the initial stages of development ([Deluc](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2009\).](#page-11-0) Accordingly, the relatively lower transcript abundance of unigenes encoding photosynthesis-related proteins in GT samples at E-L 31 stage could be caused by strong light radiation, semi-arid climate and the lack of rainfall. The ubiquitin-dependent

protein degradation pathway is demonstrated to function in plant response to abiotic stresses such as water deficit, temperature fluctuations (heat or chilling), high salt, total radiation (high intensity ultra-violet light) and oxidative stress by degrading damaged cellular proteins ([Lyzenga](#page-11-0) [and](#page-11-0) [Stone,](#page-11-0) [2011\).](#page-11-0) In the GT region, a dry climate, relatively higher total radiation and daily temperature swings might result in the up-regulation of gene expression for the ubiquitin-ligase complex and the enhancement of ubiquitindependent protein degradation in the berries at E-L 38 stage. The heavy rainfall and high relative humidity during berry ripening in CL generally increase pathogen development and survival rates, disease transmission, and grape susceptibility. As a major component of the cell wall derived from phenylpropanoid polymer, lignin was found to be associated with resistance to the defense response to pathogens in vascular plants ([Zhao](#page-11-0) [and](#page-11-0) [Dixon,](#page-11-0) [2011\).](#page-11-0) Higher expression of unigenes encoding for the synthesis of lignin monomers in CL samples at E-L 38 stage is well-interpreted as the potential mechanism of self-protection in response to pathogenic attack. Some monocarboxylic phenolic acids, such as caffeic acid, ferulic acid and p-coumaric acid, play important roles in the color stability of young red wines and their evolution during maturation and aging ([Boulton,](#page-11-0) [2001\).](#page-11-0) Compared with the CL berries, the lower transcript abundance of genes required for phenolic acids biosynthesis in berries from GT at E-L 38 stage may be related to the greater decrease of color intensity during the aging of wine from this region. F3 5 H is a key enzyme of the flavonoid and anthocyanin biosynthesis pathway that control the production of 3 ,5 -hydroxylated flavonoids, including myricetin (flavonols), prodelphinidin (flavan-3-ols) and blue/delphinidin-based anthocyanins [\(Jeong](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2006\).](#page-11-0) F3 5 H genes are present in highly redundant copy numbers in V. vinifera and the expression of members of this gene family in berries was previously reported to be up-regulated by water deficit and heating treatment, while downregulated by decreased light exposure at specific developmental stages ([Castellarin](#page-11-0) [et](#page-11-0) [al.,](#page-11-0) [2007;](#page-11-0) [Koyama](#page-11-0) [and](#page-11-0) [Goto-Yamamoto,](#page-11-0) [2008;](#page-11-0) [Falginella](#page-11-0) [et](#page-11-0) [al.,](#page-11-0) [2010;](#page-11-0) [Cohen](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2012\).](#page-11-0) The higher light radiation and temperature, dry climate and the lack of rainfall during the ripening of berries in GT are presumed to result in higher expression of some members of F3 5 H gene family in berries at E-L 35 and 38 stages, and subsequently, contributed to the higher concentration of 3 ,5 -hydroxylated flavonoids in ripe berries and more blueness and color intensity of their corresponding wines compared with those from CL regions. The significant enriched DEGs involved in secondary metabolism definitely demonstrate that environmental factors play one of the most important roles in the quality formation of grapes. The mechanism of berry coloration and ripening, metabolic regulation of flavor compounds and volatile aromas, linked to the relationships of transcriptome reprogramming, accumulation of secondary metabolites and ecological factors have been discussed in detail in another paper [\(Li](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2014\).](#page-11-0) The present study indicates that the expression of genes involved in berry development and stress-response is under the accurate regulation by putative TFs. The proteins of WRKY TF families participate in many significant physiological and biochemical processes such as seed dormancy and germination, leaf senescence, and responses to abiotic stresses in plants [\(Rushton](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2010\).](#page-11-0) PR proteins are classified into 17 families based on their sequences and biological activities, such as antifungal activity proteins (PR-1 family), thaumatin-like proteins and osmotins (PR-5 family) and ribonuclease-like proteins (PR-10 family) ([Sels](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2008\).](#page-11-0) In Arabidopsis, WRKY6 positively activates the senescenceand pathogen defense-associated PR1 promoter, and indirectly involved in the expression of Nonexpressor of PR1 (NPR1), a key regulator of the systemic acquired resistance (SAR)-dependent signal pathway ([Robatzek](#page-11-0) [and](#page-11-0) [Somssich,](#page-11-0) [2002\).](#page-11-0) WRKY7 overexpression led to the suppression of defense-related genes, including PR1 [\(Kim](#page-11-0) <span id="page-8-0"></span>et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2006\).](#page-11-0) In the two ecologically different regions, the expression patterns of defense-related genes PR-1, PR-5 and PR-10 are strongly correlated with several members of WRKY TFs, such as WRKY6 and WRKY7 detected in grape berries of three developmental stages. The multifunctional two-repeat (R2R3) MYB proteins in plants have been demonstrated to be involved in plant stress responses, such as responses to ultra-violet (UV), light, wounding, anaerobic stress and pathogens. They also participate in the regulation of secondary metabolism, meristem formation, floral and seed development, and the control of cell morphogenesis [\(Stracke](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2001;](#page-11-0) [Dubos](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2010\).](#page-11-0) Both MYB5a and MYBPA1 can activate the transcription of several flavonoid structural genes required for the synthesis of anthocyanins and PAs in grape berries [\(Deluc](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2006;](#page-11-0) [Bogs](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2007\).](#page-11-0) The cluster analysis of the transcript abundances of MYB5a, MYBA1 and DEGs in shikimate/phenylpropanoid and flavonoid biosynthesis pathways indicate that DFR and MYB5a exhibit similar expression patterns across the three developmental stages between samples from the two regions, which is consistent with the previous report ([Deluc](#page-11-0) et [al.,](#page-11-0) [2006\).](#page-11-0) Additionally, the synchronization between the expression of genes encoding three enzymes of the shikimate pathway and the expression of MYBPA1 is observed, suggesting that these genes might be candidate targets of MYBPA1. It is not reported to date concerning TFs regulating the shikimate pathway. This observation also expands our thinking for the study on coordinated regulation of the shikimate pathway and phenylpropanoid/flavonoid pathway.

### **Conclusion**

In the present study, we generated a robust reference transcriptome for berries of Cabernet Sauvignon and provided a global picture of gene expression patterns at three developmental stages of the berries grown under different ecological conditions using combination of de novo assembly and digital gene expression profiling analysis. A great number of transcripts responsible for the formation and regulation of flavor and quality characteristics in grapes weredifferentially expressedunderdifferent ecological conditions. The significant enrichment analysis of DEGs indicated that the major differences between the two regions were the various gene expression profiles in pathways of secondary metabolism and defense response.
