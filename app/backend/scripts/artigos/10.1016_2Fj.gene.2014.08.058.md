## ABSTRACT

Cotton is an important fiber plant, and it's attractive to elucidate the molecular mechanism of anther develop- 22 ment due to the close relationship between the anther fertility and boll-setting, and also fiber yield. In the present 23 paper, 47.2 million paired-end reads with average length of 82.87 bp from the anthers of TM-1 (*Gossypium* 24 *hirsutum* L.), a genetic standard line, were generated through transcriptome sequencing, and 210,965 unigenes 25 of more than 100 bp were obtained. BLAST, KEGG, COG, and GO analyses showed that the genes were enriched 26 in the processes of transcription, translation, and post-translation as well as hormone signal transduction, the 27 transcription factor families, and cell wall-related genes mainly participating in cell expansion and carbohydrate 28 metabolism. Further analysis identified 11,153 potential SSRs. A suit of 5122 primer pair sequences were de- 29 signed, and 82 of 300 randomly selected primer pairs produced reproducible amplicons that were polymorphic 30 among 22 cotton accessions from *G. hirsutum, Gossypium barbadense* and *Gossypium arboreum*. The UPGMA clus-31 tering analysis further confirmed high quality and effectiveness of these novel SSR markers. The present study 32 provided insights into the transcriptome profile of the cotton and established a public information platform for 33 functional genomics and molecular breeding. 34

## 110 2. Materials and Methods

## 111 2.1. Plant Materials

112 Plants of upland cotton, the genetic standard TM-1 (*G. hirsutum* L.), were grown in an experimental field under standard field conditions 113 in 2012. The anther at the stages of sporogenous cell division (bud 114 length < 3.0 mm), pollen mother cell meiosis (bud length = 3.1-115 4.5 mm), uninucleate microspore (bud length = 4.6-12 mm) and ma-116ture pollen (bud length > 12 mm) were harvested according to Deng 117 et al. (2010), respectively, frozen in liquid nitrogen immediately, and 118 stored at -80 °C for use. 119

## 120 2.2. RNA Isolation and Sequencing

For Illumina sequencing, the total RNA of each sample was isolated using Trizol (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) and further purified with the RNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA). RNA quality was verified using a 2100 Bioanalyzer RNA Nanochip (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA) and all samples had RNA Integrity Number (RIN) value more than 8.5. Then RNA was quantified using NanoDrop ND-1000 Spectrophotometer (Nano-Drop, Wilmington, DE).

The RNAs from four samples were mixed with equal amount, and then Illumina sequencing using the Solexa platform was performed according to the manufacturer's instructions (Illumina, San Diego, CA) in Shanghai Quanmai Bio-Technology Co., Ltd. Then, the stringent filter process on the raw sequencing data was carried out with the criteria that quality threshold is 20 (error rate 1%) to remove low-quality data, and length threshold is 35 bp to rule out the ambiguous N-containing sequences. Therefore, 47.2 million sequencing reads with 82-mer 135 length. The sequencing data are deposited in NCBI Sequence Read 136 Archive (SRA, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Traces/sra) with accession 137 number SRP041153. 138

## 2.3. De Novo Assembly and Analysis of Illumina Reads

The samples were assembled with SOAPdenovo (Li et al., 2010). 140 The reads were first combined to form longer fragments, i.e., contigs. 141 The EST sequences were downloaded from cotton database (http:// 142 gossypium.info/, cotton46a), and further mapping was performed 143 using Bowtie and Velvet software to obtain non-redundant unigenes 144 that were as long as possible.

The resulting unigenes were determined by performing BLASTX 146 (V2.2.14) (Altschul et al., 1997) searches against protein databases, 147 with the priority order of NR (non-redundant protein sequences in 148 NCBI), Swiss-Prot, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes database 149 (KEGG) (V56.0, Oct. 1, 2010) (Kanehisa et al., 2008), and COG with the 150 criteria of E-value  $\leq 1e-3$ . Based on the results of the protein database 151 annotation, Blast2GO (Conesa et al., 2005) was employed to obtain the 152 functional classification of the unigenes based on Gene Ontology (GO) 153 terms. WEGO software (Ye et al., 2006) was used to perform the GO 154 functional classification for all unigenes. Further BLASTx against 155 *Arabidopsis thaliana* Transcription Factors database (PlantTFDB), Cell 156 Wall Navigator (CWN) protein database and MAIZEWALL database, 157 respectively, were performed. The expectation (E)-value cutoff was 158 set at 1E-5.

## 3.3. Transcription Factor Genes

250

Transcription factors (TFs) are key regulators at gene transcriptional 251 level in biological processes (Riechmann et al., 2000). All isogenes of 252 *G. hirsutum* were performed by BLASTx with PlantTFDB to identify putative transcription factors expressed in the anther of *G. hirsutum*. A total 254 of 6708 isogenes matched in PlantTFDB (Table S5), which fell into 54 255 families and represented 84.4% of the 64 TF families in *A. thaliana* genome. The most abundant TF family is WRKY and represented 13.9% 257 of the total 6708 isogenes, followed by bHLH (6.9%), C3H (6.8%), NAC 258 (6.5%), MYB (5.1%) and C2H2 (5.1%). Similar to the TFs identified in 259 the ESTs from six developing xylem libraries in radiate pine (Li et al., 260 2009) and from leaves of sabaigrass (*Eulaliopsis binata*) (Zou et al., 261 2013), high abundance of WRKY, C3H, C2H2, and MYB was also observed in *G. hirsutum*.

The recent studies revealed that NAC, MYB, AUX/IAA and HD are im-264 portant in regulating secondary wall biosynthesis (Du and Groover, 265 2010; Groover and Robischon, 2006; Zhong and Ye, 2007). In this 266 study, 436 and 345 isogenes match in NAC and MYB TFs, respectively, 267 some of which may act as master switches regulating secondary wall 268 biosynthesis. The TFs identified in our isogene data could be important 269 resources for regulation of cell wall biosynthesis. 270

## 3.6. SSR Confirmation by PCR

Among the 5122 primer pairs, 300 primer pairs (Table S10) were 348 randomly selected to evaluate their application across 22 cotton 349 accessions from *G. hirsutum*, *G. barbadense* and *G. arboreum* species 350 (Table 2). A total of 143 (47.7%) primer pairs resulted in successful 351 PCR amplification with clear PCR bands, and 540 allelic loci with 155 352 polymorphic loci and 82 SSR markers with polymorphism were detect-353 ed. The number of allelic genes on each locus is 1–15, and the average 354 for each primer is 1.9. The HNAU185 locus has the most allelic loci of 355 15 (Table S11). The statistical analysis identified 27 SSRs with the 356 highest gene diversity in the cotton plants tested.

The similarity analysis across 22 cotton accessions revealed that the 358 similarity coefficient of 44.59% is above 0.800, and 10.39% varied from 359 0.700 to 0.800, which means that 50.98% of cotton accessions tested 360 had significant genetic similarity. About 0.87% is from 0.600 to 0.700, 361 and 43.72% is less than 0.400 (Table 3). 362

The further clustering of SSR verification data using UPGMA showed 363 that the genetic similarity ranged from 0.19 to 0.98. The 22 cotton 364 accessions were divided into three classes with the genetic similarity 365 coefficient of 0.19, and Class I includes shiyaxi1, Class II includes hai1, 366 7124, P62ne10, Pima\_S6 and 3–79, and the other 16 belong to Class III 367 (Fig. 5), which is highly consistent with the classic taxonomy (Table 2). 368

#### 4. Conclusions 384

Transcriptome analysis from different tissues and organs provides 385 386 the basis for functional genomics research. In the present work, the G. hirsutum anther cDNA libraries and transcriptome sequencing were 387 performed, and 210,965 unigenes were obtained. A systematic analysis 388 was further carried out including BLAST against nucleotides and protein 389 390 databases, and KEGG, COG, and GO analyses, and characterized some specific transcription factors and cell wall related genes involved in an-391 392 ther development. Based on the present transcriptome sequence, a total of 11,153 potential SSRs were identified, and the 5019 non-redundant 393 394SSR primer pairs from our transcriptome data were developed. Fur-395ther PCR verification across 22 cotton accessions from G. hirsutum, 396 G. barbadense and G. arboreum species with 300 SSR primer pairs 397 confirmed the significant polymorphism of 82 SSR loci, which suggested the potentially valuable application of the achievements made in the 398 present study for novel gene discovery, phenotype improvement and 399 other researches in cotton. 400
