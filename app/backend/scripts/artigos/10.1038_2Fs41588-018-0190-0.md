# **Polycomb-mediated gene silencing by the BAH–EMF1 complex in plants**

**Zicong Li1 , Xing Fu2,6, Yizhong Wang2,3,4,6, Renyi Liu2,5 and Yuehui He  1,3\***

**Polycomb proteins implement genome-wide transcriptional repression in multicellular organisms. The evolutionarily conserved Polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) catalyzes histone H3 Lys27 trimethylation (H3K27me3) that is read and effected by Polycomb repressive complex 1 (PRC1) in animals, but the interpretation of this mark remains unclear in plants. Here we report that in the eudicot** *Arabidopsis thaliana* **two homologous BAH (Bromo adjacent homology) domain–containing proteins form a plant-specific complex with EMBRYONIC FLOWER 1 (EMF1), and that the BAH–EMF1 complex (BAH–EMF1c) reads and effects the H3K27me3 mark and mediates genome-wide transcriptional repression. Furthermore, in the monocot rice a homolog of the**  *Arabidopsis* **BAH-domain proteins also binds methylated H3K27 and forms a complex with the rice homolog of EMF1, suggesting that BAH–EMF1c is conserved in flowering plants. Therefore, our results show that the plant-specific BAH–EMF1c fulfills PRC1-like functions in higher plants, suggesting a convergent evolution of PRC1 activity in plants and animals.**

P olycomb group proteins implement transcriptional repression and constitute a global silencing system essential to growth and development in multicellular organisms ranging from plants to human[s1](#page-6-0)[,2](#page-7-0) . Polycomb group proteins form several types of chromatin-modifying complexes to implement repressive chromatin modifications and organization at target loci[1](#page-6-0) . There are two main types of Polycomb complexes: PRC1 and PRC2. PRC2 catalyzes trimethylation of H3K27, which is read by PRC1, leading to additional repressive chromatin modifications and chromatin compaction for transcriptional repression at Polycomb target loci[1](#page-6-0),[3](#page-7-1) . In addition, PRC1 contributes to PRC2 recruitmen[t3](#page-7-1)[,4](#page-7-2) . Thus, these complexes are intimately linked and their combined activities are essential for Polycomb-mediated gene silencing in animals[3](#page-7-1) .

The core subunits of PRC2 are evolutionarily conserved between plants and animals, but several canonical PRC1 components conserved in animals are missing in the plant kingdom, including Polycomb (Pc), the reader of H3K27me3 that was first identified in *Drosophila melanogaster*, and Polyhomeotic (Ph), a protein required for PRC1 structure and chromatin compactio[n1,](#page-6-0)[2](#page-7-0) . In addition, plants lack proteins with a chromatin-compaction domain called PSC-CTR, essential for PRC1-mediated chromatin compaction in animal[s5](#page-7-3) . Instead, in the flowering plant *A. thaliana*, the plant-specific protein EMF1 is proposed to be a functional analog of PSC-CTR that implements chromatin compaction in Polycomb silencin[g5,](#page-7-3)[6](#page-7-4) . Furthermore, LIKE HETEROCHROMATIN PROTEIN 1 (LHP1), the only plant homolog of animal HP1, which recognizes methylated histone H3 Lys9 (H3K9), can bind the H3K27me3 mark and colocalizes with it genome-wide in *Arabidopsis*[7,](#page-7-5)[8](#page-7-6) . However, loss of *LHP1* function gives rise to upregulation of only several hundred genes, and the *lhp1* mutant exhibits moderate morphological defects[9](#page-7-7) ; in contrast, loss of PRC2 function leads to genome-wide elimination of H3K27me3 and de-repression of thousands of genes in *Arabidopsis*[10](#page-7-8)[,11.](#page-7-9) This raises critical questions as to whether reading and interpretation (or effecting) of the H3K27me3 mark introduced by PRC2 are essential for Polycomb silencing in plants. Here we report that the homologous plant-unique BAH-domaincontaining proteins SHORT LIFE (SHL) and EARLY BOLTING IN SHORT DAYS (EBS) in *Arabidopsis*, as well as their homologs in other species, read the H3K27me3 mark and form a complex with EMF1 (or an EMF1 homolog) to play PRC1-like roles and thus implement Polycomb silencing in higher plants.

### **Results**

**SHL and EBS interact with EMF1 and its partners.** The plantspecific EMF1 is essential to implement Polycomb silencing at thousands of loci in *Arabidopsis*[5](#page-7-3)[,6,](#page-7-4)[11](#page-7-9). We searched for EMF1 partners from chromatin-associated proteins involved in transcriptional repression by yeast two-hybrid assays and identified the BAHdomain protein SHL as an EMF1 interactor in yeast cells (Fig. [1a](#page-1-0)); moreover, the SHL homolog EBS also was found to interact with EMF1 in yeast (Fig. [1b\)](#page-1-0). SHL and EBS, including the EBS isoform (EBSminor) encoded by minor splice variants (Supplementary Figs. 1 and 2), possess a distinctive modular structure, with the BAH domain followed by a plant homeodomain (PHD) finger, and are found only in plant[s12](#page-7-10). The BAH domain can function as a scaffold for protein–protein interaction[13.](#page-7-11) Indeed, we found that the BAH domain, not the PHD finger, mediated SHL or EBS interaction with EMF1 (Fig. [1a,b\)](#page-1-0). We further conducted bimolecular fluorescence complementation in which EMF1 was fused to nonfluorescent N-terminal EYFP, whereas SHL or EBS was fused to C-terminal EYFP (cEYFP). Both SHL and EBS associated with EMF1 in the nucleus (Fig. [1c\)](#page-1-0). It has been reported that EMF1 can directly interact with A. thaliana (At) BMI1a and At BMI1b, homologs of animal BMI1 (for B lymphoma Mo-MLV Insertion Region 1 homolog), which is a canonical subunit of PRC1 that mediates its oligomerization for chromatin compaction and histone H2A monoubiquitination (H2Aub)<sup>15,16</sup>. We termed the complex containing EMF1 along with SHL or EBS the BAH-EMF1 Polycomb complex (BAH–EMF1c). BAH domains from SHL and EBS specifically bind di- and trimethylated H3K27. In addition to being a scaffold for protein-protein interaction, several BAH domains function as a nucleosome-binding unit13. We explored whether the BAH domains from SHL and EBS, BAH<sub>SHL</sub> and BAH<sub>EBS</sub>, could bind to modified N-terminal tails of histones. Histone H3 peptide pulldown assays were conducted using a recombinant GST-tagged BAH<sub>EBS</sub> (GST:BAH<sub>EBS</sub>) or GST:BAH<sub>SHL</sub>. We observed that only tri- and dimethylated H3K27 peptides, but not monomethylated H3K27, methylated H3K4 or methylated H3K9, were able to pull down GST:BAH<sub>EBS</sub> and GST:BAH<sub>SHL</sub> (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Fig. 4a-c). Thus, BAH<sub>EBS</sub> and BAH<sub>SHL</sub> not only mediate the interaction of SHL and EBS with EMF1, but also read the H3K27 methylation state of target chromatin.

Largely in agreement with previous studies<sup>12,17</sup>, *ebs* and *shl* flowered moderately earlier than wild type, but the *ebs shl* double mutant flowered much earlier (Fig. 3a and Supplementary Fig. 1d). Further *FT* expression analysis showed that it was upregulated only moderately in *ebs* and *shl*, but greatly de-repressed in *ebs shl* (Fig. 3b); hence, *SHL* and *EBS* act redundantly to repress *FT* expression, leading to floral inhibition. *SHL*, *EBS* and *LHP1*, together with *EMF1*, co-regulate genomewide gene expression. In addition to BAH–EMF1c, LHP1 also reads the H3K27me3 mark deposited by PRC2<sup>7,8</sup>. Moreover, LHP1 directly associates with EMF1<sup>14</sup> and appears to form a separate complex, as there was no direct interaction between LHP1 and EBS or SHL (Supplementary Fig. 3). We reasoned that if BAH<sub>SHL</sub>, BAH<sub>EBS</sub> and LHP1 are the readers of H3K27me3 and responsible for interpreting (or effecting) this mark and implementing global Polycomb silencing, the *ebs shl lhp1* triple mutant would exhibit drastic morphologic defects similar to those of *emf1* or loss of PRC2 function, because Polycomb silencing is essential for plant growth and development<sup>2</sup>. Indeed, *ebs shl lhp1*, like *emf1* and a partial loss-of-PRC2function mutant called *emf2* (EMF2 is a homolog of Suppressor of zeste 12, a core PRC2 subunit<sup>11</sup>), displayed the embryonic flower phenotype: mutant seeds germinated directly into a terminal flowerlike organ, skipping vegetative phases (Fig. [3c](#page-3-0)). Furthermore, *ebs shl lhp1* exhibited a stronger phenotype than *emf1* and *emf2*: embryonic calli were produced in strong triple mutants when grown for a few weeks (Fig. [3d](#page-3-0) and Supplementary Fig. 6), which is a common phenotype in mutants with a severe defect in Polycomb silencing[2](#page-7-0),[15,](#page-7-13)[21](#page-7-19).

We further conducted high-throughput sequencing of total mRNAs (RNA-seq) for mRNA extracted from *lhp1*, *ebs shl*, *ebs shl lhp1* and *emf1* plants. Consistent with a weak phenotype in *lhp1*, only 472 genes were identified with an expression change equal to or greater than 1.5-fold (false discovery rate (FDR)<0.05) (Fig. [3d](#page-3-0) and Supplementary Fig. 7). Functional loss of *SHL* and *EBS* gave rise to an expression change in over 1,500 genes, whereas in the *ebs shl lhp1* triple mutant nearly 11,000 genes displayed an expression change of at least 1.5-fold (Fig. [3e](#page-3-0) and Supplementary Table 1), suggesting a functional redundancy between LHP1 and the BAH-domain proteins SHL and EBS. Next we compared the transcriptomes in *emf1* and *ebs shl lhp1*, and observed that 73% of the upregulated genes or 77% of the downregulated ones in *emf1* were also upregulated or downregulated in *ebs shl lhp1*, respectively (Fig. [3e,f\)](#page-3-0). Thus, *SHL*, *EBS* and *LHP1*, together with *EMF1*, co-regulate thousands of genes in *Arabidopsis*.

**SHL and EBS colocalize with EMF1 at over 1,000 loci.** To address whether SHL, EBS and EMF1 indeed co-regulate global gene expression, we first mapped genome-wide occupancy of SHL and EBS using chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing (ChIP-seq). Both *SHL* and *EBS* are constitutively expressed in *Arabidopsis* (Supplementary Fig. 1c[\)12;](#page-7-10) we introduced an *EBS* gene tagged by *FLAG* (*EBS:FLAG*) or *SHL:HA* driven by their respective native promoters into *ebs shl*. Both transgenes rescued the double-mutant phenotypes at seedling stages (Supplementary Fig. 1d). Through ChIP-seq analyses, 2,356 EBS target loci and 1,942 SHL-bound loci were identified at the seedling stage (Fig. [4a](#page-5-0) and Supplementary Fig. 8). Sixty percent of the SHL target loci (1,159 loci) were also targeted by EBS (Fig. [4a](#page-5-0) and Supplementary Table 2), consistent with the>70% similarit[y12](#page-7-10) and partial functional redundancy of these two proteins.

Next we compared SHL and EBS occupancy profiles with published data of genome-wide EMF1 occupancy[6](#page-7-4) . A total of 1,293 loci (41%) targeted by EBS and/or SHL were also EMF1-bound loci (Fig. [4a\)](#page-5-0), corroborating the model that SHL or EBS forms a complex with EMF1 to regulate genome-wide gene expression. In addition, compared against a published global analysis of H3K27me3-marked regions[22,](#page-7-20) 751 loci enriched with H3K27me3 at the seedling stage were identified to be SHL and/or EBS target loci despite of differences in plant growth conditions, experimental samples and procedures, and ChIP analysis (Fig. [4a](#page-5-0)). PRC2-mediated trimethylation of H3K27 at Polycomb target loci and H3K27 demethylation are dynamic, occurring in response to developmental and environmental cues in plant[s2](#page-7-0)[,4,](#page-7-2)[23](#page-7-21)[–25](#page-7-22). There may be more EBS or SHL loci that possess the H3K27me3 mark depending on developmental stages and environmental conditions examined. In short, these results are consistent with a proposed major function of SHL and EBS being to read H3K27me3 at target loci in Polycomb repression. Notably, a number of EBS or SHL target loci, particularly a large portion of EBS loci, appear not to be bound by EMF1 or marked with H3K27me3 (Fig. [4a](#page-5-0)); hence, both proteins play a role in H3K27me3- or EMF1 independent transcriptional regulation.

Previously, it has been shown that EMF1 is enriched mainly in gene bodie[s6](#page-7-4) . We sought to determine the peak distributions of SHL and EBS over target transcription units and flanking intergenic regions. Both SHL and EBS, like EMF1, were enriched in gene bodies from the transcription start site (TSS) to regions near the transcription termination site (Fig. [4c\)](#page-5-0); the gene-body enrichment of SHL and EBS is similar to the distribution pattern of H3K27me3 (Fig. [4c](#page-5-0))[22](#page-7-20). Notably, EBS exhibited a higher occupancy for regions near the TSS, whereas SHL was more prevalent in middle of gene bodies (Fig. [4c\)](#page-5-0); in addition, EMF1 and EBS displayed a higher occupancy at regions near the TSS (Fig. [4c](#page-5-0)). These results suggest that the EBS or SHL BAH–EMF1c mainly binds to gene body chromatin.

**SHL and EBS bind to H3K27me3-marked loci.** We selected four loci co-occupied by EBS, SHL, EMF1 and/or H3K27me3, each with a distinct combination, and further validated the enrichments of EBS and/or SHL at these four loci using ChIP and quantitative PCR (qPCR) (Fig. [5b\)](#page-6-1). Consistent with the idea that SHL and EBS act redundantly to repress *FT* expression, we confirmed that both proteins bind to *FT* chromatin in gene-body regions marked with H3K27me3 (Supplementary Fig. 9a,c); in addition, we observed that SHL and EBS were enriched in the gene body of *SOC1* (Supplementary Fig. 9b,d,e). We further found that knockout of the gene encoding a principal H3K27 methyltransferase, *CURLY LEAF* (*CLF*)[2](#page-7-0) , caused a great reduction in EBS and/or SHL enrichment at the late seedling stage at *FT*, *SOC1* and three selected H3K27me3 bearing loci, *At1G02065*, *At2G02450* and *At1G79430*, but not *At4G36040*, which lacks H3K27me3 (Supplementary Fig. 10). This suggests that CLF (PRC2) plays a critical role in binding of EBS and SHL to target chromatin at H3K27me3-bearing loci.

**SHL and EBS function mainly as transcriptional repressors.** To confirm that *SHL* and *EBS* act mainly as transcriptional repressors, we examined the overlap of the genes that were misregulated in *ebs shl* with SHL and/or EBS target loci at a seedling stage. 1,054 and 461 genes were up- and downregulated, respectively, in the *ebs shl* seedling compared with wild type (Fig. [3e](#page-3-0)). 201 upregulated genes were found to be SHL and/or EBS targets, and there were statistically significant overlaps with the SHL (*P*=3.7×10<sup>−</sup>26) or EBS (*P*=4.9×10<sup>−</sup><sup>5</sup> ) target loci (Fig. [4b\)](#page-5-0). These RNA-seq data were validated at selected genes using RT (reverse transcription) qPCR (Supplementary Fig. 7b). In contrast, the overlap of the downregulated genes in *ebs shl* with EBS or SHL targets appeared to be insignificant (Supplementary Fig. 11). In addition, consistent with the occupancy of SHL and/or EBS at *At1G02065*, *At2G02450*, *At1G79430* and *At4G36040*, these genes were de-repressed in *shl*, *ebs* and/or *ebs shl* accordingly (Fig. [5c\)](#page-6-1). Taken together, these results are consistent with SHL and EBS functioning mainly as transcriptional repressors.

The levels of H3K27me3 in total histones from *lhp1*, *ebs shl* and *ebs shl lhp1* were measured by immunoblotting. Recent studies in plants and mammals indicate that PRC1 can drive the recruitment of PRC2 to target loci<sup>3,4,10,27,28</sup>. Notably, EMF1 has been reported to copurify with PRC2 from seedlings<sup>29</sup>; hence, it is possible that BAH–EMF1c may engage PRC2 to extend H3K27 trimethylation beyond its docking site or re-establish this mark on newly replicated chromatin through a positive feedback loop. Consistent with this notion that BAH–EMF1c recruits PRC2, we observed that the levels of H3K27me1 and H3K27me2, both of which are at least partly produced by PRC2 in plants<sup>30</sup>, were reduced upon loss of *SHL*, *EBS* and *LHP1* function (Fig. 5d). These are in line with SHL and EBS (BAH–EMF1c) playing a PRC1-like role, reading and maintaining the H3K27me3 mark and engaging PRC2 to re-establish it on newly replicated chromatin in the *Arabidopsis* genome.

In addition to reading H3K27me3, another activity of PRC1 is to add the H2Aub mark to target chromatin<sup>2,3</sup>. SHL and EBS, like EMF1 and LHP1, associate with At BMI1s that presumably form heterodimers with At RING1s to mediate H2A monoubiquitination in the *Arabidopsis* genome<sup>4,14,15,21</sup>. We observed a reduction of H2Aub-marked histones in *ebs shl lhp1* (Fig. 5d). Notably, At BMI1s and At RING1s were well expressed in the *ebs shl lhp1* triple mutant (Supplementary Fig. 12c). Together, these results indicate that *SHL* and *EBS* function together with *LHP1* to partly mediate global H2A monoubiquitination.

SHL and EBS regulate a set of loci bearing H3K27me3, H3K4me2 and/or H3K4me3. Recently it has been reported that both SHL and EBS can directly interact with two histone deacetylases in vitro<sup>12</sup>. Although whether these proteins are part of a complex in *Arabidopsis* has yet to be determined, it is likely that BAH–EMF1c may act in concert with histone deacetylases to mediate transcriptional repression at certain Polycomb target loci. In plants, active genes can be switched off through Polycomb silencing in response to developmental or environmental cues<sup>4,24,31</sup>, and histone deacetylases appear to facilitate such silencing via removal of the active acetylation mark (especially at H3K27) on target chromatin<sup>31</sup>. The PHD fingers from SHL and EBS have been shown to bind di- and trimethylated H3K4 in vitro and are proposed help switch off actively expressed genes<sup>12</sup>. We confirmed that SHL and EBS, through the PHD finger, bound to H3K4me2 and H3K4me3 peptides (Supplementary Fig. 4e-g). However, neither SHL nor EBS appears to be involved in the maintenance of global di- and trimethylation of H3K4 in the Arabidopsis genome (Fig. 5d). Previously, it has been shown that there are hundreds of loci (a total of 510) bearing H3K27me3, H3K4me2 and/or H3K4me3 at the seedling stage<sup>32</sup>. There is a significant overlap between these H3K4me2 or me3-H3K27me3 loci and SHL and/or EBS target loci: 204 out of the 510 loci are bound by SHL and/or EBS at a seedling stage (Supplementary Fig. 11c). Further examination of three EBS and SHL loci bearing H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 showed that an H3K4 methyltransferase, ARABIDOPSIS TRITHORAX-LIKE PROTEIN133, was required for EBS and SHL enrichment at these loci to mediate their repression (Supplementary Fig. 13), raising a possibility that EBS and SHL may bind to methylated H3K4 at certain bivalent loci to repress their expression.

### Discussion

We have identified a key component of Polycomb silencing in plants: H3K27me3 readers and effectors. SHL and EBS, together with LHP1, seem to function as the counterpart of the animal Pc protein to read the silencing hallmark H3K27me3. We show that SHL and EBS colocalize with EMF1 at over 1,000 loci and that BAH–EMF1c functions as a key effector to implement genome-wide Polycomb silencing. BAH–EMF1c constitutes a Polycomb group complex with PRC1-like activities, including binding and maintaining (and propagating) H3K27me3 and implementing chromatin compaction, which in animals are carried out by the canonical PRC1 components, including Pc and the PSC-CTR-domain proteins. Thus, although plants lack homologs of both Pc and the PSC-CTR proteins, BAH–EMF1c is tasked to fulfill the roles played by both proteins in Polycomb-mediated gene silencing.

**Processing of ChIP-seq and RNA-seq data.** Around 20 million raw paired-end reads were obtained for each sample and subsequently cleaned by Trimmomati[c47](#page-9-11). After trimming the adaptor sequences, removing low-quality bases and filtering short reads (<36 nt), clear read pairs were retained for further analysis.

To analyze ChIP-seq data, first, clean reads from input DNA and EBS:FLAG or SHL:HA ChIP samples were mapped to the *A. thaliana* genome (TAIR10) by Bowtie2 (version 2.2.8) with default parameter[s48;](#page-9-12) mapped reads with low mapping quality (MAPQ<30) or multiple positions on the genome were identified and removed by SAMtools (version 1.3)[49.](#page-9-13) Enriched peaks were identified by MACS (version 1.4) with cut-off *P* value 10<sup>−</sup><sup>2</sup> and without building the shifting mode[l50](#page-9-14). We defined the region of a target gene as the range from 1.0 kb upstream of the transcription start site (TSS) to the transcription termination site (TTS). Target regions were defined by overlap of the peaks from the two biological replicates in the ChIP-seq analysis of SHL:HA sites or from at least two of the three independent EBS:FLAG lines in the analysis of EBS sites. Each peak at a target gene was annotated by the annotatePeak function in ChIPseeker package[51.](#page-9-15)

The read coverage was normalized to 1×sequencing depth (also known as reads per genomic content, RPGC) by bamCoverage in deepTools (version 2.4.1[\)52,](#page-9-16) and RPM (reads per million) were generated by genomecov in BEDTools (version 2.25.0[\)53](#page-9-17). Visualization of the average read coverage (RPGC) over a target gene body, 1.0 kb upstream of TSS and 1.0 kb downstream of TTS was performed by plotProfile in deepTools. The read coverage (RPM) of individual genes was visualized with the Gviz packag[e54](#page-9-18). Statistical testing of the overlap between two gene lists was performed by the GeneOverlap package (see URLs).

To analyze RNA-seq data, cleaned reads first were mapped to the *Arabidopsis* genome (TAIR10) by TopHat (version 2.1.1) with default parameters[55](#page-9-19). Total number of reads mapped to each gene was calculated with the htseq-count script in HTSeq[56](#page-9-20). Genes with a normalized expression level of at least one count per million mapped reads (CPM) in three or more libraries were kept for

further analysis. EdgeR[57](#page-9-21) was used to identify genes that were differentially expressed between wild type and mutants. Genes with at least 1.5-fold change in expression (and FDR<0.05) were considered differentially expressed genes (DEGs).

**Phylogenetic analysis.** The BAH domain sequences were retrieved from NCBI by Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLAST) using the BAHEBS sequence as a query. Sequences were first aligned with Clustal W; phylogenetic analysis was conducted with MEGA7 software (see URLs) with the maximum likelihood method (JTT matrix-based model[\)58](#page-9-22). Bootstrap probabilities were conducted for 1,000 replications and the cutoff value for a condensed tree is 30%.

**Statistical analyses.** Box plots were generated with the BoxPlotR program (R package version 0.5.0; see URLs). Two-tailed *t* test was conducted using Excel, and one-way ANOVA was carried out with log-transformed flowering-time data using SigmaStat 3.5. Statistical significance of gene overlap was analyzed by Fisher's exact test using the GeneOverlap package (see URLs).
