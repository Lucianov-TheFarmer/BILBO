### Summary

- Cellular responses to oxygen deprivation are essential for survival during energy crises in plants and animals. Hypoxia caused by submergence results in reprogramming of translation dynamic in plants, but the molecular mechanisms are not well understood. Here we show that *Arabidopsis* Snf1-related protein kinase 1 (SnRK1) phosphorylates the translation initiation factor elFiso4G to regulate translation dynamic under submergence.
  - In Arabidopsis, there are two *elFiso4G* genes, *elFiso4G1* and *elFiso4G2*, which belong to the *elF4G* family. Both elFiso4Gs were phosphorylated by SnRK1 under submergence. Interestingly, the *elFiso4G1* knockout mutant, but not the *elFiso4G2* mutant, became more sensitive to submergence, implying that elFiso4G1 is involved in regulating submergence tolerance in Arabidopsis.
- Comparison of RNA sequences in the polysome fraction and the RNAs immunoprecipited by eIFiso4G1 from Col-0 and the SnRK1 and eIFiso4G1 mutants

revealed that lack of eIFiso4G1 phosphorylation disrupts the translation of specific mRNAs under submergence.

Taken together, our findings suggest that the SnRK1-elFiso4G1 relay controls the translation of an array of genes under hypoxia, including core hypoxia response genes and genes related to stress response and biosynthetic process.

Keywords: Submergence, SnRK1, elFiso4G1, Phosphorylation, Translation

#### Introduction

Hypoxia is a stress commonly encountered by animals and plants. Organisms in both kingdoms have evolved adaptive responses that allow survival under prolonged hypoxia. One major obstacle faced by most organisms in the early stages of hypoxia is an energy crisis due to blockage of the mitochondria electron transport chain (mETC). During this energy crisis, organisms adjust their cellular responses to conserve energy consumption and also adopt alternative energy production pathways. In plant cells, one mechanism that has been proposed to conserve energy consumption under hypoxia is the reduction of polyribosome (polysome) complex formation, or the loading of multiple ribosomes on single mRNAs, such as high abundance mRNAs encoding ribosomal proteins (Branco-Price et al., 2005; Branco-Price et al., 2008; Juntawong et al., 2013; Sorenson and Bailey-Serres, 2014; van Dongen and Licausi, 2015). It has been reported that the majority of the translationally impaired mRNAs are stabilized in non-polysome complexes and are rapidly recruited into

polysome complexes after the removal of oxygen deprivation (Sorenson and Bailey-Serres, 2014).

Protein synthesis is principally regulated at the translation initiation stage to allow rapid, reversible and spatial control (Jackson et al., 2010; Juntawong et al., 2013). In animals, translation initiation is generally repressed by disruption of the eIF4F complex under hypoxia (Wouters and Koritzinsky 2008; Jackson et al., 2010). The eIF4G protein in the eIF4F complex is replaced by the translation inhibitors, eIF4E-binding proteins (4E-BPs), to form the eIF4E and 4E-BP complex (Peter et al., 2015). However, a subset of mRNAs related to cell survival can bypass translational repression under hypoxia (Young et al., 2008; Spriggs et al., 2010; Uniacke et al., 2012 ). The 5' and 3' untranslated regions (UTRs) of these mRNAs and the proteins that recognize these regions determine the translational efficiency (Knaup, et al., 2009; Juntawong et al., 2013; Spriggs et al., 2010; van den Beucken et al., 2014; Browning and Bailey-Serres, 2015).

In higher plants, the regulation of translation initiation is more complicated. In Arabidopsis, there are two *elFiso4G* genes, *elFiso4G1* and *elFiso4G2*, and one *elF4G* gene (Patrick and Browning, 2012; Gallie, 2016). The elF4G protein is a subunit of the elF4F complex, which also contains elF4A and elF4E. The elFiso4G protein lacks an N terminal sequence found in elF4G, but has two HEAT domains that mediate binding to elFiso4E and 4A to form the smaller yet more abundant elFiso4F complex (Cheng and Gallie, 2007). Both types of elF4Fs are associated with resistance to different viruses (Sanfacon, 2015). Mutations of the two *elFiso4G* s negatively affect plant health and reduce protein synthesis (Lellis et al., 2010). Although elF4F and elFiso4F in plants both direct the translation of unstructured mRNAs,

they have distinct mRNA preferences (Gallie and Browning, 2001). eIF4F was found to support the translation of mRNAs that have a structured 5' UTR and lack a cap or contain multiple cistrons, whereas eIFiso4F prefers unstructured mRNAs or mRNAs with simple structures, which is also a feature of the mRNAs that are translated under hypoxia (Branco-Price et al., 2005). These findings suggest that eIFiso4Gs might be required to bypass translation repression under submergence.

In our previous study (Cho et al., 2016), we showed that phosphorylation of SnRK1 occurs during the early stages of submergence in Arabidopsis. SnRK1 was proposed as a signal hub that regulates transcription and translation to control stress responses and development (Baena-Gonzalez et al., 2007; Polge and Thomas, 2007). SnRK1 forms a complex consisting of  $\alpha$ ,  $\beta$ , and  $\gamma$  or  $\beta\gamma$  subunits to phosphorylate downstream targets (Polge and Thomas, 2007). In Arabidopsis there are two isoforms of the  $\alpha$  subunits, SnRK1.1 and SnRK1.2, which have kinase activity. The conserved K48 and K29 residues in SnRK1.1 and SnRK1.2 are essential for kinase activity (Baena-Gonzalez et al., 2007). By quantifying phosphoproteomic data for Col-0 and *SnRK1K48M*, a dominant negative mutant of *SnRK1.1*, under submergence, we found that the phosphorylation of three translation initiation factors is SnRK1-dependent (Cho et al., 2016). One of these factors, elFiso4G1, is also a direct target of SnRK1.1.

In this study, we showed that elFiso4G1 is required for submergence tolerance and the SnRK1-mediated translation of specific mRNAs, including those of the well-known hypoxia responsive genes *ADH1* and *SUS1*, under submergence. SnRK1.1-elFiso4G1 does not significantly affect polysome loading for the majority of mRNAs under submergence. By

comparing translation dynamics in the wild-type, *SnRK1K48M* and the *elFiso4G1* mutants, we found that the SnRK1-elFiso4G1 relay modulates the translational status of specific mRNAs that are mainly involved in energy balance and stress stimulation. The phosphorylation of four amino acid residues of elFiso4G1by SnRK1.1 is critical for recognition of the 5' UTRs of *ADH1* and *SUS1*. Taken together, our study revealed a novel SnRK1-elFiso4G1 signaling relay that mediates the translation of specific mRNAs, thus conferring submergence tolerance.

#### **RNA extraction and polysome fractionation**

Nine-day-old seedlings subjected to normoxia or submergence were harvested to extract RNA. Total RNA and RNA loaded on polysomes were isolated and quantified as described in Liu et al (2012). Briefly, frozen tissues were homogenized and extracted with polysome extraction (PE) buffer (200 mM Tris-HCl buffer pH 8, 50 mM KCl, 25 mM MgCl<sub>2</sub>, 50 µg/ml cycloheximide, 400 U/ml RNasin) with 1/500 (v/v) protease inhibitor cocktail (Sigma P9599), 2% polyoxyethylene 10 tridecyl ether (PTE) and 1% sodium deoxycholate (DOC). The mixture was spun at 15000g for 5 min at 4°C. The supernatant was loaded onto a 10 ml continuous sucrose gradient (15-50%) and spun at 210000g for 3.5 h at 4°C. Based on the RNA distribution determined by examining a UV<sub>254</sub> absorbance profile (model# UV-6, ISCO), the RNA was separated into three fractions using a pipette (from top to bottom): polysome free (NP) RNA (5 ml), light polysome-bound RNA (LP) (3 ml), and heavy polysome-bound RNA (HP) (2.5 ml). To normalize the efficiency of RNA purification and reverse transcription, synthetic DAP1 mRNA (GeneChip Eukaryotic Poly-A RNA Control Kit, Ambion, Foster City, CA) was added into the sucrose gradient fractions (final concentration in samples: 1:8,000). The total amount of mRNA in the three fractions was defined as 100%. Percentage of This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved.

#### **Quantitative PCR (qPCR)**

Reverse transcription and qRT-PCR were performed as described (Hsu et al., 2013). The primers for the genes analysed are listed in Table S2. The relative expression levels were calculated as the ratio between the mRNA levels in the sample at specific time points and the levels in the sample at time zero. For quantification of mRNA levels in the polysome fraction, *DAP1* was used an internal control. The  $\Delta$ Ct (threshold cycle) of each fraction was calculated as follows:  $\Delta$ Ct of NP = Ct (NP) - Ct (DAP). The PL percentage for each fraction was calculated as follows:  $2^{\Delta Ct}$  for PL/ $2^{\Delta Ct}$  for NP+LP+ HP, where the sum of  $2^{\Delta Ct}$  for NP, LP, and HP was defined as 100%.

#### Results

#### elFiso4Gs are substrates of SnRK1 in vitro

We previously reported that SnRK1-mediated signaling is triggered in the early stages of submergence in Arabidopsis (Cho et al., 2016). Through comparative phosphoproteomics analysis of Col-0 and *SnRK1K48M*, we identified three translation initiation factors, eIFiso4G1, eIF4A2 and eIF3B2, whose phosphorylation levels increased in Col-0 under submergence but remained low in *SnRK1K48M* (Table S3 and Cho et al., 2016). As SnRK1 was proposed to be involved in translational regulation (Baena-Gonzalez et al., 2007; van Dongen and Licausi, 2015), we decided to examine whether SnRK1 regulates translation by phosphorylating these translation initiation factors during submergence.

In Arabidopsis there are two elFiso4G isoforms, elFiso4G1 and elFiso4G2, which belong to the plant elF4G family (Patrick and Browning, 2012; Gallie, 2016). We examined the interaction between SnRK1.1 and the elFiso4Gs by performing BiFC experiments. nYFP-SnRK1.1 and cYFP-elFiso4G1 or cYFP-elFiso4G2 were transiently co-expressed in protoplasts to test the interaction between SnRK1.1 and each elFiso4G protein. The C-terminal region of *Agobacterium tumefaciens* VirD2 containing the bipartite nuclear localization signal (NLS) fused with mCherry was used as a nuclear marker and transient expression control (Wu et al., 2009). nYFP-SnRK1.1 and vector 3130 (pSAT-35S::cYFP-DEST) and cYFP-elFiso4G1 or cYFP-elFiso4G2 and vector 3136 (pSAT-35S::nYFP-DEST) were transfected together into protoplasts as negative controls. These assays demonstrated that SnRK1.1 interacts with both elFiso4Gs in the cytoplasm (Fig. 1a). In contrast, SnRK1.1 did not interact with elF4G in the BiFC assays (Fig. S2).

Next, we generated the recombinant proteins eIF4A2, eIF3B2, eIFiso4G1, and eIFiso4G2 and conducted *in vitro* kinase assays to assess whether they are direct targets of SnRK1. These assays showed that eIFiso4G1 and eIFiso4G2 (Fig. 1b and 1c), but not eIF4A2 and eIF3B2 (Fig. 1d and 1e), could be phosphorylated by SnRK1 *in vitro*.

#### elFiso4G1 is required for submergence adaptation

To investigate the role of the elFiso4Gs under submergence, we analyzed the T-DNA knockout mutants *elFiso4G1* (*i4g1-1* and *i4g1-2*) and *elFiso4G2* (*i4g2*) (Fig. 2a), the *elFiso4G1* and *elFiso4G2* (*i4g1-1/i4g2*) double mutant, and cMyc-tagged elFiso4G1 overexpression lines (*OE4* and *OE9*) (Fig. S3a). Loss of *elFiso4G1* did not disturb the This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved.

expression of *elFiso4G2* and vice versa (Fig. S3a). These lines were subjected to submergence and anoxia treatments in the dark. The assays revealed that *i4g1-1*, *i4g1-2* and *i4g1-1/i4g2* are more sensitive to oxygen deprivation than Col-0 (Fig. 2b and Fig. S3b, S3c). The *OE4* and *OE9* lines and Col-0 had similar degrees of submergence tolerance. (Fig. 2b). However, the sensitivity of *i4g2* to submergence was not significantly different from that of Col-0 (Fig. 2b). We also examined ADH1 accumulation in these mutants under submergence. The level of ADH1 protein was much lower in the *elFiso4g1* mutants (Fig. 2c) and in the *i4g1-1/i4g2* double mutant (Fig. S3d) than in Col-0 under submergence but was not significantly reduced in *i4g2* and the two OE lines (Fig. S3d). These results demonstrate that the elFiso4G1 is critical for hypoxia tolerance. These results, together with the finding that *SnRK1K48M* displays sensitivity to submergence and accumulates more AMP than Col-0 under submergence (Cho et al., 2016), raise the possibility that elFiso4G1 links translational control and SnRK1-mediated signaling under submergence.

#### The SnRK1-elFiso4G1 interaction is enhanced during submergence

To characterize the interaction between SnRK1.1 and elFiso4G1 during normoxia and submergence, we examined the co-fractionation of SnRK1.1 and elFiso4G1 with polysomes in sucrose density gradients. SnRK1.1 was mainly detected in the less dense ribosome-free fraction under normoxia (Fig. 3a, panel 1), but the amount of SnRK1.1 in the polysome increased under submergence (Fig. 3a, panel 2). In contrast, the amount of elFiso4G1 in the polysome fraction was not significantly different under normoxia and submergence (Fig. 3a, panels 3 and 4), suggesting that SnRK1.1 is involved in regulating translation under This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved.

submergence. To examine this possibility, we pulled down the elFiso4G1 complex using anti-cMyc antibodies and the SnRK1.1 complex using anti-SnRK1.1 antibodies from extracts of *OE9* seedlings subjected to submergence and normoxia. In both assays, the SnRK1.1-elFiso4G1 interaction was stronger under submergence than under normoxia (Fig. 3b and Fig. S3f), demonstrating that the interaction between SnRK1.1 and elFiso4G1 was enhanced under submergence. Interestingly, *elFiso4G1* and *elFiso4G2* transcript levels and abundances of the encoded proteins in Col-0 and *SnRK1K48M* were similar under submergence (Fig. S4 and Table S3). Taken together, these results indicate that SnRK1.1 might phosphorylate elFiso4G proteins under submergence.

### The residues of elFiso4G1 recognized by SnRK1 are conserved in the Brassicaceae

To determine whether SnRK1.1 directly phosphorylates elFiso4G1, the phosphorylated wild-type elFiso4G1 protein from the *in vitro* SnRK1.1 kinase assay (Fig. 1) was analyzed by LC-MS/MS (Table S4). This analysis revealed that three residues, S123, S125, and S560, of elFiso4G1 were phosphorylated (Fig. 4a), and S542, S123 and S125 were also found to be phosphorylated *in vivo* (Cho et al., 2016). By combining these results, we identified four serine residues in elFiso4G1 that are potential SnRK1.1target sites (Fig. 4a). To test whether these four residues could be phosphorylated by SnRK1.1, we generated different S/A elFiso4G1 mutants in which the serine residues were changed to alanine (S/A) (Fig. 4b and c). Hyper phosphorylation by SnRK1.1 could still be detected in the S/A single mutant and S/A double mutant elFiso4G1 proteins, but not in the S123A/S125A/S542A and S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A mutant proteins (Fig. 4b). Thus, these four serine sites are This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved.

phosphorylated by SnRK1.1. Notably, the amino acid sequence close to S123 and S560 matches the SnRK1 consensus recognition sequence,  $\phi$  XXXX**S/T**XXX $\phi$ , where  $\phi$  is a hydrophobic residue (M, L, V, I or F) and X represents a random amino acid residue (Halford and Hardie, 1998) (Fig. 4).

A comparison of Arabidopsis elFiso4G1 with orthologs from several other species ranging from mosses to higher plants showed that the residue elFiso4G1<sup>S560</sup> in the SnRK1.1 target site has been highly conserved throughout evolution (Fig 4a and Methods S6), and the conserved sequence is also identified in elFiso4G2<sup>S524</sup> (Fig. S5a). We conducted *in vitro* kinase assays and found that the mutant protein elFiso4G2<sup>S524A</sup> is still phosphorylated by SnRK1.1 (Fig. S5b). On the other hand, the consensus sequences flanking the other three target sites, elFiso4G1<sup>S123</sup>, elFiso4G1<sup>S125</sup> and elFiso4G1<sup>S542</sup>, are only conserved in the Brassicaceae (Fig. 4a), suggesting that there is a distinct mechanism for SnRK1.1-mediated regulation of elFiso4G1 in the Brassicaceae.

#### Deficiency of elFiso4G1 causes altered distribution of mRNAs in polysomal fractions

The elFiso4G1 protein is a subunit of the elFiso4F complex, which facilitates the translation of mRNAs with simple structures, which are proposed to be preferentially translated during oxygen deprivation (Gallie and Browning, 2001; Branco-Price et al., 2005). This raises the possibility that SnRK1.1 regulates translation dynamics under submergence by phosphorylating elFiso4G1. To examine this possibility, we separated total mRNAs from Col-0, *SnRK1K48M* and *i4g1-2* into polysome-free (NP), light polysome (LP) and heavy polysome (HP) fractions. However, when compared to each other, there was no significant difference in ribosome loading in the HP fraction between Col-0 and *SnRK1K48M* and between Col-0 and *i4g1-2* under either normoxia or submergence (Fig. 5b), indicating that SnRK1 and elFiso4G1 are not involved in the reduction of polysome loading of total cellular mRNAs.

Interestingly, compared to the wild-type, the distribution of mRNAs of four hypoxia marker genes in polysome fractions was altered in SnRK1K48M and i4g1-2 (Fig. 5c). In Col-0, the ADH1 mRNA was enriched in the LP fraction under submergence, and the levels of the PDC1, SUS1, and SUS4 mRNAs in the HP increased under submergence, suggesting that the translation of ADH1 was delayed or that the translational efficiency was lower than that of the other three genes under submergence. Notably, for all four marker genes, mRNA loading on the polysome was significantly lower in SnRK1K48M and i4q1-2 than in Col-0 under submergence. Under normoxia, there was no significant difference in polysome loading of PDC1, SUS1, and SUS4 mRNAs between Col-0 and SnRK1K48M, but the level of HP loading of ADH1 mRNA was higher in SnRK1K48M than in Col-0 (Fig. 5c). In contrast, the HP loading of PDC1 and SUS4 mRNAs was significantly lower in *i4g1-2* than in Col-0, but the HP loading of ADH1 and SUS1 mRNAs was the same as in Col-0. Excluding ADH1, which was more lowly expressed in Col-0 than in SnRK1K48M under submergence, the induction of the marker gene mRNAs was not lower in the SnRK1K48M and i4g1-2 than in Col-0 (Fig. S6). These results indicate that the SnRK1.1-elFiso4G1 relay regulates the translational efficiency of ADH1 and SUS1 under submergence. However, the results in Fig. 5c is not

sufficient to determine whether elFiso4G1 could modulate the translation efficiency of *PDC1* and *SUS4* mRNAs under normoxia only or under both normoxia and submergence.

# Phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 is essential for SnRK1-regulated translation under submergence

To investigate whether the phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 by SnRK1.1 is required for the translation of specific mRNAs under submergence, we transformed elFiso4G1<sup>S123A/S560A</sup> into *i4q1-2* to generate the transgenic lines 2SA-8, 2SA-12 and 2SA-14, and elFiso4G1<sup>S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A</sup> into *i4g1-2* to generate the transgenic lines *4SA-15* and *4SA-16* (Fig. S7a). Interestingly, lower levels of the ADH1 protein accumulated in *i4g1-1*, *4SA-15*, and 4SA-16 than in Col-0 under submergence (Fig. 6a), but the level of ADH1 protein in 2SA-12 and 2SA-14 was not significantly different from that in Col-0 under submergence (Fig. S7b). In addition, both the 4SA-15 and 4SA-16 lines showed higher sensitivity to submergence than Col-0 (Fig. 6b). The increased sensitivity of the 4SA-15 and 4SA-16 lines to submergence might be caused by either misfolding resulting in an unstable S/A elFiso4G1 protein or the loss of regulation by SnRK1 during submergence. We also found that the level of the elFiso4G1<sup>S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A</sup> protein remained unchanged during submergence (Fig. 6c), which is consistent with the observation based on our proteomic results that the abundance of wild-type elFiso4G1 does not change during submergence (Cho et al., 2016). We performed IP-MS and found that elFiso4G1<sup>S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A</sup> is still able to form the elFiso4F complex (Gallie et al., 2001) under normoxia and submergence (Fig. S8 and Table S5), indicating that phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 does not affect elFiso4F formation. To analyze the effects of phosphorylated elFiso4G1 on translation under submergence, we used sucrose density gradient centrifugation to separate total mRNAs from Col-0, *OE4* and *OE9*, and *4SA-15* and *4SA-16* into three fractions. The level of mRNA induction of the four marker genes under submergence did not differ among these lines (Fig. S9). Under normoxia, the mRNA levels of the four marker genes in the HP fraction in *OE4* and *OE9* were enhanced compared with Col-0, and these high levels were maintained under submergence (Fig. 6d), indicating that overexpression of wild-type elFiso4G1 in *i4g1-2* can restore the loss of *elFiso4G1* under submergence.

In *4SA-15* and *4SA-16*, the levels of *ADH1* mRNA in the LP and HP fractions were not significantly different than those in Col-0 under normoxia, but the proportion of *PDC1*, *SUS1*, and *SUS4* mRNAs in both the LP and HP (total polysome) fractions in *4SA-15* or *4SA-16* was enhanced under normoxia, indicating that a higher dosage of eIFiso4G1<sup>S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A</sup> could still enhance the mRNA translation of specific genes under normoxia. In contrast, the total polysome proportion of *ADH1*, *SUS1*, and *SUS4* mRNAs under submergence was lower in *4SA-15* and *4SA-16* than in *Col-0*. Although the total polysome proportion of *PDC1* in *4SA-15* and *4SA-16* was not significantly different from that in Col-0 under submergence, the level of *PDC1* in the HP fraction was lower than that in Col-0 (Fig. 6d). These results indicate that the mutations in eIFiso4G1<sup>S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A</sup> disrupted but did not completely abolish the translation of the mRNAs of the four marker genes under submergence.

To profile the mRNAs being loaded onto the polysome, we conducted deep sequencing of the total mRNA (mRNA<sub>total</sub>) and mRNA in the HP fraction (mRNA<sub>HP</sub>) from Col-0 (W) and three mutant lines SnRK1K48M, i4g1-2, and 4SA-15 under normoxia or 3 h submergence (Fig. 7 and Table S6). We determined the log of the fold-change (FC) for each gene by calculating the FPKM ratio between submergence and normoxia samples (see Materials and Methods for details). There were 8391 genes with a significant difference in FC in mRNA<sub>total</sub> (P < 0.05, CV < 0.5,  $|log_2FC| \ge 1$ ) or mRNA<sub>HP</sub> (P < 0.05, CV < 0.5,  $|log_2FC| \ge 0.6$ ) in at least one of the four lines. Among these 8391 genes, the mRNA<sub>total</sub> and mRNA<sub>HP</sub> of 1223 genes were up-regulated in Col-0 under submergence and translational status (TS: mRNA<sub>HP</sub>/ mRNA<sub>total</sub>) for 1078 of the 1223 genes was  $\geq \log_2$ -0.6. This indicates that the mRNAs of these hypoxia up-regulated genes could bypass the translational repression under submergence (Fig. 7a). Interestingly, the levels of mRNA<sub>HP</sub> for 115 of these 1078 genes passed (P<0.06, |log<sub>2</sub>FC of Col-0/mutant| $\geq$  0.5), and the levels of mRNA<sub>total</sub> in the mutants and Col-0 did not significantly differ (Fig. 7a and Fig. S10a), indicating that translation of these mRNAs during submergence was regulated by the SnRK1.1-elFiso4G1 relay. These 115 genes include 14 core hypoxia genes (Mustroph et al., 2009; Lee et al., 2011), such as ADH1, LBD41 and NIP2;1 (Fig. 7a and b), and were involved in stress response (FDR: 6.70E-06) and biosynthetic process (FDR:0.021) (Table S6).

To identify additional genes whose translation is up-regulated by SnRK1.1- eIFiso4G1 under submergence, we analyzed the translational status (TS) of 8391 genes and found 538 genes which showed significant differences in TS between normoxia (WC) and submergence (WS) in Col-0 (P < 0.05,  $\log_2 TS$  (WS/WC)  $\ge 0.5$  or  $\le -0.7$ ) (Table S6). Among these 538 genes, the TS of 21 genes was upregulated, indicating that their translational efficiency is enhanced under submergence. Notably, the TS of 18 of the 21 genes, including *WRKY16* and *Phosphopantetheinyl transferase (PPT)*, which are involved in immunity response and mitochondrial fatty acid biosynthesis, respectively (Saucet et al., 2015; Guan et al., 2015), was not significantly different in *SnRK1K48M, i4g1-2* and *4SA-15* compared with Col-0 (Fig. 7c). The combined analysis of TS and translational efficiency (Fig. 7b and 7c) shows that the transcripts of 133 genes, designated TUR (for Translation Up-Regulated), had up-regulated TS or were enriched in mRNA<sub>HP</sub> in lines where SnRK1.1 or eIFiso4G1 function was disrupted, indicating that SnRK1-eIFiso4G1 relay regulates translation of these genes under submergence.

To further examine the function of SnRK1-elFiso4G1 in translation regulation during submergence, mRNA loading of several TUR genes under normoxia and submergence was evaluated in transgenic plants expressing c-Myc epitope-tagged elFiso4G1 (*OE9*) and *elFiso4G1*<sup>S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A</sup> (*4SA-15*) in RIP experiments. The mRNA levels of these genes were quantified under normoxia and submergence by RIP-qPCR (Fig. 7d). There was no difference in the amount of *TUBULIN* mRNA, which was used as a negative control, in the RIPs of *OE9* or *4SA-15* under normoxia and submergence. In contrast, the precipitated mRNAs for six of the seven TUR genes were enriched under submergence in *OE9*, but not in *4SA-15* (Fig. 7d, and Fig. S10b). Interestingly, although there was not a significant difference This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved. in *LBD41* mRNA levels between the control and submergence conditions in *OE9*, there was a significant decrease in *LBD41* mRNA levels in *4SA-15* under submergence. These results indicate that the enrichment of specific mRNAs in polysomes is regulated by the SnRK1-elFiso4G1 relay under submergence.

# The elFiso4G1 protein phosphorylated by SnRK1 recognizes the capped 5' proximal region of specific mRNAs

We found that the 5'UTRs of TUR genes have similar structural features, i.e., a significantly lower GC content and lower potential to form a secondary structure (Fig. S10c). We hypothesized that the translation initiation factor elFiso4G1 phosphorylated by SnRK1.1 can recognize the 5' UTRs of mRNAs of the hypoxia response genes and enhance their translation. We transiently expressed the 5'UTRs of two TUR genes, *ADH1* and *SUS1*, (5' UTR<sub>ADH1</sub>::Luc and 5' UTR<sub>SUS1</sub>::Luc) and one high GC content sequence (vector) fused with the luciferase coding region (Fig. 8a and Fig. S11a) in Col-0 and *i4g1-2* protoplasts. Compared with the vector-only control (pNOS::Luc), higher luciferase activity was observed for both 5' UTR<sub>ADH1</sub>::Luc and 5' UTR<sub>SUS1</sub>::Luc in Col-0 protoplasts , but not in *i4g1-2* protoplasts (Fig. 8b). The *Luc* mRNA levels in Col-0 and *i4g1-2* protoplasts transformed with different constructs were the same (Fig. 8c). These results suggest that elFiso4G1 is required to enhance the translation of specific 5' UTRs.

To examine whether the phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 by SnRK1.1 is required for the translation of specific 5' UTR fragments, we transiently co-expressed the 5' UTR<sub>ADH1</sub>::Luc and 5'UTR<sub>SUS1</sub>::Luc reporters with elFiso4G1 and SnRK1.1 in Col-0 protoplasts. In both assays, transformation of SnRK1.1 alone enhanced the reporter activity while co-transformation of elFiso4G1 and SnRK1.1 (Fig. S11b) increased the reporter activity to a higher level (Fig. 8d and 8e), indicating that SnRK1.1-elFiso4G1 regulates translation by recognizing the 5' UTRs of specific genes. The elFiso4G1 S125A and S542A mutations each had a minor effect on reporter activity, but the S123A, S560A, S123A/S125A/S542A, and

S123A/S125A/S542A/S560A mutations significantly reduced the reporter activity (Fig. 8d and 8e). The levels of *Luc* mRNA expression in protoplasts transiently expressing only the reporter or co-expressing elFiso4G1 and SnRK1.1 were not significantly different (Fig. S11c). We then performed experiments to see if transient co-expression of elFiso4G1 or elFiso4G2 with 5' UTR<sub>ADH1</sub>::Luc and 5' UTR<sub>SUS1</sub>::Luc in *i4g1-2* protoplasts could restore luciferase activity. As shown in Fig. S11d and S11e, the reporter activities of 5' UTR<sub>ADH1</sub>::Luc and 5' UTR<sub>SUS1</sub>::Luc could be restored by co-expression with either elFiso4G1 or elFiso4G2. These results suggest that the SnRK1-elFiso4G relay can regulate the translation of reporters fused with the 5' UTRs of *ADH1* and *SUS1*.

# SnRK1-elFiso4G1 is involved in regulating the translation of hypoxia response genes under submergence

When plants encounter hypoxia, general translation is repressed (Branco-Price et al., 2008). However, a set of mRNAs can bypass this translational repression (Sachs et al., 1980; Branco-Price et al., 2008; van Dongen and Licausi, 2015). How cells sense the energy crisis and quickly select the mRNAs to translate under hypoxia is still unclear. We found that SnRK1 is activated within 30 min of submergence, when cells start to face energy starvation, and phosphorylates several proteins required for energy production and stress adaption during prolonged hypoxia (Cho et al., 2016). Notably, we also found that elFiso4G1, a plant-specific translation initiation factor (Patrick and Browning, 2012), was phosphorylated by SnRK1.1 under submergence (Fig. 1, 3, and Table S3). Although formation of the elFiso4F complex containing elFiso4G1 was not affected under submergence (Fig. S8), the phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 by SnRK1.1 enhanced the mRNA translation of a group of TUR genes, including ADH1, PDC1, SUS1 and SUS4 (Fig. 5, 6, and Fig. S6, S9). However, under submergence, the disruption of translation caused by overexpression of a mutant form of elFiso4G1 that lacks phosphorylation sites was relatively minor compared with that in SnRK1K48M and i4g1-2 (Fig. 5 and 6). We speculate that the translation of these mRNAs was enhanced under normoxia by overexpression of S/A-mutated elFiso4G1 (Fig. 6c). Thus, to distinguish the importance of phosphorylated elFiso4G1 under submergence, we conducted RNAseq (Fig. 7 and Fig. S11) and RIP-qPCR (Fig. 7d). These analyses revealed

that phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 by SnRK1 is essential for the enhanced translation of TUR genes under submergence.

Interestingly, these TUR genes are involved in fermentation, sucrose degradation and fatty acid biosynthesis (Fig. 7 and Table S6), which are critical for energy production under hypoxia (Hsu et al.,2011). This indicates that in order to quickly respond to hypoxia stress, SnRK1 phosphorylates elFiso4G1, which amplifies protein production and maintains energy generation and confers stress adaption. Taken together, these data reveal that the SnRK1-elFiso4G1 signaling relay enhances the translation of specific mRNAs to confer hypoxia tolerance under submergence (Fig. 2 and 9).

The elF4G family could be involved in *SnRK1*-mediated signaling under submergence. In Arabidopsis, there are two elFiso4Gs and one elF4G. Our results showed that the SnRK1-elFiso4G1 relay controls the translation of an array of genes under hypoxia. However, there is a set of genes whose translation was reduced in the *SnRK1* mutant but was not significantly reduced in *i4g1-2* (Fig. 7 and Table S6), implying that multiple players are involved in SnRK1-mediated translational regulation under submergence. In addition, the amino acid sequences surrounding the three SnRK1.1 target sites in elFiso4G1 (S123, S125, and S542) (Fig. 4) are absent in elFiso4G2. These elFiso4G1 amino acid sequences are only conserved in the Brassicaceae, and mutations of these three residues reduced elFiso4G1 function (Fig. 6, 7, and 8). However, we found that elFiso4G2 could also be phosphorylated by SnRK1 (Fig.1 and S5) and enhance the translation of reporters fused with the 5' UTRs of *ADH1* and *SUS1* in *i4g1-2* protoplasts (Fig. S11). This suggests that elFiso4G2 might display This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved. certain degree of redundancy with elFiso4G1, although the elFiso4G2 mutant has normal submergence tolerance.

The other elF4G family protein, elF4G, could also be required to bypass translational repression under submergence, since elF4G can complement elFiso4G function *in vitro* (Gallie and Browning, 2001). Based on the functional conservation of SnRK1 and AMPK in plants and animals, the SnRK1-TOR relay was proposed to mediate the repression of 5' capping-dependent translation of mRNAs in plants (Schepetilnikov and Ryabova, 2017; Li and Sheen, 2016; van Dongen and Licausi, 2015). SnRK1 could phosphorylate TOR to activate the translation inhibitors 4E-BPs that replace elF4G, resulting in the formation of the elF4E-4E-BP complex. However, phylogenetic analysis indicates that there is no 4EBP homolog in plants. We cannot exclude the possibility that the low abundance of 4E-BP like proteins might limit identification of orthologues (Schepetilnikov and Ryabova, 2017). Further study of the roles of elF4G family members under submergence will help reveal how plants control translation.

## Cells have evolved a specific program that switches on the translation initiation machinery in response to oxygen tension

In animals, although cap-mediated translation is repressed under hypoxia (Knaup et al., 2009; Thoreen et al., 2012; Miloslavski et al., 2014; Schieber and Chandel, 2014), the homologue of eIF4E is reported to activate the translation of specific mRNAs under hypoxia (Uniacke et al., 2012). In plants, the escape from general translational repression under low oxygen was also reported for some mRNAs (Branco-Price et al., 2005; Branco-Price et al., This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved.

2008; Juntawong et al., 2013). For genes such as *ADH1*, whose mRNA and protein levels are highly induced during hypoxia, it was proposed that transcriptional induction could overcome translation repression under oxygen deprivation (Juntawong et al., 2013). Our data demonstrate that under submergence, phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 by SnRK1 (Fig. 1, 3, 4, and Table S3 and S4) enhances the translation of specific mRNAs (Fig. 5-8), indicating that cells evolved a similar program by which oxygen tension modulates the translational initiation process.

Interestingly, 5' UTR-dependent control is critical for determining translational efficiencies in plants under different abiotic and biotic stresses (Roy & von Arnim, 2013; Xu *et al.*, 2017a; Xu *et al.*, 2017b; Srivastava *et al.*, 2018). We also found that phosphorylation of elFiso4G1 by SnRK1 could enhance the translation of reporter genes fused with the 5' UTRs of two hypoxia response genes, *ADH1* and *SUS1* (Fig. 8), which contain two structural elements that are conserved among TUR genes (Fig. S11). It remains to be determined whether these two elements are the core sequences required for SnRK1-elFiso4G1 relay-mediated translational regulation and how the translational complex containing phosphorylated elFiso4G1 recognizes these specific elements (Fig. 9).
