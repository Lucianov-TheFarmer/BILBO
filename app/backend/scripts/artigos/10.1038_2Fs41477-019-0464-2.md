# **Physcomitrella STEMIN transcription factor induces stem cell formation with epigenetic reprogramming**

**Masaki Ishikawa  1,2,3,10\*, Mio Morishita1,2,10, Yohei Higuchi1,3,5, Shunsuke Ichikawa  1,2,6, Takaaki Ishikawa1,3, Tomoaki Nishiyama  3,4, Yukiko Kabeya1 , Yuji Hiwatashi  1,2,7, Tetsuya Kurata1,3, Minoru Kubo1,3,8, Shuji Shigenobu 1,2, Yosuke Tamada  1,2, Yoshikatsu Sato1,3,9 and Mitsuyasu Hasebe  1,2,3\***

**Epigenetic modifications, including histone modifications, stabilize cell-specific gene expression programmes to maintain cell identities in both metazoans and land plant[s1–](#page-8-0)[3](#page-8-1) . Notwithstanding the existence of these stable cell states, in land plants, stem cells are formed from differentiated cells during post-embryonic development and regeneratio[n4](#page-8-2)–[6](#page-8-3), indicating that land plants have an intrinsic ability to regulate epigenetic memory to initiate a new gene regulatory network. However, it is less well understood how epigenetic modifications are locally regulated to influence the specific genes necessary for cellular changes without affecting other genes in a genome. In this study, we found that ectopic induction of the AP2/ERF transcription factor STEMIN1 in leaf cells of the moss** *Physcomitrella patens* **decreases a repressive chromatin mark, histone H3 lysine 27 trimethylation (H3K27me3), on its direct target genes before cell division, resulting in the conversion of leaf cells to chloronema apical stem cells. STEMIN1 and its homologues positively regulate the formation of secondary chloronema apical stem cells from chloronema cells during development. Our results suggest that STEMIN1 functions within an intrinsic mechanism underlying local H3K27me3 reprogramming to initiate stem cell formation.**

Epigenetic modifications help stabilize cell type-specific gene expression to maintain differentiated states in both land plants and metazoans. For instance, the presence of a repressive chromatin mark, histone H3 lysine 27 trimethylation (H3K27me3), at specific loci contributes to determination of tissue-specific gene expressio[n7,](#page-8-4)[8](#page-8-5) and maintenance of differentiated states[9](#page-8-6),[10.](#page-8-7) Therefore, locusspecific reprogramming of H3K27me3 is required for the rewriting of gene regulatory networks during plant development and regeneration, which could be one of the intrinsic mechanisms underlying the formation of stem cells that can self-renew and produce cells capable of differentiatio[n11](#page-8-8),[12.](#page-8-9) Although components of polycomb repressive complex 2, which catalyses H3K27me3 deposition, are known to be involved in the establishment of epigenetic memor[y13](#page-8-10),[14,](#page-8-11)[15](#page-8-12), it remains unclear what factors function in erasing that memory to permit the induction of a new cell fat[e1](#page-8-0) .

In the moss *Physcomitrella patens* (Physcomitrella), when gametophore leaves are cut and incubated on culture medium without exogenous phytohormones, leaf cells facing the cut are converted to chloronema apical stem cells that can undergo tip growth and cell division (Fig. [1a,b](#page-1-0) and Supplementary Fig. 1)[16](#page-8-13). In screens for factors involved in this reprogramming (see Methods), we found that ectopic induction of a gene encoding a member of an uncharacterized subgroup of AP2/ERF transcription factors, *STEM CELL-INDUCING FACTOR 1* (*STEMIN1*; Supplementary Fig. 2), changes leaf cells into chloronema apical stem cells even in the absence of cutting, and the chloronemata thus produced changed to caulonemata to form regular gametophores (Supplementary Fig. 1). We fused *STEMIN1* with a β-oestradiol-inducible promoter to generate plants carrying an inducible *STEMIN1* gene (GX6:NGG>STEMIN1; Supplementary Fig. 3[\)17](#page-8-14). When we cultivated gametophores of the transgenic plants with 1 µM β-oestradiol for 5 d, a portion of the leaf cells changed into chloronema apical stem cells without being cut (Fig. [1c–f](#page-1-0) and Supplementary Video 1). *STEMIN1* induction also caused the activation of promoters of the protonema-specific gene *Pp3c7\_24260* (*RM09*[\)16](#page-8-13) and the cell cycle regulatory gene *CYCD;1* (ref. [16](#page-8-13)) in reprogrammed leaf cells 24 h after induction, before the onset of tip growth (Fig. [1g,h](#page-1-0) and Supplementary Fig. 4).

Quantitative PCR with reverse transcription (RT–qPCR) analysis indicated that *STEMIN1* messenger RNA increased to 30h after the leaves were cut, and decreased at 36h (Fig. [2a\)](#page-2-0). To investigate spatiotemporal expression patterns, GUS-STEMIN1 and sGFP-STEMIN1 transgenic plants, in which the genes *uidA* and *sGFP* were inserted just before the *STEMIN1* initiation codon to produce fusion proteins GUS-STEMIN1 and sGFP-STEMIN1, respectively (Supplementary Figs. 5 and 6), were observed. A β-glucuronidase (GUS) signal was detected in leaf cells facing the cut but was not detected in protruded cells at 30h (Fig. [2b](#page-2-0) and Supplementary Fig. 5), suggesting that the signal disappeared when reprogrammed cells were protruded. On the other hand the sGFP signal was not detected at any time point, probably because of the lower sensitivity of sGFP-STEMIN1 compared to GUS-STEMIN1.

We then analysed the promoter activity of *STEMIN1* in STEMIN1pro:NGG transgenic plants, which carry a 3-kb *STEMIN1* promoter sequence attached to the fusion gene *NGG*—consisting of the SV40 nuclear localization signal (*NLS*)[18,](#page-8-15) *sGFP* and the β-glucuronidase gene (*GUS*[\)19—](#page-8-16)inserted into the *PIG1* neutral site (Supplementary Fig. 7a,b). While *STEMIN1* promoter activity was not detected in intact leaves (Supplementary Fig. 7c,d), sGFP signal was detected 24h after cutting in leaf cells facing the cut (Fig. [2c](#page-2-0), Supplementary Fig. 7e and Supplementary Video 2). Some sGFP-positive cells protruded and re-entered the cell cycle to become chloronema apical stem cells (Fig. [2c,](#page-2-0) arrowheads at 36h). After approximately two rounds of cell division, the sGFP signal in the chloronema apical stem cells diminished (Fig. [2c](#page-2-0), arrowheads at 48h). Other sGFP-positive cells did not protrude, and in these cells sGFP signals diminished by 36h (Fig. [2c,d](#page-2-0) and Supplementary Fig. 7e). Cells without sGFP signals did not protrude (Fig. [2c,d](#page-2-0) and Supplementary Fig. 7e). When a leaf cell was isolated from a leaf as previously described[20](#page-8-17), the *STEMIN1* promoter was also activated before tip growth (Fig. [2e\)](#page-2-0), indicative of cell-autonomous *STEMIN1* induction by wounding.

We next constructed *STEMIN1-*deletion mutant plants (∆stemin1; Supplementary Fig. 8). However, we detected no difference in the timing of initiation of tip growth between wild-type (WT) and ∆stemin1 plants after cutting (Fig. [2f\)](#page-2-0). Gametophore apices in which GUS and sGFP signals were detected (Supplementary Fig. 7c,d) also showed no difference. Our phylogenetic analysis of STEMINrelated protein identified two other genes in Physcomitrella closely related to *STEMIN1*, designated *STEMIN2* and *STEMIN3* (Supplementary Fig. 2), and we established triple-deletion mutant plants (∆stemin1∆stemin2∆stemin3; Supplementary Fig. 9). In these mutant plants, the number of cells exhibiting tip growth in cut leaves at 36 and 48h after cutting was significantly lower than in WT plants (Fig. [2g](#page-2-0) and Supplementary Fig. 9e). In addition, the levels of *RM09* and *CYCD;1* transcripts in cut leaves 24h after cutting were lower in the triple-mutant plants (Fig. [2h\)](#page-2-0).

We detected the promoter activities of *STEMIN2* and *STEMIN3* in leaf cells facing the cut (Supplementary Figs. 10 and 11) and found that the transcripts of these genes were less abundant than those of *STEMIN1* during stem cell formation (Supplementary Fig. 11e). Unlike *STEMIN1*, induction of *STEMIN2* or *STEMIN3* in gametophores did not change leaf cells into chloronema apical stem cells (Supplementary Figs. 12 and 13). These results indicate that STEMIN2 and STEMIN3 have functions overlapping those of STEMIN1, but they lack the ability of STEMIN1 to change intact leaf cells into stem cells (Supplementary Fig. 26).

Stem cell formation in cut leaves is also regulated by the genes *WUSCHEL-related homeobox 13-like* (*WOX13L*[\)21](#page-8-18) and *Cold-Shock Domain Protein* (*CSP*[\)22.](#page-8-19) The former is required for initiation of tip growth characteristic of chloronema apical stem cell[s21](#page-8-18) while the latter, orthologous to the mammalian reprogramming factor Lin28, positively regulates stem cell formation[22.](#page-8-19) Transcript levels of two *WOX13L* and four *CSP* genes in the ∆stemin1∆stemin2∆stemin3 line and those of three *STEMIN* genes in the *WOX13L* double-deletion lin[e21](#page-8-18) and *CSP* quadruple-deletion lin[e22](#page-8-19) increased after cutting, but their levels were lower than those in WT (Supplementary Fig. 14). These results indicate that *STEMIN* genes are not essential for activation of genes *WOX13L* and *CSP* and that *WOX13L*, *CSP* and *STEMIN* positively regulate each other. These results suggest that *WOX13L* and *CSP* function in stem cell formation in cut leaves of *STEMIN* triple-deletion mutants.

Chloronema apical stem cells at the tip of chloronema filamentous tissue repeatedly divide to produce chloronema cells. Chloronema cells two cells distant from the apical stem cells usually protrude, with tip growth, to form side-branch initial cells that subsequently become secondary chloronema apical stem cells[23](#page-8-20). We detected promoter activities of *STEMIN1* and *STEMIN2* in chloronema cells before protrusion, and the signal diminished before the first cell division (Fig. [3a–d,](#page-3-0) Supplementary Video 3 and Supplementary Fig. 15a). These activities were not detected in chloronema apical stem cells. The duration of *STEMIN1* promoter activity in forming side-branch initial cells was almost half that in stem cell formation in cut leaves (Supplementary Fig. 15b).

In this study, we show that STEMIN1 can induce reprogramming of differentiated leaf cells to chloronema apical stem cells through H3K27me3 modifications of its direct target genes (Supplementary Fig. 26). In *Arabidopsis thaliana* (Arabidopsis), H3K27me3 levels are diluted by repeated cell divisions during the change from vegetative meristem to reproductive meristem[27](#page-8-24). Since we detected the decrease in H3K27me3 before cell division, chromatin modifications caused by STEMIN1 induction are independent of cell division.

In Arabidopsis, some AP2/ERF transcription factors enhance changes from differentiated cells to callus[28](#page-8-25) and from callus to shoot meristem[s29.](#page-8-26) However, cellular and molecular functions of these transcription factors in stem cell formation are unknown. Since other land plants have orthologues of the *STEMIN* genes (Supplementary Fig. 2a), further studies of this gene family should provide insight into whether this is a general mechanism for reprogramming in land plants.

In cut leaves, we reported variations in transcriptomes between reprogramming cell[s30](#page-8-27) and lateral suppression of reprogramming by neighbouring reprogramming cells[20.](#page-8-17) The effects of STEMIN induction on cellular variations during reprogramming will be the subject of a future study.

### **Methods**

**Plant material.** Te Gransden 2004 strain of *P. patens*[31](#page-8-28) (Physcomitrella) was used as the WT strain and cultured on BCDAT medium under continuous white light at 25°[C32.](#page-8-29) Polyethylene glycol-mediated transformation and preparation of gametophores were performed as described previousl[y16,](#page-8-13)[20,](#page-8-17)[32](#page-8-29).

**Screening of factors to induce reprogramming of leaf cells.** We found that *SQUAMOSA PROMOTER BINGING PROTEIN 4* (*SBP4*: AJ968319)[33](#page-8-30) negatively regulates stem cell formation in cut leaves (an unpublished result). We induced SBP4 and SBP4 fused with the repression domain SRD[X34](#page-8-31) in cut leaves, screened genes with decreased and increased expression levels, respectively, and selected 30 candidate genes in which *STEMIN1* was included. The coding sequences of these genes were cloned into the pENTR/D-TOPO vector (Invitrogen). The resulting entry vectors were cloned by the Gateway LR recombination reaction (LR reaction, Invitrogen) into the destination vector pLGZ1 that we modified the pER8 vecto[r35](#page-8-32) for stable β-oestradiol-inducible gene expression in Physcomitrella. The generated constructs were digested with the restriction enzyme *Pme*I for gene targeting and introduced into GX6:NGG#63 plants[16,](#page-8-13)[17.](#page-8-14) Gametophores of the resultant transgenic plants were cultivated in 10mM MES buffer (pH6.0) with 1µM β-oestradiol for 5d to examine whether induction of these genes induced stem cell formation in leaves not subjected to cutting.

**Construction of β-oestradiol-inducible** *STEMIN* **plasmids.** Primers used to construct the plasmids are listed in Supplementary Table 3. To construct plasmids LGZ1-STEMIN1, LGZ1-STEMIN2, LGZ1-STEMIN3 and PTA2GX6:STEMIN1, coding sequences *STEMIN1*, *STEMIN2* and *STEMIN3* were cloned into the pENTR/D-TOPO vector (Invitrogen) to generate the plasmids pENTR:STEMIN1, pENTR:STEMIN2 and pENTR:STEMIN3, respectively. These three plasmids were subjected to the LR reaction using the destination vector pLGZ1. The generated constructs were digested with the restriction enzyme *Pme*I for gene targeting and introduced into the GX6:NGG#63 plant[16](#page-8-13)[,17](#page-8-14) to generate several independent GX6:NGG>STEMIN1, GX6:NGG>STEMIN2 and GX6:NGG>STEMIN3 plants.

For generation of PTA2GX6:STEMIN1 plants, the pENTR:STEMIN1 plasmid was subjected to the LR reaction using the destination vector pT2GX6. The generated constructs were digested with the restriction enzyme *Pme*I for gene targeting and introduced into RM09#35 and ProCYCD;1:NLS-sGFP-GUS#263 plants[16,](#page-8-13) respectively.

To construct the GX6:STEMIN1-Myc plasmid, the *STEMIN1* coding sequence without the stop codon was cloned into pENTR/D-TOPO and subjected to the LR reaction using the destination vector pGX6M.

**Plasmid construction for spatial expression analysis.** Primers used for plasmid construction are given in Supplementary Table 3. To insert the *sGFP* or *uidA* genes in frame with the *STEMIN1* coding sequence, DNA fragments of *sGFP* and *uidA* were amplified and inserted into pBluescript SK+. Subsequently, a genomic DNA fragment including a partial sequence of the *STEMIN1* promoter and 5'-untranslated region was amplified from WT genomic DNA and inserted at the 5'-end of genes *sGFP* and *uidA*. A genomic DNA fragment from the start codon to the stop codon of *STEMIN1* was fused with the 3'-end of the coding region of genes *sGFP* and *uidA*, respectively.

For the promoter-reporter lines, DNA fragments including partial sequences of promoters *STEMIN1*, *STEMIN2* and *STEMIN3* were amplified and inserted into the *Sma*I site of pPIG1bNGGII[16](#page-8-13). The generated constructs were digested with *Pme*I for gene targeting and introduced into WT Physcomitrella.

**Plasmid construction for the deletion of** *STEMIN* **genes and generation of the**  *STEMIN***-deletion mutants.** Primers used for plasmid construction are given in Supplementary Table 3. To delete *STEMIN1*, *STEMIN2* and *STEMIN3* in WT Physcomitrella, genomic fragments containing the 5' and 3'flanking regions of each gene were inserted into the 5' end and 3'region of the blasticidinS deaminase (BSD[\)36](#page-8-33) expression cassette of the plasmid p35S-loxP-BSD, of the zero expression cassett[e37](#page-8-34) of the plasmid p35S-loxP-Zeo, and of the neomycin phosphotransferase II expression cassette (nptII[\)32](#page-8-29) of the plasmid pTN182, respectively. The generated constructs were digested with restriction enzymes suitable for gene targeting.

To generate ∆stemin1∆stemin2∆stemin3 triple-deletion mutants, the *STEMIN1*-deletion construct was introduced into the WT to generate ∆stemin1 plants. Two independent deletion mutants, ∆stemin1#47 and ∆stemin1#54, were used for further transformation. The *STEMIN3*-deletion construct and subsequently the *STEMIN2*-deletion construct were introduced into ∆stemin1 plants to generate ∆stemin1∆stemin2∆stemin3 triple-deletion mutants.

To generate ∆stemin1∆stemin2 double-deletion mutants, the STEMIN2 deletion construct was introduced into ∆stemin1#47 and ∆stemin1#54 plants, respectively.

**Phylogenic analysis.** Amino acid sequences collected from the NCBInr database, as well as complementary DNA sequences determined here, were used to reconstruct the phylogeny using an updated procedure based on that described in ref. [38](#page-8-35). A total of 1,000 sequences collected with the BLASTP search against the NCBInr database as of 3 September 2018, and the proteome datasets of *Pinus taeda* (v.2.01)[39](#page-8-36), *Azolla filiculoides* (Azolla\_asm\_v.1.1)[40](#page-8-37) and *Salvinia cucullata* (Salvinia\_asm\_v.1.2)[40,](#page-8-37) were gathered. BLASTP was performed with word size set to 2 using the core STEMIN1 sequence as query. The search was separately performed for *A. thaliana*, *Amborella tricocarpa*[41](#page-8-38), *Marchantia polymorpha*[42,](#page-8-39) *P. patens*[31](#page-8-28) and *Selaginella moellendorffii*[38,](#page-8-35) and green algae (Viriplantae excluding Embryophyta). About 20 of the top sequences (for *Selaginella* 40 sequences as it is diploid, for green algae 40 sequences as the category includes many species) from

each run were collected. The collected sequences were merged in a single FASTA format file containing 244 sequences and aligned using MAFFT 7.407 (ref. [43\)](#page-8-40) with the einsi setting. The alignment was converted to a nexus format file with sites that were present in at least 95% of the taxa set as included and others as excluded. The alignment was examined with MacClade v.4.08 (ref. [44](#page-8-41)), and sequences having indels within the region were removed and 225 sequences were retained. A conserved block consisting of 56 amino acids starting (F/Y) and ending with (F/L/Y) was kept as included, and further analyses were performed on this region. Sequences identical in this region were treated as a single operational taxonomic unit (OTU) and thus 168distinct OTUs remained. RAxML 8.2.12 was used to search for the maximum likelihood tree. First, the best fit model was chosen with ProteinModelSelection.pl and LG was selected. The maximum likelihood was searched with -f a option with -#100 -m PROTGAMMALG and -p, -x parameters chosen randomly, taking 24bits from /dev/urandom. Bootstrap resampling was done using SEQBOOT from the PHYLIP package ([http://evolution.genetics.](http://evolution.genetics.washington.edu/phylip.html) [washington.edu/phylip.html](http://evolution.genetics.washington.edu/phylip.html)), 100bootstrap data were individually analysed in the same manner and the consensus was counted using CONSENSE. The bootstrap values were put on the original tree.

**RNA preparation and RT–qPCR analysis.** To analyse transcript accumulation in response to *STEMIN1* induction, 3-week-old gametophores of the GX6:NGG>STEMIN1 (16, 23 and 258) and GX6:NGG#63 lines were soaked in liquid BCDAT medium with or without β-oestradiol (0.01, 0.1 and 1 µM) for 24h. Cut leaves were prepared as previously describe[d16.](#page-8-13)

Total RNA was purified from whole gametophores, cut leaves and protonemata with the RNeasy Micro Kit (Qiagen). First-strand cDNA was synthesized using the ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix (TOYOBO). RT–qPCR was performed using either an ABI PRISM 7500 (Applied Biosystems) or a QuantStudio3 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) with the QuantiTect SYBR Green PCR Kit (Qiagen) or THUNDERBIRD SYBR qPCR Mix (TOYOBO). The sequences of primers for RT–qPCR are listed in Supplementary Table 3. Results were analysed using the comparative critical threshold method. Each transcript level determined by RT–qPCR analysis was normalized to that of α-tubulin (*TUA1*)[16](#page-8-13) during stem cell formation in cut leaves, and to that of *Pp3c9\_17670* encoding a thiosulfate sulfurtransferase during side branch formation in chloronemata.

**GUS staining.** The histochemical detection of GUS activity followed a previous repor[t32](#page-8-29).

**Microscopic analysis.** Bright-field and fluorescent images of WT and transgenic plants were observed under fluorescence microscopes BX51 and SZX16 (Olympus).

For time-lapse analyses, the distal halves of the fifth to tenth youngest leaves were excised with a razor blade. To isolate single leaf cells, cut leaves were further divided into small pieces with a razor blade and all leaf cells,except a single cell targeted for reprogramming, were ablated by the PALM microdissection system (Zeiss)[20](#page-8-17). Time-lapse analyses of cut leaves and singly isolated leaf cells was performed as reported previously[20.](#page-8-17) Images of leaf reprogramming were recorded at 20-min intervals with the digital camera ORCA-AG (Hamamatsu Photonics K. K.) using an inverted microscope IX81 (Olympus). The images were reconstructed to create a video with ImageJ [\(http://rsb.info.nih.gov/ij/\)](http://rsb.info.nih.gov/ij/). Image intensities of sGFP in transgenic plants were adjusted using Photoshop CS6 (Adobe).

**Scanning electron microscopy.** Four-week-old gametophores of the GX6:NGG>STEMIN1#23 lines were soaked in liquid BCDAT medium with 1µM β-oestradiol (WAKO) for 3d. The gametophores were frozen in liquid nitrogen and immediately observed with a scanning electron microscope (XL30, FEI), with an accelerating voltage of 10 kV.

**Measurement of frequency of side branching in chloronemata.** Under unilateral red light, chloronema apical stem cells produce almost no side branch initial cells whereas subsequent incubation under white light induces the formation of secondary chloronema apical stem cell[s23](#page-8-20)[,24.](#page-8-21) Chloronemata of WT, *STEMIN*deletion and inducible *STEMIN* lines grown on BCDAT agar medium under unilateral red light (10–15 µmolm2 s–1) for 10d were transferred to white light (approximately 50 µmolm2 s–1). Concurrently, inducible *STEMIN* lines were treated with or without 1 µM β-oestradiol. These lines were further cultivated under continuous white light. The number of side-branch initial cells was counted either 12h (WT and deletion mutants) or 24h (inducible *STEMIN* mutants) after the transfer. Chloronemata were stained with 10 µM of the lipophilic dye *N*-(3 triethylammoniumpropyl)-4-(6-(4-(diethylamino)phenyl)hexatrienyl)-pyridinium dibromide (FM4-64, ThermoFisher Scientific).

**DNA gel-blot and immunoblot analyses.** DNA gel-blot and immunoblot analyses followed previous reports[16](#page-8-13)[,45](#page-8-42).

**Digital gene expression profiling by 5′-end sequencing.** *STEMIN1*, *STEMIN2* and *STEMIN3* transcript levels (tags per million) in cut leaves of WT and the ∆wox13la∆wox13lb deletion line#186 were previously determined by 5′ digital gene expression analysi[s21,](#page-8-18)[46.](#page-8-43) The tag counts on each gene were divided by the

total number of tags mapped to the genome and multiplied by 106 to obtain transcript levels[46](#page-8-43).

**Transcriptome analysis.** Four-week-old gametophores of GX6:STEMIN1-Myc#11 and GX6:NGG#63 plants were soaked in BCDAT medium with 1 µM β-oestradiol or DMSO for 24h and were then collected with forceps. Libraries for transcriptome analysis were prepared from total RNA of the gametophores, as described previously[47.](#page-8-44) Sequencing was conducted using a HiSeq 2500 instrument (Illumina) and 50-bp single-end reads were obtained. Adaptor and low-quality sequences were removed from the reads using cutadapt v.1.9.1 (DOI, 10.14806/ej.17.1.200). The reads were analysed using the transcriptome sequence of *P. patens* v.3.3 (Ppatens\_251\_v3.3.transcript.fa) obtained from Phytozome ([https://phytozome.](https://phytozome.jgi.doe.gov/pz/portal.html) [jgi.doe.gov/pz/portal.html](https://phytozome.jgi.doe.gov/pz/portal.html)), and calculated using RNA-Seq by Expectation-Maximization (RSEM v.1.3.0) with Bowtie2. The transcriptome analysis was performed in biological triplicate. Differentially expressed genes with *q*<0.01 were extracted with the Rpackage TC[C48](#page-8-45), and those with fold change >1.5 were then selected.

**ChIP-seq.** Four-week-old gametophores with rhizoids and protonemata of plant GX6:STEMIN1-Myc#11 were soaked in BCDAT liquid medium with either 1µM β-oestradiol or DMSO for 24h, then treated with 1% formaldehyde for 20min at room temperature. Cross-linking reaction was stopped with 0.1M glycine. Subsequently the tissues were washed with Milli-Q-purified water and treated with 2% Driselase for 20min to remove rhizoids and protonemata.

ChIP was performed as reported previousl[y49](#page-8-46) with several modifications. In short, genomic DNA was extracted and sheared to an average size of approximately 100–200bp with 20Uµl –1 micrococcal nuclease (No. 2910A, Takara) for 15min at 37˚C. The reaction was stopped with 20mM EDTA and 1% SDS, and the lysates were incubated for 1h on ice. After collection of input samples, the lysates were incubated for 2h at 4°C with 10 µg of antibody (anti-Myc (No. 4A-6, Millipore), anti-H3 (No. ab1791, Abcam), anti-H3K27me3 (No. 07-449, Millipore) or anti-H3K4me3 (No. 07-473, Millipore)). Immunocomplexes were recovered with proteinG agarose (Roche), then washed and eluted with elution buffer (1% SDS, 0.84% NaHCO3). After reversal of cross-links, the samples were treated with RNaseA and proteinaseK, and DNA was recovered after phenol-chloroform extraction. The recovered DNA was dissolved in 5mM Tris-HCl (pH8.5). ChIP libraries were then prepared using a DNA SMART ChIP-seq Kit (Clontech). The libraries were then subjected to an Illumina Hiseq 1500 sequencer and reads of approximately 126bp were obtained. Biological duplicate ChIP-seq experiments for STEMIN1-Myc, H3, H3K27me3 and H3K4me3 were performed.

**ChIP-seq data analysis.** Quality control of the ChIP-sequencing output was undertaken using FASTQC v.0.11.3 ([http://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/](http://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc) [projects/fastqc](http://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc)). To map sequence reads on the Physcomitrella genome, adaptor sequences of ChIP-DNA sequences in FASTQ format were removed using cutadapt v.1.9.1. The resultant sequences were mapped to the *P. patens* V3.0 genome (Ppatens\_251\_v3.fa) of Phytozome (DOE-JGI; [https://phytozome.jgi.doe.gov/\)](https://phytozome.jgi.doe.gov/) using Bowtie2 v.2.2.6 (ref. [50\)](#page-8-47).

For STEMIN1-Myc binding sites, peaks appearing significantly higher in ChIP-DNA from β-oestradiol-treated gametophores compared to DMSO-treated gametophores were detected as STEMIN1-Myc binding sites using MACS v.1.4.2 (ref. [51\)](#page-8-48) with the default parameters. The peaks were annotated for Physcomitrella genetic information using the Rpackages ChIPpeakAnno v.3.0.0 (DOI, 10.18129/ B9.bioc.ChIPpeakAnno) and GenomicRanges v.1.22.4 (DOI,10.18129/B9.bioc. GenomicRanges). For distribution of histone H3, H3K27me3 and H3K4me3, the reads from immunocomplexes with antibodies anti-H3, anti-H3K27me3 and anti-H3K4me3 were aligned to a Physcomitrella genome using Subread v.1.5.2 (ref. [52](#page-8-49)).

The distribution of STEMIN1-Myc binding sites, histone H3, H3K27me3 and H3K4me3 was analysed using ngs.plot with two steps of normalization—that is, length normalization to be equal length and library size normalization to generate reads per million mapped reads values[53.](#page-8-50) The distributions of histone H3 and H3K27me3 on genes *CYCD;1* and *BETA*-*EXPANSIN* (*Pp3c17\_12980*) were visualized using Integrated Genomics View v.2.5.2 (refs. [54](#page-8-51)[,55\)](#page-8-52).

For analysis of the relationship between levels of H3K27me3 and transcripts in STEMIN1-regulated genes (Supplementary Fig. 22), the reads of H3K27me3 and input samples were normalized as reads per kilobase per million (RPKM) mapped sequence reads based on the formula: (number of tags mapped to each region×1,000,000,000)/(length of each region×number of total mapped tags). The RPKM values for H3K27me3 levels in each gene model were divided by those of the input RPKM values, and relative values were then calculated.
