## **ORIGINAL ARTICLE**

# Comparative RNA-Seq analysis on the regulation of cucumber sex diferentiation under diferent ratios of blue and red light

Jiali Song, Yiting Zhang, Shiwei Song, Wei Su, Riyuan Chen, Guangwen Sun, Yanwei Hao[\\*](http://orcid.org/0000-0002-6465-4885) and Houcheng Liu\*

## **Abstract**

Cucumber (*Cucumis sativus* L.) is a typical monoecism vegetable with individual male and female fowers, which has been used as a plant model for sex determination. It is well known that light is one of the most important environmental stimuli, which control the timing of the transition from vegetative growth to reproductive development. However, whether light controls sex determination remains elusive. To unravel this problem, we performed highthroughput RNA-Seq analyses, which compared the transcriptomes of shoot apices between R2B1(Red light:Blue light=2:1)-treated and R4B1(Red light:Blue light=4:1)-treated cucumber seedlings. Results showed that the higher proportion of blue light in the R2B1 treatment signifcantly induced the formation of female fowers and accelerated female fowering time in this whole study. The genes related to fowering time, such as *fowering locus T* (*FT*) and *SUPPRESSOR OF OVEREXPRESSION OF CO1* (*SOC1*), were up-regulated after R2B1 treatment. Furthermore, the transcriptome analysis showed that up-regulation and down-regulation of specifc DEGs (the diferentially expressed genes) were primarily the result of plant hormone signal transduction after treatments. The specifc DEGs related with auxin formed the highest percentage of DEGs in the plant hormone signal transduction. In addition, the expression levels of transcription factors also changed after R2B1 treatment. Thus, sex diferentiation afected by light quality might be induced by plant hormone signal transduction and transcription factors. These results provide a theoretical basis for further investigation of the regulatory mechanism of female fower formation under diferent light qualities in cucumber seedlings.

**Keywords:** RNA-seq, Auxin, Cucumber, Flower, Sex diferentiation

## **Background**

Sex diferentiation of fower buds is an important developmental process, which directly afects the product yield in plants. Cucumber (*Cucumis sativus* L.) is a typical monoecious plant with distinct male and female fowers. It has been served as a model system for studying physiological and molecular aspects of sex determination and diferentiation in plants (Bai and Xu [2013](#page-16-0)). In the young foral buds of cucumber, both the stamen primordia and

Sex diferentiation of cucumber can be afected by phytohormones, such as ethylene and gibberellin. Exogenous ethylene treatment induced female fower formation in the cucumber. Organ-specifc overexpression of *CsACO2* driven by the organ-specifc promoter P (AP3) signifcantly afected stamen but not carpel development, acting similarly to natural foral development of female cucumber fowers (Duan et al. [2008\)](#page-16-2). A conserved-residue conversion in *CsACS2* induced the formation of bisexual fowers in the cucumber (Li et al. [2009](#page-16-3)). Furthermore, ethylene signaling pathway also afects the sex diferentiation. *CsETR1*, an ethylene receptor involved in ethylene signaling transduction, had been demonstrated to play a key role in stamen arrest in female cucumber fowers (Wang et al. [2010](#page-17-2)). Ethylene-responsive gene associated with the formation of female fowers (*ERAF17*), a MADS-box gene, could be induced by ethylene and might be involved in formation of female fowers in cucumbers (Ando et al. [2001](#page-15-0)). Exogenous gibberellic acid (GA3) application promoted the formation of male fowers in gynoecious plants (Pike and Peterson [1969\)](#page-17-3). GA production in andromonoecious cucumbers was higher than that in gynoecious and monoecious plants (Junior et al. [1972\)](#page-16-4). Te GA signaling pathway is involved in stamen development in the cucumber (Zhang et al. [2014b;](#page-17-4) Fei et al. [2004](#page-16-5)). Cucumber DELLA Homolog (*CsGAIP*) is predominantly expressed in the male specifc organs during cucumber fower development and belongs to the DELLA (the negative regulators of the GA action) family. *CsGAIP* inhibited stamen development through transcriptional repression of B-class foral homeotic genes *APETALA3* (*AP3*) and *PIS-TILLATA* (*PI*) in Arabidopsis (Zhang et al. [2014a](#page-17-5)). *CsGA-MYB1*, a positive regulator involved in the GA signaling pathway, also mediates the sex expression of cucumbers. Knocking out the *CsGAMYB1* gene in cucumbers resulted in decreased ratios of nodes with male to female fowers (Zhang et al. [2014b](#page-17-4)). Te relationship between ethylene and gibberellin in mediating sex diferentiation was interpreted recently, with this study fnding that gibberellin mediates sex diferentiation via ethylene-dependent and ethylene-independent pathways in cucumbers (Zhang et al. [2017\)](#page-17-6). Apart from ethylene and gibberellin, other hormones are also involved in the fower sex differentiation of plant (Song et al. [2013\)](#page-17-7). Tis includes the abscisic acid (ABA) induction of male fower formation (Zhu et al. [2010](#page-17-8)); Jasmonic acid (JA) signaling pathway, which might participate in the abortion of male fowers (Acosta et al. [2009\)](#page-15-1); and auxin (IAA) content, which was found to increase during female fower development and decrease during male fower development (Sakata et al. [2010](#page-17-9)). Ethylene biosynthesis has probably been induced by IAA to promote female fower formation as well (Trebitsh et al. [1987](#page-17-0)). Te IAA induced *CsACS1* gene expression in cucumbers, which suggested that IAA promoted female fower formation through inducing ethylene synthesis (Trebitsh et al. [1997;](#page-17-10) Mibus and Tatlioglu [2004](#page-17-11)).

Environmental cues, such as temperature, photoperiod, nutrition, also afected sex determination in many species (Golenberg and West [2013;](#page-16-6) Korpelainen [1998](#page-16-7)). Light is one of the major external factors that infuence plant growth and development (Chen et al. [2004](#page-16-8)). Plants respond to light through mediating light receptors. Until now, known photoreceptors were divided into three classes: the UV-B (280–320 nm light) photoreceptors; the red/far-red reversible photoreceptors, known as the phytochromes PhyA–PhyE (Mathews and Sharrock [1997](#page-17-12)) and blue UVA photoreceptors. Tese blue UVA photoreceptors have three classes, which are the cryptochromes (CRY1, CRY2 and CRY3) (Mathews and Sharrock [1997](#page-17-12); Brudler et al. [2003\)](#page-16-9), phototropins (PHOT1 and PHOT2) (Briggs et al. [2001](#page-16-10)) and aureochromes (AUREO1 and AUREO2) (Ishikawa et al. [2009](#page-16-11); Takahashi et al. [2007](#page-17-13)). Tere is less evidence examining the efects of light on plant sex diferentiation. In the gametophytes of the *Her1* mutant, blue light induced male development and red light suppressed male development (Kamachi et al. [2007](#page-16-12)). Our previous studies showed that light quality afected cucumber fower formation and sex diferentiation. In our previous study, we used 4R1B (Red:Blue=4:1), R2B1 (Red:Blue=2:1), R6G2B1 (Red:Green:Blue=6:2:1) ratios supplemental light to treat the cucumber seedlings. Comparing to natural light, R2B1 treatment was the best light quality ratio for improving the female fower formation, location and fowering time, but none of them has efects on male fower formation (our unpublished data). However, the mechanism of how light quality regulates fower sex diferentiation still remains unclear. In our study, we aimed to understand the mechanism of light regulating cucumber sexual development. We performed RNA-Seq analyses to compare the transcriptomes of shoot apices between cucumber seedlings after R2B1 treatment and R4B1 treatment. Our results built a foundation for dissecting the molecular mechanism of fower sex diferentiation in cucumbers.

## <span id="page-10-0"></span>**Discussion**

## **Light quality afects cucumber fowering time**

Blue light is a strong signal in foral bud formation. In Chrysanthemum, fower budding was formed even after a longer photoperiod than a critical day length under blue light illumination (Jerzy et al. [2011](#page-16-13)). In petunia plants, foral bud formation and fowering occurred earlier under blue light treatment, while no foral buds were observed under low red irradiance. High red irradiance and temporal switching to blue light during long-term low red irradiance induced foral development (Fukuda et al. [2016](#page-16-14)). In our experiment, we found that the frst female fower opened earlier and occurred mainly at the lower node positions under a higher proportion of blue light irradiance (Table [1\)](#page-2-0). Tis suggested that blue light promoted fower bud formation and accelerated cucumber fowering.

Cryptochromes (CRY) are favo-proteins that direct a diverse array of developmental processes in response to blue light in plants (Liu et al. [2016\)](#page-16-15). *CRY1* and *CRY2* function as major blue light receptors regulating blue light-induced de-etiolation and photo-periodic fowering (Guo and Li [1998\)](#page-16-16). In Arabidopsis, *CRY1* and *CRY2* serve both distinct and partially overlapping functions in regulating photomorphogenic responses and photoperiodic fowering. Te gain-of-function mutant alleles of *CRY1* exhibited an early fowering phenotype after several days (Exner et al. [2010](#page-16-17)). In our study, we detected a higher expression of *CRY1* in cucumber seedling leaves after R2B1 treatment than R4B1 treatment (Fig. [7](#page-10-0)), which was consistent with the earlier fowering phenotype after R2B1 irradiation. Te phytochrome (phy) family of sensory photoreceptors (phyA to phyE in *Arabidopsis thaliana*) also responds to inducing foral budding. Low red/

<span id="page-11-0"></span>far-red ratio promotes fowering in Arabidopsis through *PHYA* inhibiting *PHYB* (Chory [2003](#page-16-18); Halliday et al. [2003](#page-16-19)). In our study, we detected 4 phytochrome orthologous genes in cucumber seedling leaves and shoot apices. Expression analysis showed that *PHYA* accumulated more in leaves at the R4B1-15 stage than R2B1-15 stage (Fig. [7\)](#page-10-0). In comparison, the other genes displayed a higher expression in leaves at R4B1-10 stage than R2B1-10 stage (Fig. [7\)](#page-10-0). Similar results were found in shoot apices, with *PHYA*, *PHYB*, *PHYC*, *PHYE* expressed more in the shoot apices of R4B1-10 sample than R2B1-10 sample (Fig. [7](#page-10-0)). Te expression of *CRY2* and *PHYD* were not detected in both cucumber leaves and shoot apices.

CONSTANS (*CO*) is a key transcription factor regulating fowering time. *CO* positively regulates two foral integrators. In Arabidopsis, *CO* activated *SOC1* through *FT* to promote fowering (Rosas et al. [2014;](#page-17-15) Lee and Lee [2010](#page-16-20)). In this study, compared to R2B1-15, *CO* showed a higher expression in R4B1-15 (Fig. [7\)](#page-10-0). Although *CO* transcription regulation was afected by blue light treatment, *CO* was not only positively regulated by *CRY2* and *PHYA*, but also negatively regulated by *PHYB* (Valverde et al. [2004](#page-17-16)). Te lower expression under a high proportion of blue irradiation might result from the co-regulation of the cryptochrome receptor and phytochrome receptor. In Arabidopsis, blue light could induce *SOC1* and *FT* expression to promote fowering (Hori et al. [2011\)](#page-16-21). In petunia plants, blue light induces *FBP28* expression, the orthologous gene of *SOC1* (Fukuda et al. [2011\)](#page-16-22). *SOC1* is one of the direct targets of *AGL24* and is up-regulated by *AGL24*. In Arabidopsis, *SOC1* and *AGL24* are able to up-regulate each other's expressions, both of them are MADS-domain-containing transcription factors that determine fowering time (Michaels et al. [2003](#page-17-17)). In this study, *FT* showed a higher expression in R2B1-10 and R2B1-15 than in R4B1-10 and R4B1-15 (Fig. [8b](#page-11-0)), which suggested that higher proportion of blue light irradiation accelerated cucumber fowering through inducing *FT* expression. Two *SOC1* orthologous genes and seven *AGLs* (only *AGL19* showed diferent expression) orthologous genes were found in cucumber shoot apices. *SOC1* and *AGL19* displayed the same expression pattern, with increased expression in R2B1-10 and R2B1-15 treatments (Fig. [8b](#page-11-0)). All these results suggested that a higher proportion of blue light induced cucumber fower bud formation and promoted fowering time through up-regulating expression of these fowering regulation factors.

## **Light quality afects fower sex diferentiation of cucumber seedlings through regulating plant hormone signaling transduction**

In this study, we found that under a higher proportion of blue light (R2B1) treatment, the frst female fower node declined signifcantly and the number of female fowers within ffteen plant nodes increased markedly (Table [1](#page-2-0)). Transcriptome analysis of cucumber seedlings under different light treatment by RNA-seq technology showed that there were more DEGs under R2B1 treatment. Tese DEGs were mostly involved in the hormone signaling pathway (Table [3](#page-7-0)). Tis suggested that a higher proportion of blue light regulated female fower formation through mediating plant hormone signaling pathways.

Plant sex diferentiation is closely related to plant hormones, such as ethylene, which is considered as a potent sex hormone in cucumbers that can induce formation of female fowers (Trebitsh et al. [1987;](#page-17-0) Rudich et al. [1972](#page-17-1); Li et al. [2017\)](#page-16-23). Another example is the gibberellins, which can promote the formation of male fowers via ethylene-dependent and ethylene-independent pathways in cucumber (Hao et al. [2003](#page-16-24)). ABA can promote male fower development (Zhu et al. [2010](#page-17-8)), while auxin is able to promote the female tendency of vegetables and induce female fower development through triggering ethylene synthesis (Atsmon and Tabbak [1979;](#page-16-25) Galun et al. [1962](#page-16-26)). Exogenous CTK application can turn the grape male fower into a female fower (Chang et al. [1999](#page-16-27); Negi and Olmo [1966\)](#page-17-18). JA can regulate gynoecium development (Figueroa and Browse [2015](#page-16-28)), while BR induces androecium development in maize and inhibits gynoecium development (Hartwig et al. [2011](#page-16-29); Makarevitch et al. [2012](#page-17-19)). In this study, we found that among these DEGs related to plant hormone transduction pathways, the specifc DEGs related with IAA formed the higher proportion of DEGs in plant hormone signal transduction, which was followed by the specifc DEGs related with CTK, ABA, ETH, BR, JA and SA (Fig. [5\)](#page-8-0).

Ethylene is considered as a potent sex hormone in cucumber that can induce formation of female fowers (Malepszy and Niemirowicz-Szczytt [1991](#page-17-20)). Ethylene content in shoot apices of gynoecious cucumbers is higher than that of monoecious plants (Rudich et al. [1972;](#page-17-1) Fujita and Fujieda [1981;](#page-16-30) Trebitsh et al. [1987](#page-17-0)). Treatment with exogenous ethylene or ethylene-releasing reagents can increase the numbers of female and bisexual fowers in monoecious and andromonoecious lines, respectively (Mcmurray and Miller [1968;](#page-17-21) Iwahori et al. [1969](#page-16-31)). Until now, the molecular mechanism of ethylene-regulated sex determination of cucumber has been well understood. Ethylene biosynthetic genes and ethylene signal transduction genes are involved in the sex expression of cucumber (Wang et al. [2010\)](#page-17-2). In the ethylene signal transduction pathway, the receptors, such as ETRs, function as negative regulators, while ERFs (downstream components of receptors) act as positive transcription factors to regulate sex determination (Tao et al. [2018;](#page-17-22) Prescott et al. [2016](#page-17-23)). In our study, the expression levels of some *ERFs* were increased under R2B1 treatment compared to R4B1 treatment (Fig. [6a](#page-9-0)). Tey were probably involved in cucumber sex expression through inhibiting the diferentiation into males and promoting the diferentiation into females. However, this understanding was based on bioinformatics analysis. Te precise roles of *ERFs* in cucumber fower development remained unclear and should be verifed in future studies using advanced physiological and molecular techniques. *CTR1* is a negative regulator of ethylene signaling, while *CsCTR1* expression gradually declined during male fower development and increased during female fower development (Bie et al. [2014](#page-16-32)). In our study, *CsCTR1* expression increased on the 5th day after a higher proportion of blue light treatment.

Ethylene plays a key role in plant sex determination, with several research results proving that there is communication between auxin and ethylene during plant development (Trebitsh et al. [1987;](#page-17-0) Gao et al. [2015;](#page-16-33) Wang et al. [2018\)](#page-17-24). In our study, we found a considerably number of transcription factors involving in auxin signaling pathway altered their expression levels under R2B1 treatment (Fig. [6\)](#page-9-0). IAA may play a key role in efect of light quality on sex diferentiation in cucumber. IAA has been considered as a transcription factor activated by phytochrome, which participates in signaling transduction. During the early development of plants, there are several reactions between phytochrome and auxin, such as auxin homeostasis promoted by light (Zhang et al. [2014c\)](#page-17-25). Auxin is an important regulator in the development of male fowers (Sakata et al. [2010\)](#page-17-9). Te auxin synthesis pathway can be regulated by aldehyde oxidases, which have been identifed in cucumber (Brown and Purves [1978\)](#page-16-34). Aldehyde oxidases are involved in the conversions of indole-3-acetaldehyde to IAA (Mano and Nemoto [2012\)](#page-17-26). Higher activity of aldehyde oxidase has also been measured in the auxin-overproducing superroot1 (sur1) mutant of Arabidopsis (Seo et al. [1998\)](#page-17-27). *Aldehyde oxidase* was reported to regulate auxin and ABA biosynthesis pathway (Koshiba et al. [1996](#page-16-35); Seo et al. [1998](#page-17-27), [2000\)](#page-17-28). In our study, the expression of *Aldehyde oxidase* (*Csa4G269130*) was down-regulated in R2B1 samples (Additional fle [5](#page-15-6): Table S2), which suggested that the auxin synthesis or ABA synthesis in cucumber seedlings fowers was low under a higher proportion of blue light. Tis result was contradicted against the fnding that more female fower were formed in the R2B1 samples which might result from higher auxin content. Meanwhile, it was also contrary to the most up-regulated auxin responsive genes *SAURs* (Fig. [6\)](#page-9-0). While the expression of auxin transporting gene LAX1 and PIN1 were also altered indicating that the auxin transport was changed inside the cucumber fower. Even though the low biosynthesis might be found in the fowers, the higher transportation might also cause a high auxin content in the fower tissues. However, auxin content detection and gene function analysis experiments should be performed to confrm this hypothesis.

Gibberellins, a class of tetracyclic diterpenoid phytohormones, can promote the diferentiation of cucumbers into male fowers. GA production in andromonoecious cucumbers is higher than that in gynoecious and monoecious plants (Junior et al. [1972](#page-16-4)). Exogenous GA3 application can increase the ratio of males to females in monoecious cucumbers and induce the formation of male fowers in gynoecious plants (Wittwer and Bukovac [1962](#page-17-29); Pike and Peterson [1969](#page-17-3)). In our study, we identifed two DEGs involved in the GA synthesis pathway. Ent-copalyl diphosphate synthase (CPS) is limiting for ent-kaurene production in the frst portion of the GA synthesis pathway (Prisic and Peters [2007\)](#page-17-30). *CsCPS1* was down-regulated in R2B1-5 and upregulated in R4B1-15 (Fig. [6\)](#page-9-0), which suggested that a high proportion of blue light reduces levels of GA in cucumber seedling fowers through breaking down the *CPS1* gene expression. GA2-oxidase (GA2ox) is also a key gene for GA synthesis pathway. It catalyzes catabolism and inactivation of bioactive GAs or their precursors (Schomburg and Amasino [2003;](#page-17-31) Lester et al. [1999](#page-16-36); Tomas et al. [1999](#page-17-32)). In our study, *CsGA2ox2* was upregulated in R2B1-10 samples (Fig. [6\)](#page-9-0), which suggested that a high proportion of blue light reduces active GA levels through inducing the expression of *CsGA2ox2*. In addition, the GA signaling pathway is involved in stamen and anther development in hermaphroditic plants, such as Arabidopsis and rice (*Oryza sativa*) (Aya et al. [2009;](#page-16-37) Plackett et al. [2011](#page-17-33); Sun [2011\)](#page-17-34). However, in this study, we did not identify any DEGs involved in GA signaling pathway.

PIF, phytochrome interaction factor, plays a central role in light signal transduction mediated by phytochrome (De and Prat [2014](#page-16-38)). PIF3 can activate the transcription factor *EIN3* directly, which was involved in the ethylene signaling pathway (Zhong et al. [2012](#page-17-35)). *COI1*, *JAZ*, *MYC2* and *JAR1* involved in the JA signaling pathway afects the light signal response (Kazan and Manners [2011\)](#page-16-39). *PHYB* negatively regulated ABA accumulation through mediating light signals, while *PHYA* positively regulated ABA signaling pathway under farred light treatment (Gu et al. [2012;](#page-16-40) Mach [2014\)](#page-17-36). Tus, it was seen that light could regulate plant sex diferentiation through mediating hormones by light receptors. Te phytohormones, auxin, BR, CK, ETH, GA and JA, infuenced sex diferentiation in fowering plants (Durand and Durand [2010](#page-16-41); Louis and Durand [1978](#page-17-37); Chailakhyan [1979;](#page-16-42) Irish and Nelson [1989](#page-16-43)). Our results showed that the expression of some key genes involved in ABA, auxin, CK, ETH, GA and JA biosynthesis pathways signifcantly changed during development and sex expression, which suggesting that these hormones might participate in these processes. Tus, it is interesting to investigate how these hormones interact with one another to regulate the abortion of male fowers in gynoecious plants.

## **Light quality afects fowering time of cucumber seedlings through regulating transcription factors**

A total of 433 transcription factors have been identifed by RNA-seq technology. Among them, ethylene transcription factor accounted for 24.02%, *MYB* transcription factor accounted for 20.79%, *bHLH* transcription factor accounted for 16.17% and *WRKY* transcription factor accounted for 10.62%.

Most studies have shown that *MYB*, *bHLH* and *WRKY* transcription factors play an important role in biotic stress and abiotic stress (Oh et al. [2012;](#page-17-38) Li et al. [2010](#page-16-44); Jiang and Yu [2009\)](#page-16-45), but some studies proved that they were also related to fower development (Wang et al. [2011](#page-17-39); Luo et al. [2013](#page-17-40); Zhang et al. [2014b\)](#page-17-4). In Arabidopsis, JA mediates the transcription factors *MYB21* and *MYB24* to regulate stamen development (Song et al. [2011](#page-17-41)), while *MYC5* regulated stamen development by activating JA signaling pathway through inducing *MYB21* expression. *PIFs* belongs to *bHLH* family of transcription factors, which interact with activated Pr to initiate phytochrome signaling transduction. In Arabidopsis, *PIF4* accelerated fowering by activating the *FT* gene (Kumar et al. [2016\)](#page-16-46). *WRKY* family members are key factors for the ABA response pathway (Rushton et al. [2012](#page-17-42)). It had been shown that *WRKY* transcription factors regulated plant fowering. *WRKY25* accelerated fowering through negatively regulating *AP1* directly or indirectly (Wang et al. [2011\)](#page-17-39). In pea, *GsWRKY20* promoted fowering by regulating the fowering related genes *FT*, *SOC1* and *CO* (Luo et al. [2013](#page-17-40)).

## **Materials and methods**

#### **Plant materials and treatments**

LED light produced by Kedao technology corporation (Huizhou, China) (Red light: 650–660 nm and Blue light: 450–460 nm) was chosen for this study. Cucumber (*Cucumis sativus* L. cv. Huaqing No. 5) seeds were cultivated on a sponge block with a Yamasaki culture solution. When cotyledon appeared, we placed these seedlings under light conditions as follows: 300 μmol/ m2 /s, 12/12 (light dark) at 25 °C and a relative humidity of 70–80%. Tere were 2 treatments with ratios of red to blue being 4:1 (R4B1) and 2:1 (R21B), respectively. Each treatment lasted 20 days. As the fower primordium appeared 10 days after light treatment, the shoot apices and leaves after 5, 10 and 15 days of light treatments were collected immediately fash frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until use. Two biological replicates of shoot apices were used for RNA-seq analysis and realtime RT-PCR. After 20 days of treatment, 30–40 seedlings from each treatment were transplanted into the greenhouse of South China Agriculture University with a relative humidity ranging from 70 to 85% and 28 °C/20 °C (14 h/10 h) day/night temperature for further fowering time analysis.

#### **cDNA library construction and illumina sequencing**

Te cucumber shoot apices stored at −80 °C were provided to Guangzhou Gene Denovo Biological Technology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). RNA isolation and RNA-Seq library preparation and sequencing were carried out by Guangzhou Gene Denovo Biological Technology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). In brief, the workfow of library construction for transcriptome analysis was as follows: After RNA was collected, poly(A)-containing mRNA was purifed using oligo(dT) magnetic beads. Following this, the mRNAs were fragmented and cDNA was synthesized using a random hexamer, DNA polymerase I and RNase H. Te double-stranded cDNAs were purifed and ligated to adaptors for Illumina paired-end sequencing. PCR amplication was done on the purifed cDNAs with ligated adaptors. PCR products were separated by 2% agarose gel. Te fnal PCR product chosen for sequencing is 400– 500 bp band, which were cut and recovered from the gel. Te quality and quantity of the library were verifed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer and ABI real time RT-PCR, respectively (Additional fle [6](#page-15-7): Table S3 and Additional fle [2](#page-15-3): Figure S1). Te cDNA libraries were sequenced using Illumina HiSeqTM 2500 by Gene Denovo Co. (Guangzhou, China). Te libraries of two biological replicates of shoot apices were prepared independently.

#### **Sequence read mapping and assembly**

Te original image data produced by sequencing were transferred into sequences (raw reads). Te calculating methods for raw reads was conducted according to Cock ([2010\)](#page-16-47). To obtain clean reads for further analysis, the raw reads were fltered by removing adaptor sequences, lowquality reads and reads with a percentage of unknown bases (N) of more than 10%. Following this, we used Bowtie to remove the ribosome reads (Langmead et al. [2009](#page-16-48)). Te clean reads were mapped to the cucumber genome assembly (Chinese long) v2 [\(http://www.icugi](http://www.icugi.org/cgi-bin/ICuGI/index.cgi) [.org/cgi-bin/ICuGI/index.cgi\)](http://www.icugi.org/cgi-bin/ICuGI/index.cgi) according to Kim et al. ([2013\)](#page-16-49) and Trapnell et al. ([2012\)](#page-17-43).

## **Quantifcation and diferential expression analysis of transcripts**

Te expression level of each gene was determined by the numbers of reads, which were uniquely mapped to the specifc gene and the total number of uniquely mapped reads in the sample. Reads per kilobase of exon model per million mapped reads (RPKM) were calculated according to Mortazavi to estimate gene expression levels. Te FDR was used to determine the threshold of the P-value in multiple tests. In our study, a FDR< 0.05 and an absolute value of |log2 (fold change)| > 1 were used as the threshold to determine the signifcant DEGs.

#### **GO and KEGG enrichment analysis**

To identify putative biological functions and pathways for the DEGs, the Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of gene and genomes (KEGG) database were searched for annotation. Te methods for GO and KEGG enrichment analysis were according to Young and Mao respectively (Young et al. [2010;](#page-17-44) Mao et al. [2005\)](#page-17-45).

#### **Real‑time RT‑PCR**

Total RNA was isolated from 100 mg cucumber leaves and shoot apices using Huayueyang reagent kit (with DNase I step: Pipet the DNase I onto the membrane containing RNA. Incubate at 37 °C for 30 min) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Te RNA quality and purity were verifed by Nanodrop 2000 and electrophoresis on 1.0% agarose gels. Te frst-strand cDNA was synthesized from aliquots of 1 μg of total RNA using PrimeScriptTM RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa, RR047A) in a reaction volume of 20 μL. Te synthesized cDNA was diluted 20 times with sterile water and used as the template for real-time PCR. Te reactions were carried out in a Roche LightCycler 480 system with SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (Tli RNaseH Plus) (Takara Bio, Dalian, China). Te reactions reagent mix was 5 μL SYBR Premix Ex Taq II, 1.5 μL cDNA template, 0.4 μL each primer (10 μmol/μL), and 2.7 μL nuclease-free water. Te amplifcation program was 95 °C for 30 s and 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s. Melting curve analyses were performed at the end of 40 cycles (95 °C for 5 s followed by a constant increase from 60 to 95 °C). In addition, TUA was used to normalize expression levels (Wan et al. [2010](#page-17-14)), and the relative expression of genes was calculated using the 2<sup>−</sup>∆∆Ct method (Livak and Schmittgen [2001](#page-16-50)). Te results were analyzed by Excel 2010. qPCR reactions in leaves were prepared in biological triplicate. qPCR reactions in shoot apices for RNA-seq validation were prepared in three biological replicates.

Genes used for Real-Time RT-PCR were designed on the NCBI primer blast website ([https://www.ncbi.nlm.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/) [nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/)). Te primer quality and efciency were checked by qPCR on series of diluting cDNA. Te expression of 26 genes chosen from RNAseq were analyzed by real-time RT-PCR in shoot apex samples (R2B1 and R4B1 at 5 days, 10 days and 15 days). Meanwhile, 9 genes were used for checking light signal transduction in leaves (Additional fle [7](#page-15-8): Table S4).
