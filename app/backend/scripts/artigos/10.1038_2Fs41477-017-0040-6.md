# GC-rich coding sequences reduce transposon-like, small RNA-mediated transgene silencing

Lyudmila V. Sidorenko<sup>1</sup>, Tzuu-fen Lee<sup>2,4</sup>, Aaron Woosley<sup>1</sup>, William A. Moskal<sup>1</sup>, Scott A. Bevan<sup>1</sup>, P. Ann Owens Merlo<sup>1</sup>, Terence A. Meyers<sup>2,3\*</sup>

The molecular basis of transgene susceptibility to silencing is poorly characterized in plants; thus, we evaluated several transgene design parameters as means to reduce heritable transgene silencing. Analyses of *Arabidopsis* plants with transgenes encoding a microalgal polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) synthase revealed that small RNA (sRNA)-mediated silencing, combined with the use of repetitive regulatory elements, led to aggressive transposon-like silencing of canola-biased PUFA synthase transgenes. Diversifying regulatory sequences and using native microalgal coding sequences (CDSs) with higher GC content improved transgene expression and resulted in a remarkable trans-generational stability via reduced accumulation of sRNAs and DNA methylation. Further experiments in maize with transgenes individually expressing three crystal (Cry) proteins from *Bacillus thuringiensis* (*Bt*) tested the impact of CDS recoding using different codon bias tables. Transgenes with higher GC content exhibited increased transcript and protein accumulation. These results demonstrate that the sequence composition of transgene CDSs can directly impact silencing, providing design strategies for increasing transgene expression levels and reducing risks of heritable loss of transgene expression.

ransgene silencing is a significant challenge for plant genetic engineering<sup>1</sup> as it leads to phenotypic variability and eventual loss of transgene expression<sup>2-4</sup>. Precocious silencing is especially detrimental to biotechnological applications of agriculturally important crops, such as maize and canola. Many of these species are laborious to transform and have long breeding cycles; thus, cost and risks of failure for product development increase when silencing occurs. Improved transgene design could help achieve the desired levels of transgene expression and ensure that expression is stable over multiple generations. Insights into mechanisms of silencing have been gained through studies of transposable element (TE) inactivation and silencing. These pathways are largely conserved between eudicots such as Arabidopsis and monocots such as maize5. Two major routes leading to heritable TE silencing have been described: identity-based and expression-dependent silencing<sup>6</sup>. Identity-based silencing relies on a small RNA (sRNA)-mediated transfer of silencing from an endogenous, inactivated TE to a newly introduced, active TE. Expression-dependent silencing afflicts heterologous TEs with no sequence similarity to the host genome and depends on the de novo recruitment of silencing machinery to an actively transcribed TE<sup>6,7</sup>.

Three distinct stages of heritable de novo TE silencing are recognized: initiation, establishment and maintenance<sup>6–9</sup>. In the initiation stage, silencing can be triggered by direct degradation of Pol II transcripts via the DICER-LIKE 3 (DCL3) RNA-directed DNA methylation pathway (DCL3-RdDM)<sup>10</sup>, and/or after transcript conversion to double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) via the RNA-DEPENDENT RNA POLYMERASE 6 (RDR6) RdDM pathway (RDR6-RdDM)<sup>11</sup>. In the latter pathway, dsRNAs are processed into 21/22-nucleotide (nt) short-interfering RNAs (siRNAs) that guide messenger RNA

(transcript) cleavage or translational repression, resulting in posttranscriptional gene silencing (PTGS). Establishment of heritable silencing results from the recruitment of methyltransferases during RdDM which can lead to de novo methylation of TE sequences<sup>6,11,12</sup>. Once the repressive marks of cytosine methylation are established, the silenced state of the TE is maintained and reinforced by the action of the 24-nt siRNA-directed DNA methylation pathway. This pathway relies on two plant-specific Pol II-like RNA polymerases, Pol IV and Pol V, referred to as Pol IV-RdDM<sup>5</sup>. Whereas the initial de novo methylation remains reversible, TEs are progressively silenced over generations eventually reaching a steady state of silencing. The silenced state is then maintained by Pol IV-RdDM along with other repressive chromatin modifications, and is known as transcriptional gene silencing (TGS)<sup>6,8,9,11,13</sup>. These mechanisms provide a general framework for understanding TE silencing; however, specific means for alleviating transgene silencing in biotechnological applications remain poorly understood, especially in crops. There are relatively few studies that provide detailed insights<sup>14,15</sup>, and practitioners are largely left to trial-and-error approaches to avoid transgene silencing.

Here, we present an in-depth analysis of silencing using a multigene polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) synthase system in *Arabidopsis*<sup>16</sup>. Our work provides insights into the impact of regulatory sequence diversity and increased GC content of coding sequences (CDSs) on transgene expression and trans-generational trait stability. To assess the translatability of our observations with higher GC content PUFA CDSs to other transgenes and plant species, we investigated the influence of CDS composition on the expression of transgenes encoding crystal proteins from *Bacillus thuringiensis* in maize. #### Results

PUFA synthase transgenes with repeated regulatory elements and synthetic CDSs undergo rapid transposon-like silencing in Arabidopsis. The transgenic production of docosahexaenoic and eicosapentaenoic long chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (LC-PUFAs) is achieved by seed-specific expression of four genes: PFA1, PFA2 and PFA3 encoding the PUFA synthase system from the microalga Schizochytrium sp. ATCC PTA-9695 (Schizo9695), and a gene encoding a phosphopantetheinyl-transferase (HETI), an auxiliary factor required for PFA1 activation<sup>16</sup>. To assess the inheritance of PUFA synthase transgene expression, we first focused on two constructs, pDAB454 and pDAB496 (Fig. 1, Supplementary Fig. 1a). In pDAB454, the expression of canola-biased PFA1, PFA2, PFA3 and HetI CDSs was controlled by the PvDlec2 promoter and At2S terminator driving all four transgenes. In pDAB496, both regulatory sequences and CDS optimizations were diversified by alternating PvDlec2/At2S and PvPhas/AtuOrf23 promoter/terminator pairs and incorporating a native microalgal PFA3 CDS (Fig. 1a). T<sub>2</sub> seeds from plants transformed with pDAB496 produced more LC-PUFA than those transformed with pDAB454. However, a negative gene dosage effect of transgene copy number was observed for both constructs in the T<sub>1</sub> (Supplementary Fig. 1b) and T<sub>2</sub> (Supplementary Fig. 1c) generations. Thus, a significant reduction in LC-PUFA accumulation typically occurred in subsequent generations, suggesting that plants transformed with both constructs suffered from active, trans-generational transgene silencing, although to differing extents.

Silencing is often characterized by increased sRNA and decreased transcript levels. To investigate this in the pDAB454 and pDAB496 transgenes, we employed transcript and sRNA profiling of total RNA extracted from developing siliques (Supplementary Fig. 1d). The pDAB454 transgenic plants showed abundant 24-nt siRNAs within the PFA1 and PFA2 CDSs and the PvDlec2 promoter, coinciding with low transcript and LC-PUFA levels (Supplementary Fig. 1e). In contrast, 21/22-nt siRNAs were most abundant in plants transformed with pDAB496, whereas accumulation of 24-nt siRNAs was minimal. The transgenes were still expressed, as apparent from the moderate to high LC-PUFA accumulation (Supplementary Fig. 1d,e). Our data indicate that instability of LC-PUFA production in pDAB454 transgenic plants was consistent with aggressive 24-nt-mediated Pol IV-RdDM and TGS targeting both coding and promoter regions, and that these transgenes approached a stable, silenced state. In contrast, pDAB496 most likely suffered from 21/22-nt mediated PTGS silencing, indicative of the initiation or early establishment stage of silencing.

To specifically identify the pathways involved in pDAB454 and pDAB496 silencing, we transformed these two constructs into mutant backgrounds deficient in RDR6 and SUPPRESSOR OF GENE SILENCING 3 (SGS3) involved in 21/22-nt siRNA-mediated RDR6-RdDM and PTGS, as well as RNA-DEPENDENT RNA POLYMERASE 2 (RDR2) and NUCLEAR RNA POLYMERASE D2A/D2B (NRPD2A/2B) involved in 24-nt siRNA-mediated Pol IV-RdDM and TGS (see Supplementary Methods, Supplementary Fig. 2a). These genes have roles in establishing and maintaining silencing as described in the introduction. Establishment of transgene silencing as measured by LC-PUFA levels was alleviated for both constructs, but in markedly different ways. Silencing of pDAB454 was suppressed in the rdr2 and nrpd2a/2b mutants, but was relatively unaffected in the *rdr6* and *sgs3* mutants (Fig. 1b, Supplementary Figs. 2b,c). In contrast, pDAB496 silencing was disrupted not only in the rdr2 and nrpd2a/2b mutants, but also in the rdr6 and to a lesser degree in the sgs3 mutant backgrounds

**Use of diversified regulatory elements and native microalgal CDSs reduced transgene silencing and improved LC-PUFA accumulation in** *Arabidopsis***.** We next expanded our study to include two additional constructs (Fig. [2a](#page-2-0)) with further diversified regulatory elements and CDS biases. pDAB588 contained fully diversified regulatory elements driving canola-biased genes, whereas pDAB525 had repeated *PvDlec2/At2S* regulatory elements and native microalgal versions of all three *PFA* genes. In contrast to the strong silencing in pDAB454 lines, most pDAB588 lines showed stable LC-PUFA production in T3 seeds from heterozygous parents, whereas only a slight LC-PUFA reduction was observed in seeds from homozygous plants (Fig. [2b](#page-2-0)). Thus, full diversification of regulatory sequences improved the stability of transgene expression. A more surprising observation was that for pDAB525 lines, LC-PUFA production remained high in T3 seeds from hemizygous and homozygous plants, and the transgenes continued to express stably without any sign of trait silencing for five consecutive generations (Fig. [2b](#page-2-0), Supplementary Fig. 4). Thus, this construct with all native microalgal CDS exhibited improved LC-PUFA accumulation and had remarkable trans-generational trait stability. This stability of transgene expression was observed even though all transgenes were driven by the same *PvDlec2/At2S* regulatory sequences, creating repeats that were potentially detrimental for expression when used with the canola-biased CDSs in pDAB454.

To gain further insight into the distinct attributes of silencing occurring with constructs pDAB454, pDAB588 and pDAB525, we conducted RNA profiling and protein analyses. In contrast to the first study, in which the pDAB454 transgenes were predominantly silenced, we preselected plants to represent a range of LC-PUFA expression levels. From high LC-PUFA-producing T2 plants, we selected and analysed T3 progeny representing high, medium and low LC-PUFA producers from two transgenic events of pDAB454 and pDAB588 (left-hand panel in Fig. [2c\)](#page-2-0). Because all pDAB525 plants had similar LC-PUFA levels (indicative of robust transgene expression), only one plant was selected for each of two transgenic events. Transcript and protein abundance (Fig. [2c](#page-2-0)) correlated well with LC-PUFA levels in the selected plants. Analysis of sRNA profiles revealed a dynamic picture (Fig. [2c](#page-2-0)). For pDAB454, high LC-PUFA producers predominantly accumulated 21/22-nt siRNAs derived from coding sequences, resembling ongoing PTGS. In medium LC-PUFA producers, 21/22-nt siRNAs were most abundant, although the increased abundance of 24-nt siRNAs suggested more pronounced Pol IV-RdDM. Poor LC-PUFA producers exhibited mostly 24-nt siRNAs in both coding sequences and repeated *PvDlec2* promoters, indicating a near steady-state of silencing. The shift from 21/22- to 24-nt accumulation within the CDSs and the presence of 24-nt siRNAs in the regulatory sequences is reminiscent of de novo silencing of active TEs through RDR6-RdDM[11](#page-8-8)[,12.](#page-8-9) The involvement of the 21/22-nt siRNAs in pDAB454 silencing was somewhat surprising because mutant analyses revealed no significant role for RDR6 and SGS3 (Fig. [1b](#page-1-0), Supplementary Fig. 2b). Effective engagement of Pol IV-RdDM at the repeated *PvDlec2* promoters may accelerate silencing progression, leading to shortened initiation and establishment stages of silencing, thus making RDR6- RdDM dispensable.

The transcript and siRNA profiles of pDAB588 plants yielded a slightly different result. Plants producing higher LC-PUFA levels displayed low siRNA accumulation, whereas medium producers accumulated 21-nt and began to accumulate some 24-nt siRNAs in *PFA2* and *PFA3*; low producers accumulated predominantly 24-nt siRNAs in *PFA2* and *PFA3* CDSs and 21/22-nt siRNAs in *PFA1* (Fig. [2c](#page-2-0)). In contrast to pDAB454, there were few siRNAs within the *PvDlec2*

promoter (Fig. [2c\)](#page-2-0) or other promoters of analysed pDAB588 plants (data not shown). The slower progression of silencing in pDAB588 compared to pDAB454 may result from a delayed engagement of Pol-IV RdDM and TGS, perhaps attributable to the only difference between the constructs, the diversified regulatory sequences.

Interestingly, analysis of pDAB525 demonstrated the presence of moderately abundant 21/22-nt siRNAs, indicating that some sRNAmediated transcript degradation does occur in this phenotypically stable construct. However, few 24-nt siRNAs were detected in the CDSs or the repeated *PvDlec2* promoters, suggesting that the transition from PTGS to Pol IV-RdDM and TGS was impaired in this construct. The main difference between pDAB454 and pDAB525 is the use of native microalgal PFA CDSs; therefore, these sequences must be responsible for the disruption of the transition to 24-nt siRNA accumulation.

**Improved LC-PUFA accumulation for** *Arabidopsis* **transgenes with native microalgal CDSs correlated with reduced sRNA abundances and low DNA methylation.** DNA methylation is a primary component of heritable silencing; thus, we determined the extent of cytosine methylation in the PUFA synthase transgenes. As expected, in pDAB454 and pDAB588, lower LC-PUFA levels correlated with higher cytosine methylation in all sequence contexts (CG, CHG and CHH, where H is any base but G) (Fig. [3a](#page-4-0)). The highest frequency of methylation was observed for CHG and CG (up to 42% and 32%, respectively), whereas methylation at CHH was lower (up to 6%, which is still high for genic region[s20](#page-8-17)). In addition to the increased frequency of methylated cytosines, ratios of methylation contexts among the methylated cytosines also varied (Supplementary Fig. 5); the percentage of CHH methylation decreased from 67% to 21% whereas symmetric CHG methylation increased from 27% to 68%. This shift in the ratios of methylation from siRNA-dependent CHH to siRNA-independent CHG methylation is consistent with a transition from initiation to a maintenance stage of silencing. Within the genes, denser methylation coincided with higher 21- and 24-nt siRNA abundances at the 3' end of the CDSs (Fig. [3b,](#page-4-0) Supplementary Fig. 6a), consistent with RdDM. Overall, the predominance of CHG methylation (Fig. [3c\)](#page-4-0) was distinct from that of *Arabidopsis* protein-coding genes and transposons (Fig. [3](#page-4-0), Supplementary Fig. 6b), yet similar to a transcriptionally active, transgenic *Athila* transposon undergoing inactivation via the RDR6-RdDM pathwa[y11.](#page-8-8)

To test whether silenced pDAB454 PUFA synthase transgenes can be reactivated with reduced methylation, we introgressed one of the pDAB454 events (Supplementary Fig. 3a,c) into mutants deficient in either CHH/CG or CHH/CHG methylation. Maintaining pDAB454 in these mutant backgrounds for two or three generations resulted in no LC-PUFA recovery (Supplementary Fig. 3c). This indicates that even prolonged maintenance of silenced transgenes in genetic backgrounds defective for CHH/CG or CHH/CHG methylation was not sufficient to recover expression, reinforcing the notion that heritable silencing of large transgenic stacks is difficult to disrupt. The overall patterns, densities and progression of cytosine methylation at the PUFA synthase transgenic stacks were very similar to those reported for active endogenous or heterologous transposons undergoing initiation and establishment of silencing through RDR6-RdDM[6](#page-8-4),[11.](#page-8-8) Like active transposons, LC-PUFA transgenes exhibited dsRNA-derived siRNA production from the 3' end of transcripts followed by RdDM (Fig. [3b\)](#page-4-0) and eventual establishment of dense symmetric methylation that renders steady state of silencing, characterized by siRNA-independent maintenance (Supplementary Fig. 3b).

Transgenes with synthetic GC-rich CDSs exhibit increased Cry protein accumulation in maize. Observations of high and stable PUFA synthase transgene expression in Arabidopsis and canola<sup>16</sup> led us to test whether high GC CDSs can improve expression of other types of transgenes in maize. Pest damage in maize results in considerable vield losses worldwide and transgene-based expression of proteinaceous Bt toxins is an effective tool for plant defense<sup>22</sup>. Successful plant expression of transgenes encoding proteins from bacteria requires redesign of A/T-rich (~75% A/T) bacterial CDSs to a plant-like codon usage<sup>23,24</sup>, simultaneously increasing GC content and eliminating A/T-rich stretches containing spurious polyadenvlation signals that lead to rapid mRNA decay<sup>23,25</sup>. While the impact of this re-coding on reducing transcript decay has been demonstrated previously<sup>23,25</sup>, we aimed to test whether codon biasing strategies that increase GC content above that of the average maize codon usage will improve transgene expression via reduced sRNAmediated silencing. For this, we deployed three distinct codon biasing strategies based on the codon usage table of 2,482 maize genes from the Kazusa database (Supplementary Methods). The resulting codon usage, %GC along gene lengths and %GC3/%GC statistics for the three crystal proteins (Cry1Ab, Cry1Fa and Cry2Aa) from Bt are shown in Supplementary Figs. 8 and 9. The first codon biasing strategy, denoted as Zm, utilized the average maize codon usage as closely as possible resulting in CDSs with 59.8-60.6 %GC3 and 49.4-53.9 %GC. The second biasing strategy, Zm<sup>Mod</sup>, was modified by removing predicted plant poly(A) motifs<sup>26</sup> causing modest changes of %GC3 (50.5-54.3%) and %GC (49.2-51.3%). The third biasing strategy, Zm<sup>HiGC</sup>, was further modified by preferential use of GC3 codons<sup>26</sup> producing CDSs with high %GC3 (84.8 to 87.1%) and %GC (57.4 to 59.8%). All synthetic CDSs were cloned into the same disarmed tumour-inducing (Ti) plasmid (T-DNA) vector (Fig. 4a, Supplementary Methods) and transformed in maize. Analyses of low copy (1–2 transgene copies)  $T_0$  plants revealed that *cry1Ab* and cry1Fa transgenes with ZmHiGC CDSs had higher average leaf protein accumulation, whereas transgenes with Zm and  $\rm Zm^{Mod}$  CDSs exhibited lower protein levels (Supplementary Table 4). For cry2Aa transgenes, Zm<sup>HiGC</sup> resulted in higher although not statistically different from Zm<sup>Mod</sup> average leaf protein levels (Supplementary Table 4). Further testing of  $T_1$  progeny from single-copy transgenic events (Supplementary Fig. 10a) showed that for all three Cry proteins (Cry1Ab, Cry1Fa and Cry2Aa) accumulation was the highest in plants from ZmHiGC constructs, whereas Zm and ZmMod constructs resulted in substantially lower protein levels (Fig. 4b,c, Supplementary Fig. 10b–d).

#### **Discussion**

Our results with diverse transgenes in two evolutionarily distant species provide new insights into the mechanisms of transgene silencing and yield improved design strategies that can be deployed for increasing transgene expression. Our results in *Arabidopsis* showed that canola-biased LC-PUFA transgenes with repeated regulatory sequences experienced siRNA-mediated silencing that led to heritable loss of expression resembling expression-dependent silencing of active heterologous TE[s6](#page-8-4)[,7](#page-8-5)[,11,](#page-8-8)[12](#page-8-9). The diversification of regulatory sequences and the use of one microalgal high GC CDSs delayed the onset of heritable silencing, whereas a remarkable improvement of transgene expression was achieved by using three microalgal CDSs with high GC content. These improvements were similar to those observed in mutants impaired in TGS and PTGS, and were especially remarkable because they occurred in spite of repetitive regulatory sequences that are known to increase susceptibility to silencing. While the precise mechanisms responsible for the improved stability of PUFA synthase transgene expression remain to be elucidated, increased transgene expression was reported for high GC transgenes in mammalian cell[s31](#page-8-26) and *Chlamydomonas*[32](#page-8-27)[,33.](#page-8-28) A correlation between GC- and GC3 richness and higher expression for endogenous plant genes has also been reported[34](#page-8-29). Several mechanisms acting independently, cooperatively or cumulatively may contribute to the remarkable stability of transgenes containing GC3/GC-rich CDS. In addition to the impacts of codon biasing on transcript secondary structure and translatio[n35–](#page-8-30)[37,](#page-8-31) transcription and transcript stability of GC-rich CDSs may be also affected[38,](#page-8-32) leading to lower accumulation of aberrant transcripts and 21/22-nt siRNAs—possibly below a threshold required to trigger RDR6-RdDM. Alternatively, GC-rich DNA could influence nucleosome positioning, nucleosome occupancy and histone modifications[39](#page-8-33),[40](#page-8-34), potentially creating chromatin recalcitrant to silencing and favourable for expression. Another possibility is that GC-rich DNA may be an unfavourable substrate for the Pol IV and/or Pol V complexes, the key factors in Pol IV-RdDM (reviewed[41](#page-8-35)). Additionally, it was recently shown that Pol V is absolutely required for the transition from the establishment to maintenance of TE silencing[6](#page-8-4) . Reduced processivity of Pol-IV and Pol-V complexe[s42,](#page-8-36)[43](#page-8-37), or impaired targeting of these complexes to the GC-rich unmethylated DNA, may contribute to improved trans-generational stability of transgene expression.

The broader applicability of our observations in *Arabidopsis* with PUFA synthase transgenes was further tested in maize with transgenes expressing heterologous Cry proteins. Our results demonstrated that ZmHiGC transgenes exhibited higher protein accumulation, associated with higher transcript levels and reduced siRNAs abundances. These observations in a monocot crop plant support our conclusions in the model eudicot plant *Arabidopsis* that high GC CDSs have a positive impact on transgene expression. The ZmHiGC transgenes remained expressed after four generations of testing, although variability of protein accumulation in individual transgenic plants was observed. We speculate that transitive silencing mediated by the 32 bp identical footprint is a likely cause for this variation. While we cannot exclude a possibility that transgene silencing starts independently and simultaneously at different transgenes, removing sequence identities that are greater than those required for siRNA targeting[44,](#page-8-38) or re-designing transgenes to reduce siRNA production via balanced promoter terminator combinations[45](#page-8-39), may be recommended as future strategies for minimizing possibilities of identity-based cosilencing of transgenes.

In summary, our studies of plant transgenes yielded the insight that GC-rich coding sequences impair establishment of TE-like heritable transgene silencing, resulting in increased average transcript and protein accumulation. These improvements were observed even in the presence of repeated regulatory elements in *Arabidopsis* and increased variability associated with sRNA-mediated cosilencing of the two maize transgenes. Further refinement of transgene designs based on high GC CDSs, diversification of regulatory sequences and removal of sequence identities that may transfer sRNA silencing between the transgenes provides simple and cost-effective steps for improving transgene expression, even without attenuating endogenous RNA-silencing mechanisms. These observations are applicable in a wide range of plant species.
