#### 32 **Abstract**

33

34 The OsbZIP23 transcription factor has been characterized for its essential role in drought 35 resistance in rice, but the mechanism is unknown. In this study, we first investigated the 36 transcriptional activation of OsbZIP23. A homolog of SnRK2 protein kinase SAPK2 was 37 found to interact with and phosphorylate OsbZIP23 for its transcriptional activation. 38 SAPK2 also interacted with OsPP2C49, an ABI1 homolog, which deactivated the 39 SAPK2 to inhibit the transcriptional activation activity of OsbZIP23. Next, we performed 40 genome-wide identification of OsbZIP23 targets by immunoprecipitation sequencing 41 (ChIP-seq) and RNA Sequencing (RNA-Seq) analyses in the OsbZIP23-overexpression, 42 *osbzip23* mutant, and wild-type rice under normal and drought stress conditions. 43 OsbZIP23 directly regulates a large number of reported genes that function in stress 44 response, hormone signaling, and developmental processes. Among these targets, we 45 found that OsbZIP23 could positively regulate *OsPP2C49*, and overexpression of 46 *OsPP2C49* in rice resulted in significantly decreased sensitivity of the ABA response and 47 rapid dehydration. Moreover, *OsNCED4* (9-cis-epoxycarotenoid dioxygenase 4), a key 48 gene in ABA biosynthesis, was also positively regulated by OsbZIP23. Together, our 49 results suggest that OsbZIP23 acts as a central regulator in ABA signaling and 50 biosynthesis, and drought resistance in rice.

52 **Introduction** 

53

54 Drought has become one of the most serious problems restricting the worldwide 55 productivity of rice due to global climate change, fresh water shortage, and increasing 56 population. To improve the drought resistance of rice, many approaches such as 57 marker-assisted selection, genome-wide association, and reverse genetics screening have 58 been applied and significant progress has been achieved (Hu and Xiong, 2014).

59 ABA signaling plays critical roles in the drought response, and it has been well 60 characterized after the discovery of the ABA receptor in Arabidopsis (Fujii et al., 2009; 61 Ma et al., 2009; Park et al., 2009; Santiago et al., 2009). Under drought stress, ABA 62 accumulates in plant cells and is then perceived by an ABA receptor. Pyrabactin 63 resistance 1 (PYR1)/PYR1-like (PYL)/regulatory components of the ABA receptor 64 (RCAR)) inhibits the phosphatase activities of clade A protein phosphatase 2Cs (PP2Cs), 65 and inactivation of PP2Cs causes the activation of sucrose non-fermenting-1 66 (SNF1)-related protein kinase 2 (SnRK2s), which further activates the downstream 67 substrates such as transcription factors and membrane ion channels (Fujii et al., 2009; Ma 68 et al., 2009; Park et al., 2009; Santiago et al., 2009; Zhao et al., 2013).

69 Through bioinformatics analysis, homologues of ABA signaling components in rice 70 have been identified. The rice genome contains 12 OsPYLs (Kim et al., 2012; He et al., 71 2014), 10 clade A PP2Cs (Xue et al., 2008) and 10 osmotic stress/ABA-activated protein 72 kinases (SAPKs) (Kobayashi et al., 2004). The molecular functions and interactions 73 between some of these components were investigated in recent years. Most OsPYLs 74 (OsPYL1-3, OsPYL6, and OsPYL10-11) can significantly inhibit the phosphatase 75 activity of OsPP2C06 and OsPP2C09 in the presence of ABA, respectively (He et al., 76 2014). However, OsPYL12, which cannot bind to ABA acts as a dimer form to 77 constitutively inhibit OsPP2C06 and OsPP2C09 (He et al., 2014). OsPYL/RCAR5 was 78 found to interact with OsPP2C30 and OsPP2C49 in yeast with the presence of ABA, and 79 OsPP2C30 could interact with SAPK2 in a BiFC assay that ultimately negatively 80 regulates the transcriptional activity of OsREB1 (Kim et al., 2012).

81 A genome-wide and expression analysis of clade A PP2Cs in rice and Arabidopsis 82 indicates that most of them were induced by ABA and drought stress or mannitol

83 treatment, and the ABA response-related element (ABRE) motif was significantly 84 enriched in the promoter region of them (Xue et al., 2008; Singh et al., 2010). 85 Overexpression of the rice clade A PP2C gene *OsPP2C108* in Arabidopsis enhanced the 86 tolerance to salt, mannitol and drought stresses, and sensitive to ABA treatment (Singh et 87 al., 2015). Recently, another clade A PP2C gene *OsABIL2* was demonstrated as a 88 negative regulator in ABA signaling (Li et al., 2015). In the presence of ABA the 89 OsPYL1-ABA-OsABIL2 complex causes a subcellular redistribution of OsABL2 and 90 releases the inhibition on SAPK8 and SAPK10 in the nucleus (Li et al., 2015).

91 All of the 10 SAPKs were activated by hyperosmotic stress and SAPK8, 9, and 10 92 were also activated by ABA (Kobayashi et al., 2004). The bZIP transcription factor, 93 TRAB1 (Transcription factor responsible for ABA regulation) was phosphorylated by 94 SAPK8, 9, and 10, and the Ser102 of TRAB1 was critical for its activation function 95 (Hobo et al., 1999; Kagaya et al., 2002; Kobayashi et al., 2005). Recently, TE (Tiller 96 Enhancer) was found to be another substrate of SAPK8, 9, and 10. TE interacts with 97 ABA receptors (OsPYL2, 9, and 10) and promotes their degradation by the proteasome, 98 while phosphorylated TE could inhibit its interaction with OsPYL10 and prevent the 99 degradation of OsPYL10 (Lin et al., 2015). The OSRK1 (SAPK6) can phosphorylate and 100 activate the bZIP transcription factor OREB1, and ectopic expression of OSRK1 in 101 tobacco reduced the sensitivity to ABA in germinating seeds as well as root elongation 102 (Chae et al., 2007). A more recent work using an *in vitro* phosphorylation assay and *in*  103 *vivo* dual-luciferase assay indicated that SAPK2, SAPK6, and SAPK9 can phosphorylate 104 and activate OsbZIP46, a subgroup A bZIP transcription factor (Tang et al., 2012; Kim et 105 al., 2015).

106 Subgroup A bZIP transcription factors play important roles in ABA and drought 107 stress response in plants, and their activation mechanisms are conserved between 108 Arabidopsis and rice (Lu et al., 2009; E et al., 2014). Generally, subgroup A bZIP 109 transcription factors need SnRKs to fully activate their transcriptional activation activity 110 (Kagaya et al., 2002; Kobayashi et al., 2005; Chae et al., 2007; Fujii et al., 2007; Fujii 111 and Zhu, 2009; Yoshida et al., 2010; Kim et al., 2015). In rice, 11 subgroup A bZIP 112 transcription factors have been identified, and 6 of them have been associated with 113 diverse biological functions (Hobo et al., 1999; Zou et al., 2007; Xiang et al., 2008; Zou 114 et al., 2008; Lu et al., 2009; Amir Hossain et al., 2010; Hossain et al., 2010; Yang et al., 115 2011; Tang et al., 2012). Transgenic plants with overexpressed OsbZIP23 show 116 significantly improved drought and salt tolerance and sensitivity to ABA (Xiang et al., 117 2008). OsbZIP46 (ABL1) has a high sequence identity to OsbZIP23. Unlike OsbZIP23, 118 overexpression of intact OsbZIP46 showed increased ABA sensitivity but a slightly 119 decreased drought tolerance at the seedling and reproductive stages. In contrast, 120 overexpression of a constitutively active form of OsbZIP46 (OsbZIP46CA1) could 121 activate downstream genes and enhance drought tolerance (Tang et al., 2012). However, 122 despite their importance to drought tolerance in rice, little is known about the underlying 123 molecular mechanisms of the subfamily A bZIP transcription factors, and their target 124 genes have seldom been systematically investigated in plants.

125 In the present study, we identified the upstream regulators of OsbZIP23, SAPK2, 126 and OsPP2C49, and investigated their roles in the transcriptional activation of OsbZIP23 127 in rice. SAPK2 could directly phosphorylate and activate OsbZIP23, while OsPP2C49 128 negatively regulates SAPK2 and further inhibits the transcription activity of OsbZIP23. 129 Genome-wide identification of the direct target genes of OsbZIP23 indicates that 130 OsbZIP23 regulates numerous processes including stress response, hormone signaling, 131 and development. Interestingly, we found that OsbZIP23 could directly and positively 132 regulate *OsPP2C49*, and thus regulate ABA signaling. In addition, OsbZIP23 also 133 regulated the ABA level by directly targeting the ABA synthesis gene *OsNCED4*. These 134 results establish a complete regulatory network of OsbZIP23 in the ABA signaling 135 pathway and the activation of downstream drought-related genes in rice.

### 137 **Results**

138

# 139 **Phosphorylated OsbZIP23 activates gene expression.**

140 OsbZIP23 has been characterized as a positive transcriptional regulator of drought 141 resistance in rice (Xiang et al., 2008). To further understand the transcriptional activity of 142 OsbZIP23 in rice, we performed a dual-luciferase transient transcriptional activity assay 143 in rice protoplasts. OsbZIP23 was fused with the yeast GAL4 binding domain (GAL4BD) 144 as an effector plasmid and co-transformed into rice protoplasts with a firefly luciferase

145 reporter gene driven by a promoter consisted of five copies of the GAL4 binding site 146 (GAL4-fLUC) (Fig. 1A). We only detected a slight increase in the transcriptional 147 activation activity of OsbZIP23, but not statistically significant under normal conditions 148 (Fig. 1B). However, after ABA treatment the transcriptional activation activity of 149 OsbZIP23 was significantly induced (Fig. 1B). This result suggested that OsbZIP23 acts 150 depending on the ABA signaling pathway, and that an upstream activator may be 151 required for its activation under ABA treatment.

152 The ABA signaling component SnRK2 protein kinases have been proven to 153 activate bZIP transcription factors via phosphorylation in plants, and two SnRK 154 homologs SAPK2 and SAPK6 in rice can phosphorylate OsbZIP46, which has a high 155 sequence identity with OsbZIP23 (Tang et al., 2012). We examined whether OsbZIP23 is 156 also activated by SAPKs. Bimolecular fluorescence complementation (BiFC) assay 157 suggested that OsbZIP23 could interact with SAPK2 and SAPK6 in rice protoplasts (Fig. 158 1C). The interaction between SAPK2 and OsbZIP23 was further confirmed by an *in vitro* 159 pull down assay and an *in vivo* Co-IP assay (Fig.1D and 1E). Furthermore, the 160 phosphorylation activity of SAPK2 and SAPK6 towards OsbZIP23 were demonstrated by 161 an *in vitro* phosphorylation assay (Fig. 1F). To further investigate whether the 162 phosphorylated OsbZIP23 could activate gene expression in rice, SAPK2 was 163 co-transformed with OsbZIP23 into rice protoplasts. We found the transcriptional activity 164 of OsbZIP23 was significantly enhanced even without ABA (Fig. 1B), suggesting that 165 SAPK2 can activate OsbZIP23, which is consistent with a previous study (Kim et al., 166 2015).

# 168 **Conserved Ser/Thr is required for full activation of OsbZIP23**

169 OsbZIP23 exhibits high sequence similarity with TRAB1 and OsbZIP46 (Supplemental 170 Fig. S1), whose activation has been previously reported (Kagaya et al., 2002; Tang et al., 171 2012). The phosphorylation site Ser102 in TRAB1 is conserved in OsbZIP23 (Ser106) 172 and OsbZIP46 (Ser103) (Supplemental Fig. S1). Moreover, a previous study has shown 173 that a conserved R-Q-X-S/T sequence in OREB1 can be recognized and phosphorylated 174 by OSRK1 (Chae et al., 2007). There are three R-Q-X-S/T motifs in OsbZIP23, 175 OsbZIP46, and TRAB1 (Supplemental Fig. S1). To further investigate the putative 176 phosphorylation sites of OsbZIP23, we generated multiple amino acid substitutions in 177 OsbZIP23 and checked their transcriptional activation activities in rice protoplasts 178 (Supplemental Fig. S2). We found that single amino acid substitutions (S/T to A) could 179 significantly decrease the transcriptional activation activity of OsbZIP23 under ABA 180 treatment, but no significant differences were observed among the mutants of the four 181 putative phosphorylation sites (Supplemental Fig. S2). Multiple (two to four) amino acid 182 substitutions in the putative phosphorylation sites almost abolished the transcriptional 183 activity of OsbZIP23 under ABA treatment (Supplemental Fig. S2). To further examine 184 the activation sites of OsbZIP23 by SAPK2, the mutated forms of OsbZIP23 with 185 multiple putative phosphorylation sites substituted were co-transformed with SAPK2 into 186 rice protoplasts, and the results showed that the transcriptional activity was also 187 significantly decreased (Supplemental Fig. S2). These results suggest that the four 188 Ser/Thr sites are required for the full activation of OsbZIP23 under ABA treatment and 189 through activation by SAPK2.

# 191 **OsPP2C49 negatively regulates OsbZIP23 transcriptional activity through** 192 **deactivation of SAPK2**

193 A previous study has shown that OsPP2C49, a clade A protein phosphatase 2C, could 194 interact with a putative ABA receptor OsPYL/RCAR5 in an ABA dependent fashion 195 (Kim et al., 2012). We asked whether OsPP2C49 acts in the ABA signaling pathway 196 through SAPK2 and OsbZIP23. To validate this, we preformed *in vitro* pull down, BiFC, 197 and Co-IP assays, and the results suggested that OsPP2C49 could interact with SAPK2 198 (Fig. 2, A-C). Moreover, when OsPP2C49, SAPK2, and OsbZIP23 were co-transformed 199 into the rice protoplasts, we found the transcriptional activation activity of OsbZIP23 was 200 almost abolished (Fig. 2D and 2E), suggesting that OsPP2C49 indeed acts upstream of 201 SAPK2 and negatively regulates OsbZIP23 activation. The results above together with 202 our previous genetic study (Xiang et al., 2008) suggest that OsbZIP23 functions in the 203 classical ABA signaling pathway, and its transcriptional activation is regulated through 204 phosphorylation.

# 206 **Identification of OsbZIP23 binding sites by ChIP-Seq**

207 To further understand how OsbZIP23 regulates ABA signaling and confers drought 208 resistance, we applied ChIP-Seq to identify the genome-wide binding sites of OsbZIP23. 209 The ChIP assay was performed by using a specific antibody against OsbZIP23 210 (Supplemental Fig. S3) to pull down the putative OsbZIP23-bound DNA from the leaf 211 tissues of wild-type (WT) rice Zhonghua 11 under normal growth conditions (ZH11N), 212 and under moderate drought stress treatment (ZH11D), and OsbZIP23-overexpression 213 rice under normal growth conditions (OX-OsbZIP23N). Three biological replicates for 214 each tissue were used to generate ChIP-Seq libraries. After sequencing, we obtained 215 7,797,925, 9,927,059, and 6,963,151 unique mapped reads in ZH11N, ZH11D, and 216 OX-OsbZIP23N, respectively (Supplemental Table S1). The unique mapped reads from 217 the three samples were mainly located within 500 bp upstream of the TSS (transcription 218 start site) (Fig. 3A). By using Model-based Analysis of ChIP-Seq (MACS) software 219 (Zhang et al., 2008), we identified 1738, 4260, and 11,611 peaks in ZH11N, ZH11D, and 220 OX-OsbZIP23N, respectively (Supplemental Fig. S4). Further genomic distribution 221 analysis revealed that a large proportion of OsbZIP23-associated peaks (29.11% in 222 ZH11N, 39.6% in ZH11D, and 48.52% in OX-OsbZIP23N) were located within 2,000 bp 223 upstream of the TSS region (Fig. 3B-D). Among the peaks, we identified 681, 2128, and 224 7194 putative OsbZIP23-bound genes in ZH11N, ZH11D, and OX-OsbZIP23N, 225 respectively (Fig. 3E). By counting the overlapped OsbZIP23-bound genes between the 226 ZH11N-ZH11D and ZH11D-OX-OsbZIP23N datasets (Fig. 3E, the red triangle), a total 227 of 1,471 genes were defined as OsbZIP23-bound genes involved in drought stress 228 (OsbZIP23BGD) (Supplemental File 1). Gene ontology analysis of OsbZIP23BGD 229 showed that the number of genes in response to abiotic stress stimulus, regulation of 230 transcription, and plant hormone signaling transduction were significantly enriched 231 (Supplemental Fig. S5). Furthermore, we found that 152 (10.3%, p-Value< 2.2e-16) 232 OsbZIP23BGD genes had known or suggestive functions (Extract from RicENcode, 233 http://ricencode.ncpgr.cn), and 48.7% (74 out of 152) of them were regulated by drought 234 stress (Supplemental File 1). These OsbZIP23BGD genes with known or suggestive 235 functions provide direct evidence and molecular mechanisms for OsbZIP23 in the 236 regulation of a wide range of stress and hormone responses (Supplemental Fig. S6). In 237 addition, 115 (7.8% of OsbZIP23BGD, p-value = 2.75e-4) transcription factors were 238 enriched and 35.7% (41 out of 115) of them were responsive to drought stress treatment 239 (Supplemental File 1). The quality of the ChIP-Seq results were validated by a 240 ChIP-quantitative PCR (qPCR) assay for 15 randomly selected genes (Supplemental Fig. 241 S7). To investigate the binding motifs of OsbZIP23, ±100 bp flanking sequences around 242 the peaks were submitted to MEME-ChIP (http://meme-suite.org/tools/meme-chip) to 243 search for enriched motifs. The most frequently captured motifs belonged to the ABA 244 responsive elements (ABREs) (Fig 4A and 4B). An EMSA assay with point mutation 245 verified that ACGTG is the core sequence of OsbZIP23 binding in the promoters of the 246 target genes (Fig. 4C).

#### 248 **Target genes are directly regulated by OsbZIP23 under drought stress**

249 To identify the direct target genes regulated by OsbZIP23 at the transcript level, the same 250 rice materials as those used for ChIP-Seq were used for RNA-Seq. We used both the 251 overexpression and mutant seedlings of OsbZIP23 under normal and drought stress 252 conditions to generate RNA-Seq libraries. A total of 758, 448, 664, and 1465 253 differentially expressed genes were found in *osbzip23* (compared to WT) under normal 254 conditions (*osbzip23*N), OsbZIP23-overexpresssion plants under normal conditions 255 (OX-OsbZIP23N), *osbzip23* under drought stress (*osbzip*23D), and 256 OsbZIP23-overexpression plants under moderate drought conditions (OX-OsbZIP23D), 257 respectively (Fig. 5A; Supplemental File 2). The high quality of the RNA-Seq results was 258 validated by reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) assay for 29 randomly 259 selected genes (Supplemental Fig. S8). The direct target genes of OsbZIP23 were 260 identified by combining the datasets of differentially expressed genes with the 261 OsbZIP23-bound genes from the ChIP-Seq datasets (Fig. 5B). A total of 42, 148, and 500 262 direct target genes of OsbZIP23 were identified in the ZH11N, ZH11D, and 263 OX-OsbZIP23N samples, respectively (Fig. 5C). Among them, 113 direct target genes 264 involved in drought stress (defined as OsbZIP23TGD, indicated by a red square in Fig. 265 5C; Supplemental File 3) were further analyzed for their differential expression patterns 266 (Fig. 5D). We found that 51.3% (58 out of 113) of OsbZIP23TGDs were induced by 267 drought stress treatment, while only 13.2% (15 out of 113) of OsbZIP23TGDs were 268 suppressed by drought stress treatment (Supplemental File 3). Moreover, we identified 14 269 OsbZIP23TGD genes with known function in drought response or drought resistance 270 such as OsbZIP23 itself (Xiang et al 2008), OCPI1 (Huang et al., 2007), OsLEA3-2 271 (Duan and Cai, 2012), OsSRO1c (You et al., 2013; You et al., 2014), and OsETOL1 (Du 272 et al., 2014) (Supplemental File 3). These results further demonstrate that OsbZIP23 273 plays an important role in regulating drought resistance through direct binding to the 274 promoters of drought stress related genes.

#### 276 **OsbZIP23 directly and positively regulates the expression of** *OsPP2C49*

277 By carefully checking the gene identities of the OsbZIP23TGDs, we were surprised to 278 find that *OsPP2C49* which is mentioned above is also a target gene of OsbZIP23. A 279 distinctive peak was detected approximately 1 Kb upstream of the TSS of *OsPP2C49* in 280 all three ChIP-Seq libraries (Fig. 6A and 6B). ChIP-qPCR showed that OsbZIP23 281 specifically binds to the 500 bp region upstream of the TSS (Fig. 6C). We selected a 59 282 bp sequence around the peak summit as a probe which contained a typical G-box 283 (CACGTG) to conduct EMSA, and the results confirmed the specific binding by 284 OsbZIP23 to the *OsPP2C49* promoter (Fig. 6D). RNA-Seq data suggested that the 285 transcript level of *OsPP2C49* was positively regulated by OsbZIP23, and this was 286 confirmed by RT-qPCR (Fig. 6E). Furthermore, we performed a dual-luciferase transient 287 transcriptional activity assay to further confirm the regulation of *OsPP2C49* by 288 OsbZIP23 (Fig. 6F). The *OsPP2C49* promoter-driven reporter was up-regulated only 289 when SAPK2 or ABA was added (Fig. 6G). In addition, we also observed that OsPP2C49 290 had a negative effect towards the activation of its own promoter through the deactivation 291 of SAPK2 (Fig. 6G). These results together suggest that OsbZIP23 can directly and 292 positively regulate the expression level of *OsPP2C49*.

# 294 **OsPP2C49 is a negative regulator of ABA signaling in rice**

295 Since OsPP2C49 is a close homologue of ABI1, which has been well characterized as a 296 negative regulator of ABA signaling in Arabidopsis, we further investigated the biological 297 function of OsPP2C49 in ABA signaling in rice by generating OsPP2C49-overexpression 298 plants (OX-*OsPP2C49*) to examine the ABA sensitivity (Fig. 7A). As shown in Fig 7B,

299 the relative plant height of the overexpression line (OX-*OsPP2C49*-32) was significantly

300 higher than the WT (ZH11) and the negative transgenic line (OX-*OsPP2C49*-13) under 301 ABA treatment (Fig. 7C). Moreover, we observed a rapid dehydration phenotype of the 302 OsPP2C49 over-expression lines when compared to the WT and the negative transgenic 303 controls (Fig. 7D), which is similar to the phenotype observed in the overexpression 304 plants of another ABI1-like OsPP2C gene (Li et al., 2015a). These results together 305 indicate that OsPP2C49 acts as an important negative regulator in ABA signaling and 306 drought response in rice. By using CRISPR-CAS9 technology, we generated a mutant of 307 *OsPP2C49*, which contains a point mutation in the coding region. However, this mutant 308 exhibited ABA sensitivity similar to WT (Supplemental Fig. 9), which may be due to 309 functional redundancy of the clade A PP2C subfamily.

#### 311 **OsbZIP23 affects ABA levels through the positive regulation of** *OsNCED4*

312 Interestingly, we noticed that *OsNCED4*, encoding a key enzyme in ABA synthesis, is 313 also a target gene of OsbZIP23 (Fig. 8A and 8B). A previous study showed that the 314 *OsNCED4* expression level is correlated with the ABA level in rice (Liang et al., 2014). 315 In the ChIP-Seq analysis, the *OsNCED4* promoter sequence was highly enriched in the 316 ZH11D and OX-OsbZIP23N samples, which was further confirmed by ChIP-qPCR (Fig. 317 8C). The EMSA assay with a 51 bp probe around the peak summit suggested that 318 OsbZIP23 could directly bind to the promoter of *OsNCED4* (Fig. 8D). In addition, 319 OsbZIP23 also positively affected the expression level of *OsNCED4*. In the 320 OsbZIP23-overexpression seedlings, *OsNCED4* was significantly upregulated, while 321 *OsNCED4* was downregulated in *osbzip23* (Fig. 8E). Since OsNCED4 is a key enzyme in 322 ABA synthesis, we further measured the ABA levels in the *OsbZIP23* overexpression and 323 *osbzip23* plants. As shown in Fig 8F, the ABA level was significantly increased in the 324 overexpression plants but decreased in the *osbzip23* mutants. In addition, we found other 325 four genes including *MHZ5*, *PSY1*, *LCYe* and *DSM1* that are involved in ABA 326 biosynthesis could be also directly bound by OsbZIP23 (Supplemental Fig. 10). These 327 results suggest that OsbZIP23 also functions as a positive regulator of ABA biosynthesis 328 in rice.

### 330 **Discussion**

331

332 Our previous study found that OsbZIP23 confers ABA-dependent drought resistance in 333 rice. In this study, we further revealed the role of this transcription factor in the regulation 334 of ABA signaling and the expression of downstream genes. We provided strong evidence 335 that SAPK2 and OsPP2C49 act as positive and negative regulators, respectively, of 336 OsbZIP23. We also identified the genome-wide target genes of OsbZIP23 under normal 337 and drought stress conditions. Most significantly, we found that OsbZIP23 can feedback 338 regulate the ABA signaling components and biosynthesis. These findings, with a working 339 model of OsbZIP23 function presented in Fig 9 and discussed below, have enriched our 340 understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying ABA and drought stress 341 responses.

342

#### 343 **OsbZIP23 needs phosphorylation for its transcriptional activation**

344 The subgroup A bZIP transcription factors such as TRAB1, OsbZIP46, and OsbZIP12 in 345 rice and ABI5, ABRE1, ABRE2, and ABF1 in Arabidopsis generally require ABA for 346 transcriptional activation (Gampala et al., 2002; Kobayashi et al., 2005; Yoshida et al., 347 2010; Kim et al., 2015). Recently, OsbZIP46 was found to be phosphorylated by SAPK2 348 and SAPK6 *in vitro*, and co-transformation of OsbZIP46 with SAPK2, SAPK6, or 349 SAPK9 into rice protoplasts can significantly induce the transcriptional activation activity 350 of OsbZIP46 (Kim et al., 2015). OsbZIP23 shows high similarity with OsbZIP46 351 (Nijhawan et al., 2008). We found that SAPK2 and SAPK6 could also interact and 352 phosphorylate OsbZIP23 and significantly induce the transcriptional activity of 353 OsbZIP23 *in vivo* (Fig. 1), which may indicate that both SAPK2 and SAPK6 354 phosphorylate the conserved Ser/Thr sites in OsbZIP23 and OsbZIP46 such as the 355 R-Q-X-S/T sequence (Chae et al., 2007). This hypothesis was confirmed in our further 356 experiments when we found four Ser/Thr substitutions in OsbZIP23 that almost abolish 357 SAPK2-activated transcriptional activation of OsbZIP23 (Supplemental Fig. S2). 358 However, further experiment is needed to examine which sites could be phosphorylated 359 by SAPK2 in vivo.

360 Without ABA, clade A PP2Cs deactivate SnRKs through de-phosphorylation. Both 361 OsPP2C30 and OsABIL2 could deactivate SAPK2 and then negatively regulate the 362 expression of downstream genes mainly through bZIP transcription factors (Kim et al., 363 2012; Li et al., 2015). In this work, we found that SAPK2 could interact with OsPP2C49. 364 In a dual luciferase assay, OsPP2C49 almost abolished the SAPK2-activated 365 transcriptional activation of OsbZIP23. Moreover, OsPP2C49 was shown to interact with 366 OsPYL5 in an ABA dependent manner, which further supports that OsbZIP23 367 participates in a classical ABA signaling pathway in rice (Fig. 9). The transcriptional 368 activity of OsbZIP46 differed in the activation by SAPK2, SAPK6, and SAPK9, and 369 SAPK2 showed the highest activation (Kim et al., 2015). Whether such difference exists 370 for OsbZIP23 needs to be further tested.

### 372 **OsbZIP23 regulates diverse functional genes mainly involved in stress responses**

373 In this study, we applied ChIP-Seq and RNA-Seq to investigate the downstream genes of 374 OsbZIP23. Our results provide new insights into the gene network regulated by 375 OsbZIP23 underlying the drought resistance of rice. OsbZIP23 mainly binds to the 376 regions 500 bp upstream of the TSSs in all three samples (Fig. 3A), which is consistent 377 with the transcription factor role of OsbZIP23 and with the previous ChIP-Seq results of 378 other transcription factors in Arabidopsis and maize (Zhang et al., 2011; Ram et al., 2014; 379 Li et al., 2015). Moreover, we found that the conserved ABREs are significantly enriched 380 in the OsbZIP23-bound regions (Fig. 4), indicating that OsbZIP23 may mainly target the 381 ABA-responsive genes.

382 The number of genes bound by OsbZIP23 in the three samples differ from each 383 other. A small number of genes were detected in the WT sample under normal conditions 384 (ZH11N), while over 7,000 genes were detected in the OsbZIP23-overexpression lines. 385 Notably, more than 2,000 genes were detected in the WT under drought stress conditions 386 (ZH11D), which is consistent with the expression induction of OsbZIP23 under drought 387 stress conditions (Nijhawan et al., 2008; Xiang et al., 2008). To identify OsbZIP23-bound 388 genes with high confidence, we chose the overlapping genes among the three samples, 389 defined as OsbZIP23BGD (Fig. 3E), for further analysis. The molecular functions of the 390 OsbZIP23BGD genes are very diverse (Supplemental Fig. S5). Almost half of the 391 function-known genes in the OsbZIP23BGD are involved in stress response or hormone 392 signaling including *SNAC1*, *OsbZIP72*, *DWA1*, *OsGARS3*, and *OsBZR1*, which are

393 important components in the plant stress response and hormone pathways (Supplemental 394 Fig. S6). Moreover, we also detected several developmental process related genes such as 395 *OsCO3*, *COPT1*, *PDIL1*, and *NAL1* (Supplemental Fig. S6), which are directly targeted 396 by OsbZIP23. This suggested that OsbZIP23 may also regulate developmental processes 397 in addition to its critical roles in stress response regulation. At least, we observed that the 398 *osbzip23* mutant showed slightly reduced plant height and a change in the heading date. 399 How OsbZIP23 regulates developmental processes in rice may be an interesting project 400 for further study.

401 Genome-wide identification of target genes including LONG HYPOCOTYL 5 402 (HY5), BRASSINAZOLE RESSISTANT1 (BZR1), LEAFY (LFY), and FAR-RED 403 ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL3 (FHY3) in Arabidopsis (Sun et al., 2010; Ouyang et al., 404 2011; Winter et al., 2011; Zhang et al., 2011), KNOTTED1 (KN1) in maize (Bolduc et al., 405 2012), and Oryza sativa homeobox1 (OSH1) and IDEAL PLANT ARCHITECHTURE1 406 (IPA1) in rice (Lu et al., 2013; Tsuda et al., 2014) have demonstrated that transcription 407 factors are significantly enriched. In this work, 115 transcription factors were identified 408 in OsbZIP23BGDs, and over 35% of them were regulated by drought stress 409 (Supplemental File 1). OsbZIP23BGDs include several transcription factors previously 410 shown to play an important role in drought stress, such as OsBZ8, OSVP1, SNAC1, 411 OsGRAS23, OsWRKY45, OsBZR1, TRAB1, OsbZIP72, and OsNAC5 (Supplemental 412 Fig. 6), which further implies that OsbZIP23 acts as a central regulator of drought 413 resistance in rice.

# 415 **OsbZIP23 feedback regulates ABA signaling by directly targeting OsPP2C genes**

416 A recent study showed that clade A PP2C-mediated ABA signaling has evolved in 417 ancestral land plants to control preexisting mechanisms for desiccation tolerance and 418 facilitating rapid propagation on land (Komatsu et al., 2013). As negative regulators in 419 ABA signaling, the expression levels of clade A PP2Cs are also regulated by stresses 420 (Xue et al., 2008). In our RNA-Seq analysis, 7 out of 10 clade A PP2Cs in rice were 421 upregulated by drought stress (Supplemental Table S2), which may imply that the 422 induced expression of clade A PP2Cs may be needed to keep a reasonable scale of the 423 ABA signaling in cells. Investigation of the cis-elements within the 1 Kb promoter region 424 of clade A PP2Cs in rice and Arabidopsis suggests that the frequency of ABREs in the 425 promoter region of clade A PP2Cs is much higher than that in the whole genome (Xue et 426 al., 2008; Singh et al., 2010). The high enrichment of ABREs in the promoter region of 427 PP2Cs indicates that AREB bZIP transcription factors may be involved in the regulation 428 of these PP2C genes. Several studies have reported that clade A PP2Cs were differentially 429 regulated by subgroup A bZIP transcription factors (Xiang et al., 2008; Yoshida et al., 430 2010; Tang et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2014), but it has not been proven whether the bZIP 431 transcription factors can directly regulate their expression levels. Our data clearly 432 suggests that OsbZIP23 can directly and positively regulate the expression of *OsPP2C49*, 433 which acts as a negative regulator of OsbZIP23 by deactivating SAPK2. We checked the 434 ChIP-Seq data for the other clade A *OsPP2Cs*. To our surprise, OsbZIP23 could directly 435 bind to the promoters of 8 other *OsPP2Cs* under drought stress conditions (Supplemental 436 Table S2). However, only *OsPP2C49* and *OsPP2C51* were differentially regulated by 437 OsbZIP23 in the RNA-Seq results (Supplemental Table S2), which indicates that the 438 regulation of different clade A PP2Cs may need different transcription factors such as 439 NACs and MYCs to coordinate with bZIP transcription factors for the differential 440 regulation (Singh et al., 2012; Xu et al., 2013; Maurya et al., 2015).

441 In agreement with *ABI1* and *ABI2* in Arabidopsis and *OsABIL2* in rice (Leung et al., 442 1997; Gosti et al., 1999; Komatsu et al., 2013; Li et al., 2015), overexpression of 443 OsPP2C49 in rice also caused reduced ABA insensitivity and a drought hypersensitivity 444 phenotype. However, a frame-shift mutation in *OsPP2C49*, which was generated by 445 CRISPR-CAS9 technology, did not show an obvious change in the ABA sensitivity 446 (Supplemental Fig. S9). In Arabidopsis, only the mutants with knockouts of multiple 447 clade A PP2Cs can lead to ABA hypersensitivity, and *osabil1* and *osabil2* single 448 mutations in rice also showed no phenotypic changes (Saez et al., 2006; Rubio et al., 449 2009; Li et al., 2015). Therefore, function redundancy may exist among the 10 clade A 450 OsPP2Cs, and mutations of multiple genes may be effective in enhancing the ABA 451 sensitivity and/or drought resistance of rice.

452

### 453 **OsbZIP23 positively regulates the ABA level in rice**

454 Since OsbZIP23 could directly regulate OsPP2C49, an important component of ABA 455 signaling, we wondered whether OsbZIP23 could affect the expression of other upstream 456 components in the ABA signaling pathways. However, except for eight clade A OsPP2Cs 457 and *SAPK3*, all of the *OsPYLs* and the other *SAPKs* were not found in the 458 OsbZIP23BGDs (Supplemental Table S3). Interestingly, 5 ABA synthesis related genes, 459 *DSM2*, *MHZ5*, *PSY1*, *LCY-e*, and *OsNCED4* (Du et al., 2010; Liang et al., 2014; Li et al., 460 2015; Yin et al., 2015) were found to be directly targeted by OsbZIP23 (Supplemental Fig 461 S10), and the ABA level was significantly increased in the OsbZIP23-overexpression line 462 (Fig 9). Such feedback regulation of ABA signaling and biosynthesis is similar to the 463 BZR1 transcription factor, which is a central regulator in brassinosteroid (BR) signaling 464 in Arabidopsis, and the downstream target genes of BZR1 include several BR 465 biosynthesis enzymes and BR signaling components (Sun et al., 2010).

466 Plants have evolved multiple pathways to fine tune ABA signaling including cross 467 talks with other hormone signaling. For example, BRASSINOSTEROID INSENSITIVE 468 2 (BIN2) was found to positively modulate ABA signaling through phosphorylation of 469 subgroup III SnRKs in *Arabidopsis* (Cai et al., 2014), and ARK plays an essential role in 470 the activation of SnRK2 in the moss *Physcomitrella patens* (Cai et al., 2014; Saruhashi et 471 al., 2015). Recently, ABI1 was found to be degraded through ubiquitination by PUB12 472 and PUB13 in the presence of ABA and an ABA receptor in Arabidopsis, which may be 473 regarded as a new positive regulation pathway of ABA signaling (Kong et al., 2015).

474 In this study, we found that ABA signaling as well as ABA biosynthesis can be 475 feedback regulated by OsbZIP23, which has significantly increased our understanding of 476 ABA signaling regulation (Fig. 9). The feedback up-regulation of both ABA synthesis 477 and PP2C49 is quite interesting in terms of fine control of the amplification of stress 478 signaling since PP2C49 is negative regulator of ABA signaling. We propose that 479 feedback up-regulation of ABA synthesis may help amplify ABA signaling especially 480 under the continuous drought stress, meanwhile plants have evolved other mechanisms to 481 avoid over-amplification of the signaling, for example through up-regulation OsPP2C49 482 to attenuate the ABA signaling. Moreover, OsbZIP23 acts as a central transcriptional 483 regulator in drought responses based on the fact that a large number of drought 484 stress-related genes are directly regulated by OsbZIP23 (Supplemental Fig. 6). Therefore,

485 OsbZIP23 may be a promising candidate gene for engineering drought resistance in rice.

#### 489 **Plant materials and growth conditions**

490 Rice Zhonghua 11 (ZH11) (*Oryza sativa* ssp *japonica*) was used as the wild-type. The 491 *OsbZIP23*-overexpression line and the *osbzip23* mutant were from our previous study 492 (Xiang et al., 2008). Seedlings were grown in sand/paddy (1:3) soil under natural long 493 day conditions (about 14 h light/10 h dark) in July at Wuhan. Seedlings at the four-leaf 494 stage were used for drought stress treatments. The stressed plants were harvested until all 495 of the leaves were rolled and the non-stressed samples were collected at the same time. 496 For all of the samples, the aboveground parts of the seedlings were harvested and frozen 497 in liquid nitrogen for RNA isolation or immediately into 1% formaldehyde for chromatin 498 isolation.

499 For generating overexpressed OsPP2C49 transgenic rice plants, the genome 500 fragment that contains the entire OsPP2C49 cDNA fragment amplified with the primer 501 set PP49FK/RB (Supplemental Table S4) was inserted into the *Kpn*I and *Bam*HI sites of 502 the binary expression vector pCAMBIA1301U (driven by a maize ubiquitin promoter). 503 The construct was introduced into rice ZH11 by *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* 504 (EH105)-mediated transformation. For generating *ospp2c49 mutants*, specific sgRNA 505 targeting *OsPP2C49* (Supplemental Table S4) was designed and assembled into the 506 binary expression vector pCXUN-CAS9 (sgRNA was driven by the U3 promoter). The 507 construct was introduced into rice ZH11 by *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* 508 (EH105)-mediated transformation.

## 510 **Protein extraction and Western blot**

511 For nuclear protein extraction, leaves from 4-leaf-old rice plants were ground to a fine 512 powder in liquid nitrogen and then protein was extracted with Extraction buffer 1, 2, and 513 3, which is the same as the nuclear protein extraction method in ChIP assay (Bowler et al., 514 2004). Nuclear protein was separated with a 12% SDS-PAGE gel, and transferred to a

515 PVDF membrane. The membrane was blocked and incubated with the OsbZIP23 516 polyclonal antibody (1/5000) that was generated by the Abmart Company and goat 517 anti-rabbit horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody. The signals were 518 detected with x-ray films (FUJIFILM).

519

#### 520 **ChIP-Seq**

521 Wild-type rice under normal conditions, wild-type rice under drought stress conditions, 522 and overexpressed OsbZIP23 rice under normal conditions were used for ChIP-Seq 523 analysis. ChIP was performed as described previously with some modifications (Zong et 524 al., 2013). Briefly, leaf tissues from four-leaf-old seedling were immediately fixed after 525 harvest by 1% formaldehyde under vacuum for 30 min and 3 g tissues for each sample 526 were used for chromatin isolation. Isolated chromatin was sheared to approximately 100-500 bp by Diagenode® 527 Bioruptor. For ChIP-Seq, the DNA was immunoprecipitated 528 by anti-OsbZIP23 antibody as described previously, and the precipitated DNA was 529 purified and dissolved in distilled water. For each library, three independent replicated 530 samples were prepared and then an equal weight of purified DNA from each replicate 531 was mixed together to generate the sequencing library, which was processed by the 532 Beijing Genomics Institute.

533 ChIP-Seq data processing and analysis were performed as described previously 534 (Zong et al., 2013). Briefly, raw sequencing reads from each library were mapped to the 535 rice genome (RGAP version 7.0, http://rice.plantbiology.msu.edu) using SOAP2 (Li et al., 536 2009), and only uniquely mapped reads were used for peak identification. The MACS 537 software was used to identify OsbZIP23 associated regions with default parameters. 538 The .wig files of the MACS output were visualized using Integrated Genomics Viewer 539 (V.2.3.26) (Robinson et al., 2011). A gene was regarded as an OsbZIP23 bound gene if 540 the promoter region of the gene (including 1 kb upstream of the TSS) had at least 1 bp 541 overlapping with the peaks. To identify enriched motifs, we extracted 200 bp around the 542 peak summits (100 bp upstream and 100 bp downstream) of each library and then 543 subjected them to MEME-ChIP (Machanick and Bailey, 2011; Ma et al., 2014) 544 (http://meme-suite.org/tools/meme-chip). For functional category analysis, KEGG 545 pathway information was collected from the KEGG database (Kanehisa et al., 2012) and

546 the functional category (Rice\_japonica\_mapping\_merged\_08 download) was collected 547 from the Mapman website (Thimm et al., 2004) (http://mapman.gabipd.org). The 548 enriched functions of genes bound by OsbZIP23 under drought stress were determined by 549 using Fisher's exact test. The Gene ontology (GO) of the OsbZIP23 bound genes under 550 drought stress was also performed by the AgriGO software with default parameters (Du 551 et al., 2010).

# 553 **RNA-Seq**

554 RNA-Seq was used to identify target genes of OsbZIP23 by integrating analysis with 555 ChIP-Seq data. Total RNAs were extracted from 4-leaf-old rice seedlings using TRIzol 556 (Invitrogen) reagent according to the user manual. For each library, three independent 557 replicated RNA samples were prepared and 100µg RNA of each replicate was mixed to 558 construct the sequencing library. The libraries were then sequenced with Illumina Hiseq 559 2000, which was processed by the BerryGenomics Company. Clean reads were mapped 560 to the reference genome of rice (RGAP version 7.0) using TopHat (version 2.0.8). To 561 identify differentially expressed genes between the libraries, edgeR (Robinson et al., 562 2010) was used. The gene expression levels were calculated by using RPKM method 563 (Reads Per kb per Million reads). Genes with an expression change fold ≥2 with P value 564 <0.05 were defined as differentially expressed genes.

#### 584 **Dual luciferase transcriptional activity assay in rice protoplasts**

585 Rice protoplasts were isolated from 14-day-old seedlings of ZH11 (*Oryza sativa* spp. 586 *japonica*) as described previously (Xie and Yang, 2013) with some modifications. Briefly, 587 the rice protoplasts were isolated by digesting rice sheath strips in digestion solution (10 588 mM MES pH 5.7, 0.6 M Mannitol, 1 mM CaCl2, 5 mM beta-mercaptoethanol, 0.1% BSA, 589 0.3% Cellulase RS, and 0.75% Macerozume R10) for 4 h with gentle agitation. The 590 protoplasts were then incubated in W5 solution for 30 min, collected by centrifugation at 591 100g for 8 min, and finally re-suspended in MMG solution. For transformation, 3 µg of 592 each plasmid was added together and gently mixed with 100 µl of protoplasts and 110 µl 593 of PEG-CaCl2 solution,which was then incubated at room temperature for 15 min in the 594 dark. Two volumes of W5 solution was added to stop the transformation. The 595 transformed protoplasts were collected by centrifugation and then re-suspended in WI 596 solution. The transformed protoplasts were then divided into two equal samples and 597 incubated for 12 h in the dark. After 12 h of incubation, one of the transformed 598 protoplasts was treated with 2 µM ABA in the dark for 4 h. Finally, the protoplasts were 599 collected by centrifugation at 100g for 8 min and immediately utilized in the luciferase 600 assay.

601 The Dual-Luciferase® Reporter Assay System (Promega) was used to measure the 602 luciferase activity. Briefly, the transformed protoplasts were resuspended in 60 µl of 1× 603 Passive Lysis Buffer, and 40 µl of lysate was used to measure the luciferase activity. 40 604 µl of luciferase assay substrate buffer was added into the lysate and the firefly luciferase 605 (fLUC) activity was measured with the Tecan infinite M200 system. 40 µl of Stop & Glo® 606 substrate buffer was then added to the reaction and the renilla luciferase (rLUC) 607 activity was measured. Three independent transformations for each sample were 608 performed, and the relative luciferase activity was calculated as the ratio between fLUC 609 and rLUC (fLUC/rLUC). 'None' and GAL4BD vectors were used as effectors, 610 35S-GAL4-fLUC, GAL4-fLUC and 190LUC were used as reporters, and *AtUbi*::rLUC 611 was used as an internal control. Primers used for the vector construction are listed in 612 Supplemental Table S4. All of the plasmids used in this assay were purified with the 613 QIAGEN Plasmid Midi Kit.
