# Natural variation of H3K27me3 modification in two *Arabidopsis* accessions and their hybrid

Mei Yang<sup>1</sup>, Xuncheng Wang<sup>2</sup>, Hao Huang<sup>2</sup>, Diqiu Ren<sup>1</sup>, Yan'e Su<sup>3</sup>, Pan Zhu<sup>1</sup>, Danmeng Zhu<sup>1</sup>, Liumin Fan<sup>1</sup>, Liangbi Chen<sup>3</sup>, Guangming He<sup>1\*</sup> and Xing Wang Deng<sup>1\*</sup>

<sup>1</sup>State Key Laboratory of Protein and Plant Gene Research, Peking-Tsinghua Center for Life Sciences, School of Advanced Agricultural Sciences and School of Life Sciences, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China, <sup>2</sup>Tsinghua-Peking Joint Center for Life Sciences, School of Life Sciences, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100084, China, <sup>3</sup>Department of Botany, College of Life Sciences, Hunan Normal University, Changsha 410081, China. \*Correspondences: heguangming@pku.edu.cn; deng@pku.edu.cn (Dr. Xing Wang Deng is fully responsible for distributions of all materials associated with this article)

**Abstract** Histone modifications affect gene expression, but the mechanism and biological consequence of natural variation in histone modifications remain unclear. Here, we generated genome-wide integrated maps of H3K27me3 modification and transcriptome for Col, C24 and their F<sub>1</sub> hybrid. A total of 1,828 genomic regions showing variation in H3K27me3 modification between Col and C24 were identified, most of which were associated with genic regions. Natural variation of H3K27me3 modification between parents could result in allelic bias of H3K27me3 in hybrids. Furthermore, we found that H3K27me3 variation between Col and C24 was negatively correlated with gene expression differences between two accessions, especially with those arising from the *cis*-effect. Importantly, mutation of CLF, an *Arabidopsis* methyltransferase for H3K27, altered gene expression patterns between the parents. Together, these data provide insights into natural variation of histone modifications and their association with gene expression differences between *Arabidopsis* ecotypes.

**Keywords:** Allelic bias; Arabidopsis; gene expression; H3K27me3; natural variation

## INTRODUCTION

In nuclei of eukaryotic cells, histone octamers that are wrapped with DNA constitute the fundamental units of chromatin. Post-translational covalent modifications of histones, sometimes referred to as "histone code", have been shown to play a critical role in the dynamic regulation of higher-order chromatin structure (Jenuwein and Allis 2001; Kouzarides 2007). To date, numerous types of histone modifications have been identified, including acetylation, methylation, ubiquitination, phosphorylation, glycosylation, adenosine diphosphate ribosylation and sumoylation (Kouzarides 2007). The advent of chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by genomic tiling arrays (ChIP-chip) or nextgeneration sequencing (ChIP-seq) has provided researchers with an opportunity to explore histone modification profiles on a genome-wide scale (He et al. 2011). For example, recent studies have shown that different types of histone modifications tend to occur in distinct genomic regions (Bernatavichute et al. 2008; Zhang et al. 2009; He et al. 2010; Roudier et al. 2011). Further, genome-wide distributions of histone modifications vary among different accessions or species, suggesting that variation in histone modification is a naturally occurring process (He et al. 2010; Dong et al. 2012; He et al. 2013; Rintisch et al. 2014). Furthermore, allelic bias of histone modification has been discovered in both intra- and inter-specific hybrids, which may be inherited from parents (He et al. 2010; Dong et al. 2012). However, the molecular mechanisms underlying these phenomena are not fully understood.

Histone modifications have been shown to determine the reversible transition between gene activation and gene silence by dynamically regulating both the accessibility of the genome and the recruitment of chromatin-binding proteins (Jenuwein and Allis 2001; Berger 2007; Kouzarides 2007). Specifically, H3K4me3, H3K9ac, H3K27ac and H3K36me3 are associated with gene activation, while H3K27me3 is associated with gene silence (Zhang et al. 2007; Wang et al. 2009; He et al. 2010; Zhou et al. 2010; Lafos et al. 2011). Moreover, histone modifications have been found to correlate with both gene expression variation between different species and allele-specific gene expression in hybrids (He et al. 2010; Tirosh et al. 2010; Prendergast et al. 2012; Shi et al. 2012).

H3K27me3 is a typical transcriptionally repressive mark (Kouzarides 2007). It was shown that H3K27me3 targets a large number of developmentally important transcription factor genes, which are enriched in stimulus responses (Zhang et al. 2007; Lafos et al. 2011). Taken together, these studies indicate that H3K27me3 modification may play an important role in the dynamic regulation of gene expression during development and stimulus response. H3K27me3 modification is established and maintained by Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) (Simon and Kingston 2013). The SET component of PRC2 is known to possess methyl-transferase activity, and thus has been shown to trimethylate Lys-27 of histone H3 (Czermin et al. 2002). In *Arabidopsis*, three SET domain proteins responsible for H3K27me3 modification have been identified to date, including CURLY LEAF (CLF), MEDEA (MEA), and SWINGER (SWN) (Holec and Berger 2012). Mutation in *CLF* results in pleiotropic phenotypes in *Arabidopsis*, including smaller plant size, curly leaves, early flowering and reduced fertility (Goodrich et al. 1997). It was suggested that growth defects observed in *clf* mutant may be caused by decreased levels of H3K27me3 and increased transcription activity of H3K27me3 target genes. This, in turn, indicates that *CLF* plays a critical role in the regulation of H3K27me3 modification in *Arabidopsis* (Schubert et al. 2006; Jiang et al. 2008; Lafos et al. 2011).

In this paper, we performed genome-wide analyses of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification and gene expression in Col, C<sub>24</sub> and their  $F_1$  hybrid. Natural variation of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification was observed between Col and C<sub>24</sub>, which was negatively correlated with gene expression differences. Furthermore, the patterns of gene expression variation between the parents were changed upon *CLF* mutation.

### RESULTS

# Genome-wide profiles of H3K27me3 modification in Col, C24 and Col $\times$ C24 hybrid

To investigate the natural variation of histone modification in Arabidopsis, we analyzed the genome-wide H3K27me3 profiles of Col, C24 and Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid using ChIP-seq. The 14-d-old shoots of parental lines and the hybrid were used for the construction of Illumina sequencing libraries. Two biological replicates were performed for each genotype. A total of 276.8 million reads were obtained from six sequenced libraries (Table S1). The correlation coefficients between two biological replicates for each genotype range from 0.72 to 0.83, which are high for ChIP-seq assay (Table S2). Hence, we combined reads from two biological replicates of each genotype for H3K27me3 modification analysis. Sequencing reads of Col and C24 were aligned to TAIR10 (https://www. arabidopsis.org/) and C24 reference genome (Schneeberger et al. 2011), respectively, and sequencing reads of  $Col \times C24$ hybrid were aligned to both TAIR10 and C24 reference genome. Only unique mapped reads were used for further analysis. Genomic regions associated with H3K27me3 modification were identified using model-based analysis of ChIP-seq (MACS) software (Zhang et al. 2008). Accordingly, 5 043, 5 432 and 5 940 H3K27me3 regions were identified in Col, C24 and Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid, respectively (Figure S1A; Table S3). The vast majority of H3K27me3 regions (86.3% to 88.2%) were associated with annotated genic regions in both parental lines and the hybrid (Figure S1B). Genes in H3K27me3 regions were considered as H3K27me3 target genes. H3K27me3 ChIP-seq reads were relatively enriched in genic regions of target genes (Figure S2).

# Natural variation of H3K27me3 modification between Col and C24

H3K27me3 regions detected in only one of two accessions (accession-specific H3K27me3 regions) and those detected in both accessions with differential modification levels (differentially modified H3K27me3 regions) were regarded as the natural variation of H3K27me3 modification between Col and C24. We found that 687 H3K27me3 regions were Colspecific, whereas 1098 H3K27me3 regions were C24-specific (Figure 1A). Out of 687 Col-specific H3K27me3 regions, 539 regions (78.5%) overlapped with annotated genic regions, including 679 non-transposable element (TE) genes and 30 TErelated genes (Figure 1B, C). Similarly, 886 (80.7%) of 1,098 C24-specific H3K27me3 regions overlapped with annotated genic regions, including 1,063 non-TE genes and 65 TE-related genes (Figure 1B, C). In addition, 43 differentially modified H3K27me3 regions were identified between Col and C24 by using edgeR (Robinson et al. 2010) with false discovery rate (FDR) of 0.05. Specifically, the H3K27me3 level at 32 regions was significantly higher in Col, whereas the H3K27me3 level at 11 regions was significantly higher in C24 (Figure 1A). #### Allelic bias of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>27</sub>me<sub>3</sub> modification in Col $\times$ C<sub>24</sub> hybrid

The variation of histone modifications in hybrids relative to their parent lines can display either an additive or non-additive manner, which implies that the level of histone modification in hybrids can be, but is not necessarily equal to, the average of two parents (He et al. 2010). We performed hierarchical clustering analysis of the modification levels of H3K27me3 target genes in Col, C24 and Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid. The relative level of H3K27me3 modification for each target gene was normalized to aligned reads per kilobase genic region per million mapped reads (RPKM). It was shown that the  $Col \times C_{24}$  hybrid possessed an intermediate pattern of H3K27me3 modification that lay between two parental extremes (Figure 2A). Furthermore, we found that 1,907 genes displayed additive variation pattern of H3K27me3 modification in the Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid, while only 10 genes displayed non-additive variation pattern (P < 0.05). These data indicated that the natural variation of H3K27me3 modification between Col and C24 may primarily result in additive pattern of H3K27me3 in the hybrid.

We then explored allele-specific H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification in the Col × C<sub>24</sub> hybrid: 1621 H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> regions were identified to display allelic bias of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification using the binomial test (q < 10<sup>-5</sup>) (Figure 2B). Col allele exhibited preferential allelic H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification in 1,165 regions, while C<sub>24</sub> allele exhibited preferential allelic H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification in 456 regions. We further examined the relationship between parental variation of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> and allelic bias of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> in the hybrid. Here, H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> difference between parents was represented by log<sub>2</sub>(Col/ C<sub>24</sub>), and the difference in allelic H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification was represented by log<sub>2</sub>(Col<sub>F1</sub>/C<sub>2</sub>4<sub>F1</sub>). Comparison between log<sub>2</sub>(Col/C<sub>24</sub>) and log<sub>2</sub>(Col<sub>F1</sub>/C<sub>2</sub>4<sub>F1</sub>) for each gene in 1,621 H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> regions identified above showed that allelic difference of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> in the Col × C<sub>24</sub> hybrid was highly positively correlated with H3K27me3 difference between Col and C24 (r = 0.812) (Figure 2C). These results suggested that the natural variation of H3K27me3 modification between accessions may contribute to the allelic bias of H3K27me3 in their hybrid.

### Transcriptomes of Col, C24 and Col $\times$ C24 hybrid

Next, we investigated transcriptomes of Col, C24 and Col  $\times$ C24 hybrid. Three biological replicates were performed for each genotype and 456.9 million reads were generated from nine RNA-seq libraries (Table S1). The correlation coefficient between each pair of three biological replicates for each genotype was high (0.95-0.98) (Table S2). We compared global trends of gene expression among parental lines and the hybrid. As shown in Figure 3A, gene expression trends were different among Col, C24 and Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid (Figure 3A). Then, using edgeR with FDR of 0.05, 2,571 differentially expressed genes between Col and C24 were identified, among which 1,162 genes showed higher expression in Col, whereas 1,409 genes exhibited higher expression in C24 (Figure 3B). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis by agriGO software (Du et al. 2010) revealed that differentially expressed genes were functionally enriched in the stress response pathway.

# H3K27me3 modification variation correlates with gene expression variation

## DISCUSSION

Natural phenotypic variation is known to rise from both natural genetic variation and natural epigenetic variation (Schmitz and Ecker 2012; Turck and Coupland 2014). Natural epigenetic variations include the variation in DNA methylation and the variation in histone modification. Variation in DNA methylation and its impact on plant development have been extensively documented (Richards 2011; Schmitz and Ecker 2012; Turck and Coupland 2014). However, the biological significance, inheritance patterns and regulation of the natural histone modification variation in plants are still largely unknown. In this study, we performed genome-wide comparisons of H3K27me3 modification between Arabidopsis accessions Col and C24, and their hybrid. Differential H3K27me3 modification was observed between Col and C24, which predominantly occurred in genic regions (Figure 1). H3K27me3 modification in the hybrid was mainly additive, which is consistent with a previous study (Moghaddam et al. 2011). Furthermore, allelic bias of H3K27me3 modification was observed in the Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid (Figure 2A,B) and was highly positively correlated with H3K27me3 difference between parents (Figure 2C), suggesting that the epigenetic state of genomic regions marked by H3K27me3 modification in parents was maintained after hybridization. In hybrid cells, two alleles are likely exposed to the same cellular environment. It is possible that H3K27me3 modification difference between two alleles may stem from DNA sequence polymorphisms between parents. Indeed, high single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) frequency was observed in H3K27me3 target genes, which were differentially modified between Col and C24 (Figure S4), indicating that DNA sequence polymorphisms may be associated with H3K27me3 modification variation between Col and C24.

It has previously been shown that H3K27me3 modification is linked to gene expression (Kouzarides 2007). Here, we demonstrated that H3K27me3 modification indeed negatively correlated with gene expression on a genome-wide scale (Figure 4A). Moreover, the variation in H3K27me3 modification observed between Arabidopsis accessions was also negatively correlated with gene expression variation (Figure 4B). Both cis-effect and trans-effect contributed to gene expression variation (Figure 3). H3K27me3 modification variation was mainly correlated with the gene expression variation caused by cis-effect (Figure 4C). cis-effect has been proven to result from sequence divergence of *cis*-regulatory elements (Springer and Stupar 2007; Tirosh et al. 2009; McManus et al. 2010: Shi et al. 2012). As mentioned earlier. H3K27me3 modification variation may be associated with DNA sequence polymorphisms. Therefore, our observations suggested that cis-effect may contribute to gene expression variation through induction of H3K27me3 modification variation between accessions.

CLF, a SET domain methyltransferase, is involved in trimethylation of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>27</sub> in *Arabidopsis*. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that a total of 3,537 genes were activated or repressed in *clf* mutants and their hybrid, and were functionally enriched in the stress response pathway (Figure 5B). In addition, we found that 2,961 genes were differentially expressed between *clf*<sub>Col</sub> and *clf*<sub>C24</sub>, nearly half of which showed the same expression level in Col and C24 (Figure 5C). These results indicated that CLF affects the expression of a lot of genes in Col, C24 and their F<sub>1</sub> hybrid. However, the number of differentially expressed genes associated with H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>27</sub>me<sub>3</sub> modification was small. Only 91, 33, 35 and 26 genes in "*cis* only", "*trans* only", "*cis* + *trans*"

and "*cis-trans*" category, respectively, were differentially modified by H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> between Col and C<sub>24</sub> (Figure 4C). Therefore, gene expression changes in *clf* mutant lines may be largely independent of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub> modification but rather a secondary effect of H<sub>3</sub>K<sub>2</sub>7me<sub>3</sub>-mediated repression or activation of key regulatory genes that are related to expression changes (e.g., stress-responsive genes).

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

#### Plant materials and growth conditions

The Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid was generated by crossing Arabidopsis thaliana accession Col and C24. The clf hybrid was generated by crossing a clf mutant in Col background (SALK 139371) and a clf mutant in C24 background, which was identified from our ethyl methanesulfonate mutant library. The mutation in  $clf_{C24}$ contained a G-to-A substitution at position 2,246 of CDS, which formed a D-to-N amino acid substitution in the SET domain of CLF protein. The Col  $\times$  C24 hybrid, clf hybrid, and both set parents were all grown on Murashige and Skoog plates containing 1% sucrose. The shoots of 14-d-old seedlings were used for ChIP-seq and RNA-seq. For the measurement of biomass, wt plants and clf mutant lines were all grown on Murashige and Skoog plates containing 1% sucrose for 7 d, before transferring to soil for 7 d. The shoots of seedlings were used for measuring fresh weight. All seedlings were grown under short-day conditions (8 h light at 22 °C and 16 h dark at 18 °C).

#### Sequencing library construction

For ChIP-seq, ChIP experiment was performed in accordance with previously published procedures (Gendrel et al. 2005). Briefly, 1.5 g of 14-d-old plant shoots were cross-linked by 1% formaldehyde in a vacuum. Samples were then ground to fine powder in liquid nitrogen. Chromatin was immunoprecipitated with antibody against H3K27me3 (Millipore). The eluted ChIP DNA was used to construct the sequencing library according to the Illumina's manufacturer protocol. Sequencing was then performed using an Illumina HiSeq 2000 platform. Two independent biological replicates were performed for ChIP-seq.

For RNA-seq, total RNA was extracted from shoots of 14old *Arabidopsis* seedlings using Rneasy plant mini kit (Qiagen). Messenger RNAs (mRNAs) were extracted from the total RNAs by Dynabeads oligo(dT) (Invitrogen Dynal). First- and second-strand complementary DNAs (cDNAs) were obtained using random hexamer primers and SuperScript II reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen). Double-stranded cDNAs were fragmented by nebulization and used for mRNA library construction according to the Illumina's manufacturer protocol. Sequencing was then performed using an Illumina HiSeq 2000 platform. RNA-seq was performed for three independent biological replicates.

We uploaded all the raw data to the Gene Expression Omnibus database under accession numbers GSE62615 and GSE62478.

#### RNA-seq and ChIP-seq read mapping

RNA-seq reads of Col were mapped to the Arabidopsis reference Col genome (TAIR 10) (https://www.arabidopsis.

org/) using TopHat software (Trappell et al. 2009) with parameters set to a minimum intron length 20, maximum intron length 6,000, and a maximum of two mismatches. Only unique mapped reads were extracted for the following analysis. To decrease the mapping bias for C24 RNA-seq reads, we first created a genotype-specific C24 reference genome using the SNP data from the Arabidopsis 1001 genomes project (http://1001genomes.org/). We replaced the sequence of the Col reference genome in positions with a SNP between C24 and Col to create an initial C24 genome sequence, and then mapped our RNA-seq reads of C24 to this C24 reference sequence using Tophat software. New SNPs were identified for this aligned RNA-seq data, and the reference sequence was updated from these alignments. We then performed a second round of C24 RNA-seq data alignment using the updated C24 reference genome. The aligned reads were then used for subsequent analysis. To analyze the RNA-seq data of the hybrid, we aligned the sequencing reads to both the Col reference genome and updated C24 genome. Reads were assigned to an allele based upon the highest quality alignment, and were then used to calculate allelic expression divergence.

ChIP-seq data was mapped to the Arabidopsis genome using methods similar to those used for RNA-seq, except that Bowtie was used to map our ChIP-seq reads. MACS software (Zhang et al. 2008) with the default parameters (bandwidth, 300 bp; mfold, 32; P-value of 1.00e-05) was used to identify peaks representing enriched binding sites.

#### Cis-effect and trans-effect assignment

RNA-seq reads mapped to the gene body were used for the assignment of *cis*-effect and *trans*-effect for variation in gene expression between accessions. We calculated *cis*-effect as the  $\log_2$ -transformed ratio of allelic-specific reads mapping to Col and C24 in the hybrid sample:  $\log_2(Col_{F_1}/C24_{F_1})$ . *Trans*-effect was calculated as the difference between the  $\log_2$ -transformed ratio of species-specific reads in the mixed parental and hybrid samples:  $\log_2(Col/C24)-\log_2(Col_{F_1}/C24_{F_1})$ . Binomial exact tests and Fisher's exact tests, with an FDR of 0.05, were used to identify genes with significant *cis*-effect or *trans*-effect.

### Plasmid construction and transgenes

For the transgenic complementation assay,  $\approx$ 8 Kb genomic fragments containing *CLF* gene were amplified from Col and C24 genomic DNA using 5'-CCGGAATTCCGCTTCTAAGTTACGCCCAAGTC-3' and 5'-CGCGGATCCTCCGGCTAATTTAGATGGGGC-3'. These genomic fragments were then cloned into the *EcoRI-Bam*HI site of pCambia1300 vector. The constructs were transformed into *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* GV3101, which were transformed into *clf*<sub>Col</sub> and *clf*<sub>C24</sub> via the floral dip method (Clough and Bent 1998). Transgenic T<sub>1</sub> seeds were selected on Murashige and Skoog plates containing 1% sucrose and 50 mg/L hygromycin B (Roche). Homozygous T<sub>3</sub> plants were used for crossing with *clf* mutants.
