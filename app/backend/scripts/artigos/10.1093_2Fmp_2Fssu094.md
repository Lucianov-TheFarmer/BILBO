The CLE40 and CRN/CLV2 signaling pathways antagonistically control root meristem growth in Arabidopsis

running title: CLE40 and CRN/CLV2 control root meristems

short summary: Peptide-receptor signaling is a prevalent mechanism in the regulation of stem cell differentiation in plant meristems. In this study, we elucidate the roles of the CRN/CLV2 receptor proteins and the CLE40 signaling peptide in the primary root meristem via large-scale transcriptome sequencing and phenotypic analyses, showing that both act in antagonistic pathways on proximal root meristem differentiation.

### **Abstract**

Differentiation processes in the primary root meristem are controlled by several signaling pathways that are regulated by phytohormones or by secreted peptides. Long term maintenance of an active root meristem requires that the generation of new stem cells and the loss of these from the meristem due to differentiation is precisely coordinated. Via phenotypic and large scale transcriptome analyses of mutants, we show that the signaling peptide CLE40 and the receptor proteins CLV2 and CRN act in two genetically separable pathways that antagonistically regulate cell differentiation in the proximal root meristem. *CLE40* inhibits cell differentiation throughout the primary root meristem by controlling genes with roles in abscisic acid, auxin and cytokinin signaling. *CRN* and *CLV2* jointly control target genes that promote cell differentiation specifically in the transition zone of the proximal root meristem. While CRN and CLV2 are not acting in the CLE40 signaling pathway under normal growth conditions, both proteins are required when the levels of CLE40 or related CLE peptides increase. We show here that two antagonistically acting pathways controlling root meristem differentiation can be activated by the same peptide in a dosage dependent manner.

**Key Words**: root meristem, transcriptomics, *CLE40*, *CRN*/*CLV2*, signaling peptides, phytohormones

#### **Introduction**

Postembryonic growth in plants is derived from stem cell populations in the primary meristems at the very tips of shoots and roots. The balance between upkeep and differentiation of stem cells is controlled by several hormone and peptide triggered regulatory pathways. Among these, peptides encoded by the *CLAVATA3/ESR RELATED* (*CLE*) gene family have been shown to play an important role in controlling differentiation processes within both shoot and root meristems. The founding member of the *CLE* gene family, *CLAVATA 3* (*CLV3*) was identified as a key regulator of stem cell maintenance in the shoot apical meristem [\(Brand et al.,](#page-28-0)  [2000;](#page-28-0) [Fletcher et al., 1999;](#page-29-0) [Schoof et al., 2000;](#page-31-0) [Yadav et al., 2011\)](#page-32-0). The CLV3 peptide is expressed in the stem cells of the shoot apical meristem (SAM) and perceived by a receptor complex consisting of CLAVATA1 (CLV1), CLAVATA2 (CLV2) and CORYNE (CRN) [\(Bleckmann et al., 2010;](#page-28-1) [Müller et al., 2008;](#page-30-0) [Ogawa et](#page-31-1)  [al., 2008;](#page-31-1) [Yadav et al., 2011\)](#page-32-0). Other receptors such as RPK2 and BAM1 also contribute to CLV3 perception [\(DeYoung et al., 2006;](#page-29-1) [Kinoshita et al., 2010\)](#page-30-1).

The member of the *CLE* gene family that is most closely related to *CLV3* is *CLE40*, which has been shown to be expressed in differentiated columella cells of the distal meristem and in the stele in the proximal root meristem [\(Hobe et al., 2003;](#page-29-2) [Stahl et](#page-31-2)  [al., 2009\)](#page-31-2). The distal meristem harbours the columella stem cells (CSCs), which differentiate into columella cells, whereas the proximal meristem includes all other stem cells that give rise to the stele, endodermis, cortex and epidermal cells. In the distal meristem, CLE40 and the receptor-like kinase ARABIDOPSIS CRINKLY 4 (ACR4), together with CLV1, have been shown to counteract the quiescent centre (QC) derived and stem cell promoting activity of the WUS related homeodomain transcription factor WUSCHEL RELATED HOMEOBOX 5 (WOX5). CLE40 is perceived via ACR4 and CLV1 in the CSCs and some of the differentiated columella cells [\(Stahl et al., 2013\)](#page-31-3). *cle40, acr4* or *clv1* mutants which disrupt the canonical *CLE40* signaling pathway form an additional layer of CSCs, while treatment of wildtype roots with CLE40 peptide (CLE40P) has been shown to decrease the number of CSCs. Both observations indicate that the CLE40 level regulates CSC identity. Consistent with this, the *WOX5* expression domain is laterally expanded in *cle40* and *acr4* mutants, but shifts to a more proximal position when *CLE40* signaling is increased by external addition of CLE40P [\(Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2). Together, this indicates a direct regulatory effect of *CLE40-ACR4* signaling on the *WOX5* expression domain. Since aberrant phenotypes in the distal root meristem resulting from CLE40 dosage alterations have been shown to be *ACR4* dependent, a direct *CLE40-ACR4* signaling pathway is expected to act upstream of and to control *WOX5*. Nevertheless, the increased CSC differentiation which was observed in *wox5* mutants is even further enhanced by CLE40P treatment, arguing for an additional and *WOX5* independent mechanism for stem cell fate regulation by *CLE40* and *ACR4* and *CLV1* [\(De Smet et al., 2008;](#page-28-2) [Stahl et al., 2013;](#page-31-3) [Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2).

In the proximal meristem, treatments with 26 different CLE peptides, representing the putative peptide sequences encoded by 31 different *CLE* genes, caused a premature differentiation of the root meristem [\(Ito et al., 2006\)](#page-30-2). Furthermore, at least 15 *CLE* genes (*CLE9-14*, *CLE16-20*, *CLE22*, *CLE25-27*), that encode for peptides causing root meristem differentiation show also distinct expression patterns in the root [\(Jun et](#page-30-3)  [al., 2010\)](#page-30-3), indicating that several endogenous CLE peptide dependent signaling pathways may act here. However, *CLE40* is the only *CLE* gene known so far that shows a distinct loss of function phenotype in the proximal root meristem resulting in an overall shorter root [\(Hobe et al., 2003;](#page-29-2) [Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2). It was shown to be caused by an early arrest in root growth due to increased differentiation of proliferating cells resulting in a smaller proximal meristem, which spatially coincides with the expression of *CLE40* in the stele of the proximal meristem [\(Hobe et al.,](#page-29-2)  [2003;](#page-29-2) [Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2).

The membrane associated pseudokinase CRN and the receptor-like protein CLV2 are known to be required for signaling by CLV3 in the SAM [\(Kayes and Clark,](#page-30-4)  [1998;](#page-30-4) [Müller et al., 2008\)](#page-30-0). Both proteins are also expressed in the inner tissues of the proximal root meristem overlapping with the expression pattern of *CLE40* [\(Replogle](#page-31-4)  [et al., 2011\)](#page-31-4). Loss of *CRN* or *CLV2* function causes no apparent root phenotypes, with the exception of insensitivity towards the root length reduction effect of CLE peptide treatment [\(Fiers et al., 2005;](#page-29-3) [Müller et al., 2008;](#page-30-0) [Replogle et al., 2011\)](#page-31-4). Interestingly, both *CRN* and *CLV2* are expressed in syncytia induced by nematode infection of *Arabidopsis* root tissue [\(Replogle et al., 2011\)](#page-31-4), and the soybean cyst nematode *Heterodera glycines* has been shown to express the CLE peptide encoding *HgCLE1* [\(Wang et al., 2001\)](#page-32-1). Overexpression of *HgCLE1* as well as treatment with synthetic HgCLE1 peptide in *Arabidopsis* wildtype plants cause developmental reprogramming that results in decreased SAM size, resembling the CLE40P treatment phenotype, while this effect is largely suppressed in *crn* and *clv2* mutant plants [\(Replogle et al., 2011;](#page-31-4) [Wang et al., 2005\)](#page-32-2), supporting a role of *CRN* and *CLV2* in *CLE* induced differentiation processes.

We here propose that two CLE peptide dependent pathways control root meristem differentiation in an antagonistic manner. Endogenous *CLE40* signaling in the proximal root meristem leads - via yet unknown receptors - to inhibition of premature differentiation, while CRN and CLV2 perceive a yet unidentified CLE signal promoting differentiation processes in the proximal root meristem. The latter pathway can be overactivated by application of a variety of different CLE peptides.

Via phenotypic analysis of the respective single and double mutants of *CRN*, *CLV2* and *CLE40*, we show that exogenously applied CLE peptide is perceived via CRN/CLV2, triggering differentiation in the proximal root meristem and antagonizing the differentiation inhibition of endogenous *CLE40* signaling. By performing whole transcriptome analyses, we investigated the combined function and transcriptional readout for *CRN*/*CLV2* signaling, which affects primarily target gene expression in the transition zone of the proximal root meristem. We elucidate the counteracting activities of the *CRN/CLV2* and *CLE40* signaling pathways through the identification of common but antagonistically regulated target genes and processes. Furthermore, phenotypic and spatial transcriptome analyses enable us to exclude a similar role for *CRN*/*CLV* in the control of differentiation in the distal meristem, while a broader role for *CLE40* is suggested by the diversity of transcriptionally regulated target processes.

#### **Results**

# **Proximal root meristem development is controlled by two separable CLE40 activatable pathways**

*CLE40* can control growth and development of the primary root through two different and possibly antagonistically acting pathways: (i) *cle40* loss-of-function mutants exhibit premature differentiation of the proximal root meristem, indicating that *CLE40* activity is required for meristem maintenance; (ii) overexpression or exogenous application of CLE40P can trigger a growth-restricting signaling pathway in *Arabidopsis* [\(Hobe et al., 2003;](#page-29-2) [Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2), (SFig1A).

*CRN* and *CLV2* exhibit a role in at least one of these *CLE40* dependent signaling processes, since *crn* or *clv2* mutant plants show no significant reduction of root length growth in presence of exogenously applied CLE40P [\(Hobe et al., 2003;](#page-29-2) [Stahl](#page-31-2)  [et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2), (Fig.1A).

We found that when CLE40P was added to *cle40* mutant plants, the primary root growth reduction was even further enhanced compared to *cle40-2* mutants, or after CLE40P application to wildtype (Col) plants alone (Fig.1B). This indicated that growth repression by exogenous CLE40P could counteract growth promotion by endogenous *CLE40* signaling. Interestingly, exogenous CLE40P apparently failed to activate the growth promotive *CLE40* pathway, possibly due to a lack of posttranslational processing or modification, or because the added peptide failed to reach those tissues where the corresponding receptors are expressed. To further investigate a crosstalk between the promotive (*CLE40* dependent) and the repressive (*CLV2*/*CRN* dependent) pathway, we studied the loss of *CLV2* function in *clv2 gabi/cle40-2* double mutant plants. As mentioned before, mutations in *clv2* do not lead to enhanced root meristem growth under normal growth conditions, suggesting that the repressive pathway is not continuously active. Congruently, we observed no significant difference in root length between *clv2-gabi/cle40-2* double mutant and *cle40-2* single mutant plants, although the double mutants exhibited full insensitivity towards exogenous CLE40P application (Fig.1B). This shows that the perception of exogenous CLE40P relies on *CLV2*, indicating a root length controlling *CRN*/*CLV2* dependent CLE peptide signaling pathway that functions antagonistically to endogenous *CLE40* signaling.

We further asked if the role of *CRN* and *CLV2* in the repression of root growth is phenotypically visible in their loss-of-function mutants, which has not been reported so far [\(Fiers et al., 2005;](#page-29-3) [Müller et al., 2008;](#page-30-0) [Replogle et al., 2011\)](#page-31-4). A very accurate marker for alterations in root meristem growth is the size of the meristematic zone. The proximal meristematic region was measured from the quiescent centre (QC) to the transition zone (TZ) which is defined by the appearance of cortex cells with approximately twice the size of their distal neighbours [\(Tian et al., 2002\)](#page-31-5), (SFig1A). The total number of cortex cells in the meristematic region increases during early postembryonic development, and then remains stable from approximately five days after germination (DAG) onwards, reaching an average number of about 30 (plus/minus standard deviation) cortex cells in Col-0 plants [\(Dello Ioio et al., 2007\)](#page-28-3). However, precise analysis of the root meristem cortex cell number and size revealed no alterations in *crn-3* and *clv2-gabi* mutant, compared to Col-0 wildtype plants (SFig.1B&C).

Since CLE40P signaling not only contributes to root length growth but also to differentiation processes in the distal root meristem, we analyzed a possible role of CRN and CLV2 in the perception of CLE40P in the distal meristem. For *clv2-3*, another *clv2* loss of function allele in Col-0 background, it has been shown before that loss of CLV2 function causes no alterations in CSC number and no insensitivity towards CLE40P treatment [\(Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2). However, a possible role for *CRN* in the distal root meristem had not yet been analyzed. We found that both *crn-3* and *clv2-gabi* mutants are unaffected in distal root meristem development compared to wildtype. Consistent with this, both mutants did not suppress the reduction of CSC caused by CLE40P treatment (SFig2A/B). This indicates that CRN and CLV2 execute a specific function in the perception of CLE peptides only in the proximal part of the root meristem.

#### **Identification of** *CLE* **signaling target genes**

Comparative transcriptome analyses in *crn-3*, *clv2-gabi* and *cle40-2* single mutants and the corresponding Col-0 wildtype were performed to uncover transcriptional effects in the aphenotypic *crn-3* and *clv2-gabi* mutant root meristems and to reveal mutual and distinct targets of *CRN*/*CLV2* and *CLE40* signaling.

To consider predominantly transcriptional differences in and adjacent to the apical root meristem, the tissue used for RNA isolation was limited to the very root tip. The root tips were manually cut at a position about 800 µm from the columella tip resulting in a sample containing all cells of the columella and the distal and proximal meristem, including the elongation zone, but excluding the differentiation zone. This also excludes all visible stages of differentiated root hairs or lateral roots. To eliminate artefacts due to variability in manual dissection and RNA amplification, a large number of root tips (n>200) was pooled for each RNA sample. The transcriptome sequencing resulted in 26 to 42 million reads per sample, which could be mapped with an average of 95% on the *Arabidopsis thaliana* reference genome (TAIR10) containing 28496 genes. About 85% of all genes were detected in at least one sample with at least one read.

The comparison of the *crn-3* mutant and the wildtype transcriptomes resulted in 117 differentially expressed genes with 61% up- and 39% downregulated genes (*crn* vs. wt). In the comparison between the *clv2-gabi* mutant and the wildtype transcriptomes, 527 genes were significantly differentially regulated with 83% downand 17% upregulated genes (*clv2* vs. wt) (Fig 2A). An intersection analysis of *CLV2* and *CRN* target genes (Fig 2B) showed that both mutants share a large number of differentially regulated genes, of which 99% are regulated in the same direction (Fig 2C), indicating a role in a common regulatory process.

The comparison of the *cle40-2* mutant with the wildtype transcriptome resulted in 1529 significantly differentially regulated genes (Fig.2B). About 60% of these genes were up- and 40% downregulated (*cle40* vs. wt). An intersection analysis of the *CLE40*, *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes showed that 27% of *CRN* and 25% of *CLV2* target genes were also regulated by *CLE40* (Fig 2B). However, those genes showed no uniform direction of regulation (Fig 2C).

#### *CRN***/***CLV2* **signaling promotes cell differentiation processes**

The relation of down- to upregulated genes in *clv2-gabi* recurred in the GO-term enrichment analysis, in which only one GO-term predominantly consisted of upregulated target genes and all other significantly enriched GO-terms consisted predominantly or totally of downregulated target genes (Fig 3B). GO-terms for several response processes were enriched and consisted of up- as well as downregulated genes. Furthermore, GO-terms representing cell wall organisation as well as root development and several transport processes were enriched and consisted of predominantly downregulated target genes (Fig 3B&C). The GO-term enrichment analysis for the *CRN* signaling target genes revealed only a small number of significantly enriched GO-terms and genes contributing to these were entirely downregulated in *crn-3* mutants. Half of the enriched GO-terms represent molecule transport processes (Fig 3A). Although no GO-terms representing cell wall organisation related processes were significantly enriched, an observation of the target gene list revealed several cell wall organisation related genes, most of which were downregulated in *crn-3* (Fig 4B&C). These cell wall organisation target genes appeared in the *clv2-gabi* experiment as well and build the major part of common target genes of *CRN* and *CLV2*. In *clv2-gabi*, genes of the *EXPANSIN* and *EXTENSIN* gene families, as well as *PECTIN*, *ARABINOGALACTAN PROTEIN*

(*AGP*), root hair growth and other cell wall specific genes are predominantly downregulated. The only upregulated genes in these groups are *EXTENSIN 4* (*EXT4*), the glycoside hydrolase *ALPHA-L-ARABINOFURANOSIDASE 1* (*ASD1*) and one member (AT1G65570) of the *PECTIN* gene family. Another group of *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes, already indicated by the enrichment of transport process representing GO-terms, is the *BIFUNCTIONAL INHIBITOR/LIPID-TRANSFER PROTEIN/SEED STORAGE 2S ALBUMIN SUPERFAMILY*. In *clv2-gabi*, 12 members of this gene family were downregulated and eight of them were also downregulated in the *crn-3* mutant (Fig 4A&C). Although no general function has so far been assigned for these genes, a role in the promotion of vasculature differentiation has been identified recently for a subgroup of 13 lipid transfer protein genes. These genes with a predicted function in intercellular trafficking have been named *XYLOGEN-TYPE PROTEIN* (*XYLP*) family genes after their first identified members *XYLOGEN PROTEIN 1* and *2* (*XYP1*/*2*) [\(Kobayashi et](#page-30-5)  [al., 2011\)](#page-30-5). *XYP2*, *XYLP7* and *XYLP12* were among the target genes misexpressed in *clv2-gabi*. The latter was also downregulated in *crn-3*. *XYP2* and *XYLP7* are mainly expressed in the vasculature, whereas *XYLP12* is expressed in the endodermis and pericycle cells of the differentiation zone [\(Kobayashi et al., 2011\)](#page-30-5).

Taken together, these results show that the lack of *CLV2* or *CRN* function results in the downregulation of genes contributing to cell wall organization. Therefore we suggest a function for both genes in a common pathway promoting cell wall organization, which is of major importance during expansion and differentiation of cells. Furthermore, the high number and diversity of related target genes indicates an upstream regulatory function in cell differentiation processes.

## *CLE40* **signaling inhibits cell differentiation processes**

The GO-term enrichment analysis of *CLE40* target genes revealed an enrichment of 9% GO-terms consisting of predominantly or entirely downregulated genes, 25% which contained both up- and downregulated genes and 66% with predominantly or entirely upregulated genes. Transport, response and cell junction process representing GO-terms are dominant and contain predominantly upregulated and not specifically regulated target genes (Fig 5A/B). In addition, GO-terms representing cell wall organization, differentiation and developmental processes were enriched and consisted of predominantly upregulated genes, too (Fig 5A/B). Those target genes associated with cell wall organization related functions were hardly congruent with the cell wall organization associated target genes of *CRN* and *CLV2*. *EXTENSIN* family and root hair growth genes such as *ROOT HAIR SPECIFIC* (*RHS*) family genes were exclusively upregulated, while root hair inhibiting genes were exclusively downregulated. *EXPANSIN* and *PECTIN* family genes were predominantly upregulated. In contrast, *AGP* genes were predominantly downregulated. Other cell wall related genes, rather participating in cell wall structure alteration than promotion or inhibition were found regulated in both directions (Fig5C). The upregulation of root hair growth genes and downregulation of root hair inhibition genes in *cle40* mutants, both also assigned to cell wall organization, indicates a root hair repressing function of *CLE40*. Although root hairs are not yet morphologically visible in the root meristem area used for transcriptome analysis, root hair initiation sites are already established in the proximal meristematic and elongation zone of the root meristem [\(Galway et al., 1994\)](#page-29-4).

Since *cle40-2* mutant roots exhibit a severe reduction in root length and root meristem size, the upregulation of cell wall as well as differentiation and development associated genes resulting in early differentiation correlates with the phenotype, indicating that *CLE40* mainly inhibits differentiation. This further supports the hypothesis of two antagonistic pathways regulating differentiation which depend on *CLE40* or *CRN*/*CLV2*.

#### *CLE40* **acts to control auxin signaling pathways**

In the *cle40-2* transcriptome, several components of canonical auxin signaling were found to be misregulated. *PINOID* (*PID*), a key component which is positively regulated by auxin [\(Christensen et al., 2000\)](#page-28-4), was found to be downregulated. It has been shown to interact with the calcium-binding proteins TOUCH3 (TCH3) and PID-BINDING PROTEIN 1 (PBP1) [\(Benjamins et al., 2003\)](#page-28-5); of these, *PBP1* was also downregulated in *cle40-2*. In addition, the pleiotropic *ETTIN* (*ETT*), *IAA1* and *AUXIN-INDUCED IN ROOT CULTURES 1* (*AIR1*), auxin induced genes [\(Abel et al., 1994;](#page-28-6) [Neuteboom et al., 1999;](#page-31-6) [Ulmasov et al., 1999\)](#page-31-7), were downregulated (Table 1). In contrast, *AIR3* that responds to auxin similar to *AIR1* as well as *IAA18*, *IAA34*, *ARF1*, *MONOPTEROS* (*MP*) and a member of the recently identified *PIN-LIKES* (*PILS*) auxin transport genes, AT1G76520, which have also been shown to be positively regulated by auxin [\(Feraru et al., 2012;](#page-29-5) [Hardtke and Berleth, 1998;](#page-29-6) [Liscum and](#page-30-6)  [Reed, 2002;](#page-30-6) [Uehara et al., 2008;](#page-31-8) [Ulmasov et al., 1997\)](#page-31-9), are upregulated in *cle40-2*  mutant plants (Table 1). Since auxin targets with similar roles in auxin signaling are adversatively affected due to the lack of *CLE40* function, no explicit direction of regulation can be assigned to the role of *CLE40* in auxin signaling. Therefore we assume that *CLE40* differentially affects auxin signaling.

#### *CLE40* **promotes ABA signaling**

In addition to auxin signaling, several components of the abscisic acid (ABA) response signaling pathway could be uncovered in the target gene list of *CLE40* (Table 2). A component of ABA biosynthesis, *ARABIDOPSIS ALDEHYDE OXIDASE* (*AAO*), is downregulated in *cle40-2*. Furthermore, four genes, which have been shown to encode positive regulators of ABA signaling, are downregulated, too: These are the DNA binding protein encoding *ARIA INTERACTING DOUBLE AP2 DOMAIN PROTEIN* (*ADAP*) [\(Lee et al., 2009\)](#page-30-7), the cell wall protein encoding *ARABINOGALACTAN 30* (*AGP30*) [\(van Hengel and Roberts, 2003\)](#page-31-10), *GLUTATHIONE PEROXIDASE 3* (*GPX3*) [\(Miao et al., 2006\)](#page-30-8) and *CALCINEURON B LIKE 1* (*CBL1*), whose homologue in *Brassica napus* has been shown to act in ABA signaling [\(Chen](#page-28-7)  [et al., 2012\)](#page-28-7). Genes encoding the ABA sensor PYR1-LIKE 6 (PYL6) and the protein phosphatase 2C (PP2C) HIGHLY ABA INDUCED 3 (HAI3), which have been shown to interact in ABA signaling [\(Bhaskara et al., 2012\)](#page-28-8), were downregulated, too. In addition, several targets of ABA signaling like *KINASE 2* (*KIN2*) [\(Kurkela and Borg-](#page-30-9)[Franck, 1992\)](#page-30-9), *HVA22 HOMOLOGUE A* (*HVA22A*) [\(Guo and Ho, 2008\)](#page-29-7), *TOUCH 2 / CALMODULIN LIKE 24* (*TCH2*/*CML24*) [\(Delk et al., 2005\)](#page-28-9), other *ABA RESPONSIVE PROTEIN RELATED* genes (AT5G23350, AT5G23360 and AT5G23370) [\(Finkelstein](#page-29-8)  [and Rock, 2002\)](#page-29-8) and the two interacting calcium-dependent membrane binding protein encoding genes *ANNEXIN 1* & *4* (*ANNAT1*&*4*) [\(Huh et al., 2010\)](#page-29-9) are downregulated as well. A more surprising observation was the strong upregulation of *CRUCIFERIN 2 (CRU2), CRUCIFERIN 3* (*CRU3*) and *CRUCIFERINA* (*CRA1*) in *cle40-2*. The expression of these genes is restricted to the embryo in wildtype plants, but their phosphorylation has been shown to be dependent on ABA response [\(Wan](#page-32-3)  [et al., 2007\)](#page-32-3).

Since the downregulated genes appear in different steps of ABA signaling, we conclude that *CLE40* generally promotes ABA responses.

### *CLE40* **has an inhibitory role in cytokinin signaling**

As result of loss of *CLE40* function, photosynthesis related genes were found predominantly upregulated (Figure 5A&B). The presence of proplastids and the possibility of transcriptional regulation of chloroplast genes in root tissue have been discussed before [\(Isono et al., 1997\)](#page-30-10). The transcriptional upregulation of these genes could be explained by an ectopic upregulation of cytokinin biosynthesis and/or signaling, as it was published before [\(Werner et al., 2008\)](#page-32-4) which is reflected in the regulation of additional cytokinin signaling related genes in *cle40-2*. One of them was *LONELY GUY 1* (*LOG1*), which has been shown to encode a cytokinin activating enzyme that has, amongst others, a function in lateral root formation [\(Kuroha et al.,](#page-30-11)  [2009\)](#page-30-11). No transcripts for *LOG1* were present in the Col-0 samples (Table 3), which correlates with its cytokinin activating function in lateral root development more proximal of the root tip, but low levels were present in the *cle40-2* samples, resembling the ectopic activation of photosynthesis genes. In addition, the cytokinin receptor encoding *ARABIDOPSIS HISTIDINE KINASE 3* (*AHK3*), the signaling stabilising *CYTOKININ RESPONSE FACTOR 6* (*CRF6*), [\(Cutcliffe et al., 2011\)](#page-28-10) and the positive regulator type-B encoding *ARABIDOPSIS RESPONSE REGULATOR 1* (*ARR1*) were upregulated. In contrast, the negative regulator type-A encoding *ARABIDOPSIS RESPONSE REGULATOR 15* (*ARR15*) [\(Kiba et al., 2003\)](#page-30-12) was downregulated in *cle40-2* (Table 3). Altogether, regulation of these target genes indicates a cytokinin signaling inhibiting role for *CLE40* in the root.

#### **Regional roles for** *CRN***/***CLV2* **and** *CLE40* **signaling**

The increasing availability of whole transcriptome analysis platforms led to a large number of whole transcriptome datasets that have become publicly available and function as a comprehensive resource for comparative analyses. [Nawy et al. \(2005\)](#page-30-13) identified 290 genes with a highly enriched expression in the QC. QC cells were labelled with *AGL42* promoter driven GFP and specifically isolated via fluorescence activated cell sorting (FACS) prior to a transcriptome analysis via microarray hybridisation. A comparison of the *CRN* and *CLV2* target gene lists with the QC specific dataset revealed no QC specific genes in both target gene lists, showing absense of regulation in the QC. A comparison with the *CLE40* target gene list led to 17 genes enriched in the QC (STable1), which matches the expectation according to all transcripts in the analysis. This result confirms that *CLE40* functions in the control of stem cell fate and the absence of expression and function of *CLV2* and *CRN* in the stem cell niche.

Based on a similar experimental design, [Brady et al. \(2007\)](#page-28-11) used an extended setup by increasing the number of reporter genes and adding longitudinal sections. The experiments resulted in two detailed datasets, one defining the radial assembly of root tissues represented by the specific expression of 19 different genes and the other one showing the longitudinal sequence of developmental zones partitioned in 13 sections. The radial pattern could be divided into: Quiescent centre (QC), lateral root cap (LRC), columella (COL), cortex (COR) and endodermis (END) together and separate, pericycle (PER) (represented by four individual lines), stele (STE), xylem (XYL) and phloem (PHL) (young and old stages each represented by individual lines), epidermal hair (RHC) and non-hair (NRHC) cells. The longitudinal sequence was dissected into the columella (COL), the meristematic zone subdivided into sections 1 to 6, the elongation zone (sections 7 and 8) and the maturation zone (sections 9 to 12). The comparison of overall gene expression with the expression of *CRN*, *CLV2* and *CLE40* target genes in both the radial and longitudinal datasets confirmed the shared characteristics of *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes also in terms of their similar spatial distribution (Fig 6 and 7).

The QC dataset of the radial assembly (Fig 7) confirmed the absence of QC expression among *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes and the average QC expression among *CLE40* target genes. Similar deviation from mean gene expression for all three target gene lists was only observed in xylem and phloem representing datasets. Target genes were underrepresented in young vascular cells represented by xylem reporter lines S4 and xylem\_2501 and the phloem reporter line S32, and overrepresented in maturing vascular cells represented by xylem reporter line S18 and phloem reporter lines APL and SUC2. In the lateral root cap and in the columella, *CRN* and *CLV2* target gene expression was underrepresented, whereas *CLE40* target gene expression was slightly overrepresented or close to the average. In all other radial assembly datasets, *CLE40* target genes were not deviating clearly from mean expression. In the specific cortex and endodermis reporter lines CORTEX and "scr5", both *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes where strongly overrepresented, whereas no deviation was observed in the dataset of the reporter line J0571, representing the complete ground tissue consisting of endodermis, cortex and QC. The datasets of phloem and xylem pole pericycle cells represented by the reporter lines S17 and J0121 exhibited no strong deviation of target genes. The reporter lines J2661 and "rm1000", representing fully differentiated pericycle cells and lateral root primordia, respectively, showed strong underrepresentation of *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes. In the stele dataset, represented by the meristematic zone specific reporter line "wol", both *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes were strongly overrepresented. The

17

epidermal datasets, divided in reporter lines for hair and non-hair cells, revealed strong deviation in both *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes. Both were overrepresented in hair-cells and underrepresented in non-hair-cells.

The longitudinal sections exhibited an almost constant expression of *CLE40* target genes with slight underrepresentation in sections two to five of the meristematic zone and slight overrepresentation in the sections covering the maturation zone. *CLV2* and *CRN* target gene expression showed underrepresentation in the columella and meristematic zone up to section five and strong overrepresentation in sections six and seven representing the meristematic and elongation zone sections flanking the transition zone. In the upper elongation zone the expression level drops to the overall mean expression level and subsequently exhibits overrepresentation in the adjacent sections of the meristematic zone before dropping again in the last section of the maturation zone (Fig 6).

Although the tissue samples analysed here contained also the distal root meristem, we did not detect significant changes in the expression of *WOX5* or *ACR4*, both known targets of *CLE40* signaling [\(Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2). *ACR4* is, however, expressed in a variety of tissues including the epidermis and responds to *CLE40* signaling in only a small group of columella stem cells. *WOX5* expression is mostly affected at the spatial level, and the minor quantitative changes detected for *WOX5* in our comparative analysis were not statistically significant. The majority of transcript changes that we discussed here are therefore independent of the canonical *CLE40- ACR4-WOX5* module.

Taken together, our analyses show that *CLE40* affects genes that are present in all tissues of the root with a slight preference for mature differentiating cells over young undifferentiated cells, indicated by expression in the different types of xylem and phloem cells and the slight overrepresentation in the maturation zone. *CLV2* and *CRN* target gene expression resembles the distribution of *CLE40* target genes in xylem and phloem cells, indicating a similar function on mature rather than young cells. Despite that, *CRN* and *CLV2* target gene expression shows underrepresentation in the columella, QC and lateral root cap and overrepresentation in endodermis, cortex and stele cells. This reflects the respective expression patterns of *CRN* and *CLV2* in the proximal meristem. Overrepresentation of *CRN* and *CLV2* target gene expression in the transition zone, where cells change their developmental program from elongation to differentiation, further supports a specific role for both genes to promote proximal meristem differentiation.

#### **Discussion**

#### *CLE40* **and** *CRN***/***CLV2* **act antagonistically upon differentiation**

CRN and CLV2 are components of a larger receptor complex in the shoot apical meristem and serve there to perceive the CLV3 signaling peptide. A role for both proteins in the root was first suggested by the suppression of CLE-induced root growth arrest in *crn* or *clv2* mutants. Our phenotypic and transcriptome analyses of primary root meristems mutant for *CRN*, *CLV2* or *CLE40* provide strong evidence that *CRN* and *CLV2* promote differentiation in the transition zone of the proximal meristem, and that *CLE40* acts in an antagonistic pathway inhibiting differentiation in the proximal root meristem (Fig 8), in addition to its role in the distal root meristem.

Both *crn* and *clv2* mutants are insensitive towards the root growth inhibiting effect of exogenously applied CLE peptides [\(Fiers et al., 2005;](#page-29-3) [Müller et al., 2008\)](#page-30-0). Nevertheless, both mutants still displayed distal meristem differentiation upon CLE40P application. This suggests an exclusive role of *CRN* and *CLV2* in the proximal meristem, rather than a general role in *CLE* signaling in the entire meristem. Furthermore, our detailed analyses of *crn* and *clv2* mutants showed that they were aphenotypic regarding root meristem growth, in contrast to *cle40* mutants, indicating an unequal contribution of *CRN*/*CLV2* and *CLE40* dependent signaling pathways to meristem differentiation in the proximal meristem. Noteworthy, phenotypic effects of the *CRN*/*CLV2* pathway became only apparent at elevated levels of CLE peptides, in the response to nematodes or to other environmental challenges [\(Hobe et al., 2003;](#page-29-2) [Müller et al., 2008;](#page-30-0) [Replogle et al., 2011;](#page-31-4) [Wang et al., 2001;](#page-32-1) [Wang et al., 2005\)](#page-32-2). The comparative transcriptome analyses of wildtype and *crn* or *clv2* mutant root tips further support a specific but shared function of *CLV2* and *CRN* in proximal meristem differentiation. Analysis of target genes that were predominantly downregulated in *crn*  and *clv2* mutants revealed a significant enrichment of cell wall organisation process related genes. Cell wall organisation is an essential part of differentiation processes occuring in the transition zone of the meristem, where *CRN*/*CLV2* target genes are overrepresented [\(Brady et al., 2007;](#page-28-11) [Nawy et al., 2005\)](#page-30-13). In contrast to the downregulation of differentiation markers in *crn* and *clv2*, the vast majority of differentiation related target genes were upregulated in the *cle40* mutant transcriptome. This is coincident with the phenotypic observation of increased differentiation resulting in severe root length consumption in the *cle40* mutant and further supports the antagonistic role of the *CLE40* and the *CRN*/*CLV2* dependent pathway.

For another CLE family gene, *CLE41*, two seperate functions in a common context have been recently discovered. *CLE41* is expressed in the phloem of the vasculature and was recently shown to act on vascular cell division through the meristem specific expressed PHLOEM INTERCALATED WITH XYLEM (PXY) and on vascular organisation through the broader expressed ERECTA (ER) together with PXY [\(Etchells et al., 2013\)](#page-29-10).

#### *CLE40* **acts in several hormone signaling pathways**

*CLE40* regulates a wide range of genes that comprise diverse phytohormone synthesis, signaling and hormone-regulated target genes. For example, several components of the ABA signaling pathway are downregulated in *cle40* mutants. This is consistent with the suggested role for ABA in suppressing cell differentiation in the proximal meristem [\(Zhang et al., 2010\)](#page-32-5). However, it is unlikely that *CLE40* acts through ABA in both proximal and distal root meristems, since increased ABA levels promote columella stem cell fate, while *CLE40* represses it [\(Stahl et al., 2009;](#page-31-2) [Zhang](#page-32-5)  [et al., 2010\)](#page-32-5). We therefore propose that *CLE40* stimulates ABA signaling predominantly in the proximal root meristem.

Many auxin signaling components are differentially expressed in *cle40* mutant root meristems, indicating that *CLE40* also affects auxin signaling. However, several related auxin signaling genes show opposite regulation by *CLE40*, indicating that *CLE40* affects auxin signaling in different processes. This could again reflect a differential activity of *CLE40* in the proximal and distal root meristem on cell differentiation, which is partially mediated by auxin [\(Dello Ioio et al., 2007;](#page-28-3) [Sabatini et](#page-31-11)  [al., 1999\)](#page-31-11).

Components of cytokinin signaling were differentially expressed in a unified direction. Genes inhibiting cytokinin signaling were downregulated, while those promoting cytokinin signaling were upregulated in *cle40* mutants. Cytokinin in conjunction with auxin plays an important role in the regulation of the proximal meristem size [\(Tian et](#page-31-5)  [al., 2002\)](#page-31-5), and acts specifically to position the transition zone of the root meristem

21

[\(Dello Ioio et al., 2008\)](#page-29-11). *CLE40* appears to delay this transition towards differentiation by restricting not only cytokinin signaling, but also by repressing the expression of a key enzyme for cytokinin biosynthesis, LOG1 [\(Kuroha et al., 2009\)](#page-30-11).

#### *CRN***/***CLV2* **and** *CLE40* **regulate root hair development genes**

Target genes regulated by *CRN* and *CLV2* showed a predominant expression in root hair cells, compared to non-hair cells [\(Brady et al., 2007\)](#page-28-11). The majority of these genes also contribute to cell wall organisation and cell differentiation in general [\(Gilroy and Jones, 2000\)](#page-29-12), comparable to other *CRN* and *CLV2* target genes with a function in proximal meristem differentiation. This may reflect the wide role that *CRN* and *CLV2* exert in promoting cell differentiation.

Root hair growth promoting genes were up- and root hair inhibitory genes were downregulated in *cle40* mutants. However, despite this strong regulation of a significant number of root hair growth and patterning genes, obvious alteration of root hair development was neither observed in *cle40* mutants, nor in *crn* or *clv2*. Noteworthy, members of another peptide encoding gene family, *CLE-LIKE* / *ROOT GROWTH FACTOR* / *GOLVEN* (*CLEL*/*RGF*/*GLV*) which are closely related to the *CLE* family, were found to be involved in the formation of root hairs [\(Fernandez et al.,](#page-29-13)  [2013;](#page-29-13) [Meng et al., 2012\)](#page-30-14).

## **Methods**

## **Plant Accessions**

The *Arabidopsis thaliana* ecotype Columbia *(*Col-0*)* was used as wildtype. The *crn-3* [\(Müller et al., 2008\)](#page-30-0) and *cle40-2* [\(Stahl et al., 2009\)](#page-31-2) alleles have been described before and *clv2-gabi* (GK-686A09) [\(Kleinboelting et al., 2012\)](#page-30-15) seeds were obtained from the Nottingham *Arabidopsis* Stock Centre (NASC, UK). We could show that the T-DNA-insertion in the *clv2-gabi* allele promotes expression of a neighbouring locus (see supplemental material). *clv2-gabi/cle40-2* double mutants have been generated by crossing the respective homozygous single mutants.

#### **Plant cultivation**

For sterile cultivation, *A. thaliana* seeds were surface sterilised via chlorine gas and then stratified in 0.1% aqueous agarose solution for two to three days at 4°C in the dark. For phenotypic root analyses or root tip sampling, the seeds were grown on plant agar containing germination medium (GM) consisting of Murashige and Skoog (2.2 g/l) salts with Gamborgs no. 5 vitamins , 2-(N-morpholino) ethanesulfonic acid (0.5 g/l) and sucrose (10 g/l) and grown in a vertical position at 21°C under constant light conditions. For peptide treatments, GM plates were supplemented with 1µM dodecapeptide (12AA) CLE40P or 200 nM hydroxyprolinated tridecapeptide (13AAhyp) CLE40P, giving similar results.

#### **Morphological analyses**

To analyze the root morphology of *A. thaliana* seedlings, sterilised seeds were plated on vertical plates as mentioned before and subsequently examined every 24 hours via a stereomicroscope to determine and label the day of germination individually per seedling. For analysis of the proximal root meristem length, roots of equal age were cut from seedlings and incubated in aqueous propidium iodide (10 mM) solution for plasmamembrane staining. Subsequently, the roots were observed and photographed via a confocal microscope (excitation 561 nm; emission 575 to 650 nm). For the analysis of the distal meristem, roots of equal age were cut from seedlings and stained with the modified pseudo-Schiff propidium iodide (mPS-PI) method as described in [Truernit et al. \(2008\)](#page-31-12) and subsequently observed and photographed via confocal microscopy (excitation 561 nm; emission 575 to 650 nm).

#### **High throughput RNA sequencing**

High-throughput RNA sequencing experiments were performed with a high number of at least 200 root tips per sample with three samples for Col-0, *clv2-gabi* and *cle40- 2* and two samples for *crn-3*. Since all root tip samples should represent an equal developmental stage, only seedlings with similar time of germination were considered during sampling. Root tip sampling was performed 6 days after germination with help of a binocular microscope using a razorblade to cut the samples prior to transfer in βmercaptoethanol containing buffer (RLT buffer) from the RNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden) on ice. Samples were immediately stored on -80°C and subsequently homogenised via a Precellys 24 homogeniser (Peqlab, Erlangen) and processed for RNA isolation using the RNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden). Finally the samples were again treated with DNAseI from Roche Diagnostics (Mannheim) according to appending instructions to ensure the loss of all genomic DNA contaminations. Prior to further steps, final total RNA concentrations were measured via the spectrophotometer NanoDrop 2000 (Peqlab, Erlangen) and the integrity of the total RNA samples was checked with a Bioanalyzer 2100 from (Agilent, Santa Clara, USA).

The mRNA isolation and library preparation with multiplex labelling with TruSeq Kits from Illumina (SanDiego, USA) as well as the sequencing using a HiSeq2000 from Illumina (SanDiego, USA) were performed by the Genomics & Transcriptomics laboratory of the "Biologisch-Medizinisches Forschungszentrum" (BMFZ) core facility at the Heinrich-Heine-University Düsseldorf.

#### **Data analyses**

High-throughput sequencing raw data files were filtered with respect for quality labels via CLC Genomics Workbench compatible with the respective HiSeq2000 sequencing software CASAVA and mapped to the reference genome annotation TAIR10 resulting in a high mapping efficiency of an average of 92.4% uniquely mapped genes and an average of 2.4% weighted distributed non specifically mapped genes resulting in a total of about 95% mapped genes. The raw reads of all transcript annotations of each gene locus were summarised for each gene and exported. Subsequent steps were performed using the R programming environment version 2.14.1 with the package DEseq [\(Anders and Huber, 2010\)](#page-28-12). The library size of each sample was determined and used for normalisation of all replicates of all conditions across the experiment, resulting in comparable modified read values. Subsequently, the empirical dispersion value of each gene across the replicates of each condition was estimated and used with respect to the frequency of the gene sizes for fitting. The fitted values were assigned to each gene in both conditions and later used for the test on differential expression. The empirical and fitted dispersion values were then controlled via graphical plotting (example: SFig4). After the estimation of dispersion, the fitted dispersion values were used for differential expression calling, resulting in an expression value expressed as fold change in mutant samples compared to wildtype. The significance of differential expression was expressed as associated p-values, subsequently modified via Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment for multiple testing [\(Reiner et al., 2003\)](#page-31-13), resulting in adjusted p-values. Finally, the genes were filtered for significantly differentially expressed genes at a threshold of 10% false discovery rate represented by an adjusted p-value of 0.1 or less. For intersection analyses, the data tables were compared graphically using the package VennDiagram [\(Chen and Boutros, 2012\)](#page-28-13) in addition to intersection tables. GO-term enrichment analyses were performed with the packages GOstats and MASS [\(Falcon](#page-29-14)  [and Gentleman, 2007;](#page-29-14) [Venables and Ripley, 2002\)](#page-31-14) using the database package org.At.tair.db [\(Carlson et al., 2012\)](#page-28-14) for Arabidopsis specific GO annotation. After the GO-term enrichment analysis, obtained GO-term lists were abstracted using the online tool ReviGO [\(Supek et al., 2011\)](#page-31-15).

## **Figure 8: Model of CRN/CLV2 and CLE40 signaling in the proximal root meristem.**

Two antagonistic pathways regulate differentiation in the proximal root meristem affecting root length: CLE40 inhibits differentiation via a yet unknown receptor (RECX) antagonistically to a yet unknown CLE peptide (CLEX) that promotes differentiation via CRN/CLV2. Exogenous CLE40P is perceived via the CRN/CLV2 pathway promoting differentiation.
