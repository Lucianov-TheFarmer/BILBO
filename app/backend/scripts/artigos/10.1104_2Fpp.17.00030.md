#### **Abstract** 43

Climate change-driven stresses such as extreme temperatures, water deficit, and ion imbalance 44 are projected to exacerbate and jeopardize global food security. Under field conditions, these 45 stresses usually occur simultaneously and cause damages that exceed single stresses. Here, we 46 investigated the transcriptional patterns and morpho-physiological acclimations, of 47 *Brachypodium dystachion*, to single salinity, drought and heat stresses as well as their double and 48 triple stress combinations. Hierarchical clustering analysis of morpho-physiological acclimations 49 showed that several traits exhibited a gradually aggravating effect as plants were exposed to 50 combined stresses. On the other hand, other morphological traits were dominated by salinity, 51 while some physiological traits were shaped by heat stress. Response patterns of differentially 52 expressed genes, under single and combined stresses (i.e. *common stress-genes*), were 53 maintained only among 37% of the genes, indicating a limited expression consistency among 54 partially overlapping stresses. A comparison between common stress genes and genes that were 55 uniquely expressed only under combined stresses (i.e. *combination unique-genes*), revealed a 56 significant shift from increased intensity to antagonistic responses, respectively. The different 57 transcriptional signatures imply an alteration in the mode of action under combined stresses and 58 limited ability to predict plant responses as different stresses are combined. Co-expression 59 analysis coupled with enrichment analysis revealed that each gene subset was enriched with 60 different biological processes. Common stress-genes were enriched with known stress response 61 pathways, while combination unique-genes were enriched with unique processes and genes with 62 unknown functions that hold the potential to improve stress tolerance and enhance cereal 63 productivity under suboptimal field conditions. 64

#### **INTRODUCTION** 65

In the next half-century, a significant increase in agricultural production (e.g. food, feed, fibers 66 and bio-energy) will be needed in order to meet global food demands of the growing human 67 population (Myers et al., 2017). This challenge is further exacerbated under the projected climate 68 change scenarios, which predict an aggravation in the intensity and frequency of extreme events, 69 such as temperature fluctuations, water deficit and salt toxicity (Sillmann et al., 2013; Munns and 70 Gilliham, 2015). Under these conditions, yields of staple cereal crops (e.g. bread wheat [*Triticum* 71 *aestivum*], rice [*Oryza sativa*] and maize [*Zea mays*]), which provide over 50% of human 72 calories, are predicted to decrease (Reynolds et al., 2016). Thus, elucidating the mechanisms 73 underlying plant-resilience to suboptimal field conditions and developing stress-tolerant crops is 74 the most promising strategy to ensure global food security. 75

Under natural and agricultural systems, plants have to cope with multiple environmental 76 stresses simultaneously and acclimate to ever changing environments. Nevertheless, the majority 77 of studies on plant responses to abiotic stresses focus on short and extreme single stresses at 78 early plant vegetative stages, with plant survival or recovery rates as a measurement for plant 79 stress tolerance. These studies have identified and characterized numerous stress-responsive 80 genes, however, their introgression into crop-plants has achieved limited success in the field 81 (reviewed by Reguera et al., 2012). Plant acclimations to stress conditions (Giordano 2013) vary 82 depending on the length and severity of the stress, the plant species and genotype, the plant 83 developmental stage and the examined tissue or organ (Mittler and Blumwald, 2010). The 84 complexity of the plant response is further augmented under combinations of stresses, since it 85 involves the integration of different metabolic pathways and the cross-talk between different 86 sensors and signal transduction pathways (Mittler, 2006). Although recently, there has been an 87 increase in the number of studies addressing stress combinations (Suzuki et al., 2014, Zandalinas 88 et al., 2017 and references therein), studies that focus on gradual and long-term stress 89 combinations and the effects of combined stresses on plant productivity are scanty. 90

Plant acclimation to combinations of environmental stresses elicited specific physiological, 91 molecular and metabolic responses that could not be inferred from single stress treatments, 92 especially if the combined stress resulted in antagonistic responses (reviewed by Suzuki et al., 93 2014; Prasch and Sonnewald, 2015). Tobacco (*Nicotiana tabacum*) and Arabidopsis 94 (*Arabidopsis thaliana*) plants displayed an antagonistic interaction to the combination of heat 95 and drought. Under heat stress, plant stomata were opened to cool the leaves by transpiration, but 96 when heat was combined with drought, stomata remained closed, resulting in a higher leaf 97 temperature and a lower photosynthesis rate (Rizhsky et al., 2002; Rizhsky et al., 2004). These 98 distinct physiological responses were correlated with changes at the transcriptional and 99 metabolic levels (Rizhsky et al., 2002). On the other hand, tomato (*Solanum lycopersicon*) plants 100 showed a higher degree of tolerance to a combination of heat and salt stress compared with 101 plants under salt stress alone (Rivero et al., 2014). This beneficial effect is unexpected since 102 enhanced transpiration to cool the leaf would result in an enhanced ion uptake, with concomitant 103 ion toxicity (Mittler, 2006). Transcriptional profiling of Arabidopsis plants under single stresses 104 (cold, heat, high light intensity, salinity, and flagellin) and their double combinations indicated 105 that a significant change in the transcriptome induced by double stress combinations could not be 106 predicted from the corresponding single stresses (Rasmussen et al., 2013). These and other 107 studies (reviewed by Suzuki et al., 2014) support the notion that plant responses to stress 108 combinations are regulated through interactions, either positive or negative, of different signaling 109 pathways. Yet, little is known about the genetic and physiological patterns among partially 110 overlapping stress combinations and the genetic determinants facilitating plant acclimations. 111

*Brachypodium distachyon* (L.) Beauv. is a wild temperate model grass with close 112 evolutionary relationships to wheat and barley (International *Brachypodium* Initiative, 2010). In 113 recent years, *B. distachyon* has emerged as a powerful model plant to accelerate improvement of 114 stress related traits in cereal crops (Lv et al., 2014; Priest et al., 2014; Shaar-Moshe et al. 2015; 115 Des Marais et al., 2017). Here, we employed a system biology approach to study the morpho - 116 physiological and transcriptional patterns associated with acclimations of *B. distachyon* to 117 combinations of salinity, drought and heat stresses. We hypothesized that combination-unique 118 acclimations, which occur only among combined stresses, have distinct transcriptional and 119 morpho-physiological patterns compared with common stress acclimations, which are shared 120 among single and combined stresses. The aims of the current study were to (*i*) characterize the 121 morpho-physiological acclimations under combinations of stresses and decipher their underlying 122 genetic factors, (*ii*) identify transcriptional patterns that differ between common stress- and 123 combination unique-genes and (*iii*) detect biological processes associated with physiological 124 traits which differ between the two gene subsets. 125

Comprehensive morpho-physiological analyses of *B. distachyon* plants demonstrated a 126 gradual decrease in plant performances as more stresses were combined, as well as dominance of 127 a specific stress treatment that shaped plant acclimations. Investigation of the genetic interplay, 128 between the transcriptional patterns of common stress- and combination unique-differentially 129 expressed genes (DEGs), revealed specific transcriptional signatures, within the two gene 130 subsets. Enrichment analysis of co-expressed networks identified functional stress-modules and 131 potential hub genes, among the two gene subsets that could shed light on both central and novel 132 acclimation mechanisms to combinations of salinity, drought and heat stresses. The results 133 provide a wide perspective of the physiological and transcriptional modifications under four 134 partially overlapping stress combinations and highlight the unique patterns and functional 135 pathways that are differentially regulated. In addition, the results manifest the significance of the 136 experimental design for breeding efforts towards development of resilience cereal-crops under 137 the projected climatic changes. 138

#### **RESULTS** 142

#### **Developing stress combination assays** 143

To study the acclimation of *B. distachyon* to co-occurring environmental stresses, combinatorial 144 stress assays were established. Soil-grown plants were exposed to control conditions, single 145 stresses (salinity [S], drought [D] or heat [H]), double stress combinations (S&D, S&H and D&H) 146 and triple stress combination (S&D&H). To mimic the natural Mediterranean-like field 147 conditions, we applied prolonged and progressive salinity and drought stresses. Heat stress, 148 which developed rapidly and fluctuated during day and night, was imposed at anthesis for four 149 days, similarly to a typical Mediterranean Spring heatwave. Stress combinations were 150 established by integrating single stresses (Fig. 1). With the exception of single heat stress, both 151 drought and salinity stresses and any combination of each single stress resulted in plant biomass 152 reduction (Supplemental Fig. S1). Similarly, a reduction in shoot and root growth was observed 153 four days after anthesis under all stress treatments, except for single heat stress (Fig. 2). Spike 154 size and morphology, as well as leaf size, were also altered under the different stresses and their 155 combinations (Fig. 2, A-D). 156

## **Plant acclimation to stress was shaped by a specific stress treatment or by a gradual** 158 **aggravation under combined stresses** 159

Plant acclimations to environmental stresses are consequences of stress-induced changes in 160 several processes resulting in an array of phenotypical differences. To study these differences, 161 we conducted a morpho-physiological characterization of plant acclimations to single and 162 combined stress. Traits were assembled into morphological / yield-related traits and 163 physiological categories and analyzed using hierarchical clustering. The analysis of 164 morphological and yield parameters grouped all salinity treatments to one branch, separating 165 them from all other stresses (Fig. 2E; Supplemental Fig. S2). Within the salinity and non-salinity 166 branches, heat treatments clustered either with control or with the corresponding stresses (for 167 example, the combination of heat and salinity was clustered with single salinity stress) (Fig. 2E). 168 Among the different stresses, the combination of salinity and drought and the triple combination 169 caused detrimental impacts on plant performances (e.g. reduced culm length [-59.1% and - 170 61.8%], biomass [-61.9% and -63%] and grain number [-73.2% and -82%], respectively; Fig. 2E; 171 Supplemental Fig. S2A). 172

An altered treatment partition was detected by the hierarchical clustering of physiological 173 traits. While control conditions formed an isolated branch, stress treatments were separated into 174 two sub-branches based on heat and non-heat treatments. Among heat-treatments, the double 175 stresses, S&H and D&H, were clustered together and separated from both heat and the triple 176 combination. D&H and S&D&H showed a similar pattern of significant decreases in relative water 177 content (RWC; -4.7% and -10%, respectively), transpiration rate (-61.8% and -89.5%, 178 respectively) and photosynthesis (-75.1% and -99.9%, respectively), compared with control 179 (Supplemental Fig. S2B). S&D&H showed stronger stress effects, which were opposite to control 180 for all the physiological traits (Fig. 2F; Supplemental Fig. S2B). It is worth noting that at the 181 time of sampling (i.e. four days after anthesis), heat treatments did not induce distinct visual 182 effects in the plants and that these changes became apparent two weeks later (i.e. leaf 183 senescence) (Supplemental Fig. S3). 184

### **Heat-treatments resulted in a major impact on plant transcriptome** 186

We used RNA-seq to examine global transcriptional changes in plants subjected to single and 187 combined stresses. A principal component analysis (PCA), which detected expression patterns 188 regardless to significance or fold change thresholds, revealed a high similarity among the three 189 biological replicates, within each treatment. Among treatments, a clear separation between heat 190 and non-heat treatments was observed on the first principal component (PC1), explaining 53% of 191 the total variance (Fig. 3A). Correlation coefficient (*r*) analysis of log2 fold change (FC) values 192 further indicated the similarities among heat treatments (0.68 ≤ *r* ≤ 0.83). Yet, as opposed to the 193 PCA, the triple stress combination was found to have high correlation coefficients with all 194 treatments (average *r* = 0.75), except with drought stress (*r* =0.44). Between drought and its 195 corresponding double stresses, a lower correlation (average *r* = 0.5) was found compared with 196 salinity and heat and their corresponding double stresses (average *r* = 0.68 and 0.72, 197 respectively; Fig. 3B). 198

To examine the uniqueness of plant transcriptional acclimation to combined stresses, the 199 percentage of treatment-specific differentially-expressed genes (DEGs) and the percentage DEGs 200

that were altered in at least two stresses (i.e. shared-DEGS) were assessed. Stress combination 201 treatments showed a significant 2.8-fold increase in treatment specific-DEGs compared with 202 single stress (13.3% *versus* 4.7%, respectively, *P* < 0.02; Fig. 3C). Notably, S&D had the highest 203 percentage of treatment specific-DEGs, which was in agreement with the PCA that separated 204 S&D from all other stress combinations and with the low correlation coefficient that was found 205 for S&D as compared with the other stress combinations (Fig. 3). 206

#### **Integration between transcriptional changes and physiological acclimations** 208

It has been suggested that the number of DEGs is associated with the complexity of the stress 209 and its intensity (e.g. Prasch and Sonnewald, 2013). Our results showed that in *B. distachyon*, the 210 percentage of DEGs varied extensively in response to single stresses, ranging between 0.7% and 211 16% of the *B. distachyon* transcriptome (3,313, 194 and 4,368 DEGs under salinity, drought and 212 heat stresses, respectively). Drought stress resulted in the lowest DEG number (Fig. 3C), which 213 is in accordance with a previous report (Verelst et al., 2013). In general, a prominent increase in 214 the percentage of DEGs was found under combinations of stresses, where 24-30% of the 215 transcriptome was modulated (Fig. 3C; Supplemental Table S1). However, focusing on DEG 216 numbers may not be sufficient to explain the severity and complexity of the stress. For example, 217 the highest number of DEGs was detected under the combination of salinity and heat (8,032 218 DEGs) rather than the triple stress combination (7,610 DEGs; Fig. 3C). 219

To find a more informative measurement for stress intensity and complexity, and gain a 220 better understanding on plant acclimations to different stresses, we integrated transcriptional 221 patterns and physiological traits. Functional gene categories of photosynthesis and stress 222 responses (MapMan annotations; Thimm et al., 2004) were selected for this analysis. PCA was 223 conducted to detect categories' first and second principle components (PC), which showed 224 maximal data variation and best summarize category's expression patterns. Each functional 225 category displayed a unique order of stress severity and treatments clustering. Photosynthesis 226 was adversely affected mainly by combinations of drought and heat (i.e. D&H and S&D&H), 227 whereas, stress response genes and grain yield clustered based on single *versus* combined 228 stresses, indicating gradual changes in the expression levels of stress-associated genes and yield 229 parameters, concomitant with an increase in stress intensity (Fig. 4). Among 157 photosynthesis- 230 related DEGs that were differentially regulated in at least one stress treatment, PC1, which 231 explained 47.7% of the total variance, was highly correlated with leaf photosynthetic rate (*r* = 0.8, 232 *P* = 0.03; Fig. 4A). Similarly, among 501 stress response DEGs, differentially regulated in at least 233 one stress treatment, PC2, which explained 23.4% of the variance, showed a high correlation 234 with grain yield (*r* = 0.92, *P* = 0.004; Fig. 4B). Thus, expression modifications of stress response 235 genes at anthesis may have long-term mitigating effects on plant productivity at the end of the 236 growing season. 237

#### **Common stress-DEGs characterized by partial response mode consistency** 239

To assess the extent of similarity in gene expression under single and combined stresses, and the 240 involvement of these DEGs in plant acclimations to stress combinations, we conducted a 241 transcriptional pattern analysis. Six response modes, which broadly encapsulate the major 242 patterns among single and combined stresses, were defined based on transcript FC values: 243 additive, synergistic, neutral, dominant, antagonistic and equalization (Fig. 5A). This analysis 244 focused on two gene subsets: *i*) DEGs shared among single and combined stresses (i.e. *common* 245

*stress-DEGs*) and *ii*) genes that were differentially expressed only among combinations of 246 stresses (i.e. *combination unique-DEGs*). A seven-way Venn diagram was used to detect 247 common stress-DEGs that were defined as DEGs under at least two single stresses and included 248 1,550 transcripts (Fig. 5B; Supplemental Fig. S4). These genes were assigned to the six 249 transcriptional response modes and yielded four distinct expression patterns of double and triple 250 stress combinations (Supplemental Fig. S5; Supplemental Table S2). 251

In general, the six modes can be classified into expected (i.e. neutral, additive and 252 equalization) and unexpected (i.e. synergistic, dominant and antagonistic) responses. Among the 253 response pattern of the triple stress combination, most DEGs corresponded to unexpected modes 254 (63%) compared with an average of 40% of the DEGs among response patterns of double stress 255 combinations. The transcriptional pattern of S&D was depleted with antagonistic and synergistic 256 modes, while D&H was enriched with additive mode. The transcriptional pattern of the triple 257 stress combination displayed an increment in dominant mode and a decrease in neutral mode 258 (Supplemental Fig. S5). Examination of response mode consistency, of each DEG, among the 259

four transcriptional patterns of combined stresses, revealed that on average, only 37% of the 260 DEGs maintained the same response mode (Fig. 5C; Supplemental Table S2). Although these 261 were common stress-DEGs, the majority of the genes did not maintain the same mode under the 262 partially overlapping stress combinations. 263

264

#### **Response patterns of stress combination unique- DEGs** *versus* **common stress-DEGs** 265

To test the hypothesis that combination unique acclimations have distinct transcriptional patterns 266 compared with common stress acclimations, we defined combination unique-DEGs. A four-way 267 Venn diagram, which included unique-DEGs among S&D, S&H, D&H and S&D&H, was used to 268 detect DEGs common to at least two double stresses (Fig. 5D; Supplemental Fig. S6). This 269 analysis identified 2,561 DEGs, which were assigned to the six response modes (Supplemental 270 Table S3). A comparison between the response mode partition of common stress-DEGs 271 (represented by the expression pattern of the triple stress combination) and combination unique- 272 DEGs, revealed significant differences. While common stress-DEGs, were enriched with 273 additive (27%) and synergistic (5%) modes (*P*χ 2 < 0.001), combination unique-DEGs were 274 enriched with neutral (6%) and antagonistic (44%) modes (*P*χ 2 < 0.001; Fig. 5E). 275

## **Pathway and co-expression analyses detected functional differences between common** 277 **stress- and combination unique-DEGs** 278

To explore the biological functions among common stress- and combination unique-DEGs, we 279 performed an enrichment analysis of biological pathways, based on MapMan annotations. This 280 analysis detected seven enriched categories among common stress-DEGs, including transport 281 (i.e. transport of peptides, amino acids and major intrinsic proteins) and metabolism of amino 282 acids, carbohydrates (i.e. raffinose family of oligosaccharides and trehalose), secondary 283 metabolites (i.e. isoprenoids and phenylpropanoids) and hormones. Among combination unique- 284 DEGs only one category of photosynthesis was enriched (Supplemental Fig. S7). 285

A weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) of the two gene subsets, in 286 which an a priori partition to response modes was not defined, was used to uncover relationships 287 between genes, based on their correlation patterns, and identify core functional clusters (i.e. 288 modules) of highly co-expressed genes (Langfelder and Horvath, 2008). We detected 12 and 22 289 modules among common stress- and combination unique-DEGs, respectively, including on 290 average, 95% of the DEGs in each subset (Supplemental Fig. S8, A and B). Enrichment analysis 291 of response modes within the modules, revealed that 75% and 91% of the modules were enriched 292 with at least one response mode, among common stress- and combination unique-DEGs, 293 respectively. Within each module, on average, 58% of the genes were assigned to one response 294 mode. These results demonstrated the coherence between response pattern and WGCNA 295 (Supplemental Fig. S9, A and B). 296

WGCNA was further used to identify associations between gene expression and stress 297 acclimation physiological traits (i.e. relative water content [RWC] and osmotic adjustment 298 [OA]). Among common stress-DEGs, six and three modules correlated significantly between 299 module eigengene (ME, the first principal component of a given module; Langfelder and 300 Horvath, 2007) and RWC or OA, respectively (r ≥ |0.6|, *P* ≤ 0.05; Supplemental Fig. S10A; 301 Supplemental Table S4). Among combination unique-DEGs, six and five modules were 302 significantly correlated with RWC or OA, respectively (r ≥ |0.6|, *P* ≤ 0.05; Supplemental Fig. 303 S10B; Supplemental Table S5). Module membership (i.e. the correlation of ME and gene 304 expression profile), quantify the similarity of all genes to each module and implies highly 305 connected intra-modular hub genes. On average, 80% of the aforementioned modules had a high 306 correlation (*r* > 0.5) between module membership and gene significance (i.e. correlation of gene 307 expression and the physiological traits) (Supplemental Fig. S11). This may indicate that central 308 genes within these modules were also associated with RWC or OA. 309

Modules, significantly correlated with RWC or OA, were subjected to enrichment analysis 310 of biological pathways (FDR ≤ 0.05). By allocating the common stress-DEGs into functional 311 modules, we were able to detect additional processes among four of the modules, including post- 312 translational modification (module 01), signaling (module 02), transport (module 03) and 313 development (module 04) (Fig. 6B; Supplemental Table S6). Among combination unique-DEGs, 314 four modules that showed correlation with physiological traits were also enriched with biological 315 pathways. Most of these pathways were annotated as not assigned or unknown (modules 04 & 316 05), and none of them was detected among common stress-DEGs. These pathways included 317 numerous genes that encoded PPR repeat-containing proteins (module 02 & 04), DNA synthesis 318 and chromatin structure (module 02) and mitochondrial electron transport/ATP synthesis genes 319 (module 01) (Fig. 6C; Supplemental Table S7). Notably, photosynthesis which was the only 320 process detected among combination unique-DEGs, was not detected by WGCNA (Fig. 6, A and 321 C). 322

#### **DISCUSSION** 350

The Near Eastern Mediterranean region, where *Brachypodium* has evolved (Vogel et al., 2006), 351 is characterized by a long, hot, dry summer and a short, mild, wet winter (Loss and Siddique, 352 1994). In this region, soil salinity is a growing problem due to both natural and anthropogenic 353 effects (Munns and Gilliham, 2015). Salinity, drought and heat, usually develop as gradual and 354 chronic stresses that occur simultaneously (e.g. salinity and drought) or as a combination of 355 chronic and transient stresses (e.g. terminal drought and heatwaves). These stresses reach a high 356 level of severity at the reproductive stage, which is one of the most sensitive stages in 357 determining yield under stress for temperate grasses (Barnabas et al., 2008). To gain knowledge 358 on temperate grass acclimations to naturally co-occurring stresses, we developed an experimental 359 strategy that mimicked the Mediterranean-like field conditions. Plant acclimations to abiotic 360 stresses are affected by the plant species, the developmental stage of the plant and the nature of 361 the stress. Therefore, the stress protocol (i.e. progressive or short stress, climatic conditions and 362 growth substrate of the plants) as well as sampling dates and plant organ were carefully selected 363 and managed. All single and combined stresses were maintained at sub-lethal levels to simulate 364 naturally occurring long-term stresses and to ensure grain production (Fig. 2; Supplemental Fig. 365 S2). We used yield-related traits, rather than plant survival, to assess plant acclimation to stress 366 (Peleg et al., 2011). 367

Our working hypothesis was that combination unique acclimations show specific morph- 368 physiological pattern compared with common stress acclimations. However, hierarchical 369 clustering of morphological and physiological traits revealed two different responses: increased 370 stress intensity under combinations of stresses compared with single stresses, and dominance of 371 either salt- or heat-treatments (Fig. 2, E and F; Supplemental Fig. S2). Several physiological 372 traits (e.g. photosynthesis rate and stomatal conductance) and yield related traits (e.g. total 373 biomass and grains per plant), declined more under combined stresses compared with single 374 stresses; on the other hand, morphological traits were most influenced by salt treatments. The 375 long exposure of the plants to elevated salt concentrations, starting at an early developmental 376 stage, resulted in smaller plants (Munns, 2002). Salt toxicity and reduced soil osmotic potential 377 resulted in a reduction in the amount of energy acquired by the plants (by reducing 378 photosynthesis rate and leaf area) and in the redistribution of energy from growth and grain 379 filling to stress defense mechanisms, which subsequently decreased yields (Munns and Gilliham, 380 2015; Fig. 2; Supplemental Fig. S2). Under heat treatments, RWC values were significantly 381 lower than the control plants. This reduction can explain the decrease in stomatal conductance 382 and photosynthesis rates, especially under combinations of heat stress (Fig. 2E; Supplemental 383 Fig. S2B). This may be associated with the limited ability of the plants to fulfill the high 384 atmospheric demand as indicated by leaf VPD (~2.5-fold higher under heat stress as compared 385 with control conditions). 386

In general, *B. distachyon* plants demonstrated a high phenotypic plasticity, i.e. the ability 387 of a single genotype to produce a range of phenotypes under different environments (Bradshaw, 388 1965) (Fig. 2; Supplemental Fig. S2). Plasticity, which is genetically controlled, can contribute to 389 species' evolution since it provides a defense against rapid climate change and assists in rapid 390 adaptation (Nicotra et al., 2010). Physiological plasticity and its importance in plant acclimation 391 to environmental stress conditions, was previously reported for various wild grasses that 392 originated from the eastern Mediterranean region, such as wild emmer wheat (*T. turgidum* spp. 393 *dicoccoides* [Körn.] Thell.; Peleg et al., 2005) and wild barley (*Hordeum spontaneum* Koch; 394 Volis et al., 2002). 395

To better understand the transcriptional changes that are involved in plant acclimations to 396 single and combined stresses, we analyzed the transcriptional patterns under stress. Similarly to 397 our findings, combined application of heat, drought and virus in Arabidopsis, highlighted heat as 398 a major stress factor that resulted in a higher number of DEGs, compared with the other 399 treatments, and with a profound effect on the plant transcriptome (Prasch and Sonnewald, 2013). 400 These changes can result from alleviation of loci silencing within heterochromatin under heat 401 stress, through heat-induced reduction of nucleosome occupancy (Lang-Mladek et al., 2010). It 402 has been postulated that the decondensation of heterochromatin is important for transcriptional 403 activation of heterochromatin-embedded targets that may be involved in heat tolerance (Pecinka 404 et al., 2010). 405

Under combinations of stresses, plants undergo changes in gene expression that are in part 406 common with the corresponding single stresses (i.e. common stress-DEGs) and are in part 407 unique to stress combinations (i.e. combination unique-DEGs). The extent and composition of 408 combination unique-DEGs varied among different studies, but it has been shown that the 409 multifactorial nature of stress combinations limits the ability to predict plants' transcriptional 410 responses based on the corresponding single stresses (Prasch and Sonnewald, 2013; Rasmussen 411 et al., 2013; Sewelam et al., 2014; Suzuki et al., 2016). Investigation of the transcriptional 412 changes of 1,550 common stress-DEGs, yielded four different response patterns of triple and 413 double stress combinations that varied in response mode partitions (Supplemental Fig. S5). This 414 analysis enabled, for the first time, to quantify the extent of gene expression consistency and the 415 maintenance of expression patterns, among partially overlapping stress combinations. 416 Interestingly, comparison between the transcriptional patterns of the triple- and the three double- 417 stress combinations showed that only 37% of the common stress-DEGs maintained the same 418 response mode (Fig. 5C; Supplemental Table S2). These results suggest the fine-tuning in the 419 mode of action of DEGs common to single and combined stresses, under different stress 420 combinations, limiting our ability to deduce the function and contribution of specific genes to 421 stress acclimation based on the corresponding single stresses. 422

Comparison between common stress-DEGs and combination unique-DEGs (2,561 genes), 423 revealed a significant shift from increased stress intensity (i.e. enrichment in additive and 424 synergistic modes) to opposite responses (i.e. enrichment in antagonistic mode), respectively 425 (Fig. 5E). The majority (99%) of the combination unique-DEGs that were assigned to the 426 antagonistic mode were differentially expressed under at least two double stress combinations, 427 while their expression under the triple stress combination remained unchanged (Supplemental 428 Table S3). Moreover, among stress combination unique-DEGs, only 53% of the genes were 429 differentially expressed under the triple stress combination (Supplemental Fig. S6), as opposed to 430 90% among common stress-DEGs (Supplemental Fig. S4). The enrichment in antagonistic mode 431 under combined stresses, can result from different signaling pathways that interact and inhibit 432 one another (Suzuki et al., 2014), a process that may be further exacerbated under the triple 433 stress combination. It is also possible that the increased stress intensity under the triple stress 434 combination resulted in a partial shutdown of stress combination unique pathways, so that 435 mainly conserved stress pathways (i.e. common stress-DEGs) were activated. Alternatively, 436 since 10% of the genes, under the triple stress combination, were uniquely expressed only under 437 this combination (Fig. 3C), specific pathways regulating plant acclimation to stress may be 438 activated (Atkinson and Urwin, 2012). The partition of DEGs to expected and unexpected 439 responses, further supports the specificity of plant acclimations to each stress combination. 440 Rasmussen *et al*., (2013) found that on average 61% of the genes expressed among various 441 double stresses were assigned to unexpected responses. Here we show that among common 442 stress-DEGs, unexpected modes constituted, on average, 40% and 63% of the plant response to 443 double and triple stress combinations, respectively, whereas among combination unique-DEGs, 444 88% of the genes were assigned to unpredictable modes (Fig. 5E; Supplemental Fig. S5). These 445 results indicated that the predictability of the transcriptional response was diminished as the 446 complexity of the stress and the specificity of gene expression increased. 447

Common stress- and combination unique-DEGs differ not only in their transcriptional 448 signature but also between their functional pathways. Although this difference may result from 449 distinct strategies that the plants adopted to cope with combined stresses, we cannot exclude the 450 possibility that it is a result of gaps in our knowledge about the effects of combined stresses, 451 given the scarcity of studies that focused on the response(s) of plants to stress combinations. 452 Enrichment analysis of common stress-DEGs, complemented by WGCNA, detected processes 453 that are known to be involved in environmental stress acclimation. For example, degradation and 454 transport of amino acids, metabolism of the raffinose family of oligosaccharides, trehalose 455 (Krasensky and Jonak, 2012), isoprenoids and phenylpropanoids (Wahid et al., 2007; Tattini et 456 al., 2015), post-translational modifications and signaling (Cabello et al., 2014; Zhu, 2016) (Fig. 457 5A; Fig. 6B; Supplemental Table S6). These processes are suggested as core stress processes that 458 are functionally conserved under both single and combined stresses. Among combination 459 unique-DEGs, we identified specific and unique enriched pathways that were not detected 460 among common stress-DEGs, for example DNA synthesis and chromatin structure (module 02). 461 Investigation of the genes within this module revealed that 28% of them (55 genes) predicted to 462 be involved in chloroplast nucleoid metabolism and function (Supplemental Table S9). 463 Nucleoids are comprised of multiple copies of the plastid chromosome that are organized in 464 DNA-protein complexes (Sakai et al., 2004). The proteins in theses complexes are involved in 465 DNA- and RNA-associated functions, posttranscriptional and translational processes as well as 466 additional unknown functions. Mutant analyses and transgenic approaches of numerous 467 nucleoid-associated genes revealed a plethora of phenotypes, which affected not only chloroplast 468 development and plastid metabolism, but also hormone metabolism, plant morphology and stress 469 responses (reviewed by Molonek et al., 2016). In maize leaves, nucleoid-enriched proteome 470 shifted the predominating function from RNA metabolism in undeveloped plastids to translation 471 and homeostasis in mature chloroplasts (Majeran et al., 2012). Interestingly, our results showed 472 that the majority of the nucleoid-associated genes (47%), involved in known functions, were 473 assigned to RNA metabolic functions. This result suggests that combinations of stresses may 474 cause perturbations at the steady state level and functionality of the chloroplast nucleoids and 475 may partially explain the low photosynthesis rates and decreased plant performances under 476 combined stresses compared with single stresses. This and other processes (e.g. mitochondrial 477 electron transport), which are specifically involved in plant acclimations to stress combinations, 478 can serve as novel targets for the development of resilient crops to naturally co-occurring long- 479 term stresses. 480

*Conclusions* 482

The current study aims to bridge the gap between genomics and physiological acclimations to 483 naturally co-occurring long-term abiotic stresses, by employing a system biology approach. 484 Dissection of plant transcriptome into common stress- and combination unique-DEGs, revealed 485 specific transcriptional signatures and functional processes, within each gene subset. The 486 increment in stress intensity among common stress-DEGs correlated well with the pattern 487 detected by morpho-physiological analyses and with the enriched pathways that are involved in 488 canonic stress processes. Among combination unique-DEGs, response patterns shifted towards 489 antagonistic responses, indicating the limited ability to predict plant responses when several 490 stresses are combined, and the unique acclimations to each of the partially overlapping stress 491 combinations. The processes that are specifically involved in plant acclimations to combined 492 stresses are proposed as a basis for future research and breeding efforts towards developing crop 493 plants better adapted to the predicted climatic changes. 494

#### **Data processing and analysis** 577

FastQ files, generated from raw data obtained from Illumina sequencing, were processed and 578 filtered with the following publically available tools. Quality evaluation of each sample was 579 manually inspected using FastQC (http://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc). 580 Barcode removal and filtering of low quality reads were executed using the command line tools 581 cutadapt (https://code.google.com/p/cutadapt -e 0.1, -O 5, -m 15) and FASTX-Toolkit 582 (http://hannonlab.cshl.edu/fastx\_toolkit; fastq\_quality\_trimmer: -l 35, -t 28, fastq\_quality\_filter: 583 -q 20, -p 90), respectively. Sample sequences were aligned to the *B. distachyon* reference 584 genome (Bd21-3 v.1; http://www.brachypodium.org) using the Bowtie2 aligner (http://bowtie- 585 bio.sourceforge.net/bowtie2/index.shtml) within TopHat2 586 (http://ccb.jhu.edu/software/tophat/index.shtml; -i 20 -I 10000) (Kim et al., 2013), producing on 587 average a 24-fold (±6.2 SE) sequence coverage. The Bioconductor package GenomicFeatures 588 was used to retrieve transcript-related features from *B. distachyon* GTF file 589 (ftp://ftp.ensemblgenomes.org/pub/release-31/plants/gtf/brachypodium\_distachyon) and read 590 count table of all samples was produced with the Bioconductor package GenomicAlignments 591 (Lawrence et al., 2013). Differential expression analysis of count data and data visualization 592 were conducted with the DESeq2 package (Love et al., 2014). We used the function 593 rlogTransform to transform the data to regularized logarithm (rlog) values. This transformation is 594 similar to a log2 transformation for genes with high counts, while for genes with low counts it 595 shrinks together the values towards the genes' averages across all samples (Love et al., 2014). A 596 PCA was performed to explore the transcriptome and visualize sample-to-sample distances. To 597 detect DEGs, a 5% false discovery rate (FDR) correction for multiple comparisons was 598 determined (Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995), and a minimal |0.5| log2FC threshold was applied. 599 These thresholds resulted in detection of 13,580 genes that were differentially regulated in at 600 least one stress treatment. Venn diagrams created using the publically available software Venn 601 diagrams (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn). Functional annotation of DEGs to 602 biological processes and pathways was conducted with MapMan tool 603 (http://mapman.gabipd.org/web/guest/mapman), which allows genes to be tentatively assigned, 604 even when their function is only roughly known (Thimm et al., 2004). Pathway enrichment 605 analysis was performed with the functional enrichment analysis software tool FunRich (FDR ≤ 606 0.05; http://www.funrich.org; Pathan et al., 2015), using MapMan annotation as reference data. 607

#### **Expression patterns of common stress- and combination unique-DEGs** 609

A seven-way Venn diagram that included significant DEGs within each stress treatment was 610 used to identify DEGs common to at least two single stresses. These genes were defined as 611 common stress-DEGs. Stress combination unique-DEGs were detected by first subtracting DEGs 612 common between each combined stress and its corresponding single stresses. Next, a four-way 613 Venn diagram, which included the unique DEG lists of S&D, S&H, D&H and S&D&H, was used to 614 identify DEGs common to at least two double stress combinations. All together 1,550 and 2,561 615 genes were identified as common stress- and combination unique-DEGs, respectively. To 616 determine gene expression patterns among the two gene subsets, log2FC values of non- 617 significant transcripts (FDR > 0.05), were replaced with zeros, assuming minimal effect of 618 random FC variation. Likewise, log2FC values < |0.5| were replaced with zeros, to minimize 619 background noise. log2FC values of each gene, under the different treatments, were used to 620 assign genes to six predefined transcriptional modes: additive, synergistic, neutral, dominant, 621 antagonistic and equalization. Standard deviations (SD) of stress combination log2FC values 622 were used to differentiate between modes. Common stress-DEGs yielded four expression 623 patterns that were generated by comparing between FC values of triple or double stress 624 combinations and the corresponding single stresses. Combination unique-DEGs resulted in one 625 expression pattern, generated by comparing between FC values of triple stress combination and 626 double stress combinations. For simplicity, definition of each response mode is explained using 627 the common stress-DEGs. Transcripts assigned to additive, synergistic or neutral modes, were 628

either up- or down-regulated among the single and combined stresses (i.e. similar expression 629 pattern). Additive and synergistic modes included transcripts with stress combination log2FC 630 values (±SD) equal to or higher than log2FC summation of the corresponding single stresses, 631 respectively. Neutral mode included transcripts that maintained similar log2FC values (±SD of 632 stress combination) among single and combined stresses. Transcripts with opposite expression 633 patterns (e.g. up-regulated in one treatment and down-regulated in another treatment) were 634 assigned to antagonistic or equalization modes. Antagonistic mode included transcripts with 635 opposite log2FC values or control levels under stress combinations compared whit the 636 corresponding single stresses, whereas equalization mode included transcripts that their log2FC 637 value under stress combination was equivalent (±SD) to the summation of log2FC of the 638 corresponding single stresses. Dominant mode comprised of transcripts from both similar and 639 opposite expression patterns that their log2FC value under single stress(es) was equal to log2FC 640 value under the stress combination (±SD). Transcripts that did not match the aforementioned 641 modes were assigned to not assigned (NA) mode. Circos on-line tool (http://circos.ca; 642 Krzywinski et al., 2009) was used to visualize mode consistency across the four patterns of 643 common stress-DEGs. 644

#### **Weighted gene co-expression network analysis** 646

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was conducted based on Langfelder 647 and Horvath (2008) and the WGCNA package R scripts available at 648 (https://labs.genetics.ucla.edu/horvath/CoexpressionNetwork/Rpackages/WGCNA/index.html). 649 Co-expression networks were constructed based on common stress- and combination unique- 650 DEGs. RNA-seq count data of these genes was rlog-transformed using the function 651 rlogTransform, which is implemented in DESeq2 package (Love et al., 2014). This data was 652 used to define a co-expression similarity matrix using the absolute values of Pearson correlation 653 coefficient between pairwise gene expression profiles. The co-expression similarity was 654 transformed into connection strengths (i.e. adjacency matrix) by raising values to a power of 14 655 and 13, for common stress- and combination unique-DEGs, respectively, which best approximate 656 scale-free topologies (model fit > 0.8; Supplemental Fig. S13, A and B). Gene sets with 657 expression pattern that were highly correlated across samples (i.e. modules), were determined 658 based on topological overlap dissimilarity measure in conjunction with linkage hierarchical 659 clustering using the blockwiseModules function. Similar transcripts were merged into modules 660 based on a height cut of 0.15 with a minimum module size of 25 genes. Module eigengene that 661 best represents module expression pattern and gene significance, the correlation between gene 662 expression and RWC or AO, were used to define quantitative measures of module membership 663 (*P* ≤ 0.05). Intra-modular connectivity was subsequently used to identify module hub genes. 664 Modules, significantly correlated with physiological traits (r ≥ 0.6, *P* ≤ 0.05) were exported for 665 visualization in Cytoscape (http://www.cytoscape.org; Shannon et al., 2003) using the 666 exportNetworkToCytoscape function, implemented in WGCNA, and adjacency thresholds of 667 0.15 and 0.25, for common stress- and combination unique-DEGS, respectively. Within 668 Cytoscape, networks were constructed using Spring Embedded layout method. Final figures 669 generated based on Cytoscape edge thresholds did not include all genes and their connectivities 670 as detected by WGCNA. However, gene annotations, based on MapMan bin allocation and 671 enrichment analysis, performed with FunRich software (http://www.funrich.org), comprised all 672 genes within modules that were significantly correlated with physiological traits. 673

Template cDNA was diluted by 16-fold, 682 based on a standard curve of four serial dilution points.
