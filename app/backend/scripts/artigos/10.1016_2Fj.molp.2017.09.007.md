### 17 In Brief

- 18 Histone variant H2A.Z near TSS negatively correlates with gene expression in *Arabidopsis*.
- 19 H2A.Z represses gene expression by establishing low gene accessibility at +1 nucleosome,
- 20 maintaining high gene accessibility at -1 nucleosome and promoting H3K27me3 histone
- 21 modifications at enhancers.

#### 22 ABSTRACT

Deposition of the histone variant H2A.Z at gene bodies regulates transcription by modifying 23 chromatin accessibility in plants. However, the role of H2A.Z enrichment at the promoter and 24 enhancer regions is unclear, and how H2A.Z interacts with other mechanisms of chromatin 25 modification to regulate gene expression remains obscure. Here, we mapped genome-wide 26 H2A.Z, H3K4me3, H3K27me3, Pol II and nucleosome occupancy in Arabidopsis 27 inflorescence. We showed that H2A.Z preferentially associated with H3K4me3 at promoters, 28 while it was found with H3K27me3 at enhancers, and that H2A.Z deposition negatively 29 correlated with gene expression. Additionally, we demonstrated that H2A.Z represses gene 30 expression by establishing low gene accessibility at +1 nucleosome, and maintaining high 31 gene accessibility at -1 nucleosome. We further showed that the high measures of gene 32 responsiveness correlate with H2A.Z associated closed +1 nucleosome structure. Moreover, 33 we found that H2A.Z represses enhancer activity by promoting H3K27me3 and preventing 34 H3K4me3 histone modifications. This study provides a framework for future studies of 35 H2A.Z functions and opens up new aspects of study for decoding the interplay between 36 chromatin modification and histone variants in transcriptional control. 37

#### 42 INTRODUCTION

Eukaryotic DNA is packaged into a compact structure termed chromatin. The basic unit of 43 44 chromatin is the nucleosome, consisting of 147 bp of DNA wrapped around the octamer, a structure including one H3-H4 tetramers consisting of two copies of Histone 3 and Histone 4 45 46 and two H2A-H2B dimers. Eukaryotic genomes possess several histone variants, and each of them confers different properties to the nucleosome, which in turn affects numerous 47 biological processes, most commonly transcription. Histones can also be modified post-48 translationally and in turn affect transcription. All these possibilities lead to a very dynamic 49 50 chromatin (Ernst et al., 2011). Chromatin serves as a useful means to compress genetic information but represents a barrier for multiple cellular processes such as DNA replication, 51 transcription and accessibility to DNA. Histone variants can function to regulate the general 52 structure of chromatin and the accessibility of sequence-specific binding proteins and 53 enzymes to DNA. Replacement of canonical histones with specific variant types is one of the 54

55 mechanisms that the cell can use to alter nucleosome structure, stability, dynamics and 56 ultimately DNA accessibility (Talbert and Henikoff, 2010). Incorporation of histone variants 57 into chromatin can promote changes in transcription that regulate the proper timing of 58 developmental decisions and appropriate responses to external environmental cues (Weber 59 and Henikoff, 2014).

H2A.Z, one of the most enigmatic and well-conserved histone variants, is involved in 60 multiple chromosomal processes such as genome integrity, heterochromatin establishment, 61 chromosomal segregation, DNA repair, transcriptional regulation, cell-cycle progression and 62 thermosensory response (Brickner et al., 2007; Kumar and Wigge, 2010; Luk et al., 2010; 63 Zlatanova and Thakar, 2008). H2A.Z frequently co-localizes with transcription start sites 64 (TSSs), often flanking low nucleosome regions. This feature seems to be conserved across 65 protozoa, fungi, animals and plants (Barski et al., 2007; Creyghton et al., 2008; Guillemette 66 et al., 2005; Li et al., 2005; Petter et al., 2011; Zilberman et al., 2008). 67

The conserved H2A.Z distribution pattern at the TSS region across species has led to a 68 considerable effort to understand the functional relationship between H2A.Z and 69 transcription. In yeast, H2A.Z is enriched in small regions within the promoters of inactive 70 genes, but is generally absent from promoters of highly active genes (Guillemette et al., 2005; 71 Millar et al., 2006). In contrast, studies in animal systems revealed that H2A.Z exhibits a 72 positive correlation with transcription (Barski et al., 2007; Hardy et al., 2009; Hu et al., 2013; 73 Mavrich et al., 2008). In plants, H2A.Z enrichment within gene bodies correlates with lower 74 transcription levels and higher variability in expression patterns (Coleman-Derr and 75 Zilberman, 2012a; Zilberman et al., 2008). Recent reports consistently showed that the levels 76 of H2A.Z in the gene body of drought-responsive genes negatively correlate with transcript 77 levels in Arabidopsis (Sura et al., 2017). However, the effect of the distribution of H2A.Z 78 around the TSS on gene expression in plants is not obvious, although a dual role of H2A.Z at 79 the first nucleosome downstream of a TSS (+1 nucleosome) has been recently proposed (Sura 80 et al., 2017). To date, the mechanism by which H2A.Z interacts with other factors such as 81 nucleosome composition or histone modification to influence transcription remains unclear. 82

To understand how H2A.Z functions together with other mechanisms of chromatin regulation in the control of gene expression, we investigated the distribution patterns of H2A.Z, H3K4me3, H3K27me3 mark and RNA polymerase II (Pol II) occupancy using chromatin immunoprecipitation-seq (ChIP-seq) with inflorescence tissues, because extensive evidences have shown that H2A.Z plays an important role in regulating plant reproductive development, including flowering time, inflorescence architecture, floral organ development,

meiosis and plant gamete development (Cai et al., 2017; Choi et al., 2007; Choi et al., 2013; 89 Deal et al., 2005; Deal et al., 2007; Qin et al., 2014), but the underlying mechanism is not 90 known. We also analyzed general nucleosome occupancy and DNA accessibility using 91 Micrococcal Nuclease-seq (MNase-seq). Incorporation of H2A.Z in nucleosomes is mediated 92 by the ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling complex SWR1 (Luk et al., 2010; Mizuguchi et 93 al., 2004; Wu et al., 2005a). ARP6 is a subunit of the SWR1 complex, and the *arp6* mutation 94 95 results in depletion of H2A.Z from chromatin and leads to defects in plant reproductive development (Choi et al., 2007; Choi et al., 2013; Deal et al., 2005; Deal et al., 2007; Kumar 96 and Wigge, 2010; March-Diaz et al., 2008; Martin-Trillo et al., 2006; Noh and Amasino, 97 2003; Qin et al., 2014; Smith et al., 2010). To elucidate the role of H2A.Z in gene expression 98 and investigate the underlying mechanism, we performed association analysis of H2A.Z 99 deposition with nucleosome occupancy, histone modifications and gene transcription level in 100 wild-type (WT) and *arp6* mutant plants. Our data indicate a negative correlation between 101 H2A.Z at TSS and transcription levels. We showed that H2A.Z at +1 nucleosome leads to a 102 closed nucleosome structure, low chromatin accessibility and low levels of H3K4me3, which 103 are required to repress gene transcription and regulate gene responsiveness. 104

#### 105 **RESULTS**

#### 106 H2A.Z is preferentially associated with H3K4me3 at promoters

We selected well-established epigenetic marks widely used in yeast, animals, and plants to 107 investigate the distribution patterns of chromatin marks and characterize chromatin states in 108 Arabidopsis inflorescence genome-wide (Ho et al., 2014). We performed chromatin 109 immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing (ChIP-seq) using antibodies against the histone 110 variant H2A.Z. This led to the identification of 23,190 peaks ("decorated" 19,976 genes) 111 using the SICER software (Figure 1A, Supplemental Table 1) (Zang et al., 2009). H2A.Z 112 peaks were mostly in promoters (positioned -1 kb to 0, TSS designated as 0) and 5' UTR 113 regions (Figure 1B, Supplemental Table 2). 114

To test whether H2A.Z deposition correlates with histone modifications in inflorescences, we performed ChIP-seq using antibodies against H3K4me3, H3K27me3, and Pol II. Thus, we identified 16,254 (15,671 target genes), 6,586 (6,183 target genes) and 8,294 (7,755 target genes) H3K4me3, H3K27me3 and RNA Pol II peaks respectively (Figure 1A, Supplemental Table 3-5), which are comparable to previous studies (Veluchamy et al., 2016; Zhai et al., 2015). They were all enriched mostly in promoter regions (Figure 1B,

Supplemental Table 2). Globally, 52% (Fisher's exact test, p < 2.2E-16) of the H2A.Z peaks in promoters co-localize with H3K4me3 peaks, while only 25% (Fisher's exact test, p < 2.2E-16) of the H2A.Z peaks in promoters associated with H3K27me3 peaks (Figure 1C, left panel), indicating that the spatial distribution of H2A.Z in the promoter regions correlated more with H3K4me3 than with H3K27me3.

#### 126 H2A.Z is highly correlated with H3K27me3 at enhancers

Enhancers, independently of their distance and orientation of their target genes, play a central 127 role in controlling transcriptional programs by integrating internal and external signals 128 through the recruitment of specific transcription factors and co-activator complexes, to 129 coordinately regulate the transcription of different subsets of target genes (Lupien et al., 130 2008). We examined the levels of H2A.Z, H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 at putative enhancers 131 defined by intergenic DNase I hypersensitive sites (DHSs) using floral tissue (Zhu et al., 132 2015). In contrast to promoter regions, we observed a lower level of correlation of H2A.Z 133 sites with H3K4me3 (25%) (Fisher's exact test, p < 2.2E-16) than with H3K27me3 (43%) 134 (Fisher's exact test, p < 2.2E-16) at enhancers (Figure 1C, right panel). 135

While enhancers generally co-localize with H3K4me1 (Heintzman et al., 2007), active 136 enhancers are also known to be associated with H3K27ac (Wang et al., 2008; Zhu et al., 137 2015) and H3K4me3 (Pekowska et al., 2011), and poised enhancers are associated by 138 H3K27me3 (Rada-Iglesias et al., 2011; Zhu et al., 2015). To define 'chromatin states' that 139 closely associate with transcriptional programs, we next integrated the ChIP-seq data 140 generated from this study (H2A.Z, H3K4me3, and H3K27me3) with the ChIP-seq data from 141 a previous study (H3K27ac and H3K4me1) (Zhang et al., 2016). Using a hidden Markov 142 143 model (ChromHMM), we constructed a combinational epigenetic modification map (Ernst and Kellis, 2010). The analysis captures two types of chromatin information: one indicated 144 by a set of 'emission' parameters that captures the frequency in which different combinations 145 of chromatin marks correlate with each other; the second, indicates 'transition' parameters 146 and calculates the probability with which different chromatin states concur spatially in the 147 genome (see Methods). This approach has the advantage of capturing regulatory elements 148 with greater reliability, robustness, and precision than by studying individual marks. Overall, 149 we defined ten different chromatin states that have a continuous emission probability from 150 zero (white) to one (blue) (Figure 1D and 1E). All the states tend to remain in their own state 151 (Figure 1E), except state 10 (defined by H3K4me3 and H2A.Z), which is most associated 152 with TSS (Figure 1F and Supplemental Figure 1A) and has a high probability of transitioning 153

to state 9 (defined by H3K27Ac, H3K4me3 and H2A.Z), state 7 (no enrichment) and state 3
(defined by H2A.Z only), indicating the transient association of H2A.Z with active marks at
TSS.

We found that the ten chromatin states were preferentially associated with specific 157 genomic features (Figure 1D and 1F). For example, the H3K27me3 defined state 1 was 158 mostly enriched (Figure 1F) in enhancer regions (Supplemental Figure 1B), whereas state 2 159 (defined by H2A.Z and H3K27me3) is distributed widely across different genomic regions 160 with the highest enrichment being in exons. State 3 (H2A.Z only) was preferentially 161 associated with enhancer and transcriptional end site (TES) (Figure 1F and Supplemental 162 Figure 1B and 1C). Taken together, these data show that H2A.Z and H3K27me3 were highly 163 correlated at poised enhancers, which represents the majority of enhancers in Arabidopsis 164 floral tissue. 165

#### 166 Enhancers associated with both H2A.Z and H3K27me3 are more likely to be inactive

To study the function of H2A.Z at enhancers and understand how H2A.Z functions together 167 with histone modifications in the regulation of enhancer activity, we analyzed the expression 168 levels of putative target genes (the most proximal genes flanking each of the enhancer) (Zhu 169 et al., 2015) by RNA-seq and found that the expression levels of putative target genes for 170 H2A.Z-bound enhancers are significantly lower than those of non-H2A.Z-bound enhancers 171 (Figure 1G, p<E-30, K-S test). When genes located in  $\pm 10$  kb of the predicated enhancers 172 was designated as the putative target genes of enhancers, increased gene expression level of 173 putative target genes for non-H2A.Z-bound enhancers compared to that of H2A.Z-bound 174 enhancers was consistently obtained (Supplemental Figure 2A). These results suggested a 175 negative role of H2A.Z in regulating enhancer activity. We have shown above that H2A.Z 176 preferentially associated with H3K27me3 at enhancers. To further determine the functions of 177 H2A.Z at H3K27me3-bound poised enhancers, we compared the expression levels of 178 predicted target genes for H3K27me3-bound enhancers with H2A.Z (w/) to those without 179 H2A.Z (w/o) and found that the expression levels of predicted target genes (the most 180 proximal genes flanking each of the enhancer) for H3K27me3-bound enhancers with H2A.Z 181 are dramatically lower than those without H2A.Z (Figure 1H). These results together 182 indicated the repressive function of H2A.Z at enhancers and implied that the enhancers with 183 both H2A.Z and H3K27me3 are more likely to be inactive. 184

#### 185 H2A.Z negatively correlates with gene expression levels

H2A.Z is preferentially distributed in promoter regions with highest enrichment in the first 186 (+1) nucleosome after the TSS (Zilberman et al., 2008). This pattern of H2A.Z deposition is 187 conserved from yeast to humans (Barski et al., 2007; Raisner et al., 2005). Consistently, we 188 found that the 56% of H2A.Z peak in Arabidopsis inflorescence is located inside the 189 promoter region (-1 kb to TSS) (Figure 2A), and 46% of promoters are associated with 190 H2A.Z (Figure 2B). Therefore, in this study we focused on the function of H2A.Z near TSS 191 (-1 kb to +1 kb) including promoters. To investigate the correlation between H2A.Z near TSS 192 and gene expression levels, we divided the 17,494 expressed genes (FPKM  $\geq$  1) into four 193 equal-sized groups according to their expression levels. H2A.Z around TSS including the +1 194 nucleosome was found to negatively correlate with gene expression level, with the most 195 highly expressed genes showing the lowest levels of H2A.Z and the least expressed genes 196 showing the highest levels of H2A.Z (Figure 2C), similarly to that for the repressive 197 H3K27me3 mark (Figure 2D), indicating a negative role of H2A.Z near TSS in gene 198 expression. In agreement with this notion, we examined that the H2A.Z-bound promoters of 199 genes have lower expression levels relative to all expressed genes (Figure 2E, p<E-30, K-S 200 test) and non-H2A.Z-bound promoters of genes (Figure 2E, p<E-30, K-S test). As expected, 201 we observed a positive correlation of gene expression with the active mark H3K4me3 and 202 Pol II enrichment near TSS (Figure 2F and 2G). Our finding that H2A.Z negatively correlates 203 with gene expression is in contrast to earlier reports where H2A.Z near the TSS is most 204 enriched in the moderately transcribed genes (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman, 2012; Zilberman 205 206 et al., 2008). To understand the discrepancies, we used all Arabidopsis genes rather than expressed genes (FPKM≥1) for the correlation analysis, then obtained similar results with the 207 previous reports (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman, 2012; Zilberman et al., 2008) (Supplemental 208 Figure 2B). 209

To further study the correlation between H2A.Z near TSS and gene expression levels, 210 we performed integrative analysis of H2A.Z ChIP-seq and RNA-seq data using Bayesian 211 mixture models (Klein et al., 2014) to detect genes with differences between WT and *arp6*, a 212 well-accepted H2A.Z incorporation mutant (Deal et al., 2007), defective in both transcription 213 and H2A.Z deposition. The results showed that more transcripts were classified into the 214 negative (560) than the positive (172) component (Figure 3A and 3B; Supplemental Table 6), 215 similarly to the analysis for the repressive mark H3K27me3 (Figure 3C and 3D; 216 Supplemental Table 7), and in contrast to the analysis for the active mark H3K4me3 (Figure 217 3E and 3F; Supplemental Table 8), indicating a negative correlation between H2A.Z and gene 218

expression. When we used gene body (TSS to TES) rather than  $\pm 1$  kb around TSS regions for 219 this analysis, we also found that more transcripts were classified into the negative (402) than 220 the positive (127) component (Supplemental Table 9), suggesting that H2A.Z at gene bodies 221 also negatively correlates with gene transcription levels, in agreement with previous findings 222 (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman 2012b; Sura et al., 2017). To verify the negative correlation 223 between H2A.Z and gene expression identified from the H2A.Z ChIP-seq and RNA-seq data, 224 225 we randomly selected four *arp6* up-regulated genes for qRT–PCR and ChIP-qPCR assay. The results showed that the *arp6* increased expression pattern is consistently associated with *arp6* 226 reduced H2A.Z enrichment (Supplemental Figure 3A-3D). Among the 560 transcripts in the 227 negative cluster of H2A.Z around TSS, we found that almost all of them are responsive genes 228 mostly to environmental cues (temperature, light) but also to internal signals (ie. oxidative 229 stress) (Supplemental Figure 2C). These data together demonstrated that H2A.Z plays 230 dominant roles in repressing stress responsive genes and involves in plant response to 231 temperature and light. However, the 172 positively regulated genes were chiefly enriched in 232 circadian rhythm process, suggesting a possible role for H2A.Z in the circadian regulation of 233 a subset of genes (Supplemental Figure 2D). 234

# H2A.Z deposition at +1 nucleosome is associated with low gene accessibility and H2A.Z at -1 nucleosome is associated with high gene accessibility

It is well accepted that H2A.Z influences gene transcription by regulating chromatin structure 237 and gene accessibility (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman, 2012b). To investigate whether the 238 repressive role of H2A.Z in transcription control in *Arabidopsis* inflorescence is through the 239 modulation of nucleosome structure, we performed MNase accessibility assays followed by 240 241 sequencing. MNase digests the nucleosome-free DNA and the linker regions between nucleosomes, providing an accurate estimation of nucleosome occupancy and gene 242 accessibility (Petesch and Lis, 2008). We found that WT nucleosome occupancy exhibits a 243 profile similar to H2A.Z, having the highest enrichment at +1 nucleosome and lowest 244 enrichment at -1 nucleosome (Figure 4A and 4B). Moreover, we observed that genes 245 occupied with H2A.Z have higher levels of +1 nucleosome occupancy and lower -1 level of 246 nucleosome occupancy compared to genes that are devoid of H2A.Z (Figure 4C), implying 247 that H2A.Z deposition at +1 nucleosome is associated with high nucleosome occupancy and 248 low gene accessibility, while H2A.Z at -1 nucleosome is associated with low nucleosome 249 occupancy and high gene accessibility. To determine whether H2A.Z regulate gene 250 expression through affect gene accessibility, we divided the H2A.Z-bound genes into two 251

groups according to their nucleosome occupancy level (RPM  $\geq 1$ , RPM: read counts per 252 million) at +1 and -1 nucleosome, respectively, and compared their expression levels. We 253 found that the genes with lower nucleosome occupancy at -1 nucleosome had lower 254 expression level (Figure 4D) (p = 0.0076, K-S test), while the genes with lower nucleosome 255 occupancy at +1 nucleosome had higher expression level (Figure 4E) (p < E-30, K-S test), 256 indicating that nucleosome occupancy at -1 positively correlated with gene expression while 257 nucleosome occupancy at +1 negatively correlated with gene expression. Given that H2A.Z 258 is negatively correlated with gene expression levels, we proposed that H2A.Z deposition at 259 +1 nucleosome is required for repression of gene expression presumably resulting in higher 260 nucleosome occupancy at +1 nucleosome and inhibiting the access of positive transcriptional 261 machinery. Consistently with this, we detected the lowest levels of H3K4me3 and Pol II, 262 generated by the SET1 H3K4 methyltransferase (Ng et al., 2003) (Figure 2F and 2G), at +1 263 nucleosome in the lowest expressed genes that have highest H2A.Z and H3K27me3 264 enrichment at +1 nucleosome (Figure 2C and 2D). Furthermore, H2A,Z at -1 nucleosome is 265 associated with lower nucleosome occupancy (Figure 4C), which may lead to increased 266 accessibility to negative transcriptional machinery and is required to repress gene 267 transcription. Consistently, we examined highest levels of H3K27me3, generated by the 268 PRC2 complex (Schwartz et al., 2006), at -1 nucleosome in the lowest expressed genes that 269 have highest H2A.Z enrichment at -1 nucleosome (Figure 2C and 2D). When we examined 270 the signal of the five chromatin marks, H2A.Z, H3K4me3, H3K27me3, Pol II and 271 nucleosome occupancy together, we found that the peaks of all the signals were positioned-272 well after the TSS (Figure 4A and 4B and F-H), with H2A.Z and nucleosome occupancy 273 having the most similar expression profiles (Figure 4Ii-iii), implying a role for H2A.Z in 274 regulating chromatin accessibility. It is noteworthy that only mono-nucleosome-sized 275 fragments were gel purified according to the method (Petesch and Lis, 2008), which resulted 276 in the signal of nucleosome itself lower than that of H2A.Z (Figure 4Ii-iii). 277

# *arp6* mutation results in increased gene accessibility at +1 nucleosome and reduced gene accessibility at -1 nucleosome

To confirm the effects of H2A.Z on nucleosome structure and gene accessibility, we analyzed genome-wide nucleosome occupancy in *arp6*, a mutant causing deficiencies in H2A.Z incorporation (Figure 4A) (Deal et al., 2007). We identified 24,741 peaks of H2A.Z ("decorated" 19,090 genes) in *arp6* background. The results showed that the *arp6* mutant has lower nucleosome occupancy at +1 nucleosome while higher nucleosome occupancy at -1

nucleosome compared to WT (Figure 4B), suggesting the arp6 mutation results in increased 285 gene accessibility at +1 nucleosome accessibility and reduced gene accessibility at -1 286 nucleosome. Randomly selected four genes that show *arp6* reduced nucleosome occupancy at 287 +1 nucleosome and increased nucleosome occupancy at -1 nucleosome in MNase-seq 288 analysis exhibited similar changes in *arp6* compared to WT by MNase-qPCR assay 289 (Supplemental Figure 4A-4D). Although the majority of the H2A.Z peaks (58.5%) displayed 290 291 decreased intensity in *arp6*, we found that 24.4% of H2A.Z peaks showed a more intense peak in *arp6* (Figure 5A, Supplemental Table 10-11). The increased H2A.Z loci in *arp6* 292 mutant are enriched in the promoter, 3' UTR and exon, while H2A.Z reduced loci in arp6 293 mutant are mainly detected in promoter (Figure 5B). 294

To better visualize the changes of H2A.Z distribution between WT and the *arp6* mutant, 295 we subtracted the WT H2A.Z occupancy data set from the *arp6* H2A.Z data, so that a red 296 signal represents increased H2A.Z incorporation in *arp6*, and blue signal indicates decreased 297 H2A.Z incorporation in *arp6* (Figure 5Ci). We then sorted all transcripts according to their 298 signal level and generated a heap map using the metaseq software (Dale et al., 2014). The 299 amount of transcripts with reduced H2A.Z in *arp6* (below the white bar) was found to exceed 300 by far those with increased H2A.Z in *arp6* (above the white bar) (Figure 5Ci). The *arp6* 301 reduced H2A.Z sites (blue) are preferentially located immediately downstream of the TSS 302 (Figure 5Ci). 303

We then performed gene ontology (GO) enrichment using the Cytoscape plugin BINGO 304 program (Maere et al., 2005). We found that the top GO terms of *arp6* reduced H2A.Z loci 305 are in genes mainly associated with localization, developmental and biological processes 306 (Supplemental Figure 5A), in line with the developmental defects in *arp6* (Deal et al., 2005). 307 While *arp6* increased H2A.Z loci showed preference towards modification, DNA metabolic 308 process and response to stimulus (Supplemental Figure 5A). We further performed KEGG 309 pathway analysis using clusterProfiler program (Yu et al., 2012). In this case, results 310 indicated that *arp6* reduced H2A.Z loci are in genes mainly involved in glycometabolism and 311 biogenesis pathway, such as starch and sucrose metabolism (Supplemental Figure 5B). 312 Whereas, arp6 increased H2A.Z loci occurs at genes involved in amino acid metabolism 313 pathways, such as lysine degradation, valine, leucine, and isoleucine degradation 314 (Supplemental Figure 5B). 315

As we showed above, although *arp6* results in overall reduced H2A.Z occupancy, genes with increased H2A.Z level in *arp6* also exist. To avoid the disturbance from the *arp6* induced H2A.Z loci and better understand how H2A.Z repress gene expression through

affecting nucleosome structure, we compared nucleosome occupancy for the genes that 319 exhibits both reduced H2A.Z enrichment and up-regulation in *arp6* compared to WT. We thus 320 performed RNA-seq analysis using WT and *arp6* mutant inflorescence tissue and identified 321 differentially expressed genes using cuffdiff (Trapnell et al., 2012). Results showed that there 322 are 1,478 genes up-regulated and 949 genes down-regulated in *arp6* (Supplemental Figure 323 6A, Supplemental Table 12). A total of 864 genes with both reduced H2A.Z enrichment and 324 up-regulation in *arp6* were identified. We found that these genes also exhibited lower 325 nucleosome occupancy at +1 nucleosome and higher nucleosome occupancy at -1 326 nucleosome compared with WT (Supplemental Figure 6B and 6C). Taken together these data 327 suggested that H2A.Z deposition at +1 nucleosome causes high nucleosome occupancy and 328 low accessibility, while H2A.Z deposition at -1 nucleosome is required for low nucleosome 329 occupancy and high accessibility, which are important for H2A.Z repressing gene expression. 330

#### arp6 mutation widely affects the distribution of H3K4me3, H3K27me3 and Pol II

In mammalian cells, H2A.Z promotes H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 histone modification by 332 facilitating the access of the MLL and PRC2 complexes (Hu et al., 2013). To test if H2A.Z 333 deposition has any effect on histone modification and Pol II occupancy in Arabidopsis, we 334 performed H3K4me3, H3K27me3, and Pol II ChIP-seq in *arp6* mutant. The results also 335 showed that arp6 mutation caused overall reduced levels of Pol II, H3K4me3 and 336 H3K27me3 around TSS (Figure 4F-4H) with more decreased than increased loci of 337 H3K4me3, H3K27me3 and Pol II (Figure 5A, Supplemental Table 13-18). The *arp6* altered 338 H3K4me3, H3K27me3, and Pol II loci are mainly distributed in the promoter (Figure 5B). 339 H3K27me3 showed the highest percentage (69.1%) for reduction (Figure 5A). In light of the 340 mechanism revealed by mammalian system, it is possible that in plants H2A.Z may also 341 regulate H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 through affecting the accessibility of the complexes that 342 mediates the histone modifications. 343

To visualize the changes in chromatin patterns caused by the *arp6* mutation, we also 344 generated heatmaps using the metaseq software (Dale et al., 2014), and clustered the genes by 345 signal intensity. Our heat map results showed that the arp6 increased and decreased 346 H3K4me3 loci were far less than that of H2A.Z, and these differential H3K4me3 loci were 347 very specifically localized in the post-TSS < 500bp region (Figure 5Ci-ii). # H2A.Z-associated closed +1 nucleosome structure correlates with higher measures of gene responsiveness

Previous work has shown that H2A.Z deposition in gene bodies positively correlates with 395 gene responsiveness-the degree to which a gene is differentially expressed among different 396 condition or tissues (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman, 2012b). A recent study also suggested 397 H2A.Z enrichment in gene bodies correlates with higher responsiveness to drought-stress 398 (Sura et al., 2017). However, the role of H2A.Z at TSS in the control of gene responsiveness 399 has not been determined, although a weak negative correlation for H2A.Z at TSS and gene 400 responsiveness to drought-stress was detected (Sura et al., 2017). Our H2A.Z ChIP-seq and 401 RNA-seq data obtained from both WT and *arp6* provide an opportunity to analyze this 402 relationship more accurately. Given that the *arp6* mutation could cause reduced and increased 403 H2A.Z deposition, and altered gene expression levels but the dominant role of H2A.Z is 404 repressing gene expression, we postulated that using genes that exhibit reduced H2A.Z 405 406 enrichment and also up-regulated expression level in *arp6* for this correlation analysis is more appropriate than using genes that displayed altered expression levels, regardless of 407 whether their H2A.Z enrichment is increased. 408

Here, we define gene responsiveness as the transcription change between WT and *arp6*. 409 We divided the 864 genes showing reduced H2A.Z enrichment with up-regulated expression 410 levels in arp6 into three subgroups according to the change in their expression (log<sub>2</sub> fold-411 change; from 1 to 1.5, n = 318, from 1.5 to 2.5, n = 307, and > 2.5, n = 239). We found that 412 H2A.Z levels at +1 nucleosome positively correlates with gene responsiveness, genes with 413 414 higher responsiveness measures showing higher enrichment of H2A.Z at +1 nucleosome (Figure 6A). Moreover, nucleosome occupancy at +1 nucleosome is also positively correlated 415 with gene responsiveness (Figure 6B), suggesting that low accessibility of +1 nucleosome 416 associated with high H2A.Z deposition correlates with high measures of gene responsiveness. 417 Furthermore, we found that the high responsive genes have more H3K27me3 (Figure 6C) and 418

less H3K4me3 (Figure 6D) and Pol II at +1 nucleosome (Figure 6E). This supports our
finding that H2A.Z enrichment at +1 nucleosome regulates gene responsiveness by causing a
closed +1 nucleosome structure leading to reduced accessibility to the transcriptional
machinery.

#### 423 H2A.Z regulates enhancer activity likely by modulating histone modifications

To explore whether H2A.Z also affects chromatin accessibility to regulate the activity of 424 enhancers as it does in promoters, we evaluated nucleosome occupancy for the H2A.Z-bound 425 enhancers with decreased H2A.Z enrichment in *arp6* (Figure 7A). Our data showed that there 426 is no obvious difference of nucleosome occupancy (p = 0.304002, K-S test) (Figure 7B); 427 however, significantly higher levels of H3K4me3 (p = 5.14E-8, K-S test) (Figure 7C) and 428 lower levels of H3K27me3 (Figure 7D) (p = 6.06E-3, K-S test) for these enhancers in *arp6* 429 compared with WT were observed. These results suggested that H2A.Z regulates the activity 430 of enhancers most likely by modulating histone modifications rather than chromatin 431 accessibility. The expression levels of putative target genes of these H2A.Z-bound enhancers 432 with decreased H2A.Z enrichment in *arp6* are indeed increased in *arp6* compared to WT to 433 some degree (p = 0.362662, K-S test) (Figure 7E and Supplemental Figure 13A). We further 434 analyzed the H2A.Z and histone modification status for the enhancers whose putative target 435 genes' expressed levels are increased in *arp6* compared to WT (Figure 7F and Supplemental 436 Figure 13B). We found that these *arp6*-activated enhancers have significantly lower levels of 437 H2A.Z (Figure 7G, Supplemental Figure 13C) and higher level of H3K4me3 in arp6 438 compared to the WT (Figure 7H, Supplemental Figure 13D). In contrast, the levels of 439 H3K27me3 in these enhancers are also increased in *arp6* but much less significant than in the 440 441 case of H3K4me3 (Figure 7I, Supplemental Figure 13E). These results together implied that H2A.Z co-localizes with H3K27me3 to mark poised enhancers and H2A.Z represses 442 enhancer activity more likely by preventing the enrichment of H3K4me3 at enhancers in 443 Arabidopsis. 444

#### 445 **DISCUSSION**

#### 446 H2A.Z near the TSS represses gene expression in *Arabidopsis*

Genome-wide studies from yeast to human have provided strong evidence of H2A.Z
placement at the promoter regions of a large subset of genes across species, although the
precise distribution patterns differ slightly among eukaryotes (Cuadrado et al., 2010;
Guillemette et al., 2005). This non-random pattern of H2A.Z distribution is consistent with its

role in transcriptional regulation. In budding yeast and human cells, H2A.Z nucleosomes flank the TSS, forming a nucleosome-free region (NFR) at the TSS (Draker et al., 2011; Sarcinella et al., 2007). The presence of H2A.Z at gene promoters is negatively correlated with transcription levels in yeast, but positively correlated in human cells and in *Drosophila* (Blackledge et al., 2014; Stock et al., 2007).

In plants, previous studies (and this study), showed that H2A.Z peak is well-located at 456 the first (+1) nucleosome after TSS, similarly to the pattern exhibited in other eukaryotes 457 (Zilberman et al., 2008). Evidence indicates that the presence of H2A.Z within gene bodies is 458 correlated with lower transcription level, presumably by destabilizing constitutive gene 459 expression rather than promoting gene body DNA methylation (Coleman-Derr and 460 Zilberman, 2012a). A recent study also detected a negative correlation between H2A.Z at 461 gene bodies and transcription levels, and revealed a decreased occupancy of H2A.Z across 462 gene bodies upon drought stress dependent transcriptional activation, while a positive role 463 H2A.Z at +1 nucleosome for transcriptional activity for some genes (Sura et al., 2017). In this 464 study, we found that H2A.Z enrichment around the TSS including the +1 nucleosome peak 465 negatively correlates with gene expression levels (Figure 2C), similarly to the finding in yeast 466 (Guillemette et al., 2005). In addition, there are more negative than positive components as 467 shown by the Bayesian mixture model generated by the integrative analysis using H2A.Z 468 ChIP-seq and RNA-seq data in both WT and *arp6* conditions (Figure 3A-B), also suggesting 469 that H2A.Z around TSS mainly plays a negative role in regulating gene expression. Thus, we 470 concluded that H2A.Z at +1 nucleosome is required to repress gene expression. This notion is 471 in agreement with the previous conclusion (Kumar and Wigge, 2010) that in WT, at non 472 inductive conditions, the +1 nucleosome plays a role in maintaining transcription in a poised 473 state, as demonstrated by the presence of H2A.Z-containing +1 nucleosome limiting the up-474 regulation of gene expression of drought and heat induced genes (Kumar and Wigge, 2010; 475 Sura et al., 2017). This is also in line with studies in *C. elegans* and *Drosophila*, which have 476 suggested that the H2A.Z +1 nucleosome may maintain transcription in a poised state likely 477 by blocking passage of RNA Pol II (Mavrich et al., 2008; Whittle et al., 2008). 478

Our study detected a negative correlation between H2A.Z near TSS and gene expression levels in *Arabidopsis*, in contrast to previous reports that H2A.Z near the TSS is most enriched in the moderately transcribed genes (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman, 2012b; Zilberman et al., 2008). This discrepancy may be due to different tissues being analyzed in the previous study (roots, rosette leaves) and our study (inflorescence). An alternative explanation of the discrepancy may be related to different sets of genes being analyzed in the

different studies. In our study, we analyzed only the expressed genes (FPKM  $\geq$  1) rather than 485 all Arabidopsis genes. We divided them into four quarters according to their expression 486 levels, and found that H2A.Z around the TSS is negatively correlated with gene expression 487 level. Although our data suggest that H2A.Z near the TSS plays a dominant role in repressing 488 gene expression, we agree with the assumption raised recently that the presence of H2A.Z at 489 +1 nucleosome in some genes is required for maintenance of transcriptional activity (Sura et 490 491 al., 2017), as previously demonstrated in genes such as FLC, MAF4, MAF5, PRE1 gene family. (Cai et al., 2017; Deal et al., 2007). 492

#### 493 H2A.Z at +1 nucleosome positively regulates gene responsiveness in *Arabidopsis*

Previous studies showed that H2A.Z enrichment in gene bodies correlates with gene 494 responsiveness in plants (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman, 2012b; Sura et al., 2017). Because 495 the deposition of H2A.Z and DNA methylation at gene body show opposing correlations with 496 gene responsiveness (Coleman-Derr and Zilberman, 2012b), and H2A.Z and DNA 497 methylation are genome-wide negatively correlated (Zilberman et al., 2008), it was proposed 498 that a major function of gene body DNA methylation is to exclude H2A.Z from constitutively 499 expressed genes, and that H2A.Z deposition in gene bodies promotes variability in levels and 500 patterns of gene expression. Consistently, a recent study showed that the level of H2A.Z 501 enrichment in gene bodies positively correlates with gene responsiveness to drought stress in 502 Arabidopsis (Sura et al., 2017). However, the role of H2A.Z at the TSS in the control of gene 503 responsiveness has not been determined. Using the Spearman's rank correlation method, Sura 504 et al. (2017) analyzed all H2A.Z-containing genes, grouped them into percentiles based on 505 their responsiveness calculated as a change in expression level between control and drought 506 507 condition, and detected a weak negative correlation for H2A.Z at the TSS and gene responsiveness to drought-stress. However, the authors analyzed the relationship between 508 H2A.Z and gene responsiveness conditions only using WT materials in control and drought-509 stress. Therefore, it was not possible to test the relationship in detail and propose the actual 510 role of H2A.Z in control of gene responsiveness without analyzing the situation when H2A.Z 511 function is perturbed. Our H2A.Z ChIP-seq and RNA-seq data obtained from both WT and 512 *arp6* provide an opportunity to analyze this relationship more specifically. The previous study 513 and this study showed that the *arp6* mutation could cause both reduced and increased H2A.Z 514 deposition, and both reduced and increased gene expression, and that H2A.Z plays a 515 dominant role in repressing gene expression (Sura et al., 2017). To avoid the confusion with 516 the data indicating that an increased H2A.Z signal in *arp6* background is possibly mediated 517

by a SWR1 independent H2A.Z deposition pathway (Jeronimo et al., 2015), we focused only 518 on the genes that exhibit reduced H2A.Z enrichment and also up-regulated expression level 519 in *arp6* for this correlation analysis. We decided this approach would be more appropriate 520 than analyzing all H2A.Z-containing genes that displayed altered expression level without 521 considering the disturbance from the *arp6* increased H2A.Z loci. We revealed that H2A.Z 522 level at +1 nucleosome positively correlates with gene responsiveness (Figure 6A). Previous 523 studies of the roles of H2A.Z in regulating the expression of temperature-responsive genes, 524 phosphate starvation responsive gene and systemic acquired resistance genes also suggested 525 that the presence of H2A.Z near the TSS contributes to the responsiveness of genes in 526 Arabidopsis (Kumar and Wigge, 2010; March-Diaz et al., 2008; Smith et al., 2010). The 527 result that the most responsive genes have the lowest Pol II level (Figure 6E) is consistent 528 with the observations that the lowest Pol II binding genes were associated with the lowest 529 expression level (Figure 2G) and highest H2A.Z level (Figure 2A). 530

#### 531 **Dual effects of H2A.Z on the gene accessibility**

H2A.Z is implicated in transcriptional regulation in many organisms by controlling the 532 packing and unpacking of the chromatin to regulate DNA accessibility (Weber and Henikoff, 533 2014). In mice embryonic stem cells, H2A.Z deposition results in decreased nucleosome 534 occupancy at a subset of genes, leading to increased chromatin accessibility and providing 535 opportunities for the binding of transcription factors, which is required to maintain 536 pluripotency of embryonic stem cells (Hu et al., 2013). In contrast, Arabidopsis H2A.Z-537 containing nucleosomes wrap DNA more tightly than H2A-containing nucleosomes at a 538 thermosensory gene, HSP70 (Kumar and Wigge, 2010). Consistently, our genome-wide 539 540 studies revealed that H2A.Z deposition at +1 nucleosome is associated with low gene accessibility while H2A.Z deposition at -1 nucleosome leads to high gene accessibility. We 541 proposed that the Arabidopsis H2A.Z +1 nucleosome may play a key role in inhibiting 542 transcription by causing a closed nucleosome structure and decreasing local chromatin 543 accessibility, which may inhibit access to positive transcriptional machinery, and H2A.Z at -1 544 nucleosome may lead to open nucleosome conformation and increased accessibility to 545 negative transcriptional machinery and is required for repressing gene transcription. Support 546 for this model comes from work on the study of the function of H2A.Z in inflorescence 547 architecture control showing that reduction of H2A.Z deposition by arp6 mutation in 548 promoters and +1 nucleosome of *PRE1* and *PRE6* genes is associated with different effects 549 on nucleosome occupancy and different alternation of chromatin accessibility (Cai et al., 550

551 2017). Therefore, we suggested that H2AZ deposition in different genomic regions could 552 result in different genome accessibilities, which regulates gene transcription through either 553 inhibiting or facilitating the binding of both activating and repressive complexes, thus 554 potentially exerting a dual role in transcriptional control.

H2A.Z is highly enriched at the promoter regions of a large subset of genes across cell 555 types, consistently with a role in transcriptional regulation. Although the precise patterns of 556 enrichment differ slightly among eukaryotes, genome-wide studies from yeast to human 557 indicate that H2A.Z-containing nucleosomes flank the nucleosome-depleted region at the 558 TSS. In vitro studies have demonstrated that the +1 nucleosome poses a sizeable barrier to 559 transcription and can direct the orientation of the pre-initiation complex (PICs) and 560 subsequent transcriptional elongation (Nock et al., 2012; Rhee and Pugh, 2012). In 561 Drosophila, +1 nucleosome obstructs Pol II transit, resulting in the increased stalling and 562 backtracking of the polymerase (Weber et al., 2014). In line with this published evidence, our 563 genomic observations have shown that in WT the RNA Pol II peak corresponds to the region 564 occupied by the H2A.Z +1 nucleosome. Additionally, H2A.Z nucleosomes wrap DNA more 565 tightly at +1 nucleosome, which impede Pol II progress, indicating that in WT the H2A.Z +1566 nucleosome plays a role in maintaining transcription in a poised state. Knockout of ARP6 567 568 results in H2A.Z enrichment at +1 nucleosome being reduced, and the RNA Pol II at the +1 nucleosome is also decreased (Figure 4A and 4D), further demonstrating that the H2A.Z +1 569 570 nucleosomes may maintain Pol II in a paused state, which is consistent with studies in C. elegans, Drosophila and Arabidopsis (Kumar and Wigge, 2010; Mavrich et al., 2008; Whittle 571 572 et al., 2008).

Given the altered H2A.Z deposition in association with the dynamic changes of 573 H3K4me3, H3K27me3 and Pol II distribution and nucleosome occupancy in arp6, we 574 propose a model with WT Arabidopsis plants (Figure 8A), where H2A.Z deposits into +1 575 nucleosome resulting in higher nucleosome occupancy at +1 nucleosome, which may prevent 576 the access of a transcriptional activator, and lower nucleosome occupancy at -1 nucleosome 577 facilitating the access of transcriptional repressors to the promoter region, thus inhibiting 578 gene expression. In addition, the repressed gene expression by H2A.Z is associated with high 579 enrichment of H3K27me3, low level of H3K4me3 and obstructed Pol II transit at +1 580 nucleosome. In *arp6* (Figure 8B), H2A containing nucleosome is associated with a closed -1 581 nucleosome structure, which may inhibit access of transcriptional repressors, and open +1 582 nucleosome structure that may facilitate the binding of transcriptional activators and reduce 583 the high-energy barrier to Pol II progression, thus promoting gene expression. Moreover, the 584

#### 593 ARP6 may function in both SWR1-dependent and -independent manner

Actin and the structurally similar actin-related proteins (ARPs) are major components of 594 nucleosome remodeling complexes in the nucleus. ARP6 is an evolutionarily conserved 595 nuclear ARP (Jin et al., 2005; Kato et al., 2001; Ohfuchi et al., 2006). In the yeast SWR1 596 complex, ARP6 appears to form a subcomplex with SWC2, SWC3, and SWC6 and helps 597 bridge this subcomplex with a second one containing SWR1, to form the functional SWR-C 598 (Wu et al., 2005b). Additionally, H2A.Z deposition in *arp6* mutants is disrupted in many 599 species, which is consistent with ARP6 being a part of SWR1, and contributing to 600 transcriptional regulation by exchanging H2A for H2A.Z (Yoshida et al., 2010). 601

Some nuclear ARPs are thought to have functions beyond that of the remodeling 602 complex. ARP proteins, for example, ARP4 (BAF53 in mammals), are involved in multiple 603 complexes so that the phenotypes associated with ARP gene deletion are more extensive than 604 605 those provoked by loss of a complex-specific ATPase subunit. Indeed, ARP4 is essential for viability in yeast, and the protein is a component of both INO80- and SWR-C remodeling 606 607 complexes and NuA4 histone acetyltransferase, which carry out distinct nuclear functions (Galarneau et al., 2000; Harata et al., 2000; Sunada et al., 2005). Intriguingly, biochemical 608 fractionation suggests that even these three complexes do not account for the entire nuclear 609 complement of ARP4. Additionally, the human ARP8 is implicated in mitotic chromosome 610 phenotypes that could not be attributed to the hINO80 chromatin remodeling complex to 611 which it belongs (Aoyama et al., 2008). In budding yeast, ARP6 has functions independent of 612 its catalytic chromatin remodeling partner SWR1. ARP6 binds to multiple promoters and 613 subtelomeric zones at which SWR1 does not bind, including promoters of ribosomal protein 614 genes (RP). Transcript measurements showed that ARP6 alters RP gene regulation 615 independently of SWR1-mediated Htz1 deposition (Yoshida et al., 2010). In this study, we 616 showed that *arp6* mutation leads to a decrease in most of H2A.Z sites, but a subset of loci 617

showed increased H2A.Z (Figure 5A). This indicates that in addition to the major role of
ARP6 in promoting the deposition of H2A.Z, ARP6 may also exert functions independent of
SWR1 complex.

#### 621 H2A.Z is an important negative regulator of enhancer activity in plants

Enhancers play a central role in transcriptional programs by integrating signals (e.g., from 622 developmental, differentiation or hormonal stimuli) through the recruitment of specific 623 transcription factors and co-activator complexes to coordinately regulate gene expression. 624 Extensive studies have shown that H2A.Z plays important roles in transcription mainly by 625 controlling promoters and the gene body (Weber and Henikoff, 2014). Yet, our understanding 626 of its effect on enhancer activity is still in its infancy. Recent genome-scale studies have 627 reported that H2A.Z is present at enhancers that are more likely to be active in mammals 628 (Brunelle et al., 2015a; Hu et al., 2013). However, the role of H2A.Z in enhancer activity 629 control remains unknown in plants, as enhancers in plants have just been identified recently 630 (Zhu et al., 2015). Different from its function in mammals, our genome-wide observations 631 provide evidence that H2A.Z plays a negative role in regulating enhancer activity in 632 Arabidopsis. A schematic summary of our principal mechanistic conclusions at enhancers is 633 present in Figure 8. We found that H2A.Z is not present at all enhancers but rather its 634 presence marks the inactive ones, defined as having a significantly higher level of 635 H3K27me3, a marker for poised enhancers, lower expression of their putative target genes, as 636 well as higher level of nucleosome occupancy. These conclusions are drawn from the direct 637 comparison between H2A.Z-occupied and -unoccupied enhancers. 638

It has been demonstrated that H2A.Z serves as a regulator of enhancer function at a 639 640 subgroup of active enhancers by recruiting RNA Pol II to the enhancers, promoting the transcription of enhancer-associated noncoding RNAs and stabilizing the possible enhancer-641 promoter interactions (Brunelle et al., 2015b). The different combination of H2A.Z with 642 specific histone modifications may also contribute to the regulation of H2A.Z in enhancer 643 function (Hu et al., 2013). Our analyses support a role for H2A.Z in regulating transcription 644 by modulating histone modifications at enhancers. We showed that reduction of H2A.Z 645 enrichment in arp6 is associated with lower level of H3K4me3 and higher level of 646 H3K27me3, but comparable nucleosome occupancy. We further found that H2A.Z 647 enrichment at the arp6 activated enhancers dramatically declined, and the H3K4me3 648 drastically increased in *arp6* compared to WT. Thus, we proposed that H2A.Z inhibition of 649 enhancer activity is likely achieved by preventing the enrichment of H3K4me3 at enhancers 650

in *Arabidopsis*. In contrast, the study in mammalian cells showed that H2A.Z preferentially
co-localized with H3K4me3 at enhancers (Hu et al., 2013), and H2A.Z levels at enhancer
positively correlated with H2K27ac, a mark of enhancer activity (Creyghton et al., 2010;
Rada-Iglesias et al., 2011), suggesting its positive function in promoting enhancer activity in
mammalian cells. It will be of great interest to reveal the complex and new interlink between
H2A.Z and other epigenetic players in enhancer activity control in future studies.

signals unlikely to appear by chance (Zang et al., 2009). The clustering strategy enables to
identify dispersed epigenetic domains (e.g., histone modifications) from ChIP-Seq data with
high confidence. A sub-module of the SICER program was further used to predict genomic
regions that showed significant changes in chromatin accessibility from ChIP-Seq of *arp6*mutant under stringent parameters (FDR<0.001; FC>1). The peak annotation was performed
by R package ChIPseeker (Yu et al., 2015).

Chromatin states across the genome were defined using ChromHMM (Ernst and Kellis, 2012) with default parameters, except for binary size of 10bp of five chromatin marks. The ten ChromHMM defined states were used to calculate the percentage of the genome occupied by any mark. State enrichment in different genomic features was calculated dividing the percentage of nucleotides occupied by a particular state in a particular genomic feature by the percentage of nucleotides that this genomic feature represents relative to the entire genome.

Then the R language was used to plot the boxplot to 741 analyze the gene expression level between the two group genes. 742

#### 760 **RNA-seq data analysis**

Raw reads were filtered by removing the adapter sequences and low quality sequences. We used the *Arabidopsis thaliana* genome from Tair 10 as reference. The clean reads were aligned to the reference genome with Tophat (Trapnell et al., 2009). Alignment results were then processed using the Cufflinks (Trapnell et al., 2012) for gene quantification. Furthermore, Cuffdiff was used to identify the differentially expressed genes (fold change  $\geq 2$ , FDR $\leq 0.05$ ) between each sample.
