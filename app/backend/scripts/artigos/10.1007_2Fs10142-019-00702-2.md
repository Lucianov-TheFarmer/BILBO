# ORIGINAL ARTICLE

# Pod-shattering characteristics differences between two groups of soybeans are associated with specific changes in gene expression

Xiang Kang<sup>1</sup> & Jingjing Cai<sup>1</sup> & Yexin Chen<sup>1</sup> & Yuchuan Yan<sup>1</sup> & Songtao Yang<sup>1</sup> & Reqing He<sup>1</sup> & Dong Wang<sup>1</sup> & Youlin Zhu<sup>1</sup>

Received: 7 May 2019 / Revised: 10 July 2019 /Accepted: 22 July 2019 # Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2019

#### Abstract

Soybean is an economically important leguminous crop, and pod dehiscence of soybean could cause huge yield loss. In this study, we measured fruit-cracking forces and percentages of dehisced pods for ten soybean accessions, then separated them into two groups as shattering-sensitive (SS) and shattering-resistant (SR) soybeans. Pod transcriptomes from these two groups were analyzed, and 225 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between SS and SR soybeans. Some of these DEGs have been previously reported to be associated with pod dehiscence in soybean. The expression patterns of selected DEGs were validated by real-time quantitative reverse transcription PCR, which confirmed the expression changes found in RNA-seq analysis. We also de novo identified 246 soybean pod-long intergenic ncRNAs (lincRNAs), 401 intronic lncRNAs, and 23 antisense lncRNAs from these transcriptomes. Furthermore, genes and lincRNAs co-expression network analysis showed that there are distinct expression patterns between SS and SR soybeans in some co-expression modules. In conclusion, we systematically investigated potential genes and molecular pathways as candidates for differences in soybean pod dehiscence and will provide a useful resource for molecular breeding of soybeans.

Keywords Pod dehiscence . RNA-seq . Long noncoding RNA . Soybean

## Materials and methods

#### Plant materials and growth conditions

Seeds of soybeans were grown in the field in Nanchang (Jiangxi Province, China) under natural conditions. Materials for measurements of both shattering index and shattering mechanical force were collected from R8 stage (Fehr and Caviness [1977](#page-8-0)) soybean plants. Pods used for RNA sequencing were collected at 18 days after flowering.

#### Evaluations on pod dehiscence percentage and pod shattering mechanical force

The pod dehiscence percentage was analyzed according to the previous study (Romkaew and Umezaki [2015\)](#page-8-0). In brief, 50 brown pods (fully mature) were collected and kept in an oven at 60 °C for 7 h, then the number of shattering pods was counted.

Pod shattering mechanical force was measured using a digital mechanical force gauge (HANDPI, China) according to the previous study (Dong et al. [2014](#page-8-0)), and at least five pods were selected and measured for each soybean accession. There are three biological replicates per soybean accession.

#### RNA isolation, RNA sequencing, data analyses, and real-time PCR assay

For each soybean accession, ten pods that are removed seeds were used for RNA sequencing and real-time RT-PCR, and total RNAs were extracted from soybean pods using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Sequencing libraries were constructed through applying TruSeq RNA Library Prep Kit v2 (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) in accordance with the manufacturer's instruction and sequenced by ANOROAD (Beijing, China) on the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform. Differential expression gene (DEG) and lncRNA analyses were conducted according to our previous study (Yu et al. [2017\)](#page-9-0). In summary, low quality and adaptor sequences were trimmed using trim\_galore (version 0.4.4), and then clean reads were mapped to soybean reference genome Wm82.a2.v1 using tophat2 (Schmutz et al. [2010](#page-8-0); Kim et al. [2013](#page-8-0)) with following settings: -N 5—read-edit-dist 5. Raw read counts for all reference genes in Wm82.a2.v1 were calculated using featureCount in subread package (version 1.6.2) (Liao et al. [2014](#page-8-0)). Genes with very low expression values (CPM, counts per million > 1 in only one sample) were discarded before DE analysis. DEGs were detected using edgeR and selected with FDR (false discovery rate) < 0.05 between comparisons. LncRNAs were identified by assembling transcripts based on mapped reads and then discarding protein-coding transcripts according to steps illustrated in Fig. [4a.](#page-6-0) The co-expression network analysis for proteincoding genes and lncRNAs was performed using WGCNA with following parameter(s): soft power = 40 (Langfelder and Horvath [2008\)](#page-8-0).

For real-time qPCR, 1 μg of RNA was subject to reverse transcription reaction using the TransScript II One-Step gDNA Removal and cDNA Synthesis SuperMix kit (TransGen, Biotech). The qRT-PCR was performed as previously described (Wang et al. [2014](#page-9-0)), and the CYP2 mRNA was used as an internal control. The experiment was carried out with three biological replicates. Primers are listed in Table S1.

Therefore, we classified 015, 018, 044, 071, and 060 as shattering-resistant (SR) soybeans, and 055, 072, 038, 109, and 092 as shatteringsusceptible (SS) soybeans.

#### Differential gene expression analysis between shattering-resistant and shattering-susceptible soybeans

In order to investigate genes and potential regulatory networks associated with pod dehiscence in soybean, we performed RNA sequencing on pods from ten soybean accessions using Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform. More than 115 million reads were produced for ten soybean samples containing a total of approximate 34.7 billion base pairs (Table S2). When mapping clean reads to the reference genome Williams 82 (Schmutz et al. [2010\)](#page-8-0), high mapping rates, ranging from 67.9 to 87%, were achieved. Multidimensional scaling (MDS) plot was generated based on expression profiles of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between SR and SS soybeans (Fig. [2a](#page-4-0)), revealing relatively distinct expression pattern between SR and SS soybeans. In total, 225 DEGs were identified, including 188 upregulated genes and 37 downregulated genes through comparing SR with SS (Fig. [2b](#page-4-0) and Table S3). Then Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed on all 225 DEGs compared SR with SS soybeans. With respect to GO biological process terms, genes involved in "lipid metabolic process" and "lipid biosynthetic process" were significantly over-represented. Gene products located in "nutrient reservoir activity" were significantly enriched in these DEGs according to functional annotation of the GO molecular function category. And "membrane" was a cellular component term with the most significant enrichment (Fig. [2c\)](#page-4-0). These results indicate that genes associated with membrane formation process may play important roles in regulation of soybean pod dehiscence.

To validate the expression pattern of identified DEGs, we randomly selected four DEGs including Glyma.20G073400, Glyma.06G008100, Glyma.13G238500, and Glyma.19G231900 to examine their expression patterns by qRT-PCR, and the expression changes were consistent with the RNA-seq results (Fig. [3a](#page-5-0)). ATMSH1 encoding MUTL protein homolog 1 is the homologous gene of Glyma.20G073400 in Arabidopsis thaliana, and loss of its function would lead to phenotypic changes including dwarfed growth and reduced internode elongation (Xu et al. [2012](#page-9-0)). Homologous gene of Glyma19G231900 encodes an alpha/ beta-hydrolases superfamily protein in Arabidopsis thaliana, which is involved in the synthesis of poly-hydroxylated cutin monomers (Pineau et al. [2017](#page-8-0)). The functions of both Glyma.13G238500 and Glyma.06G008100 are still unknown. Transcriptional levels of these three genes including Glyma.20G073400, Glyma.06G008100, and Glyma.13G238500 were significantly higher in SR than that of SS, while the expression trend of Glyma.19G231900 was opposite (Fig. [3a\)](#page-5-0) (Glyma.19G231900, p < 0.05; Glyma.20G073400, p < 0.05; Glyma.13G238500, p < 0.05; Glyma.06G008100, p < 0.05; two-sample independent t test), which is consistent with the findings from our RNA-seq data. In addition, we studied expression patterns of two reported soybean pod dehiscence genes including SHAT1-5 and Pdh1 in these ten soybean samples (Dong et al. [2014](#page-8-0); Funatsuki et al. [2014](#page-8-0)). There was no significant expression difference of SHAT1-5 between SS and SR soybeans (p > 0.05, twosample independent t test). However, the transcriptional levels of Pdh1 were significantly higher in SS compared with SR (p < 0.01, two-sample independent t test) (Fig. [3b](#page-5-0)), suggesting that Pdh1 is involved in the regulation of pod dehiscence of soybean samples used in this study. Moreover, by examining the correlation between DEGs identified in this study and previously reported soybean pod dehiscence QTLs (Grant et al. [2010\)](#page-8-0), 11 (out of 188) upregulated, and 7 (out of 37) downregulated genes were found to be inside these QTLs (Table S4), further indicating that DEGs identified in our analysis were good candidates for the functional study of soybean pod dehiscence in future.

### De novo identification of long noncoding RNA from soybean pods

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNA) are widely existing in plant cell, and play important roles in plant development and physiology (Ariel et al. [2015](#page-8-0)), such as TE-lincRNA11195 involved in the plant response to abscisic acid (ABA) (Wang et al. [2017](#page-9-0)). We have comprehensively identified lncRNAs from our soybean pods RNA-seq data by applying a previously developed pipeline (Yu et al. [2017\)](#page-9-0). A total of 66,298 long transcripts greater than 200 nucleotide (nt) were assembled from all ten soybean samples at first. After removing potential protein-coding transcripts with multiple steps of strict filtration, we obtained 670 candidate lncRNAs of soybean pods (Fig. [4a](#page-6-0)). These 670 candidate lncRNAs constituted of 246 long intergenic ncRNAs (lincRNAs), 401 intronic lncRNAs, and 23 antisense lncRNAs. Then we analyzed expression patterns of these lncRNAs on ten soybean accessions and found that all three types of lncRNAs showed similar expression profiles in both SS and SR soybeans according to RNA-seq data, though some of them exhibited the sample-specific expression pattern (Fig. [4b\)](#page-6-0).

Next, some basic characteristics of lncRNAs were investigated. The majority of lncRNAs were less than 2 kilobase (kb) long, and most of them were transcripts with two exons (Fig. [4c\)](#page-6-0). # Co-expression networks of lincRNAs and genes in soybean pod

Previous studies have confirmed that numerous co-expressed genes usually appear in the same pathway or form complexes that regulate gene expression downstream in plants (Chen et al. 2018, D'haeseleer et al. 2000, Liao et al. 2011, Ruan et al. 2010). Therefore, we rebuilt co-expression networks using soybean reference genes and lincRNAs identified from our RNA-seq data, and 69 co-expression modules that represent clusters of transcripts with highly correlated expression pattern across ten soybean samples were reconstructed (Table S5 and Fig. S1). In these reconstructed co-expression modules, 41 modules included not only reference genes but also lincRNAs. There were 1 to 45 lincRNAs co-expressed with reference genes in different co-expression modules (Table S5). In addition, a number of these co-expression modules were observed to be over-expressed or under-expressed in one or several individual soybean samples (Fig. S2). Interestingly, we found that transcripts in a module "darkorchid2", containing 3 lincRNAs and 46 genes, showed overall over-expression in SR soybeans and under-expression in SS soybeans (Fig. 5a). # Discussion

Pod dehiscence is an important agronomic trait, and it could cause huge yield loss before soybean maturity. Although some soybean pod dehiscence–associated genes have been identified, the underlying mechanism of pod dehiscence is still not comprehensively explained. In this study, we measured two pod-shattering characteristics of ten soybean accessions and then separated them into two groups as SS and SR soybeans. SS soybeans containing 055, 072, 038, 109, and 092 had both remarkably lower fruit-cracking forces and higher dehisced pods percentages comparing with SR soybeans including 015, 018, 044, 071 and 060. We then identified 225 DEGs, including 188 upregulated and 37 downregulated DEGs, through analyzing RNA-seq data between SR and SS soybeans. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that genes associated with membrane formation process were overrepresented in these DEGs, indicating membrane formation process might be the cause of pod dehiscence difference between SS and SR soybeans.

More importantly, our study found that several DEGs were linked to known pod dehiscence QTLs in soybean. Eighteen DEGs were found to overlap with QTLs of pod dehiscence in soybean. One of them was a reported pod-dehiscence gene Glyma.16G141400 (Pdh1), further supporting that soybean samples selected here for pod dehiscence study are representative. However, SHAT1-5 was not detected as differentially expressed in our study, probably because SHAT1-5 expression could be evidently observed specifically in FCC (Dong et al. [2014\)](#page-8-0). Interestingly, we observed a gene named Glyma.19G231900 among these 18 DEGs, and its transcriptional levels in SS were higher than that of SR, which is consistent with the Pdh1 gene expression pattern between SS and SR soybeans (Fig. [3](#page-5-0)). The homolog of Glyma.19G231900 in

<span id="page-8-0"></span>Arabidopsis thaliana is AtEH1 encoding a cytosolic epoxide hydrolase involved in the synthesis of poly-hydroxylated cutin monomers (Pineau et al. 2017). Cutin is a key component of the cuticle, which forms the interface between the aerial part of the plant and its environment, and both its lipophilic character and chemical structure contribute to protecting plants from environmental stress factors that prevail in terrestrial ecosystems. It is known that Pdh1 regulates dehiscing force, the torsion of pod walls under low humidity (Funatsuki et al. 2014). Therefore, we speculate that Glyma.19G231900 is involved in Pdh-directed pod dehiscence regulation through controlling cutin synthesis in soybean, and effect of Glyma.19G231900 on soybean pod dehiscence is still needed to be studied thoroughly in future. Taking these results together, DEGs identified in this study would be valuable resources for investigating pod dehiscence differences in soybean.

LncRNA has been known to be involved in plant growth and development, such as overexpression of LRK antisense intergenic RNA (LAIR) increases rice grain yield and upregulates the expression of several LRK genes (Wang et al. [2018](#page-9-0)). In this study, we have identified 670 LncRNAs through analyzing pod RNA-seq data of ten soybean accessions. The comparison of these 670 lncRNAs with our previously identified 1575 lncRNAs from 13 seed samples showed that only 124 out of our 670 lncRNAs overlap with previously identified lncRNAs, indicating that most of these lncRNAs are potentially tissue- or developmental stage-specific expressed (Yu et al. [2017](#page-9-0)). Examination of the distribution correlation between lincRNA and its nearby gene showed that lincRNAs preferentially located within 10 kb of their corresponding adjacent genes (Fig. [4d](#page-6-0)), implying that soybean pod lincRNAs might play roles in the regulation of their neighboring proteincoding genes expression (Orom et al. 2010). Moreover, genes and lincRNAs co-expression network analysis showed that transcripts in some modules have distinct expression patterns between SS and SR soybeans (Fig. [5a\)](#page-7-0). Combing with functional annotations of co-expressed protein-coding genes, the functional roles of lincRNAs involved in soybean pod development might be inferred.
